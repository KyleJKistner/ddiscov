# Opportunity screening and commitment template

Use this record to compare a specific commitment under the [selection policy](selection-policy.md). Summarize its decision and link it from [current opportunities](current-shortlist.md). Keep supporting science in its existing dossier. Several records may concern the same asset when their deliverables or economic terms differ.

Label each material claim as observed, quoted, estimated or proposed, with a source and date. During screening, use best estimates and ranges for costs, time, success and economic capture; state the rationale and confidence. Use **unknown** where no defensible estimate is available and show how plausible alternatives affect the ranking. Identify the evidence that could reverse the conclusion.

## 1. Define the opportunity

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Identity | Record ID, exact asset/intervention, indication or genotype, route and intended benefit; link existing candidate identifiers. |
| Commitment | The next bounded tranche, its deliverable and the decision that deliverable changes. |
| Starting position | Available evidence, material, product, data and access; the development stage appropriate to this commitment. |
| Economic interest | The proposed or offered asset, option, license, equity, revenue participation, contracted payment or other interest ddiscov would retain. |
| Status | Proposed work, an actual offer, an authorized commitment or completed work; distinguish each clearly. |
| Decision stage | Broad screen, deeper desk shortlist, counterparty inquiry or commitment diligence. Match the evidence requirement to this stage. |
| Decision date | Assessment date, evidence cutoff and time-sensitive facts requiring verification. |
| Cash-recovery condition | Reference the private mandate for capital base, required net return, realization horizon and minimum acceptable probability; publish only established/unresolved status, not personal values. |

Choose the starting stage and form of participation on their merits. Existing articles, new synthesis, formulation, preclinical evidence and clinical development can each support a useful commitment. Describe the contribution and financial capture available for this particular package.

## 2. Bound the cost and exposure

| Input | Record |
|---|---|
| User capital limits | Keep these values in the private mandate: deployable project capital, maximum tolerable cumulative loss, losses already incurred against that limit, affordable loss per project, total concurrent exposure limit, reserve to preserve and acceptable liquidity horizon. These remain **unknown until supplied by the user**. |
| Incremental budget | New cash required from today, currency, payment timing, quote/estimate date, scope and uncertainty range. Treat historical spending as sunk. |
| All-in resources | Include materials, access, operators, product work, analysis, rights, transaction costs and overhead where relevant; state exclusions. Show operator effort and opportunity cost separately from cash. |
| Maximum committed exposure | Cash and binding obligations through the next genuine stopping point, including cancellation costs, contingent payments, overruns and dependencies. Explain how exposure is bounded. |
| Timing and liquidity | Time to an interpretable result, time to usable proceeds or handoff, cash tied up, and the basis for any recoverable value. |
| Portfolio effect | Capital remaining after failure; shared scientific, supplier, manufacturing, counterparty and financing risks with other commitments. |

Unknown capital limits permit further bounded analysis; they leave affordability unresolved. Reassess exposure when the scope changes or a nominally small tranche requires a larger inseparable commitment.

## 3. Explain how value is retained

Name the patient/product need, relevant alternatives and plausible demand. Identify the type of buyer, licensee, partner, customer or funder that could value the result, its next decision and the evidence supporting that relationship. Distinguish a plausible counterparty from demonstrated interest or an offer.

Record obtainable data, regulatory-use, product and commercial interests, their scope and status, and the access needed to perform the work. State any proposed terms, downstream sharing and dilution. A low-cost diligence tranche can resolve these uncertainties; a substantial commitment needs correspondingly stronger support for attainable interests and a realizable financial route.

For a self-funded new venture, begin with 100% founder equity and itemize separate asset-license costs, revenue sharing, collaborators and subsequent dilution. State why the prospective recipient would obtain this contribution from ddiscov instead of its existing team, rights holder or vendors. Identify the earliest credible payment event and whether new human work is actually necessary before that event.

Describe the remaining development, regulatory, manufacturing and financing path to proceeds. Value the share ddiscov can retain after those costs and terms. Explain which assumptions depend on a future transaction or financing and what happens if it fails.

Show the cash waterfall from company/customer receipts through required spending, senior claims, fees and taxes to investor distributions. Identify restrictions on grants and in-kind support, whether payments depend on favorable results, and how conforming but negative work, cancellation and collection are handled. Keep paper valuation, unspent capital and investor cash receipts distinct.

## 4. Compare outcome branches

| Branch | Decision consequence | Financial consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Success | Define an interpretable, decision-relevant result and the next action it enables. | Additional cash/time, obtainable transaction or financing, retained proceeds/value, dilution and remaining failure risks. |
| Failure | Define the result supporting stopping, narrowing or another stated action. | Loss through the stopping point, residual obligations and any supported recoverable value. |
| Inconclusive | Identify likely causes and whether a finite remedy could change the decision. | Remedy cost and exposure; the alternative of stopping with remaining capital. |
| Financing unavailable | Determine whether an affordable handoff, smaller continuation or stop is possible after a technically useful result. | Stranded capital, obligations, any revised terms and supported residual value. |

Assess separately the chance of an interpretable technical result, the chance it changes a consequential decision, the chance of a paid transaction and the chance of required financing. State dependencies and confidence. A screening probability can be a subjective analyst prior; identify its event, rationale and range, and preserve correlated risks in the scenarios. Do not present a prior as a measured candidate frequency.

## 5. Make the next-dollar decision

Compare this package with the best available alternative and preserving cash over a consistent horizon. Show estimated incremental retained net value, value per incremental dollar, timing and maximum exposure using coherent scenarios. Where proceeds are especially uncertain, show the retained cash needed to break even under stated probability assumptions. State a provisional rank or tie, the sensitivity of that judgment and the next desk check. Count future costs, dilution and residual value once.

State the best feasible action available without this tranche. Separate the current value of any already-held interest from the incremental value created or protected by the proposed spending.

Conclude with the next action, its rationale, the relevant loss constraint, and the specific evidence or terms that would reverse it. Keep later tranches as separate decisions.
