## BLU3797: a specific affirmative next step

The following BLU assessment is retained from round 6. Round 7 deepens the two clinical challengers and adds no new BLU efficacy or safety observation.

### Why the initial project is justified

Catalytic PIK3C2B genetics, exact-compound animal activity, a defined chemical article and public human-model precedents support a staged project. They do not already demonstrate exact-BLU human-force recovery. The joined upstream/final chemical route still needs an independently authenticated batch; the p.L463S patient model, p.E48*/corrected pair and independently constructed null routes need functional qualification in their intended use. [Prior human proof](../research/rounds/round5/blu3797-human-proof.md) · [Biology review](../research/rounds/round5/round5_blu_biology.md) · [Pharmacology review](../research/rounds/round5/round5_blu_pharmacology.md)

The next milestone is finite: begin with one specified disease/control system and a qualified molecule, establish a useful deficit and interpretable target engagement, then seek an exploratory function signal. Additional independent backgrounds and a fuller tissue window belong to confirmation. An unsuccessful material or assay qualification does not establish target failure. The [funding memo](../research/rounds/round6/round6_blu_milestone.md) specifies advance, redirect and stop branches.

### The mitochondrial challenge remains relevant but bounded

The October 2026 Science paper reports heart failure after **combined cardiomyocyte C2α/C2β deletion**. The accessible abstract does not settle single-isoform cardiac effects. The underlying Cambridge thesis shows mitochondrial fragmentation after individual β depletion in transformed cells, and its catalytic add-back experiment uses dual depletion. These are one research project, not independent replications, and neither is an exact-BLU toxicity experiment. [Science primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42848893/) · [Cambridge thesis](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/2aa25ead-0909-4db0-bf05-2aa16861a7bd)

Counterevidence matters: single-isoform perturbations retain vascular function in a separate dataset, and protein-retaining kinase-dead β mice have reassuring reported long-term histology. The recovered histology supplement lacks an animal denominator, while vascular redundancy cannot settle myocardial mitochondrial function. The appropriate next check is useful muscle function alongside mitochondrial function at the muscle-effective exposure, with C2α/VPS34 selectivity and genetic/chemical distinctions retained. Morphology alone is not a fatal result; equal-free-concentration culture overlap with unknown tissue distribution remains unresolved. [Integrated challenge and source limits](../research/rounds/round6/round6_blu_translation.md)

### Human and respiratory claims need their own anchors

A published adult local Mtm1-deletion model supplies a maintenance-disease route beyond juvenile development; it does not demonstrate BLU rescue. In an orthogonal DNM2 genetic experiment, near-normal resting breathing coexists with reduced maximal diaphragm force. Limb-muscle performance, resting breathing and respiratory capacity are different claims. [Adult and diaphragm evidence](../research/rounds/round6/round6_blu_translation.md)

The newly reviewed adult Unite-CNM baseline report uses the same 26-person cohort as an earlier hepatic analysis. Its MTM1 subset has substantial age/sex structure, and muscle/plasma DNM2 do not behave as interchangeable biomarkers. A figure-caption mean inconsistency remains unresolved. DYN101 was nonrandomized despite inconsistent wording in a later article. TAM4MTM's investigator transcript reports seven participants versus six in the older registry; no efficacy estimate is invented. Hepatic findings from those different articles inform monitoring and population choice without becoming evidence of BLU toxicity. [Clinical translation](../research/rounds/round6/round6_clinical_translation.md) · [Arithmetic and registry audit](../research/rounds/round6/round6_clinical_translation_audit.json)

## Enasidenib: a defined pediatric article/exposure and organ-function project

The clearest severe-cardiomyopathy observation remains one original child: LVEF 20–25% at baseline, 35–40% at six months and 40–45% at eight months, with subsequent fluctuation and concomitant care. The second original child had normal baseline systolic function. Gold's independent child had milder abnormalities. Strong D-2-HG suppression and meaningful reported function support development, while neurological reversal and neonatal prevention remain separate claims. [Clinical evidence and lineage](../research/rounds/round7/enasidenib_clinical.md)

Five outcome records across three publications do not establish a complete five-person census; linkage of two skin records is incomplete. The five-week-old 2026 report remains metadata-only. The 177-cell audit of Gold's one-child laboratory table finds adverse-date, reference-range and percentage conflicts that require a better prospective exposure/laboratory timeline. [Audit](../research/rounds/round7/enasidenib_clinical_audit.json)

Actual pediatric sprinkle use and a granted public minitablet disclosure remove the old generic formulation-absence premise. They do not prove historical-lot identity, current supply or an infant administration bridge. Current adult pharmacology includes slow accumulation, an active metabolite, interactions and plausible A3 antagonism; it supplies neither an infant regimen nor a validated lifelong safety boundary. [Product and staged proof](../research/rounds/round7/enasidenib_product.md)

The affirmative next investment is one defined pediatric article and linked exposure/PD/organ-function qualification package. A ready biochemical cancer-line route is not cardiac proof, and a new cardiac model is optional when it discriminates a real development question. Existing clinical programs and formulation claims make improved delivery, burden, exposure or clinical evidence the contribution to establish. [Independent challenge](../research/rounds/round7/review_enas_by_rux_clinical.md)

## Ruxolitinib: controlled useful HHD control with a specified article

Five distinguishable reports describe topical 1.5% use with different induction, background and maintenance contexts. The conference duplicate is excluded; a separate 1%+naltrexone case stays separate; the nine-patient preprint remains abstract-only and unpooled. The historical cases specify active/concentration/route, not every brand or lot. Useful ongoing control is the supported hypothesis. [Clinical dossier](../research/rounds/round7/rux_hhd_clinical.md)

A different oral-drug HHD trial had substantial improvement in its small placebo group. This is a design warning, not a ruxolitinib failure or a transferable placebo rate. A concurrent comparison is essential. The next proof preselects one meaningful clinical benefit, preserves patient-level and maintenance denominators, handles rescue/background changes and measures exposure alongside applied grams and skin state. [Development proof](../research/rounds/round7/rux_hhd_development_proof.md)

The June 2026 label and primary AD PK studies define the marketed article and show a high-exposure tail. They do not establish a safe HHD exposure threshold. A qualified vehicle-control route is one option; a randomized whole-product add-on comparison is a concrete alternative. Human clinical proof need not wait for a new immune-free ATP2C1 model. [Product/exposure and public-rights dossier](../research/rounds/round7/rux_hhd_product.md)

KB111 has a newer five-patient enrollment-complete update with dosing ongoing and no verified outcomes; guselkumab is another active HHD program. Existing formulation/composition claims and an active branded franchise remain relevant to value capture. A controlled dataset is worth creating on scientific grounds, while a standalone asset still needs differentiated benefit and an executable product route. [Clinical challenge](../research/rounds/round7/review_rux_by_enas_clinical.md) · [Product challenge](../research/rounds/round7/rux_hhd_product_review.md)
