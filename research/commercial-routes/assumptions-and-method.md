# Commercial-route comparison: assumptions and method

Review date: 9 November 2026 UTC / 8 November Los Angeles. Starting commit: `1aa3c918200179e0d1fb7764a2f316333ed7659d`. The user authorized comparing earlier licensing, contribution to an existing program, and opportunities with existing human evidence after questioning the assumed cost and need to sponsor a human trial. This is public-source research. No inquiry, registration, submission, purchase, clinical work or funding commitment is included.

## Objective and boundaries

The objective remains retained financial value with a high probability of capital recovery and survivable downside. Approximately $500,000 is a reference, not a hard cap; $750,000 was a scenario, not an authorization or a verified program price. The starting capitalization assumption for a new self-funded venture is **100% founder ownership**. Asset-license payments, sublicensing participation, royalties, debt, collaborators' interests and later equity dilution are separate variables requiring actual terms. Company ownership does not establish control of third-party technology.

Human trials are one possible development stage, not the default milestone. This comparison asks whether a smaller piece of work has commercial value before ddiscov finances new patient enrollment. It does not assume that avoiding a human trial automatically increases expected return, nor that a buyer will pay for an experiment just because it is inexpensive.

## What the earlier numbers established

The [erlotinib economic model](../phase2-program/erlotinib/economics-inputs.json) describes a lean, sponsor-funded human pilot. Its $672,000 independent-route total was:

- $225,000 product/CMC work: four analyst allowances of $30k, $70k, $65k and $60k.
- $115,000 site/visit/travel work: $30k setup/closeout, 75 assumed $700 visits, $17.5k travel, $10k contacts and $5k logistics.
- $180,000 other sample, data, regulatory, ethics, legal/access and sponsor/administrative allowances.
- Twenty percent contingency, taking the 15-person scenario to $624k.
- Four more participants at an assumed $10k each plus contingency, taking it to $672k.

The transfer scenario substitutes a $90k product allowance, reducing the 19-person total by $162k to $510k. $750k less $672k is $78k of unallocated capacity, not a validated reserve. These totals were **not built from a named CRO/CDMO, a geographic pricing model, quotations, documented comparable contracts or a sourced labor-rate build**. No Chinese, Indian, US or European procurement baseline was established. The cost verifier validates arithmetic only. Preserve the scenarios for traceability; do not reuse them as market-validated estimates for a different route.

The subsequent conversational 25–50% retained-interest examples were sensitivities, not expected founder ownership. The conversational approximately 80% failure estimate was an uncalibrated judgment, not the clinical model's output. The few-million-dollar exit discussion was a favorable transaction hypothesis informed by imperfect comparators, not an expected value. **None is an input to the current route ordering.** This qualification changes the strength of the financial conclusion; it does not erase the underlying clinical/product uncertainties.

## Compare a complete transaction hypothesis

Every route must identify:

1. The exact asset/use and what evidence already exists.
2. The specific missing result, product capability or right that an identifiable counterparty could value.
3. Why the counterparty would obtain it from ddiscov rather than its current team or vendors.
4. A bounded deliverable with acceptance criteria, exclusions and necessary source materials/data.
5. The intended payment event and retained interest, distinguishing a license, service fee, funded collaboration, grant, prize and equity financing.
6. Cash committed through the first meaningful stop, including non-cancellable obligations and timing of receipts.
7. The alternative if the result is negative, inconclusive, commercially unneeded or cannot be financed.

A public partnering page supports a prospect, not an available transaction. A public grant or prize supports a defined application opportunity, not certain revenue. Existing clinical evidence can reduce scientific uncertainty while leaving no unfilled commercial role. An option or license can preserve control without funding an entire development program, but an option fee alone does not purchase a buyer or a valuable result.

## Evidence and price labels

| Label | Meaning | Appropriate use |
|---|---|---|
| Verified public term | A current published fee, award amount or contract term with exact scope and source | Apply only to the described program/service; check eligibility and additional obligations |
| Public unit-price anchor | A dated rate for a defined service, customer class and unit | Useful for that component; not a full-program quote or regulatory qualification |
| Capability verified, price unresolved | Provider describes relevant equipment/service/site, but price and scope fit are unknown | Include in a comparative sourcing brief; do not insert a country discount |
| Explicit planning assumption | Analyst quantity, effort, rate, probability or timing with a stated rationale | Use sensitivities for screening, not an asserted market price or calibrated forecast |
| Unresolved | Evidence cannot support an amount or classification | Keep open; do not silently replace with zero or a favorable term |

At this screening stage, quotes are not a prerequisite for comparing routes. They become necessary before a material paid commitment. Where no defensible unit rate exists, the present comparison specifies quantities and deliverables and keeps the price unresolved rather than producing another precise total. A public external academic rate is not a substitute for validated regulated bioanalysis, a GMP lot, QA review, shipping or rights to proprietary methods.

## Retained cash and exposure

For a service contract, compare cash collectible under acceptance/payment terms with **all** delivery and subcontracting costs, owner effort, rework, insurance, tax and financing. A signed fee still has collection and delivery risk. Deposits and separately accepted milestones can reduce cash exposed; a promised reimbursement does not fund an invoice before it arrives.

For a license, distinguish cash payable at signing from contingencies, future royalties and a notional company valuation. Deduct actual license/sublicense obligations and transaction costs. An equity raise or money restricted to future trials is not founder cash recovery. A grant normally supports permitted research costs; an award or prize must be evaluated under its own conditions and is not necessarily a repeatable business.

The decision is the marginal commitment available today. Do not spend the whole reference budget because it exists, and do not assume that a negative early gate means the whole budget was lost. Equally, an early stop preserves unspent cash; it does not prove the drug or commercial hypothesis succeeded.

## Current public license-structure benchmark

[NCI Start-Up 3.0](https://techtransfer.cancer.gov/partnering/licensing-agreements/startup-license), updated June 2025 and checked for this review, describes a full exclusive patent-commercialization license with an initial two-year evaluation phase, a **$5,000 execution royalty**, a known share of ongoing patent expenses and negotiated downstream royalties. It applies to selected NCI technologies and qualifying companies; it is not an offer for erlotinib, APR-246 or any other candidate in this comparison. The fee is not the total cost of licensing or development. Applicant eligibility and technology suitability need confirmation later.

This is evidence that staged, ownership-preserving license structures exist, not evidence that a specific asset is available cheaply. The older generic NIH $2,000 evaluation-license description should not be substituted for current institute-specific terms. The [current NIH page](https://www.techtransfer.nih.gov/partnerships/nih-start-exclusive-license-agreements) explicitly notes decentralized, case-by-case availability. No application has been submitted.
