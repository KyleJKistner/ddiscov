# Local-product alternatives with a possible handoff before new human trials

**Reviewed 9 November 2026 UTC. Status: public-source comparison, not an offer, commissioned study or authorized expenditure.** The question is which existing human signal could support a specific product/data contribution that someone else would pay for, without ddiscov first sponsoring another clinical trial. Historical candidate ranks and hypothetical pilot budgets do not answer that question.

## Decision

**None of these three currently has a verified, available package that converts a bounded nonclinical investment into likely cash recovery.** The first desk priority is access to the existing APR-246/AEC article and rights, followed by a narrowly differentiated insulin product-gap test. These independent checks can run in parallel; the APR question has more concrete named artifacts to recover, while the insulin contribution still needs to be identified. Ocular DNase is primarily an existing-owner contribution hypothesis because the relevant institutional technology is already exclusively licensed.

This is an order for resolving missing information, not a claim that newer evidence displaced APR-246 scientifically. [The APR ranking audit](../phase2-program/apr-ranking-audit.md) remains intact. A small human series can support an attractive package if access, price and capture are favorable; a larger series does not compensate for having nothing differentiated to sell.

Priorities 1 and 2 can reverse immediately: a documented insulin product need with usable access and payment terms could dominate an unobtainable APR option; a paid, narrowly scoped DNase mandate could dominate both speculative assets. Preserve capital if none supplies a credible reason for payment. These rankings do not estimate Phase II success, financial failure probability, valuation or retained percentage.

No ddiscov-specific advantage in proprietary access, specialist execution or distribution has yet been established for these packages. A prospective buyer could license institutional rights directly or hire the same CRO. Merely funding generic work does not establish a profitable intermediary role; the contribution must create a demonstrable advantage or be contracted before execution.

## Evidence and the commercial constraint it actually resolves

### Insulin: broader human experience, increasingly occupied product space

The [existing local-product audit](../phase2-program/ranking-review/local-products.md#exact-topical-insulin--refractory-noninfectious-ped) retains the clinical evidence: the 2022 insulin/serum comparison was historical, with 61 insulin-treated and 23 serum-treated cases, mixed causes and unequal observation periods. The 2026 Lixi series used a different Humalog-derived article; high closure frequency did not demonstrate restoration of corneal sensation, and recurrence included a melting event requiring surgery. These support a focused clinical direction, not interchangeable evidence for any insulin presentation.

A newly checked 2026 primary stability report examined specified human-insulin formulations and packaging. Normal-saline and Liquifilm preparations retained measured concentration within the study criterion for **60 days unopened refrigerated and 15 days under simulated in-use conditions**; Systane preparations became turbid. This is not a validated commercial shelf life for our product, but it means that simply generating another “insulin eye drops can be stable” report is unlikely to create scarcity or a defensible asset. [S1]

EyeDura's July 2025 company disclosure identifies **EYED-002 for neurotrophic keratitis**, a preservative-free sustained-release platform and a claimed patent portfolio through 2044. It reports a preliminary ten-person healing result; this remains an uncontrolled company report, not independent confirmation or a comparator trial. The disclosure sought strategic collaborations, which demonstrates a partnering posture, not a request to buy ddiscov work. Its proposed Q3 2026 IND submission was a plan; this review did not verify that NK submission occurred. [S2]

Separately, the official record for **NCT07619482** now reports that Stanford's phase 1/2 EYED-002 **glaucoma** study actually started on 10 September 2026, with status “enrolling by invitation” and an update posted 13 October 2026. The registered product and indication differ substantially from the simple insulin/PED thesis. This verifies that a named proprietary insulin ophthalmic product has entered another clinical program; it does not establish efficacy in glaucoma or NK, or that the products are clinically interchangeable. [S3]

**Commercial inference:** the residual opportunity is likely a specific unresolved operating requirement, not broad discovery of topical insulin or generic claims of reduced dosing burden. A developer might pay for independently transferable process control, a suitable presentation in an underserved setting, or a demonstrable manufacturing advantage. No such unmet specification is verified here. Availability of inexpensive compounded treatment can also weaken premium pricing and exclusivity. New patents, commercial sterile production, regulatory reliance on historical treatment and demand would each need their own evidence.

### APR-246: preserve the human signal; determine whether the package is obtainable

The original 2020 report describes **two children** with AEC, aged nine and fifteen, treated with topical APR-246. One palm had a contralateral vehicle comparison; the other child's scalp treatment was uncontrolled. Improved covering and pain are relevant clinical observations. The study also found differentiation improvement without reducing p63 protein aggregation. Do not describe these patients as an independent replicated trial, proof of barrier restoration, or a correction of the causal mutation. [S4]

The [Round 25 product/model/rights audit](../rounds/round25/apr246-advancement.md) is retained rather than restarting preclinical discovery. It recovered a useful newer AEC model but **no APR rescue in that model**, no completed article transfer and no available license. A fresh generic cell screen is not automatically the next best expenditure after human observations.

The official US patent record previously verified in that audit is **US12521392B2**, issued 13 January 2026, naming INSERM, Université de Paris and AP-HP. Claim 1 includes AEC and ectodermal dysplasia affecting at least one cornea; dependent skin/cream claims retain that predicate. This is not an inference that skin use is free of rights. The audited European record, EP19740548, remained under examination, with a specific amended-claims document still requiring retrieval. Current title, scope, country coverage and licensability are unresolved. [S5–S6]

Imagine's current public technology-transfer dermatology catalog lists an ectodermal-dysplasia wound-healing patent with a title matching the audited APR family. This is a more concrete institutional route than an unidentified owner. The retrieved catalog entry does not specify APR-246, a patent number, available territory, exclusivity or terms: the family mapping is an inference to confirm, and a public listing is not a license offer. [S12]

Aprea's current Q3 2026 SEC filing records APR-246 patent-maintenance, regulatory and program-closeout costs, rather than ongoing trials. It also states that the compound's chemical structure is public-domain while its portfolio includes method-of-use and formulation claims and other application claims. **An inactive oncology program is neither an abandoned rights package nor proof that Aprea has rights over every proposed AEC use.** Applicability of each relevant family must be mapped. The oncology Phase III failure remains a different indication and regimen, not a new negative AEC result. [S7]

**Commercial inference:** APR is potentially more distinct as an optionable rare-disease program than another insulin formulation, but the stronger case requires a package: historical article records and access, relevant rights, usable clinical data, and a bounded bridge that a named class of licensee values. Disease rarity may facilitate focused development and simultaneously limit the revenue opportunity. No defensible eligible-patient count, net revenue forecast, willing licensee, or binding price is established here. Do not replace those gaps with a percentage of a broad dermatology market or an arbitrary royalty/equity assumption.

### Ocular DNase: credible evidence does not make an unoccupied asset

The retained evidence comprises a 2019 randomized severe tear-deficiency study (47 randomized) and a 2024 ocular-GVHD report (58 enrolled). The former's staining result does not imply a significant between-arm symptom result; the latter's registered primary staining analysis does not establish the claimed between-group clinical benefit by itself. Same-center studies and overlapping dates do not justify treating the summed populations as independent replicated evidence without participant-level reconciliation. The [retained-candidate audit](../phase2-program/ranking-review/retained-candidates.md) and [ocular team record](../phase2-program/teams/06-ocular-local-delivery.md) preserve those qualifications. [S8–S9]

Neutrolis announced an **exclusive UIC license for intellectual property covering topical DNase use in the eye on 23 December 2024**. Its proposed L304 is a novel DNase from its own platform, while investigator Sandeep Jain is a company adviser. UIC confirmed the relationship in April 2025. These are primary sources for the existence and stated scope of the transaction; they do not disclose the complete agreement or prove current sublicense availability. [S10–S11]

**Commercial inference:** the obvious combination of ocular-DNase rights, investigator expertise and prior clinical learning is already assembled elsewhere. A speculative ddiscov replication or reformulation could be useful science yet add little bargaining power. A specific owner-requested capability gap, ownership of a complementary invention, or an actual unserved territorial/field opportunity could change that conclusion. None is established by this review. Public company activity in other DNase programs is not evidence that L304 has reached a particular clinical stage.

## Scope that can be priced without hiding a human trial

The immediate output for each live hypothesis is an **opportunity specification**, not a laboratory purchase order: exact article/use, existing evidence and competing solutions, needed inputs, proposed output ownership, why the output changes a counterparty decision, and the unresolved access/payment question. All external work below remains proposed and unpriced. Execution duration, maximum contracted loss and time to any payment remain unknown until scope, access and terms exist; no development allowance is presented as a CRO quote.

| Work package if the commercial hypothesis survives | Units an operator should price | Acceptance and genuine stopping point |
|---|---|---|
| Insulin product/process transfer | Fixed reference and candidate presentations; number of batches, containers, storage/in-use conditions and time points; specified potency/impurity/physical/microbial methods; method transfer, raw data and final report. | A buyer-relevant specification that exceeds or differs usefully from existing public methods. Stop if equivalent performance is already available more easily. No new human-efficacy claim from a stability result. |
| APR existing-article bridge | Defined records and material transfer; identity/product comparability; relevant skin retention/exposure and local integrity measurements; one justified functional question if still decision-relevant; data/report rights. | Historical-to-new article linkage and resolution of a named transfer or translation uncertainty. Stop if access fails, the product cannot be bridged, or further required development falls outside an attainable handoff. |
| DNase contribution | The owner's named missing assay/data/product deliverable, with fixed samples/datasets, transfer destination, acceptance tests and IP schedule. | Accepted deliverable earns its stated payment or contractual consideration. Stop if the owner already has it, does not need it, or offers no credible capture. |

Country and vendor choice follow this specification. No Chinese, US or European CRO has been selected, approached or quoted in this work. Price comparison must include method suitability, sample/material access, quality records, output rights, transfer/rework burden, management time and cancellation terms. A nominally cheaper study that cannot be used by the intended recipient is not a cheaper package.

**Financial branches:** technical success still may produce no license or payment; inconclusive work may require an optional, separately priced remedy; unavailable financing can strand even usable data. Until terms exist, assume no recoverable sale value in a downside budget. A paid contribution can move market-demand risk before execution only to the extent the actual contract provides enforceable payment and covers delivery obligations. No model here assumes donated clinical work, automatically free public-domain regulatory data, or that contracting a laboratory assigns all rights to ddiscov.
