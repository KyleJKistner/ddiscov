# Sourcing and cost evidence for a nonclinical topical package

Reviewed 9 November 2026. Desk research only: no vendor has been selected, contacted or paid. This record concerns topical erlotinib for KRT16 Arg127Cys keratoderma and potentially transferable topical-development services. It does not propose a human trial.

## Decision

**China, India, the US, the UK and the EU contain plausible suppliers for parts of this work. Public evidence does not establish which would be cheapest for our exact package.** Medicilon, MedPharm and Nuvisan advertise relatively integrated topical development; WuXi AppTec DMPK and Vimta offer relevant testing capabilities. These are candidates for later comparison, not approved suppliers or quotes.

The earlier erlotinib allowances, including the $225,000 CMC subtotal, were planning assumptions rather than vendor-derived prices. This review does not convert them into quotes or replace them with another apparently precise total. It finds one genuine public analytical unit-price anchor, separates the purchasable work, and identifies inputs necessary for a defensible estimate. The $750,000 conversation scenario is not a required spend or an authorization.

For an early commercial route, the first question is **which missing product result a potential licensee or sponsor would use**, and whether ddiscov could own or license that result. A cheap formulation package without an attainable interest or downstream use may have little investment value. A laboratory service agreement also does not itself convey third-party compound, formulation or indication rights.

One company may offer several roles, but buying laboratory work does not require buying a clinical trial. Human tissue used ex vivo is also a different scope from dosing participants.

## One usable public price anchor — narrow scope only

The [UMass Amherst IALS core-facility rate page](https://www.umass.edu/ials/core-facility-rates), checked 9 November 2026, links FY27 approved fees and lists these **external/industry** mass-spectrometry rates:

| Published service | USD rate |
|---|---:|
| LC-MS analysis | $243 per sample |
| LC-MS setup | $150 per batch |
| Advanced acquisition/analysis staff | $245 per hour |
| Additional supplies | At cost |

The industry column establishes a commercial-user category, not acceptance of this project. The page says rates can change. Specific sample eligibility, contracting, confidentiality, data rights, method suitability and the distinction between research testing and regulated testing need confirmation. This is not an IVPT, stability, formulation, GLP or GMP quote.

For an eligible narrow research service, the published-rate arithmetic is `243 × N + 150 × B + 245 × H + supplies`, where `N` is billable samples, `B` is setup batches and `H` is separately chargeable staff time. Confirm inclusion rules to avoid double counting. Sample preparation, method development, standards, QC samples and repeats can materially change billable counts. We have not established those inputs, so no package total is calculated from this anchor.

## Scope that could actually be compared

These are proposed work packages, not statements that each is necessary. Select the package against the commercialization question before estimating or commissioning it.

| Package | Smallest useful output and decision | Keep separate |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Existing-product gap assessment** | Inventory the exact historical/current articles, formulation/process records, analytical data, stability evidence and rights of reliance. Produce a transferability/gap table identifying which records can be used and the one or two missing results that determine continuation. | No automatic new formulation, animal program, GMP batch or patient study. Existing records receive cost credit only when demonstrably usable. |
| **B. Bounded formulation/delivery comparison** | If A exposes a consequential product gap, define reference article, candidate article(s), intended skin compartment and a prespecified comparison. Deliver reproducible formulations/process records, fit-for-purpose analytical methods, interpretable release/skin-delivery results and a short stability screen sufficient to choose a lead, revise or stop. | An IVPT result does not establish clinical benefit or eliminate the need to bridge to the positive historical article. Disease-specific functional work is separately scoped if required by the next decision. |
| **C. Transfer or clinical-supply option** | Only after a recipient or justified next program requires it: transfer methods/process, manufacture the agreed batch, supply release/stability records and a usable data package. Price this as an option with a separate decision. | GMP manufacture is distinct from regulatory submission, trial setup, recruitment, patient visits, insurance and monitoring. None of those belongs silently in the nonclinical package. |

For B, an integrated proposal from Medicilon, MedPharm or Nuvisan could be compared with formulation plus testing split between suppliers, including WuXi DMPK or Vimta. This is a sourcing hypothesis: no provider's willingness to accept this exact small program is established. Splitting the work may introduce transfer, shipping, duplicate setup and project-management costs; integration may introduce a larger minimum scope. Neither is automatically cheaper.

An early license or sponsor-contribution route must additionally name the recipient's problem, the deliverable they could use, ddiscov's proposed rights and a plausible payment trigger. A completed laboratory report alone is not a cash-recovery milestone.

## Estimate method and geographic controls

Before comparing prices, fix the same article, quality level, output rights and scope. Otherwise a low number may simply omit work. At public-screening stage maintain three separate columns: **published unit price**, **explicit planning assumption**, and **unpriced dependency**. Missing prices remain unknown.

| Cost driver | Inputs to fix or vary in conservative/central/favorable cases |
|---|---|
| Article and formulation | API form, supplier/grade, reference availability, candidate count, batch size/count, containment, container and process records. |
| Analytical work | Analytes, matrices, extraction needs, method development versus transfer, qualification/validation purpose, standards, QC samples, reporting and raw-data access. |
| IVRT/IVPT | Question and comparator; method establishment; tissue type/source; independent donor count; technical replicates; time points; skin layers/receptor samples; recovery/mass-balance criteria; failed-run and repeat policy. Donors and cells are not interchangeable biological replication. |
| Stability | Product/batch count, conditions, time points, tests per pull, packaging and retention. A short screen is not a validated shelf life. |
| Transfer and quality | Research versus regulated output, audit/QA costs, method/process transfer, raw data, deviation reports, data reliance, ownership and rights to use background methods. |
| Geography and logistics | Named execution site and subcontractors; material/tissue procurement and permitted shipment route; temperature control, customs/brokerage, taxes, currency and shipping. Verify applicable restrictions for the actual transaction rather than presuming all shipments are feasible. |
| Human effort and changes | Vendor project management, ddiscov oversight, report review, additional iterations, delays and cancellation/termination obligations. |

Compute each scenario from `fixed package fees + Σ(quantity × applicable unit rate) + materials + logistics/taxes + separately identified options`. Document inclusions and exclusions; do not add a generic contingency percentage as if validated. Use named failure branches instead: failed analytical transfer, unavailable tissue, uninterpretable run or reformulation cycle. Show both the additional cost and whether it can be declined at a genuine stopping point.

For each supplier eventually compare the same **cash through the first decision**, **maximum non-cancellable exposure**, **elapsed time**, **ddiscov effort**, and **cost through the recipient-usable deliverable**. Quote currencies and dates must remain visible. Neither a national salary assumption nor an unsupported “China discount” substitutes for this comparison. Scientific, contracting and logistics differences can outweigh nominal assay fees.

## What this evidence changes

There are credible geographic sourcing alternatives, including Chinese and Indian laboratories. An early product package need not include patient dosing. The public evidence is sufficient to define a competitive scope, but insufficient to claim a particular vendor, an all-in price, a country saving, a licensable outcome or a high likelihood of recovering the investment.

The next public-stage task is to choose the commercialization question and fill the scope quantities from the product dossier. Provider pricing becomes decision-useful after that work. No inquiry is required to recognize the narrower route or to reject the unsupported precision of the earlier budget; no inquiry or commitment was made in this review.
