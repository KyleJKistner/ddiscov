# Contributing to an existing erlotinib program

Reviewed 9 November 2026. This is a public-source commercial screen, with no outreach, quotations, offers or commissioned work. The proposed use remains topical erlotinib for painful **adult KRT16 p.Arg127Cys keratoderma**, subject to the product and clinical limitations in the [erlotinib dossier](../phase2-program/erlotinib/README.md).

## Decision

**Investigate a narrowly defined contribution to an existing program before financing an independent clinical study. Do not yet treat that contribution as an investable or paying opportunity.** The strongest public lead is Sol-Gel: an existing topical product, human exposure experience and an explicit corporate willingness to consider out-licensing. This supports investigating access to an asset. It does **not** establish that Sol-Gel wants to buy ddiscov's work, lacks money, needs our proposed experiments, or will transfer attractive economics.

Imagine offers a second, concrete technology-transfer lead, with a pediatric topical-erlotinib patent in its public partnering catalog. Alembic's US orphan designation makes it a relevant stakeholder, but its exact development program and any contribution it might purchase remain unresolved. These are research priorities, not a numerical financial ranking. No public, erlotinib-specific paid development request was identified for any of the three.

The Sol-Gel product is not interchangeable with the historical compounded ointment merely because both contain erlotinib. The positive adult observations are not replicated adult SGT-210 efficacy evidence. The Darier result is contrary evidence for broad topical-platform extrapolation, although it does not itself establish failure in KRT16 disease. The [product](../phase2-program/erlotinib/product-cmc.md) and [rights](../phase2-program/erlotinib/ip-regulatory.md) reviews govern those separate questions.

Orphan sponsorship, patent ownership, rights to manufacture, rights to use data and commercial exclusivity are different interests. The EMA transfer history cannot identify all present owners or licensees. A proposed agreement must cover the particular interest being acquired; working alongside a named institution does not resolve the rest.

## A pre-human contribution that could be worth purchasing

These are proposed scopes, not claims of unmet sponsor needs. ddiscov's demonstrated contribution here is research synthesis and decision framing. It has not demonstrated an accredited laboratory, proprietary disease models, exclusive clinical access or a delivery technology. Buying a CRO experiment does not automatically create a differentiated asset.

| Work product | What makes it useful | Required inputs and acceptance condition | Financial limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Product-to-evidence and gap report** | Links each efficacy/safety claim to the exact article, genotype, treatment context and data; identifies one consequential uncertainty and a specific resolve/stop decision. | Relevant sponsor reports and article specifications under appropriate access; buyer agrees the uncertainty is unresolved and worth addressing. Deliver a traceable report, competing explanations and a costable follow-on scope. | The public literature review alone is readily reproducible. Do not presume that an unsolicited dossier has a buyer or an exclusive resale value. |
| **Targeted delivery/barrier package** | Tests an acknowledged uncertainty about local delivery, target engagement or detrimental barrier effects using the actual proposed article and relevant controls. | Sponsor-approved question; qualified model/operator; material rights; interpretable controls; written assay/data acceptance criteria. The [existing technical dossier](../phase2-program/erlotinib/README.md) supplies possible questions, not a ready-to-order protocol. | If the sponsor already has adequate data or the model cannot resolve its decision, the incremental value may be negligible. Laboratory execution and repeat-work terms remain unpriced. |
| **Analytical or manufacturing transfer package** | Makes an existing article reproducible or transferable where that is a documented bottleneck. | Access to formulation/process records, reference materials and an appropriate development laboratory; agreed specifications, methods, stability scope and transfer deliverables. | Established developers may already have this capability. Generic formulation development is not a demonstrated sponsor need. No authority to copy protected know-how is assumed. |
| **Permissioned existing-data analysis and study-readiness package** | Extracts a specific decision from existing individual-level data or establishes whether a later study is feasible and interpretable. | Lawful data access; prespecified analysis question; documented data quality; relevant investigator input. Any registry access, patient-derived material or new collection needs its own permissions. | This can improve the next decision but cannot manufacture prospective efficacy evidence. No recruitment, dosing or new human study is included. |

**The sequence matters:** establish the decision gap; agree the deliverable, access and economics; obtain comparable execution quotes; then commission the approved work. At present, only the public-source gap map can be completed without further inputs. A speculative laboratory package should not be bought simply because it can be performed before a trial.

## Four different economic arrangements

All prices, margins, milestone amounts, royalty rates and equity percentages below are **unpriced**. None has been offered. The former $60,000/$78,000 early-package allowances are not evidence of a sponsor's willingness to pay or current vendor prices.

| Arrangement | Cash direction and realizable event | Rights and downside that must be explicit |
|---|---|---|
| **Paid services or a fixed deliverable** | Sponsor pays ddiscov for accepted work, preferably through an initial payment and completion payments aligned with external bills. This is the most direct early-cash route if a real buyer exists. | Objective acceptance criteria; payment for properly completed negative results; work/fee cap; change orders; costs on early termination; who owns outputs; permitted subcontracting. Gross contract value is not profit. |
| **Sponsor-funded joint research** | Sponsor funds agreed research, potentially with a management fee or agreed margin. Restricted research funding is not cash available for the founder to withdraw. | Background and new IP, publication, data access, regulatory use, unused funds, overruns and termination. Any retained license or royalty is separately negotiated. |
| **ddiscov-funded research for an option, royalty or field interest** | ddiscov pays now. Cash recovery depends on a later option exercise, reimbursement event, milestone, sublicense or sales. | Enforceable interest before spending; who controls continuation; expiry; development obligations; dilution; reversion; rights if the sponsor abandons the program. This remains at-risk drug-development investment despite being called a partnership. |
| **License-in of existing article/data followed by development** | ddiscov may pay access fees and development costs; a later transaction would need to monetize the added evidence and rights. | Defined field, territory, exclusivity, supply, manufacture, reference rights, sublicensing, royalties, milestones and termination. Reusing an existing package could avoid duplication, but its completeness and terms control the saving. |

For a paid package, compare **contract receipts minus external execution, internal labor/overhead and financing/termination costs**, including timing and collection risk. Report contingent royalty/option value separately from cash. For every arrangement, separate ownership of ddiscov itself from ownership and obligations of the licensed asset. There is no evidentiary basis to assume that the founder must surrender 50–75% of their company or, conversely, that supplying funding earns a particular share of a sponsor's asset.

## Compared with an independent trial

An appropriately contracted pre-human contribution can put less cash at risk and bring payment forward: its payable event can be delivery of agreed work, rather than successful efficacy, financing and sale of a drug program. That advantage exists only after a solvent buyer and enforceable payment terms are established. Today it is a conditional route, not a supported probability of return.

An independent clinical program provides a different potential asset: controlled human evidence and greater development control, to the extent rights permit. It also requires product qualification, supply, regulatory and clinical execution, follow-up, financing and a subsequent buyer. These burdens cannot be eliminated by naming a cheap overseas CRO. Choosing a country or CRO does not resolve product ownership, efficacy uncertainty, or who ultimately pays for the result.

Neither approach warrants an invented failure percentage. A completed paid analytical task could meet its contractual goal even when its biological conclusion is negative; an informative negative clinical trial can still lose the investor most of their capital. These are different outcome definitions.

## A genuine funding call, but not a commercial exit

PC Project's current call accepts rolling LOIs during 2026 and advertises **Catalyst grants up to $50,000** and **Champion grants up to $100,000**. The former targets early-career independent investigators; the latter targets established investigators. Drug screening and repurposing are within the stated research scope. A selected LOI precedes an invited full application. The published 2027 cycle opens 1 January, with LOIs due 1 February. [S7–S8]

This is a real source to examine for a suitable investigator-led project. It is **not** a purchase order for ddiscov, an award commitment, or evidence that a for-profit applicant and its proposed subcontracting/indirect costs qualify. The accessible current call did not resolve those terms; retrieval of the older detailed guidelines failed. Eligibility, allowable costs, IP/data terms, payment timing and matching requirements must therefore be verified before it enters a budget. Funding would offset eligible project costs, not automatically repay previously invested founder capital or create profit. No grant application was submitted.

Targeted searches of the three counterparties' public program/partnering pages and PC funding sources located no specific open commercial request to buy the proposed erlotinib package. That is a search result, not proof that no private opportunity exists.

## Next evidence required and stopping rule

1. **Sol-Gel first:** determine whether adult KRT16 disease is an unaddressed development question, what existing data resolve it, and whether a contribution would be purchased, jointly funded or solely ddiscov-funded. Public out-licensing interest makes an access discussion plausible, not a paid-work thesis proven.
2. **Imagine second:** resolve the exact article, institution/owner map and available evaluation or license scope. Separate pediatric portfolio rights from the proposed adult use. A relevant investigator and model relationship could support a grant application, subject to eligibility.
3. **Alembic third:** resolve route, article and development status before designing work for its program.

For each, the decisive artifact is an agreed scope and commercial term outline covering the next payment, complete costs through that payment, rights and maximum loss. Subsequent private inquiries would require a separate authorized outreach step. **Stop before external experimental spending if no consequential gap, usable access or credible compensation/retained-interest mechanism can be established.** Preserve capital or compare another asset rather than completing an attractive but unsolicited study.
