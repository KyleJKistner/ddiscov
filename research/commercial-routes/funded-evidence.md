# ReGen evidence-package prize: a real route, no qualified nomination yet

Reviewed 9 November 2026. Research only: no registration, submission, contact, spending or new study. This is an ancillary evidence-synthesis opportunity, not a replacement for developing a drug asset or evidence that an asset can be licensed.

## Decision

**NIH has a concrete mechanism for paying for an evidence package without requiring the entrant to run a new human study. None of the five requested ddiscov packages is presently qualified for it.** The exclusion of new formulations is particularly decisive for topical erlotinib. The health-system-cost requirement also cuts against the rare-disease packages. These are program-fit findings, not lower scientific rankings.

The closest additional existing candidate is **unchanged generic timolol ophthalmic gel for diabetic-foot-ulcer healing**, but its clinical evidence remains problematic. Keep it as a bounded desk-screen question; do not label it an eligible or competitive submission. No hypothetical award enters portfolio revenue.

## Verified mechanism and limits

The [NIH NCATS ReGen Drugs Evidence Package Challenge](https://www.nih.gov/challenges/repurposing-generic-regen-drugs-evidence-package-challenge) offers up to **40 awards of $100,000**. NIH may change/cancel the challenge or make no awards. The [portal](https://regendrugschallenge.org/) gives registration dates of **2 November 2026–15 January 2027** and a **26 March 2027** package deadline; NIH lists winners on **2 July 2027**. An award is competitive prize income, not a purchase order, research grant, license or commitment to finance later development. Payment timing after selection is not established here.

The [priority rules](https://regendrugschallenge.org/challenge-priorities/) reject proposals requiring a new formulation, those lacking a clinical efficacy signal, and conditions costing the healthcare system less than $1 billion annually. Named priority areas include diabetes, cancer, chronic kidney disease and common cardiovascular, neurological and mental-health conditions. They favor a different disease class from the approved indication and uses that are not already commonly prescribed off label. A costly individual patient's treatment is not evidence of aggregate disease cost above the threshold.

The [judging criteria](https://regendrugschallenge.org/judging-criteria/) call for the **FDA-approved formulation** proposed for use and an analysis of benefit, safety, regimen and evidence. The [FAQ](https://regendrugschallenge.org/faqs/) additionally requires the drug and active ingredient to have no unexpired patent or regulatory-exclusivity protection. Separately, the judging criteria say a proposed-use patent has no bearing on evaluation; do not infer that any existing patent necessarily disqualifies every formulation without examining its scope.

The [submission instructions](https://regendrugschallenge.org/submission-details/) require clinical evidence beyond individual case reports or a single institution's experience. Existing trials or systematic real-world evidence can support entry; evidence gaps may be disclosed. The deliverable is a searchable PDF with a one-page overview, up to 14 evidence pages, and references/IP discussion outside that limit. NIH gives additional weight to published evidence generated by a team member; an outside literature synthesis does not automatically compete equally with its source investigators.

**Interpretation remains open for single-center trials:** the priority rules qualify the institutional-experience exclusion with absence of a clinical trial or systematic real-world analysis, while other wording is broader. A single-center randomized trial may therefore meet the evidence-form requirement. This review does not impose a blanket multicenter or independent-replication eligibility rule; scientific replication strength and organizer eligibility are separate questions.

Timolol does not receive an efficacy upgrade from challenge fit. The [existing audit](../phase2-program/ranking-review/retained-candidates.md#topical-timolol--chronic-diabetic-foot-ulcer-healing) records BAART's 8/21 versus 7/27 primary closure counts, without demonstrated superiority. A [2025 second-center trial](https://www.ijsurgery.com/index.php/isj/article/view/11154) randomized 30 patients but added local insulin with timolol; it cannot isolate timolol's effect. Nor should selected healed-wound follow-up be counted as an independent healing trial. These limit evidence strength even if BAART satisfies the evidence-form requirement; its single-center design alone does not establish ineligibility.

## Obtainable economics, rights and next commitment

An eligible individual/team/entity can compete. Under the FAQ, private entities need US incorporation and primary business; individual monetary recipients need US citizenship or permanent residence and must be at least 18. Nonqualifying foreign team members cannot receive prize money. Government-employment restrictions and a six-submission-per-person maximum also apply. **The user's/entity's eligibility is unknown.**

NIH's terms retain entrants' other IP but require nonexclusive rights to publish/share drug–disease pairs and practice the solution for government noncommercial education/research. Evidence packages themselves are not publicly displayed. Entrants must have rights to disclose/use the submitted materials. This is not confidential commercial-option negotiation, and authorship of a synthesis does not confer ownership of its underlying drug or clinical data.

No CRO is needed for this initial screen. No cash budget, quotation, winning probability or expected return is established. For any later proposal, count analyst/operator time, evidence retrieval, statistical/clinical review and permissible data access. A $100,000 prize cannot support $750,000 of attributable expenditure on prize economics alone; even a smaller outlay cannot be justified without a credible eligible entry and competitive assessment. Taxes, team allocation and costs reduce retained receipts.

**Stopping rule:** preserve capital unless a desk review clears an exact existing product/use, qualifying clinical evidence and patient lineage, disease-cost/class eligibility, current generic/protection status and entrant eligibility. For timolol, first resolve the full randomized evidence and exact incremental contribution; retain adverse findings. If these gates fail, stop this route. Any later registration or submission is a separate decision; this review authorizes neither.

Source-access note: the NIH announcement was retrieved through its current indexed official text after direct access failed. Portal rules were opened directly. No full patent clearance, application drafting or general search for a different prize-oriented candidate was performed.
