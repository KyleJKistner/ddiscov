# Commercial routes before funding our own human trial

**Decision date: 9 November 2026 UTC / 8 November Los Angeles.** This review compares a smaller asset package for licensing, a defined contribution to an existing sponsor, and opportunities with stronger existing human evidence. Six specialist reviews and an independent challenge inform the conclusion. This is completed public-source diligence; no inquiries, registrations, purchases or clinical work were undertaken.

## Judgment

**Prioritize access to an existing human-anchored asset and a specific commercial gap before buying further development.** The leading desk-diligence paths are APR-246/AEC article-and-rights access and existing topical-erlotinib product access. A differentiated insulin product remains a secondary hypothesis. These are information priorities, not a validated financial ranking or a recommendation to commit $750,000.

The earlier erlotinib pilot answered what a small clinical program might entail. It did not establish that financing that program is the best use of the user's money. A smaller pre-human package can preserve more capital and founder control, but only creates an investment opportunity if someone values the resulting rights, product or evidence. We found credible public access leads, **no paid sponsor mandate and no transaction demonstrating likely capital recovery**.

## Comparison of the three routes

| Route | What we could own or earn | Work before our own human trial | Route to cash and principal risk | Current disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **License an existing asset, resolve a defined gap, then partner** | An agreed field/territory option or license; transferable product/data rights and new work. A self-funded company starts with 100% founder equity. | Recover the existing article and usable records; resolve one material formulation, delivery or transfer uncertainty; assemble a complete handoff. | Upfront sublicense/assignment payment, possibly later milestones or royalties. Successful technical work can still have no buyer; license diligence obligations can force further spending. | **Best fit for continued drug-asset diligence.** APR/AEC and existing topical erlotinib supply the most concrete current questions. Access, price and pre-human buyer demand remain unverified. |
| **Deliver a defined contribution to an existing program** | A contracted fee, funded work or explicitly negotiated interest. These are different forms of consideration. | Sponsor-specified analytical, product or evidence task outside work already assigned, with accepted deliverables and usable source data/material. | A paid contract can reduce demand risk before execution. An unpaid contribution for speculative equity/royalties leaves us financing risk; a grant covers permitted research rather than guaranteeing profit. | **Potentially attractive if paid, but no actual paid assignment found.** Sol-Gel's licensing invitation supports an access prospect, not a services order. A services business also requires skills or access beyond owning capital. |
| **Join an asset with stronger existing clinical evidence** | Only the license, contract or investment interest that an existing owner actually makes available. | A necessary residual task; more human data do not automatically create such a task. | Payment or later licensing depends on a gap the established program cannot meet more easily itself. Existing sponsorship can reduce development needs while reducing our bargaining advantage. | **Reserve route.** Ocular DNase, mavoglurant, enasidenib, trametinib and azetukalner have no demonstrated unassigned commercial package for us today. |

The routes overlap: an existing-sponsor license can be the starting point for the first route. Compare the actual rights, obligations and next payment event, not the route's name. None has a defensible numerical cash-recovery probability from the current evidence.

## What the money would buy

There is no sound new all-in price to substitute for the old pilot estimate. The [cost audit](sourcing-and-costs.md) identifies Medicilon and WuXi capabilities in China, Vimta in India, MedPharm in the UK/US and Nuvisan in France. Their relevant offerings differ, and public materials do not supply comparable project quotations. We have selected no CRO and assumed no country discount.

The scopes are now separable: **A**, existing-product records and gap review; **B**, a bounded formulation/analytical/delivery package if the gap survives; **C**, later transfer or GMP supply only when the next user needs it. A and B do not automatically include clinical lots, patient recruitment or a trial. The priceable units, exclusions, acceptance requirements and stop conditions are specified in the specialist files. Missing prices do not prevent this provisional ordering; they do prevent claiming a particular package costs a particular amount.

Two public benchmarks illustrate possibilities without pricing our assets. NCI Start-Up 3.0 describes a $5,000 execution royalty plus patent expenses and negotiated downstream economics for qualifying licenses to selected NCI technologies. It is **not an APR or erlotinib offer**. The NIH ReGen challenge offers competitive $100,000 evidence-package prizes, but its formulation and other eligibility rules exclude or leave unqualified the five main repurposing packages screened. Timolol/diabetic foot ulcer is a closer scope fit with unresolved evidence; no submission is recommended. PC Project's grant call is also research funding, not proof of a commercial exit.

The [assumption audit](assumptions-and-method.md) supersedes the earlier conversation's financial framing:

- $500,000 is a reference and $750,000 a scenario, not an instruction to spend either amount.
- Founder equity starts at 100%; asset royalties, co-development consideration and future dilution require separate terms.
- Earlier $510k/$672k totals were unquoted human-pilot scenarios. Arithmetic checks do not validate market cost or operational feasibility.
- The conversational approximately 80% failure estimate and few-million-dollar exit hypothesis are not calibrated forecasts and are not used here.

## Completed output and next bounded work

This pass supplies a rights/product-gap comparison, eight candidate/use assessments, separate nonclinical work scopes, a named-provider map, price-provenance correction and a funded-evidence eligibility screen. It does not leave the next step as a generic request for quotes.

The next public-source deliverable should be **one paired access memo for APR and erlotinib**: exact rights and jurisdiction map, recoverable versus nonpublic product/data inventory, overlap with existing developers, a single proposed value-creating bridge per asset, and a specific pre-human payment hypothesis. Retrieve named outstanding documents rather than repeating broad scientific screening. If the missing facts are only nonpublic terms, records or willingness to deal, record those limits and stop the desk pass. That produces a narrow, reviewable basis for any later authorized inquiries; it does not authorize them now.

Commissioning B requires more than a plausible molecule: the proposed interest must be attainable, the experiment must resolve the intended recipient's decision, and spending must stop before an unfunded clinical obligation. If all plausible licensees require a new human efficacy trial first, compare that full clinical proposition afresh. Do not hide it inside an early licensing package.
