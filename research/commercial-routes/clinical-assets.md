# Existing clinical assets: earlier participation without sponsoring a new trial

Reviewed 9 November 2026. Scope: four existing programs with human evidence, assessed as possible alternatives to financing a new topical-erlotinib trial. This is public-source screening, not a claim that a transaction or paid assignment is available. No sponsor, investigator or vendor has been contacted.

**Conclusion:** none of these four currently supplies an evidenced, attainable investment package for approximately $750,000. Mavoglurant is the best structural example of an existing owner combining a licensed asset with publicly supported clinical development. Enasidenib offers a narrower product-gap question worth resolving. Trametinib already has funded clinical and analytical infrastructure. Azetukalner is a late-stage efficacy benchmark with an established commercial sponsor. This orders further investigation of participation routes; it is not an estimate of investment returns or a revision of their clinical-evidence ranking.

The relevant test is whether ddiscov can supply a necessary, differentiated result and receive an enforceable payment or economic interest. Paying for useful research, owning a service business and owning drug rights are different propositions. A wholly founder-funded new company starts with 100% founder equity; ownership of that company does not itself grant rights to any of these assets. Actual license burdens, investor dilution and revenue sharing require actual terms.

## 1. Mavoglurant: an existing financing route, not an unowned asset

The randomized cocaine-use-disorder trial enrolled 71 participants, treated 68 and analyzed 65 for the primary endpoint. The registered difference in proportion of cocaine-use days was −8.7 percentage points, with a 95% confidence interval of −16.1 to −1.3. Completion differed: 22/31 treated participants receiving mavoglurant and 32/37 receiving placebo completed. Observation through discontinuation and differential attrition matter when interpreting the result; this is not a replicated confirmatory effect. The 2025 publication does not constitute a second cohort. [M1]

STALICLA's January 2023 agreement acquired worldwide rights from Novartis across substance-use, neurodevelopmental and other indications. The announced consideration included upfront fees, equity, contingent milestones and royalties. The headline milestone maximum is neither a disclosed upfront purchase price nor a price available to ddiscov. In March 2023, STALICLA announced a NIDA CRADA under which NIDA would cover the planned Phase III trial under its own account. These are an existing owner and a financing structure; they do not establish spare funding or a subcontract available to us. [M2–M3]

The current sponsor pipeline describes modified-release STP7 development with NIDA. The Phase I cocaine-interaction study NCT06273540 now reports **completed**, with 21 participants and actual completion on 7 October 2024; its last posted update was 23 April 2026. No results are posted in that record. Completed conduct does not demonstrate a favorable interaction result or commencement of Phase III. [M4–M5]

**Bounded proposed deliverable:** a residual-work map matching public trial limitations and product requirements to the sponsor's already assigned work. A missing-data/retention analysis, endpoint qualification or product bridge is potentially useful only if the sponsor needs it, relevant data can be used, and it changes a live decision. Buying a CRO study before establishing those conditions creates no assured buyer.

**Commercial gate:** identify one unassigned deliverable, its accepting decision maker, permitted data/material use and a payment or rights route. A paid assignment would produce service revenue, not automatically drug royalties. Any option would need a defined field, background-IP access, diligence obligations, termination terms and downstream burdens. None is public. If there is no residual need, this case remains a precedent rather than an opportunity.

## 2. Enasidenib: resolve the existing-product question first

The 2023 clinical report concerns two children with D2HGA2, including one with severe cardiomyopathy. It reports biochemical normalization and clinical improvements, but is uncontrolled. A separate 2024 case describes treatment-associated liver abnormalities and a changed administration schedule. These observations support a disease-specific development question; they do not establish an infant dosing standard, controlled clinical efficacy or a financeable product business. [E1–E2]

It would be a substantive diligence error to infer an empty pediatric-formulation niche from adult tablets alone. The EMA's 2022 pediatric-plan decision for enasidenib identifies Bristol Myers Squibb as applicant and describes an age-appropriate oral sprinkle, pediatric pharmacokinetic/exposure work and AML development. The AML plan is not evidence that the formulation solves administration or exposure in D2HGA2 infants. Separately, NCT04310527 compared an investigational sprinkle with tablets in 28 healthy adults, completing in December 2019; the record has no posted results and was last updated in March 2020. It therefore supplies no public demonstration of equivalence or infant adequacy. [E3–E4]

**Bounded proposed deliverable:** establish whether a specific infant administration problem remains after access to the existing sprinkle is considered. The initial output is a product-requirements and evidence-gap assessment: actual required dose range, obtainable article, delivery method, demonstrated dose recovery, acceptable excipients and available exposure data. Only an evidenced gap would justify a subsequent laboratory qualification package. Laboratory delivery testing could answer a physical-product question without new human dosing; it cannot establish clinical exposure or safety equivalence on its own.

**Commercial gate:** determine who can provide the existing article and authorize use of its data, who would use an improved product or qualification result, and whether that party can pay or grant a relevant interest. The 2022 applicant and historical trial sponsor are leads, not a completed current chain-of-title or license analysis. A treating institution's scientific interest is not evidence of purchasing authority. Stop the product thesis if the existing article is adequate or there is no attainable commercial route. Continue an indication-development thesis only as a separately scoped proposition with its clinical obligations stated.

## 3. Trametinib: funded confirmation already includes the obvious support work

The retrospective multicenter study compared 30 treated patients with 31 controls and found fewer composite events with treatment. Historical controls, treatment selection, previous surgery and care-era differences limit causal interpretation; the analysis includes previously reported patients and is not an independent replication of all earlier case observations. It supports prospective study rather than a dependable investment-success percentage. [T1]

The October 2026 BabyMERIT announcement describes 25 treated infants compared with approximately 100 historical controls. Mount Sinai coordinates clinical work, Carelon Research/New England Research Institutes coordinates data, and Pediatric Heart Network core laboratories handle centralized imaging and biomarkers. NIH's award record confirms an initial Mount Sinai award of $946,567. The announcement's larger six-year figures include subsequent phases subject to approval; they are neither all cash available today nor a quoted price for our proposed work. [T2–T3]

**Bounded proposed deliverable:** first establish a distinct required output beyond the named teams' remit. Another generic registry, analysis platform, biomarker assay or historical-control dataset risks duplicating work the program already assigns. A needed gap might support an institution-approved contract; none was identified in the reviewed primary sources.

**Commercial gate:** the trial institution can potentially procure research services within its authority; it cannot be presumed able to grant the manufacturer's product rights. A license, service contract, grant subaward and philanthropic contribution have different economic consequences. Without a documented scope gap and an authorized payment route, this is not a reason to deploy capital. Its value in this comparison is demonstrating that stronger human evidence and public funding can coexist with little evident room for a new private contributor.

## 4. Azetukalner: preserve the benchmark, do not invent access

Xenon announced an NDA submission for focal seizures on 17 September 2026 based on its Phase IIb and Phase III program. Submission is not approval. The same update reported a temporary pause in new psychiatry enrollment during review of neuropsychiatric events, while epilepsy studies continued. Indication-specific evidence and safety context must remain separate. [A1]

Xenon reported approximately $1.2 billion in cash, equivalents and marketable securities at 30 June 2026. Its SEC filing explicitly says the company evaluates commercial partners for certain products, including azetukalner, and/or markets. It would be wrong to declare partnerships impossible. It would be equally wrong to treat that general statement as a purchasable $750,000 interest or an invitation to fund an independent experiment. [A2]

**Bounded proposed deliverable and commercial gate:** no differentiated small-capital development deliverable is established. A regional commercial partnership would require a territory, regulatory and distribution capabilities, supply terms and a negotiated allocation of obligations. None follows from the founder's available cash. Purchasing publicly traded Xenon shares is a separate securities decision, providing company-level exposure rather than an owned private development project; this review makes no securities recommendation.

## What changes the conclusion

The next useful evidence is **an actual incremental need with an attainable economic route**, not another favorable mechanism review. Public screening can reject duplicated or implausible packages before inquiries, while leaving scope, price and terms explicitly unknown.

For mavoglurant, the material unresolved fact is a sponsor-defined task outside the existing program. For enasidenib, it is a documented residual problem with the obtainable pediatric article. Trametinib needs an unassigned function despite existing funded infrastructure. Azetukalner needs a concrete partnership fit beyond financing capacity. Until such a fact emerges, all four remain weak capital-allocation cases. None supports a defensible expected-return, probability-of-cash-recovery or full development-cost estimate from the reviewed material. Geographic CRO selection cannot fix that missing commercial premise.
