## What changed

1. **Withdrawal exposes a product-design question:** The primary BLU3797 mouse paper states that function declined after drug cessation even though SQSTM1/P62 remained improved. Its use of “reversible” refers in part to the **reversibility of therapeutic effect**, not evidence of durable, drug-free anatomical repair. This makes longitudinal maintenance-versus-pulse testing and later-stage recovery a distinct high-information gate, in addition to the existing acute force/engagement/VPS34 test. See [BLU durability review](blu3797-durability-human-anchor.md).
2. **Human XLMTM histology gives a discordant efficacy anchor:** In the AT132/ASPIRO human biopsy substudy, organelle location and later fiber size improved during clinical respiratory/motor benefit, while central/internal nucleation and triad counts did not show significant change. BLU3797's mouse central-nucleation improvement must not become a required human response biomarker or a surrogate for force. This is an *external modality* and overlapping ASPIRO patient source, not BLU efficacy. See [BLU review](blu3797-durability-human-anchor.md).
3. **The ASP2957 competitor is now more precisely defined:** Astellas's active phase-1/2 VALOR/ASP2957 program is a single-infusion MTM1 gene-replacement trial for approximately nine boys at or below 36 months with severe invasive ventilator dependence (at least 20 hours/day, tracheostomy) and stringent hepatobiliary criteria. First patient was dosed by the 27-Apr-2026 communication; **no ASP2957 efficacy estimate was located**. Unlike ASP2957's current trial subset, older survivors and gene-therapy-ineligible patients are an *unproven*, plausible testable differentiation hypothesis for a safely repeatable oral PIK3C2B inhibitor. The earlier AT132 results are real human efficacy *and* fatal hepatobiliary toxicity for that exact predecessor, not an ASP2957 outcome. See [competitor review](aspiro-asp2957-product-challenge.md).

## Decision integration

**Keep BLU3797 first as a staged proof, not S-tier; no promotion from this work.** Its mouse pharmacology is more encouraging than a bare developmental biomarker story, but neither its short-course mouse functional recovery nor its apparent mouse morphological normalization solves chronic rescue in mature human disease. Existing equal-free-exposure, target engagement and C3 preservation requirements stay in force.

The **new discriminating package** adds (i) longitudinal absolute force/fatigue after a pre-established deficit, (ii) a vehicle/continuous/pulse-and-washout/rechallenge comparison under individually measured exposure, (iii) an older or more mature qualified disease state versus an immature model with the same positive delayed-functional rescue control, (iv) independent donor backgrounds and donor-level uncertainty, and (v) clinically anchored efficacy readouts that do not depend on normalization of central nuclei, bulk PI3P or p62 alone. A durable off-drug benefit is not compulsory: a **safe, useful and sustained on-treatment benefit** could still be a development proposition, but would raise the importance of chronic tissue selectivity, tolerability and practical administration. If only an early developmental intervention works, narrow the patient claim instead of advertising later-life reversal.

**Competitive allocation:** ASP2957 and historical AT132 remain *external* comparator programs, not independent owned discovery leads. The 2023 AT132 substudy is from ASPIRO and must not be counted as an independent trial from its main ASPIRO report. No strong new shortlist reserve was promoted. Exact8015-P2, NDC-9009, AAT and pantethine/PPCS decisions remain unchanged; no new functional evidence for them was claimed.
