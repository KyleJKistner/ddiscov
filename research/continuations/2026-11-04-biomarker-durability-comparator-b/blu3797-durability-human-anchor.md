# BLU3797: withdrawal durability and human histology as a translational stress test

**Evidence checked 2026-11-04.** A primary-source synthesis to test a product-level hypothesis not resolved by the prior source-cell, VPS34 or basic human-force reviews. This is not a new efficacy measurement, a dose recommendation or evidence of molecular harm.

## Question and bottom line

**Question:** Does short-course BLU3797 exposure produce a maintained gain in already-deficient muscle, a transient on-drug functional effect, or a stage-dependent developmental correction—and what human histology should be used as evidence of benefit?

**Conclusion:** The original paper directly shows a withdrawal-associated decline of measured mouse function; it does not show durable drug-free rescue. The paper's “fully reversible” phrasing should not be read as durable repair. Independently, human ASPIRO biopsies show that some classic XLMTM pathology markers, particularly central/internal nucleation, can remain abnormal during clinically meaningful improvement with **a different modality**, MTM1 gene replacement. This argues for a function-first, longitudinal, stage-stratified human assay rather than optimizing BLU dose for central nuclei, p62 or fusion. The evidence cannot tell whether BLU must be continuous in patients, whether its mechanism changes with age, or whether a drug-free improvement is impossible.

## A. Exact mouse experiment and denominator discipline

The [Shearer et al. 2026 JCI Insight primary article](https://insight.jci.org/articles/view/198568), DOI [10.1172/jci.insight.198568](https://doi.org/10.1172/jci.insight.198568), has **three distinct evidence units**:

* **Daily-dose disease rescue:** `Mtm1−/y` mice treated orally with the exact S-BLU3797 structure from postnatal day P22. In the round-4 source workbook audit, P25 wire-hang values for disease vehicle were n=5, mean **8.08 s**, and for BLU3797 nominal 25, 50, 100 mg/kg once daily were n=6/6/7, means **27.31/37.68/60.0 s**, respectively. All seven 100 mg/kg entries were at the **60-s assay ceiling**, so equivalence to wild-type absolute force is not demonstrated. By P29 values in surviving treated cohorts were 11.50/28.08/57.99 s, n=6/6/7; the source table lacked matched P29 disease vehicle entries. Those figures are **source-data descriptive means**, not new inferential estimates or proof of per-muscle specific force.
* **Late-start/withdrawal experiment:** Article Results discuss starting **P22 versus P25** at reported **75 mg/kg oral BID**, with improved viability and wire-hang function and, **following drug withdrawal, similar functional decline** in both start arms. Supplemental Fig S6A has a regimen discrepancy: one source-workbook field describes the P22 arm as `BIDx5 and QDx3`, whereas the supplement caption describes five days BID; this was already flagged in [round-4 audit](../../rounds/round4/blu3797-audit.md). The released Fig S6D contains **two** populated identical baseline values (15.9 and 6.4 s) in each start group; at their later sampled assessment P27 (P22 start) and P30 (P25 start), **five** functional entries each, averaging 59.84 and 53.46 s. Remaining later denominators fall and animal IDs linking force, PK and survival are unavailable. Those measurements show a plausible late-start response, **not** a clean within-animal responder trajectory or unbiased comparative durability estimate.
* **Three-dose versus five-dose miRNA experiment:** Treatment starts **P22**, every **36 hours**, three versus five doses in separate arms. The authors associate continuous treatment with normalization of selected miRNA and SFRP1 readouts compared with earlier stopping; these are **transcript/protein differences**, not independent muscle force replicates or human molecular pharmacodynamic validation. The article states p62 remained reduced even after late-start withdrawal while measured function declined. Thus the dissociation argues against using an isolated terminal p62 response as a substitute for maintained force. No isolated pulse/washout/rechallenge experiment with linked individual PK and direct force measurements is established by these summaries.

The paper itself characterizes these functional effects as “reversible”; its Results explicitly describe **decline after final dose**. This does not demonstrate irreversible structural damage, tachyphylaxis, rebound toxicity or a particular pharmacokinetic half-life. Decline could reflect loss of target inhibition, renewed disease progression, natural history in sick survivors, attrition, other time-dependent variables or combinations. The already known missing timed **unbound** muscle/plasma concentrations prevent attribution to occupancy loss.

### Consequence for the development claim

There are at least four empirically distinguishable models:

| Model | Useful intervention hypothesis | Discriminating observation |
|---|---|---|
| A. Ongoing inhibition | Benefit exists while C2β is engaged; withdrawal loses benefit | Force tracks free muscle exposure/engagement and is regained after re-exposure, at a tolerable repeat-use window |
| B. Developmental pulse | An early pulse permits persistent maturation without exposure | Function remains improved after verified washout and normalizes only when treated in a developmentally competent window |
| C. Early-only rescue | Newly developing diseased myotubes respond; later established weak tissue does not | Early tissues improve but matched mature, **positively modifiable** tissues do not, despite equal free engagement |
| D. Unrelated assay/cohort effect | Apparent benefit overstates true contractile gain | Effects change after measuring absolute twitch/tetanic force, fatigue, geometry and selection/attrition at donor/animal unit |

The mouse paper modestly favors that **ongoing inhibitor exposure matters** in this disease setting, but does not identify one model as exclusively correct. A drug that requires ongoing dosing can still be highly useful if the chronic safety and exposure case works. Repeated lifelong exposure, however, makes the [VPS34 disease-specific antitarget](../2026-11-04T0900-0800/blu3797-vps34-antitarget.md) and neural, liver and heart windows substantially more relevant than if a brief pulse were curative.

## B. Independent human functional–histological evidence: AT132 is not BLU

[Lawlor et al., *eBioMedicine* 2024 (online 12 Dec 2023), DOI 10.1016/j.ebiom.2023.104894](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10758703/), investigated muscle biopsies from **ten boys** with XLMTM treated in the ASPIRO trial with **resamirigene bilparvovec (AT132; rAAV8-Des-hMTM1)**. Biopsies were obtained before treatment and at **24 and 48 weeks**; **nine** 48-week biopsies were evaluable for fiber-size improvement. This is **same AT132 ASPIRO patient program**, not independent trial corroboration.

Observed sequence in that report:
* **24 weeks:** organelle localization improved markedly, whereas many fibers had not yet enlarged.
* **48 weeks:** larger myofiber size in nine evaluable biopsies; improved organelle localization persisted.
* **Not significantly changed:** internal/central nucleation, numbers of triad structures, fiber-type distribution and satellite-cell counts in the reported analysis.
* Clinical motor/respiratory improvement in the parent clinical program occurred over this period; the biopsies do **not** prove which single histological variable mediates improvement.

The [Shieh et al. *Lancet Neurology* 2023 ASPIRO primary trial report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37977713/), DOI [10.1016/S1474-4422(23)00313-7](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(23)00313-7), contains meaningful improvement in ventilation and motor function **and** treatment-associated fatal cholestatic liver failure. It had seven low-dose recipients and seventeen high-dose recipients after delayed allocations, fourteen untreated control participants including twelve INCEPTUS controls, and four treatment-group deaths (one lower, three higher dose). Its modified design and exploratory as-treated analysis prohibit a simple randomized efficacy interpretation. The histology substudy overlaps the clinical trial; do not count efficacy twice.

**Consequence for BLU:** An effective *different* disease therapy did not reliably fix internal/central nucleation. Therefore the fact that BLU corrects central nuclei in mouse muscle is neither sufficient for functional benefit nor an obligatory human target-product criterion. This observation does **not** show BLU will improve fiber size, organelle distribution or force; modalities have different mechanisms and pathology trajectories. It is a calibration of biomarker validity, not cross-article evidence transfer.

### Functional hierarchy for the proposed BLU proof

1. **Primary:** Human MTM1 deficient versus corrected/healthy tissue **absolute force**, specific force normalized with validated geometry only as a companion, fatigue and contraction kinetics, measured pre-deficit and followed longitudinally. Report the independent **donor/genotype/clone/batch** structure rather than wells/images as independent n. Define minimum useful improvement and assay variability beforehand.
2. **Functional orthogonality:** Qualify twitch/tetanus response, stimulus-to-force transmission, appropriate calcium kinetics and/or co-culture neuromuscular performance; show that the assay responds to a delayed effective positive intervention after the same established deficit.
3. **Mechanism/exposure:** Independently authenticate exact S-BLU3797; same-sample **PIK3C2B engagement** and **VPS34 function** at measured **free** cellular/muscle concentration and duration, with orthogonal chemical/genetic perturbation as feasible.
4. **Secondary morphology:** Organelle localization, fiber caliber, membrane trafficking, central nuclei, bulk PI3P, SQSTM1/p62, myostatin, miRNAs and myoblast fusion. Mechanistic context, not replacements for the primary function endpoint.
5. **Durability and stage:** Continuous on-drug versus matched short-pulse/washout/rechallenge after a definite deficit in an early differentiating tissue and a more mature model; contemporaneous vehicle and healthy controls, as well as a positive delayed rescue control. Choose time courses from **verified exposure clearance/target de-engagement**, not arbitrary nominal washout hours. Do not interpret a failed re-challenge if material degraded or the tissue lost contractile responsiveness.

**Advance:** A practical sustained on-treatment force/fatigue gain in genetically relevant, already weak tissue with a measured feasible exposure window; off-drug durability is a separate value enhancer rather than a required success. **Narrow:** Only early postnatal/differentiation benefit implies a development-phase indication rather than unqualified older-child restoration. **Redirect chemistry:** Genetic C2β rescue plus BLU chemistry-specific target failure or C3 overlap. **Downgrade target:** Two independent qualified mature human disease backgrounds fail under verified C2β inhibition while a same-stage functional positive control succeeds. **Uninterpretable:** Negative assays without demonstrated post-deficit modifiability, stable untreated trajectory or exposure.

## C. Unresolved uncertainties; do not convert into negative data

* Human muscle-force efficacy of exact BLU3797: **not measured** in cited human models.
* Link of the same mouse's force, dosing history, and precise total **or free** PK: **not established** in released workbook.
* Mouse plasma levels are sampling-time-undocumented reported levels, not proven free muscle/brain/liver/heart concentrations; no inference of human dosing or trough.
* PIK3C3 NanoBRET published 14,366 nM remains incompatible with the released all-zero response rows; no measured relevant-exposure VPS34 safety window.
* Physical stock, fully qualified human mature model, rights and FTO: separate unknowns. Public structure and joined route are stronger facts than receipt or clearance.
* Human ASPIRO histology does not establish BLU mode of action, nor does AT132 hepatobiliary toxicity establish BLU hepatotoxicity.
