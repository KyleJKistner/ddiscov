# Competitor challenge — does the updated treatment landscape displace BLU3797?

**Checked 4 November 2026.** Purpose: independent falsification of the rank-one **next-proof value**, not a claim of clinical/financial rank. No outreach, purchasing, trial recruitment or rights assertion.

## 1. ASP2957 is an active **dosed** competitor, not only a registry entry

The [Astellas company-authored community letter dated 27 April 2026](https://myotubulartrust.org/astellas-pharmaceuticals-announce-dosing-of-first-patient-with-x-linked-yotubular-myomthepathy-in-valor-gene-therapy-trial/), republished in full by Myotubular Trust, states that the **first participant had been dosed in VALOR** with investigational gene therapy ASP2957. This strengthens the existing registry characterization from *recruiting* to publicly confirmed first dose. It does **not** supply efficacy or survival findings.

The same letter says **AT132 remains on clinical hold**, and Astellas recognized a financial impairment to prioritize ASP2957. The sponsor expressly says the impairment decision was **not due to a new scientific or safety finding**. Thus it is wrong to infer either that gene therapy is generally unsafe from this letter or that the program has been abandoned.

[VALOR, NCT07052929](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07052929), official registry last posted 3 September 2026 (according to the round-5 check), is a phase-1/2, small, male, ventilator-dependent, age-≤36-month trial with important cholestatic exclusions. The registry and letter are distinct sources for **status and actual dosing**, not independent clinical outcome cohorts.

**Portfolio impact:** a systemic inhibitor must show a real benefit-risk and value proposition versus a one-time gene replacement program. A hypothetical niche in older patients, people ineligible for gene therapy, repeatable oral dosing, reversibility or delayed rescue is a *differentiation hypothesis*; it is not a validated free commercial population or evidence that BLU is safe in liver-vulnerable individuals. Astellas's progress increases the value of a short, sharp human-function proof and reduces the value of funding diffuse biomarkers only.

## 2. Pantethine/PPCS remains a plausible challenger, but the causal bridge is still thinner than the headline

For comparison with BLU, read the exact [Zhang et al. 2025 PPCS study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43856-025-01017-z), the [round-5 human-function review](../../rounds/round5/ppcs-clinical-function-challenge.md) and [chemical-species challenge](../../rounds/round5/round5_pantethine_chemistry_challenge.md).

| Dimension | BLU3797 / XLMTM | Pantethine-labelled / PPCS cardiomyopathy |
|---|---|---|
| In-human disease evidence | A qualified **human 3D force deficit** exists as a model route; **no BLU-rescued human force result** | Clinical improvement observed among treated PPCS patients, confounded by standard cardiac care, natural history and overlap; two relevant patient-derived cardiac backgrounds have functional observations |
| Exact chemical entity | Specified S isomer with joined public synthetic route, **no received independent authenticated lot** | 2025 paper names *pantethine* yet lists **Sigma 16702**, currently catalogued as *pantetheine*; bath species and the oral active exposure bridge require analytical resolution |
| Most direct functional result | Genetic kinase inactivation rescues mouse force; exact BLU's animal hanging/survival and cell fusion do not equate to mature human force | Treated patient cardiac patches showed stabilization of decline, **not significant force increase**, with other partial cellular and clinical improvements; clinical series uncontrolled |
| Key discriminating proof | Post-deficit human force/fatigue benefit with C2β target engagement, C3 preservation and measured vulnerable-tissue exposure | Confirm actual reagent and active species, demonstrate human-plausible heart exposure and post-deficit force/fatigue or rhythmic function in distinct patient backgrounds; controlled clinical attribution remains difficult |
| Value capture | Patent and program ownership unresolved; clinical gene-therapy competition now dosed | Common nutritional substrate route; a differentiated deliverable species/formulation/response strategy must do more than replicate supplementation practice |

**Conditional reallocation:** Pantethine/PPCS should move ahead if an independently authenticated oral-plausible active species produces robust post-deficit human cardiac force/rhythm rescue in multiple backgrounds, *and* there is a believable differentiated route. A positive case report alone or a 500 µM parent bath alone is not enough. Conversely a BLU failure in mature human muscle despite proven contemporary target suppression and delayed-function positive controls would downgrade BLU and increase the relative value of this human-evidence challenger.

## 3. The central relative conclusion

BLU retains the **higher-information staged mechanistic test**: rare genotype, causal target genetics, exact mouse-active article and available 3D force readout. Pantethine has closer-to-human clinical observations and better apparent practical starting exposure familiarity, but a less certain exact active chemical species and a confounded heart-function effect. ASP2957 is a **dosed active development competitor**, not a candidate asset in this portfolio and not a demonstrated clinical efficacy benchmark.

Nothing in this continuation upgrades BLU, pantethine, or a gene-therapy comparator to S-tier; the limiting evidence remains *different* in each case. Do not conflate actual first dosing (27 April 2026), registry update (3 September 2026), a published paper date, and the present review date.
