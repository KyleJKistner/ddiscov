# BLU3797 continuation — PIK3C3/VPS34 as a disease-context antitarget

**Evidence checked: 4 November 2026, 09:00 Pacific; literature-only analysis.** This is a cross-source qualification of the published BLU3797 PIK3C3 selectivity problem against prior MTM1 epistasis. It adds no experiments, retrieved physical lots, owner communication, or new pharmacokinetic measurements. The [round-5 pharmacology audit](../../rounds/round5/round5_blu_pharmacology.md), [biology review](../../rounds/round5/round5_blu_biology.md), [human proof](../../rounds/round5/blu3797-human-proof.md), and controlling [shortlist](../../../decisions/current-shortlist.md) remain authoritative on their separate facts.

## Executive decision

**PIK3C3 is more than a generic PI3K selectivity-panel entry in MTM1 disease: its genetic reduction worsened survival in the same Mtm1-null setting that benefits from PIK3C2B reduction.** This turns the unreconciled BLU3797–PIK3C3 NanoBRET result into a *disease-mechanism-specific qualification*. It does **not** show that BLU3797 inhibits PIK3C3 at useful exposure or that BLU3797 is unsafe. The source-released PIK3C3 dose responses are all zero; the published finite 14,366 nM value is not independently reconstructible. Both the apparent genetic antitarget and the lack of an actual compound-specific C3 curve must stay visible.

**Allocation:** keep BLU3797 first as a staged human-function proof, but explicitly pair the benefit/engagement comparison with an independently qualified *PIK3C3 preservation* test at the same achieved muscle exposures. If C3 off-target activity appears at the muscle-useful concentrations, qualify a different C2β scaffold, rather than treating any pharmacological failure as a target failure. Do not promote to S-tier.

## A. Disease-context genetic evidence: opposing direction of effect

The primary [Sabha et al., *JCI* 2016](https://www.jci.org/articles/view/86841), DOI [10.1172/JCI86841](https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI86841), directly contrasts kinase gene reductions in myotubular myopathy:

| Exact perturbation and disease context | Observed unit / result | What it demonstrates | What it cannot demonstrate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Muscle-specific **Pik3c2b** deletion in **Mtm1-null** mice; other P21/P30 post-symptomatic inducible Cre cohorts | Strong prevention and post-onset improvement in the reported animal phenotype | C2β is a plausible compensating target; timing evidence extends beyond pure embryonic prevention | No BLU3797 concentration–response, kinase occupancy–force curve, or proof of mature human rescue |
| **Pik3c3^fl/+;Acta1-Cre** in **Mtm1-null** mice, versus Mtm1-null controls | Median survival **28 days**, **n=5**, versus **32.5 days**, **n=8**, reported **P=0.01** (Fig. 5B). More severe symptoms; LC3 alterations (not a full flux measurement). | Reducing class-III PI3K in this disease/genetic context can worsen an important organism-level endpoint | This is lifelong *gene-dosage* perturbation with a small cohort, not a 14 µM drug effect, acute kinase occupancy threshold, or independent human drug-safety result |
| **Pik3c3^fl/+;Acta1-Cre**, without Mtm1-null | No obvious clinical or histological phenotype in the examined mice | Disease background modifies sensitivity | Does not establish the degree of partial kinase inhibition that would be safe in humans |
| **Pik3c3^fl/fl;Acta1-Cre** in Mtm1-null | Authors recovered **one live-born double KO**, severely affected and dead by 3 weeks; missing expected double-KO offspring | Additional warning for profound genetic depletion | Embryonic lethality was *suggested*, not experimentally proven. One survivor cannot estimate a penetrance or dose response |

**Important comparison:** PIK3C2B and PIK3C3 both contribute to PI3P, but membrane localization and biology differed. The 2016 study reported reduced perinuclear PI3P staining after C2β reduction versus diminished membrane-associated staining after C3 reduction. Therefore bulk PI3P reduction is not a valid common efficacy surrogate. The authors proposed impaired autophagy as one possible basis for the C3-worsened phenotype; LC3 data alone do not settle this mechanism.

An independent [Sabha et al., *American Journal of Pathology* 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4044722/), DOI [10.1016/j.ajpath.2014.02.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajpath.2014.03.012), characterized **Pik3c3^fl/fl;Ckmm-Cre** mice. Quadriceps retained ~11% of WT PIK3C3 protein, with whole-muscle PI3P about half of controls. These mice developed dystrophic skeletal muscle pathology, **severe dilated cardiomyopathy**, and died at **median 54 days, n=11**. The study suspected cardiac involvement in mortality but did not definitively establish the cause of death. Because **Ckmm-Cre also targets heart**, the phenotype cannot be described as a skeletal-muscle-exclusive consequence. This model supports an essential function for C3 in striated muscle/heart at *large lifelong genetic reductions*, not a tolerability margin for an adult small-molecule inhibitor. C3-null liver/heart experiments in [Jaber et al., 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3277541/) add organ-system rationale, but are not pharmacological BLU experiments.

**Human-stage extrapolation caution:** the 2014 quadriceps qPCR data show developmental changes in class-II/class-III PI3K expression, with higher Pik3c2b and Pik3c3 expression in older sampled mice. This is bulk transcript, not protein activity or human data. It motivates comparing immature and later-state human muscle rather than assuming P22 mouse and day-27 engineered tissues cover chronic older-patient treatment.

## B. The exact BLU3797 antitarget assay is unresolved, not negative or positive

The [Shearer et al., *JCI Insight*, 9 April / 22 May 2026](https://insight.jci.org/articles/view/198568), DOI [10.1172/jci.insight.198568](https://doi.org/10.1172/jci.insight.198568), reports **PIK3C2B NanoBRET IC50 68.2 nM** and **PIK3C3/VPS34 NanoBRET IC50 14,366 nM**. The nominal quotient is **~211-fold**, *conditional on treating both published numbers as comparably resolved*. That condition fails for C3: the published [supplementary source workbook](https://df6sxcketz7bb.cloudfront.net/manuscripts/198000/198568/jci.insight.198568.sdval.xlsx), locally audited in round 5, contains **20 literal zeros** at concentrations spanning **20,000 to 0.076294 nM** in the PIK3C3 response cells, duplicated in the figure worksheet. Zero response across those rows cannot be fitted to a finite 14,366 nM half-maximal inhibitory point. An unreported independent fit, display/linking error, or actual curve difference remains possible. **No precise corrected C3 IC50 is inferred.**

The published biochemical **PIK3C2B IC50 28 nM** was measured against reported **60 nM nominal enzyme**, requiring caution about active-protein fraction and tight-binding/ligand-depletion when translating IC50 into true affinity. The source lacks unbound concentration in the cell assay and native human muscle. A biochemical potency ratio, an unvalidated finite C3 value, a NanoBRET tracer displacement, and a living muscle tissue window are distinct evidence types.

**Do not compare the article's released end-of-study plasma ng/mL directly against a nominal cellular C3 IC50.** The mouse plasma has no reported postdose hours, measured unbound fraction, muscle/plasma Kp, or active-species tissue distribution. At the higher BID regimen, total *reported* plasma concentrations of 21,000–32,700 ng/mL are not free C3-site concentrations. Neither a high total value nor a nominal ratio establishes actual C3 occupancy. A qualitative drug-risk claim from that division would be unsound.

There are three mutually distinct states:

1. **C3 sparing at muscle-effective exposure:** no live-cell or muscle C3 inhibition within the achieved unbound range; the genetic caution remains but ceases to be a demonstrated BLU limitation.
2. **C3 inhibition overlapping muscle benefit:** C3 becomes a plausible antagonist to the desired biological effect and a muscle/heart/liver liability needing direct function and exposure testing. A biphasic response becomes a testable hypothesis, *not* an assumed observed one.
3. **Unreconstructible published assay without independent data:** preserve uncertainty; do not label the article selective or nonselective for C3 in the exposure range of interest.

## C. A discriminating minimal evidence sequence

These are **proposed** proof elements, not executed protocols or a clinical dosing plan.

| Gate | Specific measurement / comparison | Decision changed |
|---|---|---|
| **0. Article/assay qualification** | Authenticate the exact S stereoisomer and chemically characterize dissolved, stable exposure in the actual culture. Independently repeat **PIK3C2B and PIK3C3** engagement/concentration-response with suitable controls and dilution series, accounting for assay substrate/tracer and serum conditions. | Clarifies whether C3 sparing is real at relevant *assay* concentrations; do not borrow the reported 14,366 nM as a free affinity. |
| **1. Useful delayed function** | In independent MTM1 disease backgrounds, show a prestudy force/fatigue deficit and an interpretable vehicle trajectory, then allocate post-deficit BLU, vehicle, and a delayed functional positive intervention. Include genetically corrected controls for causality and report donor/clone/batch units. | Separates disease dependence, modifiability and useful drug efficacy. Curi p.L463S is a standalone patient line; the published p.E48*/corrected and healthy/KO lines are other starting backgrounds, not proven ready-to-run 3D force pairs. |
| **2. C2β-versus-C3 mechanism** | At **the same measured concentrations that improve force**, measure C2β engagement and a C3-sensitive cellular functional readout, preferably with a distinct orthogonal control for C3 inhibition. Preserve **autophagic flux** (not LC3 abundance alone), morphology, calcium handling, absolute active force, fatigue, and geometry/attachment measures. | If force benefit and C2β engagement coexist with spared C3 function, more convincing target window. If C3 suppression tracks an upper-dose functional loss, explore the opposing-kinase hypothesis. |
| **3. Chemical challenge** | Independently qualify the exact [WO2025003330A1 compound 47](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2025003330A1/en) for cell entry, target/off-target functional engagement and material identity *before* comparing function. Its patent Table 5 showed **−13%** assay-normalized VPS34 inhibition at **10 µM**; that is not a negative in vivo PK study or proof of zero inhibition. Compound 91 had 31% VPS34 inhibition and multiple other functional hits in its distinct 10 µM panel. | If target-genetic manipulation and two differently profiled molecules agree, reduce chemotype idiosyncrasy. If only BLU fails at adequate C2β suppression, redirect chemistry. |
| **4. Systemic translation** | Connect **free muscle-effective** exposure and duration to measured or bounded **free muscle, brain, liver and heart** exposure, including any active metabolites; assess disease-relevant hepatobiliary function and neuronal network physiology. | A within-culture C2β:C3 margin is not a systemic or chronic pediatric margin. Avoid interpreting same-nominal-dose culture comparisons as tissue distribution. |

**Highest-value near-term update:** whether measurable **VPS34 activity is preserved when C2β engagement produces *useful post-deficit human force rescue***. This is narrower and more informative than a broad safety panel. A nonresponse in a model that no delayed positive intervention can repair does not falsify the target. Prior MTM1 correction prevents disease development but does not by itself qualify reversibility after an established deficit.

**Dose-design interpretation:** If a dose range yields improved fusion/morphology yet no force, do not move the force endpoint to an easier marker. If a force curve peaks and then declines, do not attribute it to VPS34 without a *contemporaneous* direct off-target functional measurement and non–C3 explanations. Confirm technical repeatability and independent biological backgrounds before using narrow confidence intervals for promotion.

## D. Status, caveats, and falsifiers

- **Directly established:** genetic C2β target benefit in Mtm1-null mice; genetic C3 depletion worsening survival in a small Mtm1-null mouse cohort; severe effects from strong C3 deletion in muscle/heart; exact BLU mouse efficacy and one reported C3 selectivity number whose raw values do not support finite fitting.
- **Strong inference:** C3 is a disease-context *antitarget to preserve* when qualifying a C2β inhibitor for XLMTM. This is a selection-relevant risk hypothesis, not observed BLU toxicity.
- **Not established:** C3 inhibition by BLU at effective free muscle exposure; species-specific chronic safety; human force rescue; causal C3 mediation of high-dose harm; direct potency equivalence between the 2014/2016 lifelong genetic models and pharmacology.
- **If C3 sparing holds:** move attention back to the dominant unanswered human-function and tissue-distribution questions.
- **If C3 inhibition overlaps useful exposure and impairs muscle function:** demote BLU as an article; a more selective scaffold or exposure strategy may retain target value.
- **If C2β-specific perturbation fails after an independently proven delayed-functional rescue in multiple qualified patient backgrounds:** downgrade the mature target thesis itself.
