# Topical erlotinib: biology, transportability and safety challenge

**Review date:** 9 November 2026 UTC (8 November Los Angeles). **Exact starting claim:** a reproducible topical erlotinib product, with specified background keratolytic care, can improve pain and useful function in adults with **KRT16 c.379C>T, p.Arg127Cys painful hereditary palmoplantar keratoderma** at an acceptable chronic treatment burden. This is narrower than all pachyonychia congenita (PC), all KRT16 variants, all keratodermas, or oral erlotinib.

**Assessment:** a credible human-supported pathway hypothesis with an unproven local therapeutic window. The strongest reason to continue is the exact-use human signal, supported by lesional biology. The strongest biological reason for restraint is that the same EGFR pathway participates in maintaining and repairing the barrier being treated. Less callus and lower phospho-ERK are not sufficient definitions of success.

## What is established, and what remains a hypothesis

| Evidence | What it supports | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Jiang et al. found that EGF/TGF-α induce K6/K16 expression in human keratinocyte reporter experiments.[1] | An EGFR-to-stress-keratin regulatory connection. | Selective suppression of mutant K16, correction of its structure, or an optimal amount of normal K16 to retain. |
| Basset et al. found increased TRPV3, EGFR ligands/receptors and downstream ERK/mTOR activity in PC lesions; three patients improved during oral treatment.[2] | Disease-tissue relevance and a plausible interruptible amplification pathway. | Proof that EGFR overactivity initiates Arg127Cys disease rather than partly responding to injury; a topical dose-response relationship. |
| Cheng et al. demonstrated TRPV3–TGF-α–EGFR functional coupling and a role in epidermal differentiation/barrier formation.[3] | A mechanistic connection between the channel, growth-factor signaling and cornification. | Interchangeability of TRPV3 gain-of-function Olmsted syndrome and a KRT16 keratinopathy. |
| Kerns et al. linked K16 deficiency to impaired NRF2/redox responses in mice, with related findings in human PC lesions.[4] | K16 biology extends beyond mechanical scaffolding or excess keratin production. | That a Krt16-null mouse predicts treatment effects in heterozygous Arg127Cys disease. |

The working model is **mutant-keratin disease and mechanical stress → abnormal keratinocyte activation/repair → excessive EGFR-linked signaling and cornification/pain**, with inhibition potentially interrupting part of this loop. This is a synthesis, not a fully demonstrated linear causal chain. A persistent structural defect could coexist with treatable secondary inflammation, differentiation abnormalities or nociceptive signaling. Symptom control would remain valuable without mutation correction, provided the protective function of the skin is preserved.

In Basset's supplementary patient table, the treated cases were KRT16 p.Met121Lys and two KRT6A p.Leu468Pro cases; an additional KRT16 p.Leu132Pro case was not treated. Thus its mechanistic and oral observations are not independent exact-variant therapeutic replication.[2] Images or sampled tissue fields also are not additional independent patients.

## The biological countercase is substantive

EGFR biology is context dependent. An earlier primary study found that sustained EGF exposure impaired differentiation programs and increased water loss in organotypic skin.[5] Conversely, a 2026 experimental study found that erlotinib disrupted functional tight junctions, with altered claudin-1/ZO-1 localization, EGFR/Src signaling and actomyosin organization.[6] Its cultured-cell exposure was **2.5 µM**; ex vivo human skin received intradermal experimental exposure. These were not a clinical application of the proposed ointment to Arg127Cys plantar skin. This establishes a relevant hazard mechanism, not the clinical concentration at which it becomes unacceptable.

The combined inference is a **state-, layer-, exposure- and time-dependent therapeutic window**, not “EGFR inhibition repairs the barrier” or “EGFR inhibition necessarily makes this disease worse.” Normalizing pathological activation while preserving repair is plausible but must be observed. Culture-medium concentrations, topical percentage strength and unbound viable-epidermis concentrations cannot be compared directly to calculate a safety margin.

Greco et al. provide a particularly relevant human warning: oral treatment relieved pain but produced painful fissures in one keratinopathy patient. They proposed that aggressive removal of hyperkeratosis can expose underlying fragility.[7] That explanation is plausible rather than proven. Nevertheless, it makes pain, fissuring, wound closure and function more decision relevant than maximal visible callus clearance.

A 2026 conference abstract adds mechanistic support: a KRT16-derived line showed heightened EGF responsiveness; erlotinib reduced ERK activation, and a KRT6A(N171K) three-dimensional model improved its architecture/differentiation at 100 nM. One KRT16 patient's pre/post-treatment callus phosphoproteomics suggested compensatory BRAF signaling.[8] The abstract does not identify that KRT16 variant or a topical exposure bridge. It neither establishes acquired clinical resistance nor adds six independently documented responders: its “n = 6” statement is an anecdotal background claim without a patient ledger. This recent work changes the assays worth considering, not the evidentiary weight of the original controlled observations.

## Transportability must be explicit

| Source population or route | Appropriate contribution to this program | Remaining bridge |
|---|---|---|
| Lee/Lowe Arg127Cys mother and son, ages 66 and 30.[9] | Closest human evidence: topical treatment, exact variant, blinded opposite-foot comparisons. | One pedigree; unequal response; product composition/exposure incompletely specified; independent families and reproducible treatment needed. |
| Other topical PC cases.[7] | Warn against assuming every topical preparation delivers adequate benefit. The KRT6A case initially improved before plateau; the result was inadequate overall, not a clean zero-effect experiment. | Different variants and a 0.2% oil-in-water cream; no demonstrated local exposure equivalence. |
| Oral PC observations.[2,7] | Supports pharmacological responsiveness in some keratinopathies and identifies dose-limiting cutaneous problems. | Systemic distribution, genotype, local tissue concentration and concomitant care differ. Oral analgesia does not prove topical access to the relevant compartment. |
| Oral TRPV3-related Olmsted reports.[10,11] | Stronger gene-proximal rationale for suppressing a TRPV3/EGFR loop; clinical pathway plausibility. | Different causal lesion, syndrome, age mix and delivery. Their outcomes cannot be pooled into an exact-product success rate. |
| SGT-210 topical platform.[12,13] | Relevant human formulation/platform precedent and an important vehicle-controlled failure in another disease. | The sponsor's product, Darier disease and application conditions are different. Neither its PK claim nor its failure transfers automatically to this use. |

Lee/Lowe used 10% salicylic-acid care alongside later treatment; adherence differed, and benefit was not uniform. The article does not report applied drug mass or quantitative skin/plasma exposure.[9] The detailed case ledger and timing ambiguities belong in [human-evidence.md](human-evidence.md). No pediatric Arg127Cys safety inference follows from two adults, even though children with other diseases have received erlotinib.

The registered oral study **NCT06545695** was still not yet recruiting in its 3 August 2026 update, with estimated start July 2027. It plans adult, open-label treatment across several keratinopathies; it currently supplies no efficacy or safety results for this product.[14]

Additional 2026 observations sharpen the boundary. A primary series connected activating **EGFR L858R** variants to a distinct keratoderma/acanthosis-nigricans syndrome with pulmonary disease; systemic EGFR inhibition improved disease. Its oral erlotinib case developed transient folliculitis and cheilitis.[16] This supplies stronger gene-proximal causal support for a different disorder, not evidence that Arg127Cys is an EGFR-driven disease. A separate child with TRPV3-related Olmsted syndrome reportedly maintained benefit without adverse events through 52 months of **erlotinib plus acitretin**.[17] That is useful long-duration observation, but one combination-treated child cannot qualify either monotherapy or the topical KRT16 product. Additional recent oral case records and their access limitations are retained in the human-evidence ledger rather than pooled here.

## Exposure: the uncertainty that links efficacy and safety

A nominal 1% ointment is not an epidermal concentration. Dissolved fraction, release, residence time, applied mass/area, keratin thickness, fissures, debridement, occlusion and vehicle all potentially alter exposure. These are product-development variables to measure or control, not explanations to invoke after every negative outcome. See [product-cmc.md](product-cmc.md) for the formulation bridge.

There are at least three plausible exposure states: insufficient viable-epidermis delivery; a local useful window; or excessive local and/or systemic delivery. Little plasma drug is compatible with ineffective, effective or locally harmful exposure. Greco's undetectable residual blood result in one topical case therefore does not demonstrate adequate local delivery, and the report does not supply a validated therapeutic skin threshold.[7]

Sol-Gel's 2025 annual report, filed in 2026, describes a completed maximal-use PK study with limited absorption and no adherence-affecting safety findings. The cited disclosure does not give the quantitative concentration-time data, assay limits or patient-level relationship to skin integrity required here.[12] In December 2025 the company reported no differentiation from vehicle in its Darier Phase 1b efficacy assessments and discontinued that indication.[13] Low systemic exposure is feasible in some circumstances; efficacy remains a separate question. Neither statement qualifies the Lee preparation or a newly developed formulation.

**Decision implication:** connect final-product release/penetration evidence to a clinically interpretable, bounded exposure assessment. Include the intended keratolytic/debridement regimen and realistically affected skin. Do not force patients to acquire injury to create a worst case; characterize naturally occurring fissures and changes during treatment. As callus thickness or fissuring changes, absorption may change, making longitudinal exposure relevant. This feedback possibility is a risk to test, not an established observed event in the two cases.

## Safety that matters for chronic painful keratoderma

The US oral erlotinib label is for tablets, not a topical keratoderma product. It identifies serious pulmonary, hepatic, renal, gastrointestinal, severe cutaneous, ocular and embryo-fetal hazards; rash/diarrhea and drug interactions are also relevant. Pediatric safety/effectiveness are not established. Systemic CYP3A4/1A2 interactions remain relevant if meaningful absorption occurs, while oral food/gastric-pH effects must not be copied as a topical absorption model.[15] Oncology adverse-event percentages cannot estimate risk in a small-area topical program.

The program should separately resolve:

- **Local net benefit:** irritation, dermatitis, tenderness, maceration, new/deeper fissures, erosion, infection and delayed repair versus pain/function improvement. Assess ordinary weight bearing; reduced pain that results from reduced activity is not a treatment success.
- **Chronic acceptability:** adherence, time applying treatment, keratolytic/debridement burden, treatment interruptions and symptoms between applications. A tolerable short challenge does not settle repeated-use safety.
- **Systemic exposure and symptoms:** validated sampling/assay sensitivity, actual treated area and amount, relevant clinical/laboratory surveillance and a proportionate interaction review. Rare systemic events cannot be excluded by a small feasibility study.
- **Special circumstances:** pregnancy/reproductive exposure, breastfeeding, mucosal/eye transfer, open or infected skin and pediatric use require explicit clinical-development handling. Initial adult evidence is the defensible starting scope; a child's surface-area/exposure and lifetime-use questions require a separate justification.
- **Background care:** salicylic acid and debridement can influence benefit, irritation and absorption. Evaluate the proposed regimen as a specified combination of care practices rather than treating the keratolytic as an inert nuisance variable.

These are design requirements derived from the intended use and observed uncertainty, not an instruction to prescribe or compound a regimen. Clinical hold/restart rules, exposure boundaries and laboratory triggers need a qualified investigator, toxicology/regulatory assessment and the actual product data. No defensible universal plasma cutoff, permitted fissure count, or chronic exposure margin can be invented from the current reports. “No adverse events in two people” is evidence of observed tolerability under those circumstances, not a demonstration of safety.

## Alternative explanations and direct ways to distinguish them

| Competing explanation | Evidence that would change the judgment |
|---|---|
| Vehicle emollience, keratolysis or more frequent debridement explains improvement. | Matched vehicle with the same stabilized care schedule, prospectively recorded adherence/debridement, and a difference attributable to assigned drug. If only the care regimen changes outcomes, the erlotinib claim loses support. |
| Natural fluctuation, regression after a flare or changed mechanical loading explains it. | Repeated baseline measurement and concurrent comparison, tracking activity and footwear; reproducible benefit without an activity reduction explaining the pain score. |
| Expectation or unblinding explains pain benefit. | Blinded local pain assessment, evaluation of masking, corroborating useful function and rescue-treatment burden. A photograph alone is insufficient. |
| Shared family biology or one unusually strong responder dominates. | Comparable direction and worthwhile magnitude in independent pedigrees; analysis retaining every enrolled patient and failures. Multiple visits or two feet cannot create replication. |
| Drug reaches a marker but not the disease-relevant process. | Target engagement accompanied by clinically useful benefit. Adequate exposure plus lower pERK but no benefit weakens the intervention thesis rather than justifying a biomarker-only success. |
| Apparent efficacy is excessive thinning of protective skin. | Maintained/improved pain and function with stable repair, fissuring and infection burden across continued use. Worsening barrier injury can outweigh a thinner callus. |
| Response fades through adaptation. | Longitudinal clinical/exposure assessment; mechanistic sampling only if loss of response would alter a decision. BRAF enrichment alone does not establish resistance or justify combination therapy. |

## A proportionate validation and stopping package

**Before a decisive efficacy comparison:** establish the test product, intended care regimen, fit-for-purpose local/systemic exposure rationale and prospectively agreed patient-relevant benefit and tolerability criteria. The clinically meaningful effect and acceptable uncertainty must be specified by the clinical/statistical team with patient input before outcomes are known. Do not optimize nominal potency in isolation.

**Useful optional translational work:** if available quickly enough to inform product selection, test an authenticated Arg127Cys patient-derived model against appropriate normal and disease comparators. Jointly assess pathway modulation, differentiation and functional barrier/injury responses across plausible exposure—not pERK alone. Independent donor material is more valuable than many replicate wells from one donor. Such work can reject an injurious formulation or a misleading marker without proving clinical efficacy.

**Human tissue work:** opportunistic baseline/on-treatment sampling can test whether the relevant epidermal layer is reached and whether signaling changes accompany benefit. Noninvasive material or clinically indicated samples may be preferable to new plantar wounds. Small biopsies are justified only when their result can alter a product, dose or go/no-go choice; neither mandatory new biopsies nor a new animal program is a prerequisite inferred from these papers.

**Continue** when a reproducible product achieves worthwhile controlled benefit, local tolerability and a credible chronic exposure/safety plan in more than the originating pedigree. A marker response strengthens interpretation but cannot replace benefit.

**Stop or redesign the exact product/use** when an adequately delivered, adhered-to and interpretable comparison excludes worthwhile benefit; injury or treatment burden offsets benefit; or adequate local exposure cannot be separated from unacceptable toxicity. A reproducible increase in fissures/nonhealing or infection is especially consequential even if appearance improves.

**Call the result inconclusive** when exposure, adherence, comparator balance or measurement was not established. Permit only a finite, independently justified remedy; “delivery may have failed” must not become an unlimited exemption from falsification. Broader genotype development needs new evidence, not automatic expansion after a single-family signal.

## Calibrated conclusion

This candidate starts closer to the clinical question than a molecule supported only by an animal model: the route, target tissue and exact genotype have been used in people, and there is supporting disease biology. That can improve the informativeness of the next experiment. It does **not** establish above-base-rate Phase II success, a validated topical therapeutic window, or an advantage over APR-246 on a different disease's clinical claim. The most credible path to improving the odds is to resolve product exposure, independent-patient benefit and preserved skin function together. Additional mechanism papers without those bridges would add confidence faster than they add reliability.
