# Topical erlotinib: a clinical experiment that can change the decision

Review date: 2026-11-08 (America/Los_Angeles). This is a development concept and reproducible design sensitivity analysis, not a clinical protocol, prescribing instruction, regulatory agreement, or estimate of Phase II success probability.

## Decision

The most informative initial clinical option is a randomized, blinded, vehicle-controlled **paired-foot study of local plantar pain**, on identical standardized background care, in adults with genetically confirmed **KRT16 p.Arg127Cys** and substantial bilateral symptoms. It is attractive only if the final formulation can be blinded, feet can be treated separately, systemic exposure is low enough to preserve a local comparison, and patients can reliably attribute pain to each foot. A positive study would support a local symptomatic effect of the defined product plus background care. It would not establish improved whole-person mobility, durable disease modification, all-genotype efficacy, or commercial viability.

The exact-genotype human precedent is two relatives, not two independent replications. No numerical randomized active-minus-vehicle pain contrast or usable variance is available in the published report. Its dramatic baseline-to-treatment improvement cannot power the next trial. The analysis below deliberately uses assumptions rather than fitting those observations. [S1]

The key statistical finding is uncomfortable but useful: under an assumed true 2-point benefit and paired-contrast SD of 2.5, 12 independent completers have about a 71% chance of detecting a positive signal, but only a 9% chance of putting the entire 95% confidence interval above an illustrative 1.5-point worthwhile-effect threshold. A small trial can justify another investment without establishing the effect precisely.

## Target profile and first clinical claim

| Item | Minimum development objective | Boundary that prevents overclaiming |
|---|---|---|
| Initial population | Adults with laboratory-confirmed KRT16 p.Arg127Cys, painful bilateral plantar keratoderma, and stable supportive care | Describe phenotype as well as genotype; this variant spans focal non-epidermolytic PPK/PC terminology. Do not require nail findings that would exclude the actual target phenotype. |
| Product | One reproducible topical erlotinib formulation with characterized strength, stability, delivery, exposure, and matched vehicle | The publication's 1% preparation is a historical lead, not a demonstrated optimized or trial-ready formulation. |
| Benefit | Added reduction in local plantar pain beyond identical background care and vehicle | A proposed 1–2 point between-treatment NRS difference is an analyst range, not a PC-validated minimum clinically important difference. |
| Function | Concordant improvement in patient-prioritized activity or burden in subsequent whole-person testing | One active foot cannot establish the effect of treating both feet on walking. |
| Tolerability | Usable on painful plantar skin without treatment-limiting local injury or clinically concerning systemic exposure | Absence of serious toxicity in two relatives does not establish chronic safety. Product/clinical pharmacology work defines monitoring and stopping criteria. |
| Burden | Treatment and diaries patients can sustain alongside existing foot care | Keratolysis, visits, and application time count toward product burden and adherence. |
| Durability | Benefit maintained through a prespecified controlled observation window; recurrence after stopping characterized separately | An open extension is supportive safety/durability information, not a new randomized comparison. |

Start with adults because initial formulation/exposure uncertainty and outcome development are substantial. Pediatric development and other KRT16 variants remain explicit later questions, not permanent exclusions. Broader genotypes must enter separate prespecified strata/cohorts with a rationale; pooling them after weak exact-genotype enrollment would change the question while obscuring heterogeneity.

## Recruitment: distinguish a registry from a recruitable cohort

The official January 2018 IPCRR mutation table listed **60 p.Arg127Cys individuals in 20 families**. A newer official table, internally dated **January 2021**, listed **88 individuals in 33 families**; its URL contains a 2018 upload directory, which is not the date of its contents. A natural-history analysis published in 2021 included **13 p.Arg127Cys individuals** in its analytic cohort. These are different denominators: 13 is neither a prevalence estimate nor a current recruitment ceiling; neither 60 nor 88 is a current verified eligible adult count, and people are not independent families. The newer historical count improves the starting feasibility case without establishing present-day recruitment. [S2, S3]

The registry's scope also includes related palmoplantar disorders. Total people connected to PC Project, genetically evaluated people, all PC genotypes, exact-variant carriers, symptomatic eligible adults, consented reachable people, and independent pedigrees are distinct counts. [S4] None of the public sources supplies the last two counts for this experiment.

Before financing enrollment, obtain a consent-compatible feasibility summary through an authorized future process: exact variant and diagnostic documentation; age; country/site reachability; bilateral pain and care stability; family membership; prior/current EGFR treatment; willingness to accept a vehicle-treated foot and study burden; and concurrent trial commitments. No outreach or access to private registry data has occurred for this review. Prefer at least one participant per independent pedigree before heavily sampling relatives. If only a handful of pedigrees are available, treat family-level uncertainty as a central feasibility constraint.

**Planning assumption, not a forecast:** a pilot targeting 12 independent evaluable participants might randomize 15 if loss is independently 20%, but 15 gives 12 *expected* completers and only a 64.8% chance of at least 12. Randomizing 18 gives 94.9%, and 19 gives 97.7%, under that same assumption. Family-correlated loss or failure to recruit can be worse. Thus a 15-person budget requires a recruitment/completion reserve, not the statement “15 guarantees 12.”

## Choose the design to match the claim

| Option | What it can answer | Principal threat | Position |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blinded randomized active foot versus vehicle foot, same care on both | Whether the product adds local benefit within a person | Opposite-foot exposure, loading changes, inability to assign pain to one foot, local irritation revealing allocation | Preferred first efficacy concept if these assumptions are demonstrated credible |
| Blinded whole-person parallel allocation, both feet active versus both feet vehicle | Whether treating the patient improves pain, function, and daily activity | Greater between-person variability; small exact-genotype population; family/genotype imbalance | Preferred design for a whole-person mobility claim; may require a broader justified population and more participants |
| Whole-person randomized crossover | Within-person reversible symptomatic effect on pain/function | Unknown washout, persistent keratin/skin changes, rebound, period/season and behavior effects | Do not choose just to rescue an underpowered parallel trial; first establish reversibility and feasible washout |
| Multiple randomized N-of-1 sequences | Reproducibility of rapid reversible individual effects | Same carryover threats; only stable adherent participants enter; selected responders do not estimate population benefit | Later option only if onset/offset support repeated switching |
| Open extension, external registry or historical comparator | Tolerability, persistence, natural-history context | Expectation, background-care change, regression to mean, selection | Supportive only for this subjective fluctuating condition |

A paired-foot design reduces some between-person confounding, but does not make feet biologically isolated. Relief on one side can increase walking and pain on the other; altered loading can also reduce control-foot pain. Systemic absorption could improve both feet, masking a real effect. Cross-application can do the same. A null paired contrast therefore needs exposure, adherence, activity, and application-separation evidence before being interpreted as target failure. Conversely, irritation-induced unblinding can exaggerate a subjective benefit. Record perceived allocation without using it to redefine the primary analysis.

## Endpoint development and background care

The PCQoL instrument has published reliability and construct-validation work. FFI has PC-specific evaluation including repeated assessments. These establish useful candidates, not a validated exact-genotype treatment-response threshold. Neither a whole-person FFI nor PCQoL score can be assigned to one foot without modifying its context of use. [S5, S6]

A small primary activity-tracker study followed 12 adults with PC and 12 matched controls across seasons. The PC-control step-count difference and within-PC pain/step association did not meet conventional statistical significance. Its between-person pain SD is not the SD of a randomized paired treatment contrast. Activity monitoring can contextualize pain and loading; it is not a validated surrogate for symptomatic benefit. [S7]

The endpoint warning is concrete: the sponsor reported that VAPAUS, a 24-week randomized blinded study of topical rapamycin with 87 participants, did not show benefit on its patient global assessment of activities-difficulty primary endpoint. This does not prove erlotinib will fail or prove that endpoint was invalid. It does show that a plausible topical approach and encouraging earlier work do not ensure controlled functional benefit. [S8]

Proposed measurement package:

1. **Primary candidate:** a foot-specific daily 0–10 pain item with a defined recall period and context (for example, pain with usual standing/walking during the past day). Qualitative work must establish that patients interpret it consistently and can distinguish feet. Do not force unusual exercise that changes the construct or creates avoidable pain. Record time standing/walking and activity restriction as context.
2. **One prespecified summary:** baseline-adjusted active-versus-control difference using an end-of-treatment window, provisionally the final 14 days of a 12-week blinded period. Define minimum diary completion and scoring before seeing allocation. Averaging reduces noise; it does not turn days into independent participants. Do not select the best week after inspecting results.
3. **Supportive outcomes:** standardized blinded photographs and prespecified lesion/fissure assessment; rescue use; patient global change; existing whole-person FFI/PCQoL at low-burden intervals; tolerability and exposure. Photographic improvement or target engagement alone is not a validated clinical surrogate.
4. **Interpretability:** patient anchors and distributional information should inform worthwhile change. A 30% within-person improvement rule and a 1.5-point between-treatment difference answer different questions; neither is established here as a PC-specific responder threshold. Report absolute and relative changes transparently without switching the primary success definition.

The Lee report's improvement after adding salicylic acid to both feet and poor adherence in the son make background keratolysis central. [S1] Use identical, feasible, stabilized supportive care on both sides, including a documented keratolytic/debridement policy, footwear and allowed rescue. The first question is the **incremental effect of the defined erlotinib product on that care**, not erlotinib monotherapy or an uncontrolled combination. A tiny four-arm formulation/keratolytic factorial would dilute an already scarce sample. If a specific keratolytic is essential to delivery, it is part of the regimen's value, tolerability, and reproducibility.

A 2–4 week care/diary run-in is a planning option to establish use, baseline variability, missingness and burden. It should not retrospectively discard placebo responders. Bilateral pain must leave room for improvement; a provisional mean pain threshold such as 4/10 on both feet is a design assumption requiring review, not a validated eligibility criterion. Such enrichment can exclude milder p.Arg127Cys phenotypes and limit generalizability. Record floor/ceiling frequencies, baseline asymmetry, concurrent pain sources, and use of avoidance to keep reported pain low. Randomize foot allocation with concealment; avoid simply choosing the worst foot for active treatment.

## Estimand, dependency and missing data

For the paired concept, define a positive individual contrast as:

`(baseline active-foot pain − end-window active-foot pain) − (baseline control-foot pain − end-window control-foot pain)`.

The target is the average local added benefit in the eligible randomized population under the agreed background-care/rescue policy. Baseline adjustment, foot asymmetry and analysis of daily trajectories require a prespecified analysis plan. A compact analysis can use one adjusted contrast per person; a repeated-measures model may improve precision but must retain patient and family dependencies. Avoid a richly parameterized random-effects model that a handful of families cannot support. Report individual paired results and uncertainty, with a family-level sensitivity or appropriately restricted randomization-based analysis. Statistical robustness cannot manufacture independent pedigrees.

Prespecify how treatment discontinuation, rescue analgesia, altered keratolysis, infection, unblinding and treatment of the control foot affect interpretation. Continue collecting outcomes after stopping study product when feasible. A treatment-policy question can include outcomes after allowed rescue; supplementary analyses should show rescue/tolerability differences so pain relief purchased through extra rescue is visible. A hypothetical no-rescue estimand needs additional unverifiable assumptions and is not interchangeable. Define handling of bilateral systemic rescue explicitly.

Do not use last-observation-carried-forward or remove nonresponders as a default. Show patterns/reasons of missingness and prespecified sensitivity to worse missing outcomes on active treatment, reference-based assumptions where defensible, and tipping points. Missing diaries are different from full withdrawal. The computation below models only random complete loss and therefore understates the problem when irritation, nonresponse or burden drive dropout.

These design choices follow the questions emphasized in FDA rare-disease guidance, ICH E9(R1) estimands guidance, and FDA's October 2025 final fit-for-purpose clinical-outcome-assessment guidance. They are proposed applications, not FDA endorsement. One primary endpoint/window and a declared supportive hierarchy avoid searching across pain, photographs, function and timepoints for a favorable claim. [S9–S12]

## Reproducible operating-characteristic analysis

Files: [inputs](design-inputs.json), [model](design-model.py), [all 864 scenarios](design-results.csv). Run from any directory with Python, NumPy and SciPy:

```bash
python research/phase2-program/erlotinib/design-model.py
python research/phase2-program/erlotinib/design-model.py --check
```

The model generates normal-distribution sufficient statistics for a study-level mean contrast and estimated standard error. It does **not** generate fictional patient records, fit the two reported cases, or estimate a drug-success prior. Each base design scenario has 100,000 Monte Carlo replications, seed 20261108; common random draws compare effects/attenuation within a base scenario. Maximum Monte Carlo standard error of a frequency is approximately 0.16 percentage points. The 864 rows are sensitivity scenarios, not independent evidence.

| Input | Explicit assumed values |
|---|---|
| Randomized people | 8, 12, 20, 30 |
| True active benefit, NRS points | 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5 |
| Contrast/outcome SD | 1.5, 2.5, 3.5; none estimated from Lee |
| Complete loss | 0% or independently 20%; whole-family loss in the family scenario |
| Contrast attenuation | 0% or 25%, representing a sensitivity to loss of separability/adherence; no mechanism is fitted |
| Worthwhile-effect threshold | 1.5 NRS points, analyst illustration only |
| Dependency | Independent paired participants; paired participants in two-person families with contrast ICC 0.25; equal-allocation whole-person parallel trial |

For independent paired designs, SD describes the between-person distribution of the adjusted paired contrast. For parallel designs, it describes within-arm outcome variation; the same numerical SD is **not** a measured equivalence between designs. The parallel rows illustrate a pain outcome on the same scale, not the operating characteristics of FFI, mobility or another scale. For the family scenario, the analysis uses independent two-person family means with SD `SD × sqrt(ICC + (1 − ICC)/2)` and family-based degrees of freedom. Three or more complete independent units (and three per arm for parallel studies) are required for a model result. This validity rule is an analytical guardrail, not a recommendation to run a three-unit clinical trial.

Keep the success events distinct:

- **Positive statistical signal:** lower two-sided 95% CI exceeds zero. Under the modeled zero effect, this positive-direction event occurs about 2.5% of the time.
- **Point estimate looks worthwhile:** estimated benefit is at least 1.5 points, regardless of uncertainty.
- **Illustrative advance rule:** both the positive signal and point-estimate threshold hold. This is a possible pilot investment rule, not a registration criterion.
- **Strong threshold evidence:** lower 95% CI exceeds 1.5 points. This is much harder than signal detection.
- **Illustrative futility:** upper 95% CI is below 1.5 points. Other valid studies are inconclusive under the advance/futility rules; failed minimum completeness is reported separately.

At the assumed worthwhile threshold itself, an unbiased point estimate exceeds that threshold only about half the time, even as sample size grows. Requiring the entire confidence interval to exceed the true effect occurs only about 2.5% of the time. These are different inferential questions, not a contradiction. Under the illustrative 2-point true effect/SD 2.5 assumptions, a noncentral-t calculation needs approximately **199 independent complete paired participants** for 80% probability of a lower 95% CI above 1.5. This is a sensitivity illustration of the precision cost, not a feasible enrollment proposal.

The less stringent pilot rule also advances some sub-worthwhile effects: with true benefit only 1 point and SD 2.5, it advances about 19.0% of 12-person studies and 18.3% of 20-person studies. With zero effect those frequencies are 1.24% and 0.37%. Selecting the rule requires valuing both false advancement and false rejection; a small P value alone does not solve that choice.

Model limits matter: normal contrasts are unbounded approximations to bounded ratings; effect heterogeneity, floor/ceiling effects, time trends, informative withdrawal, measurement misinterpretation, baseline-adjustment uncertainty, family-size imbalance, model misspecification, exposure failures, and failure to recruit are not simulated. The family ICC and 25% attenuation are scenario values, not estimates. These frequencies are neither posterior probabilities that erlotinib works nor the unconditional chance of passing Phase II.

Internal checks validate nonoverlapping advance/futility events, exhaustive disposition, monotonic signal/advance frequency as assumed effect rises, positive null error, and agreement of complete independent-paired signal frequencies with noncentral-t calculations. The checked CSV reproduces exactly with the recorded inputs in the available NumPy/SciPy environment (2.3.5/1.17.0).

## Resource envelope and investment gates

The roughly **$500,000 reference budget is not a hard cap**. Study size should follow the evidence needed and achievable recruitment, with the cost of larger or staged options made explicit. The sensitivity model has not been altered to force a design under that reference amount; a more expensive design can be preferable if it materially reduces the risk of an inconclusive or misleading result.

A planning pilot is **12 independent evaluable participants**, 1–3 expert sites (base two), 2–4 weeks run-in, 12 weeks blinded treatment, and approximately 4 weeks follow-up: about **18–20 weeks per participant**. A base assumption of five in-person visits and weekly remote diary/safety support during treatment implies about 75 participant-visits and 180 weekly contacts for 15 randomized people, before screening failures or reserves. These are costing inputs, not clinically approved schedules. Recruitment may dominate calendar time; overlapping enrollment does not make the registry available immediately. Formulation/exposure work, central photography, randomization/packaging, data management and endpoint work add substantial costs beyond visit counts.

The next investment should purchase specific uncertainty reduction:

1. **Before committing to efficacy enrollment:** a stable deliverable product and credible local/systemic exposure rationale; a matched-vehicle/blinding plan; feasible standardized background care; a defensible pain measure; and a recruitable independent-pedigree count. Stop or redesign if these fail.
2. **Before locking trial size:** use blinded feasibility/natural-history information to refine diary burden, attrition, variability and enrollment, without looking for a favorable treatment effect. Any sample-size adaptation and maximum recruitment must be prespecified.
3. **After the controlled pilot:** weigh effect and CI, consistency across independent families, rescue use, tolerability, exposure, adherence and photographs together under a prespecified decision rule. A signal driven by one family or accompanied by loss of blinding calls for replication. An uncertain null with poor delivery calls for a product decision, not automatic dismissal of the biology.
4. **Before making a mobility or broad-PC claim:** run an appropriately controlled whole-person study, with population and endpoint evidence supporting that claim. Do not relabel a positive local pilot as completed Phase II de-risking.
