# Topical erlotinib: product, CMC and exposure decision

Review date: **9 November 2026 UTC**. Public-source diligence and proposed work only; no formulation manufacture, human testing, supplier inquiry or spending has been performed. This record concerns a reproducible topical erlotinib product for painful plantar KRT16-associated keratoderma, initially anchored to the Arg127Cys family signal. Costs are scope-based planning estimates in USD, not quotations.

## Decision

**The molecule is obtainable; the clinical product has not yet been shown to be transferable.** The best next product step is recovery and comparison of the original successful article and any accessible sponsor product dossier, followed by a bounded analytical and delivery bridge. A new cream with the same nominal percentage cannot inherit the case report's efficacy, the published cream's stability, and SGT-210's exposure claims simultaneously.

There are three viable-looking branches, with materially different evidence and economics:

| Branch | Why consider it | Evidence that can carry | Binding uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reproduce the Lee/Lowe article with its documented co-treatment | Closest to the positive genotype-specific human observation | The original clinical observation, conditional on a credible product/use match | Original formula, source material, batch records, strength basis, product performance and availability are absent from the paper |
| Obtain an established SGT-210 product and usable dossier | Potentially avoids rebuilding manufacturing and some safety work | Only studies linked to that exact formulation, process, specifications, container and use | Supply, reliance/reference rights and lot equivalence remain unknown; different strength/vehicle is not Lee replication |
| Independent API-based development | More direct control of product and future manufacturing | General molecule knowledge and formulation hazards; the original observations remain supporting rationale | More CMC cost, new local-exposure uncertainty and potentially additional safety bridging |

Do not default to an independently compounded 1% product merely because the original case report used that label. Conversely, inability to obtain the original article is not proof the molecule fails: it changes the program into new product development and changes the budget.

## What the primary evidence actually establishes

### Positive clinical article

[Lee and Lowe, JAMA Dermatology, online 5 January 2022, DOI 10.1001/jamadermatol.2021.5302](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamadermatology/article-abstract/2787624), [full article hosted by PC Project](https://www.pachyonychia.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/LeeG2022Topical-Erlotinib.-pdf.pdf): two related KRT16 Arg127Cys patients received locally compounded ointment. The informative higher-strength comparison used nominal 1% erlotinib or placebo with separate 10% salicylic-acid cream. Benefit differed between mother and son; the son reported poor keratolytic adherence. Initial maternal 0.1% monotherapy produced little improvement. Daily application is explicit for that initial phase; the paper does not restate every subsequent frequency. Maternal salicylic acid was twice weekly. The paper does not identify the pharmacy, vehicle composition, API versus tablets, salt-strength convention, applied mass, batch assays, shelf life or systemic PK. The full PDF was retrieved from cached indexing after direct retrieval returned errors.

This evidence supports a narrowly specified hypothesis, not a ready-to-transfer manufacturing dossier. Unrecorded historical variables must remain unknown rather than be filled with a convenient modern pharmacy formula.

### Negative or insufficient topical clinical article

[Greco et al., Biomedicines, 2022, DOI 10.3390/biomedicines10040841](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9028469/): the 0.2% oil-in-water cream was used at 1 g per foot twice daily in two PC patients with different mutations from Lee's family. One could not maintain oral-treatment benefit after switching topical; the other had partial initial improvement followed by inadequate further benefit. The latter's residual blood erlotinib was undetectable, without a reported analytical lower limit. Oral benefit and dose-limiting cutaneous effects are separate observations. Authors proposed insufficient topical concentration as an explanation; they did not demonstrate insufficient tissue exposure.

**Inference:** low nominal strength, formulation failure, insufficient target delivery and genotype/clinical heterogeneity remain competing explanations. This is neither a clean target knockout nor proof that increasing concentration fixes delivery. Plasma non-detection cannot distinguish successful local retention from failure to cross the stratum corneum.

### Published formulation and stability article

[Nguyen et al., Molecules, 5 February 2022, DOI 10.3390/molecules27031070](https://www.mdpi.com/1420-3049/27/3/1070), [full PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/molecules/molecules-27-01070/article_deploy/molecules-27-01070.pdf): a tablet-derived 0.2% DMSO/Nourivan Antiox cream retained 90–110% assay in three neutral-pH batches through 42 days at room temperature; acidic batches retained about 57% after 21 days. Stability-indicating HPLC identified oxidation/light liabilities. Storage used internally varnished aluminum tubes. Direct tablet-powder blending caused graininess. No skin-delivery study was reported.

**Calculated inconsistency:** the abstract/discussion describe 5% v/w DMSO, but optimized 0.2% text specifies a 20 mg/mL extract: 200 mg per 100 g would require approximately 10 mL. This does not prove batches were misprepared. Recover the batch formula and reconcile the paper's tablet/hydrochloride terminology with marketed active-moiety labeling.

These results do not establish Lee-ointment equivalence, commercial shelf life or preservation during repeated opening.

### Sponsor product and maximum-use exposure

[Sol-Gel's 2023 MUSE announcement](https://ir.sol-gel.com/node/8431), also [filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1684693/000117891323001854/zk2329723.htm), describes 12 healthy adults and 28-day exposure, minimal systemic absorption and predominantly mild adverse events. This is a company disclosure, not a full clinical study report. Current [company pipeline information](https://www.sol-gel.com/pipeline/) should be read alongside the [2026 annual report's account of negative December 2025 Darier unblinding](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1684693/000117891326001552/zk2634571.htm); manufacturing progress does not establish efficacy in another disease.

[Sol-Gel patent application US20230390291A1, Example 13](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20230390291A1/en) gives more specific exposure information: 3.5% and 5% ointments, six healthy adults per group, about 15% body surface area, once daily for 28 days, with two further sampling days. HPLC-MS/MS measured erlotinib and OSI-420. The stated quantitation limits were **97.73 ng/mL parent and 9.783 ng/mL metabolite**; results were below those limits and no PK analysis was performed. The same application contains distinct 5% hydrochloride and 5.46% hydrochloride examples, illustrating the need to resolve strength convention. Patent examples and tested clinical lots are not automatically interchangeable.

**Inference:** this is reassuring bounded exposure evidence, not zero absorption and not a quantitative safety margin for fissured, keratolytic-treated plantar skin. Access to the clinical report, bioanalytical validation, individual concentration records and exact tested article is necessary before assigning a regulatory or economic credit. A public patent is not permission to use sponsor regulatory data.

Earlier [US11633399B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11633399B2/en) reports formulation examples with differing solvents and short accelerated assay observations, including several months at 40°C. These are not a validated commercial shelf life or proof of dermal delivery. Its granted claims and the later application's pending claims must be evaluated separately; see the IP/regulatory record. Scientific disclosure of a recipe does not resolve freedom to operate.

## Product identity: what must be pinned down

[The marketed Tarceva label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=5783976b-0862-44e5-9b19-0f41d236d5c3) describes hydrochloride salt, molecular weight 429.90, with pH-dependent aqueous solubility and a maximum around 0.4 mg/mL near pH 2. Tablet strength is expressed as erlotinib equivalent. The free base has a calculated molecular weight of approximately 393.44 after subtracting HCl; consequently, **1.00% hydrochloride corresponds to about 0.915% erlotinib free-base equivalent**, before purity or water corrections. This arithmetic is a strength-accounting illustration, not a formulation recommendation.

| Item | Definition needed before an article is called a replication | Consequence of a change |
|---|---|---|
| Starting material | API manufacturer/grade, salt and solid form, assay convention, impurity profile; or exact tablet manufacturer, formulation and extraction history | Approved oral generic equivalence does not establish equivalent extraction, residual excipients, topical release or stability |
| Finished concentration | Active-moiety versus salt basis; dissolved versus suspended fractions; representative content sampling | Nominal percentage alone can conceal different available drug and different mass per application |
| Vehicle | Full qualitative/quantitative composition, excipient grade, pH where meaningful, water content/activity, physical structure | Solubility, drug activity, crystallization, barrier effects, local tolerance and preservation may change together |
| Process | Reproducible controlled process and hold times; mixing/filling homogeneity; change history | Equal ingredients do not guarantee equal semisolid microstructure or performance |
| Container and use | Actual contact materials, closure, light protection, dose presentation, shipping, storage and repeated opening | Adsorption, leachables, water/solvent loss, contamination and delivered mass can defeat acceptable bulk assay |
| Co-treatment | Salicylic product identity, adherence, separation/co-application, debridement, emollients, footwear and occlusion | Changes the tested intervention and can change barrier exposure, tolerability and measured pain |

A qualified API route may ultimately be more controllable than extracting tablets. It is still a change from an unverified historical source, and cannot receive an automatic bridge. Research-grade reference material is suitable for an assay standard only where qualified for that purpose; it is not a substitute for clinical manufacturing controls.

A residual-solvent classification also does not establish tolerance of a deliberately retained topical solvent on fissured skin. Excipient exposure, local irritation and sensitization must be assessed for the actual formulation and repeated-use conditions.

## Delivery and exposure: the information that changes the decision

The desired engineering outcome is reproducible delivery to viable diseased epidermis with acceptable local tolerance and bounded systemic exposure. Total drug in a cream, total drug in a skin sample and drug reaching a receptor are different quantities. Thick hyperkeratosis may obstruct delivery while fissures or keratolytic treatment may increase it. These are testable competing conditions, not permission to assume all diseased feet absorb more or less than healthy skin.

The reviewed public documents do **not** establish a quantitative relationship among delivered dose, viable plantar epidermal concentration, EGFR inhibition and pain improvement for the successful article. This is a decisive gap. It can be addressed proportionately rather than by starting an unrelated animal efficacy program:

1. **Separate product release from skin permeation.** A discriminatory in-vitro release method can reveal manufacturing changes; it does not by itself show that the drug reaches viable epidermis. A human-skin assessment should distinguish surface residue, stratum-corneum retention, viable epidermis/dermis and receptor-fluid passage, with recovery/mass-balance and tissue-integrity controls. Without these distinctions, high “skin retention” can simply mean unabsorbed surface drug.
2. **Use relevant tissue where possible, record limitations where not.** Plantar and hyperkeratotic tissue is harder to obtain than routine surgical abdominal skin. A well-characterized available human-skin model can rank gross delivery failures, but cannot validate patient plantar exposure. Do not postpone an otherwise informative human study indefinitely to create a bespoke disease model. A small clinical exposure/target-engagement component may be more valuable when disease tissue cannot be qualified economically.
3. **Bridge the intervention actually proposed.** Compare the intended active and matching vehicle under the intended keratolytic/background-care conditions. The proposed study is not automatically a coformulated erlotinib/salicylic product. Mixing the two into a single container creates additional compatibility and stability questions; keeping historically separate preparations does not remove the need to understand their use together on skin.
4. **Use sensitivity appropriate to the decision.** A plasma method must distinguish an acceptably low exposure from a potentially relevant one, with parent/metabolite specificity and recorded limits. The patent's approximately 98 ng/mL parent threshold cannot simply become the program's “no systemic exposure” acceptance rule. Select a threshold from a justified safety assessment, then demonstrate method performance at that threshold. An arbitrary percentage of oncology trough exposure is not a validated benign-disease safety margin.
5. **Predefine interpretation.** Stable product plus negligible viable-epidermal delivery makes a negative efficacy test uninformative about the target. Adequate delivery/target engagement without useful benefit is a stronger clinical stop. Increasing strength after each negative result without a bounded hypothesis makes the program open-ended.

Adjacent literature does not close this gap: a [2025 erlotinib/methotrexate phospholipid-complex gel study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40571845/) concerns a combined preclinical psoriasis formulation; a [2026 erlotinib ionic-liquid gel study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42310188/) concerns localized breast-cancer delivery. These demonstrate alternative formulation research, not validated single-agent plantar dosing or equivalence. Maximizing transdermal passage is not the same design objective as epidermal delivery with limited systemic exposure.

## Split-site use is a product requirement

For a contralateral-foot comparison, provide distinguishable accountability codes but matched presentation and sensory characteristics. Differences in color, odor, texture, stinging or residue can reveal treatment assignment. Verify active-free vehicle content and assess matching before relying on blinding. A vehicle can itself alter hydration, fissuring and pain; it is an active part of the comparator intervention.

The product/use plan should quantify application amount and adherence, document treated surface, record tube accountability, and control foreseeable transfer through hands, socks, footwear and adjacent skin. Standardize background care and capture differences in fissuring and callus removal. Local improvement can alter gait and loading on both feet; that clinical interference remains even if chemical cross-contamination is prevented. Contralateral design therefore saves participants only when these threats are acceptably bounded.

Painless thinning is not the sole goal. The clinical gate must include fissures, erosions, irritation and useful walking function. More penetration or more thinning can worsen fragile weight-bearing skin. Local-tolerance and exposure observations belong in the same decision as efficacy, not in a post hoc explanation of a failed product.

## Staged engineering deliverables and stops

These are proposed decision deliverables, not laboratory instructions or a regulatory mandate. Approval requirements and any nonclinical additions depend on the exact article, population and route. Work should be contracted by stage, with explicit ownership and reusable records.

| Stage | Minimum useful deliverable | Advance if | Stop or reprice if |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Define the article | Historical/sponsor document map; exact composition and strength reconciliation; starting-material and container identity; evidence-to-lot map; gap assessment and assay-transfer plan | A reproducible candidate and feasible source are identified, with a bounded bridge | Source records are unavailable and program still relies on “exact replication”; essential rights or supply unavailable; safety-critical composition unresolved |
| B. Establish a reliable product | Fit-for-purpose identity, assay and impurity methods; physical characterization; representative sampling; preferably three independent small batches; microbial/preservation risk assessment; initial real-time, stress and in-use/container observations | Relevant lots meet justified limits and support the proposed supply/retesting schedule; no unexplained precipitation or rapid potency loss | Unexplained impurity or drift, unacceptable local excipient exposure, nonuniformity, or repeated reformulation with no bounded hypothesis |
| C. Establish interpretable delivery | Discriminatory release and limited human-skin delivery/retention assessment, active/vehicle/co-treatment rationale; qualified plasma assay; written plan for remaining patient exposure uncertainty | A credible local-delivery hypothesis survives and the planned human study can distinguish delivery failure from lack of benefit | Drug remains predominantly surface-bound with no credible target access; model cannot answer the question within budget; systemic/local safety margin cannot be justified |
| D. Supply the actual study | Controlled active and matched-vehicle lots, release certificates, suitable coded container/labeling, shipping/accountability plan and ongoing stability commitment | Study duration and resupply plan are supported by the actual released product | Manufacturing cannot reproduce the qualified article; product expires before interpretable follow-up; blinding or supply continuity fails |

Short initial stability can support a carefully bounded early study with ongoing testing and resupply where accepted; it does not establish commercial shelf life. Three batches are a proposed reproducibility check here, not a claim that full commercial process validation is universally required before a small study. Record retained samples and change controls so a positive clinical result remains reproducible.

## Cost and calendar: scope determines affordability

Independent development/transfer estimates include technical personnel effort, routine materials, method work, reports and ordinary contractor margin. They assume one primary formulation and at most a limited rescue iteration, existing analytical equipment, nonsterile semisolid manufacture, and a small initial clinical supply. They exclude rights and negotiations, patient/site costs, patient PK collection, broad GLP safety studies, general sponsor operations, commercial process validation and long-term registration stability. Serious new toxicology or repeated formulation invention requires a separate decision and budget.

| Stage | Low | Base | High | Typical base elapsed time once inputs are available |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| A. Article/dossier and transfer assessment | $15,000 | $30,000 | $60,000 | 2–6 weeks |
| B. Analytical, physical, microbial and early stability qualification | $35,000 | $70,000 | $140,000 | 8–16 weeks |
| C. Delivery and bioanalytical feasibility | $30,000 | $65,000 | $140,000 | 8–14 weeks; partly concurrent after article selection |
| D. Initial clinical active/vehicle supply and stability support | $30,000 | $60,000 | $120,000 | 6–12 weeks after qualification |
| **Independent branch total** | **$110,000** | **$225,000** | **$460,000** | **Approximately 4–6 / 6–10 / 10–15 months for low/base/high scope** |

These are engineering estimates, not current supplier prices or a statistical confidence interval. Low assumes unusually clean transfer and successful first-pass methods; base includes limited iteration and realistic records; high includes substantial redevelopment or repeat studies but not an unlimited discovery program. Elapsed ranges allow overlap; row durations must not be mechanically summed. Recruitment, access negotiations and regulatory review can add separate delay.

**Conditional existing-product branch:** approximately **$45,000 / $90,000 / $180,000**, over roughly **2–4 / 4–6 / 6–9 months**, for dossier inspection, targeted bridging and supply coordination **only if** an owner supplies fit-for-purpose manufacturing, stability, bioanalytical/safety records and active/vehicle lots. This replaces covered portions of A–D; do not add both totals. It is not a purchase/license price and must not receive credit merely because SGT-210 has existed in humans. Missing matched vehicle, poor assay sensitivity or a changed formulation can remove much of the apparent saving.

The previous $75,000 product allowance could fit a favorable existing-dossier contribution, but is not a sound default for independent product development. The independent base alone adds about **$150,000** relative to that allowance, before clinical operations and any new safety requirement. The user has clarified that approximately $500,000 is a reference budget, not a strict cap. These extra costs change the incremental-value and reserve comparison rather than automatically disqualifying independent development. The first meaningful stopping point is Stage A, not commitment to all four stages.

## What public evidence can and cannot buy down

| Gap | Current classification | Least expensive credible resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Original positive article not identified beyond nominal label | Repairable if records survive; fatal to a claim of exact replication if they do not | Historical batch/specification records; if absent, explicitly select a new-product branch |
| No demonstrated patient plantar exposure relationship | Repairable but central | Bounded delivery work plus interpretable early patient exposure/target-engagement plan |
| Published cream formula contains reconciliation ambiguity | Repairable | Source batch formula and analytical reconciliation, not inference from prose |
| Existing sponsor data not available for reliance | Commercial/access uncertainty, potentially fatal to low-budget branch | Scope/rights assessment of actual offered dossier; no assumed free access |
| Rapid instability, variable potency or unacceptable excipient tolerance | Potential product stop | One predefined rescue iteration with adequate remaining capital; otherwise stop |
| No useful effect despite credible product, exposure and adequate clinical test | Clinical stop | Do not keep changing formulation to protect the original hypothesis |
| No obtainable economic interest in resulting product/data | Fatal to this investment package even if medically promising | Different transaction structure or stop; generic ingredient access does not answer this |

## Regulatory context and boundaries

[FDA's CMC information page](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/investigational-new-drug-application-ind/ind-applications-clinical-investigations-chemistry-manufacturing-and-control-cmc-information) calls for phase-appropriate assurance of identity, quality, purity and strength, including placebo and product stability. Its current first-in-human flexibility should not be misread as eliminating evidence needed for a later efficacy study. [FDA's May 2003 Phase 2/3 CMC guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/inds-phase-2-and-phase-3-studies-chemistry-manufacturing-and-controls-information) is the closer stage-specific reference.

[FDA's final March 2026 Q3 characterization guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/physicochemical-and-structural-q3-characterization-topical-drug-products-submitted-andas) offers a useful framework for physical and structural comparability of semisolids. It concerns ANDAs; it does not make an unapproved topical erlotinib product an approved generic or create a shortcut from oral tablets.

[FDA's May 2019 maximal-use guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/maximal-usage-trials-topically-applied-active-ingredients-being-considered-inclusion-over-counter) concerns OTC-monograph ingredients. Its exposure-design principles are informative; its scope is not a claim that this prescription investigational product must follow an OTC protocol. [FDA's October 2015 reformulated/alternate-route safety guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/nonclinical-safety-evaluation-reformulated-drug-products-and-products-intended-administration) is relevant to assessing what oral and existing topical knowledge can support. The IP/regulatory and clinical records own the final route and study requirements.

The decision-changing output of CMC diligence is a **specific, reproducible article with an interpretable delivery and safety bridge at an affordable price**. Current public evidence is sufficient to justify that bounded assessment. It is insufficient to claim a clinic-ready product, a transferable commercial shelf life, negligible systemic exposure in the proposed patients, or a high probability of Phase II success.
