# Topical erlotinib: market, competition and capital economics

Decision date: 8 November 2026 (America/Los_Angeles); sources checked through 9 November UTC. Candidate R26-LOC-01. Scope: a reproducible topical product for painful **KRT16 p.Arg127Cys palmoplantar keratoderma**, with broader PC/PPK expansion treated as additional development. This is research, not an authorization to spend or approach counterparties. All development prices below are estimates, not quotes or offers. Structured inputs and arithmetic are in [economics-inputs.json](economics-inputs.json).

**Conclusion:** the narrow indication is an interesting clinical experiment but an unproven cash-generating investment. Using $500,000 as a reference capital level rather than a hard cap, a small controlled pilot comes near that amount only under favorable product-access and execution conditions. Our central estimate is **$462,000 with a transferable dossier and matching supplied product**, versus **$624,000 for independent product qualification**, before a major new safety program. Neither package establishes a regulatory path, an interested buyer, or a high probability of recovering cash. The narrow genotype market and competing topical programs materially weaken the case for a self-funded standalone product. More capital can make the experiment feasible; it cannot by itself establish that the retained return is attractive. Preserve the scientific hypothesis while rejecting an unconditional independent-development commitment.

| PC Project 2026 impact report, through October 8, 2026 [S3] | 3,212 patients connected; 1,849 participants completed genetic evaluations; 72 countries | Reach of a wider patient/diagnostic infrastructure. The current PC data page distinguishes connected, fully enrolled and genetically confirmed categories [S4]. Multiplying the 2018 R127C count by later registry growth would combine incompatible dates and categories. No reliable current R127C country, age, severity, contactability or competing-trial count was retrieved. The 2018 and 2021 PDFs were available as indexed primary-source text; direct downloads failed. This limits source inspection and is recorded rather than hidden. The selection/definition difference between the registry counts and Wu's 13 remains unresolved; the newer count does not resolve it.

A simple recruitment sensitivity illustrates the bottleneck, without claiming observed conversion rates: if 50% of reachable patients meet clinical criteria, 60% can attend participating sites, and 50% consent, the combined yield is 15%. Recruiting 15 would then require 100 reachable patients; at 30% yield it requires 50. Related family members cannot automatically supply independent replication. The design team's base 15 randomized patients yields an expected 12 completers at 20% independent loss, but only a 64.8% chance of at least 12; 19 raises that modeled probability to 97.7%. Recruitment and family structure can therefore overturn the apparent affordability before drug efficacy is tested. See [clinical-design.md](clinical-design.md).

Broader KRT16, other PC genes and unrelated PPK are not free market expansion. They require evidence that mechanism, local exposure, efficacy and tolerability transfer. A KRT16-enriched pilot and genotype-stratified expansion are different packages; mixing them simply to fill a small trial can destroy the precision that made R127C attractive.

## Revenue sensitivity, not a market forecast

There is no validated current count of reimbursable R127C patients and no validated net price for this product. These deliberately small **served-patient and annual net-price axes** show scale; they are not prevalence estimates, willingness-to-pay evidence or projected sales.

| Continuously treated, paying patients | $3,000/year net price | $12,000/year net price | $30,000/year net price |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | $30,000 | $120,000 | $300,000 |
| 25 | $75,000 | $300,000 | $750,000 |
| 50 | $150,000 | $600,000 | $1,500,000 |

Revenue is before manufacturing, distribution, pharmacovigilance, reimbursement support, returns, royalties and ongoing studies. Net price here is after hypothetical discounts but before these operating costs. Numbers of treated patients incorporate persistence, not merely prescriptions written. As an illustrative operating sensitivity, $600,000 revenue at a 60% contribution margin and $300,000 annual fixed costs leaves $60,000 before tax, financing and development recovery. Those margin and cost assumptions are not observed. Even a positive narrow-indication result may be too small for an independent commercial organization. Expansion to 100 or 300 paying patients is not a justified base case until the additional populations and access are established.

The product must improve daily function and burden, not only callus appearance. Chronic application to soles, waiting/drying time, adherence around mobility and footwear, irritation, continued keratolysis/debridement, and uncertain duration after withdrawal all affect persistence and value. A topical treatment may reduce systemic exposure and injection burden, but it does not earn premium pricing from route alone.

## Competitive and strategic context

| Approach | Public evidence and present relevance | Implication for erlotinib |
|---|---|---|
| Current symptomatic care | Debridement, keratolytics, footwear/off-loading, pain and sweating management are described by PC Project; retinoids may thin callus without reliably relieving pain [S5]. | Need incremental pain/function benefit on standardized background care. Patients already spend time managing disease; a new cream adds burden unless its benefit is clear. |
| Topical sirolimus/rapamycin, PTX-022 / QTORIN | The 87-person, 24-week randomized VAPAUS Phase III trial failed its patient-reported activities-difficulty primary endpoint; issuer reported no drug-related withdrawals or serious adverse events [S6]. | A warning against converting early signals into Phase II/III confidence. Pain, function, vehicle and endpoint behavior matter. This failure does not prove EGFR inhibition fails. |
| Topical TRPV3 inhibitor KM-001, Kamari/Orvida | UK HRA results describe 17 adults with PC/PPPK1, 12 or 16 weeks of twice-daily 1% cream, open label. Up to half of PC patients had meaningful pain reduction; possibly related local events occurred in 3/17 [S7]. A 2025 issuer update described 26 completers across two studies, including 20 PC [S8]. These overlap earlier cohorts and must not be added together. | A directly relevant competing topical program with more treated patients but unblinded efficacy. No head-to-head superiority conclusion is possible. Raises the differentiation and partnership bar. |
| Oral TRPV3 inhibitor KM-023 / ontivatrep | Orvida's pipeline and registry NCT07893483 describe a planned open-label adult PC study, estimated 10 participants, not yet recruiting in the October 26, 2026 record; planned start December 2026 [S9]. | Competes for the same specialist sites and patients. Future recruitment dates are estimates; the trial is not a completed efficacy result. |
| Botulinum toxin | Small uncontrolled PC series report pain benefit and repeated-treatment experience; a 2019/2020 report followed five patients over 30 treatments [S10]. | Existing symptomatic alternative with injection/procedural burden and limited comparative evidence. Do not claim zero effective alternatives. |
| Allele/gene strategies | TD101 tested mutation-specific siRNA in one KRT6A N171K patient with a blinded within-person design [S11]. Delivery is a major practical constraint. | Proof that precise allele approaches are possible, not evidence for R127C clinical benefit. Long-term competitors, not a currently established substitute. |
| Other erlotinib programs | Planned oral study NCT06545695: estimated 44, not yet recruiting, estimated July 2027 start in the August 2026 record. The EMA's 2024 PC orphan compound with the long biphenyl-pyrrolidine chemical name is **KM-001**, not an unidentified additional drug: FDA's public GSRS record maps that name to KM-001, UNII D5834H4GSQ and TRPV3 inhibition [S15]. This avoids double-counting competition.

Sol-Gel's 2025 annual report describes healthy-volunteer exposure work and the December 2025 Darier study's failure to differentiate from vehicle; the company stopped that indication and described considering other small feasibility studies [S16]. Its August 2026 communication emphasizes Phase III SGT-610 and additional basal-cell-carcinoma opportunities; the corporate website retains SGT-210 and invites partnerships [S17]. This is a plausible strategic opening for a limited-indication collaboration, **not evidence of willingness to license, a price, or priority for R127C**. Failed Darier efficacy and low systemic exposure answer different questions. The latter cannot establish local chronic safety or R127C benefit. Company-wide R&D spending is not a valid per-program cost estimate.

## Four economic routes

| Package | Obtainable interest that would be needed | Why it could work | Why it could fail; present decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product/indication rights plus small controlled proof | Transferable manufacturing and regulatory-reference access, rights to the exact data and product, defined territory/indication, clear onward licensing and supply | Credits existing CMC and safety work; avoids rebuilding a topical platform | Access may be unavailable or costly; formulation may not match positive evidence; retained economics may be thin. **Conditional lead route**, not established. |
| Contribution to an existing sponsor program | Contracted paid deliverable, reimbursed study contribution, option or milestone share with clear data rights | Smaller fixed overhead; an existing sponsor can continue successful work | An unrestricted research grant or illiquid minority interest may create no cash recovery. Prefer contract cash and bounded obligations where attainable; no offer exists. |
| Independent generic reformulation | A usable formulation/data position, freedom to operate and licensable differentiation | More operational control and potentially larger retained share | Cheap API does not confer product identity, commercial rights, local safety, regulatory reliance or customers. Central pilot cost exceeds the $500k reference, while $750k can accommodate the modeled 19-person case before major additional requirements. **Do not treat as the default funded route.** |
| Stop or choose another candidate | Cash remains uncommitted | Preserves the ability to fund a better package | Loses this opportunity if it later succeeds. **Rational if access, recruitment or total capital requirements fail**, even with a promising mechanism. |

An option, royalty or equity percentage is valuable only after its scope, priority, dilution, expenses, sublicensing treatment, termination rights and payment triggers are specified. The strongest failure case is that the only qualified article is controlled elsewhere while relevant product/regimen claims or another party's development advantage leave little valuable interest for ddiscov. This is a scenario, not a concluded legal barrier; see [ip-regulatory.md](ip-regulatory.md). No economic interest is presently documented as obtainable. Existing suppliers, a registry, institutional interest and an orphan designation are not counterparties' commitments.

Statistical adequacy is unresolved: this buys a decision pilot, not a powered Phase II claim or registration package.

A supplied product matching its own dossier is not necessarily the product used in Lee's positive family report. Owner/investment-management effort outside the priced vendors remains an unquantified opportunity cost; a hired replacement would add cash. ### What more capital buys

The user clarified that $500k is **not a strict budget**. The investment question is whether incremental spending creates enough decision value and retainable upside, not whether a package fits under that number. Use the 19-randomized sensitivity for a more credible completion reserve:

| Reference capital available | Difference after $510k transfer pilot | Difference after $672k independent pilot | Incremental decision value and limit |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| $500k | −$10k | −$172k | Near the transfer case but insufficient for its modeled completion reserve; forcing a smaller trial could save little while increasing inconclusive-result risk. |
| $750k | $240k | $78k | Can cover the independent central pilot or a transfer pilot with a meaningful buffer. Pays for defined product qualification and additional patients; still not enough assurance against a substantial new safety program. |
| $1m | $490k | $328k | Can support justified additional exposure/stability work, recruitment effort, longer observation or additional participants after the gates. It does not prove that those additions are needed or enough for registration. |

These differences are unallocated capital, not a prescribed reserve or expected profit. The extra $250k from $500k to $750k is particularly useful if access is unavailable but an independent article can be qualified: it changes operational feasibility. The next $250k has less obvious marginal value until delivery, outcome variability and recruitment are known. Spend it on a named uncertainty, not on a larger trial by default. More subjects can narrow an estimate only if enough independent eligible patients exist, exposure is appropriate and the endpoint is informative. Money cannot alone create exclusivity, a willing licensee, a broader effective genotype population or a payer-supported price.

A staged $750k–$1m funding capacity can therefore be sensible without committing that amount: retain G0/G1 stop decisions, reserve clinical funds only after the article and population are credible, and reprice the actual next stage. A small controlled pilot may become feasible while the standalone commercial proposition remains unattractive. Compare the incremental $250k against retaining cash or another candidate using the same outcome and ownership standard.

### Continuation and funding failure

These totals **exclude** a newly required GLP local/systemic safety program, substantial reformulation after failure, longer chronic exposure work, commercial-scale process validation, a powered multicentre efficacy trial, a marketing submission, launch and sales infrastructure. A positive pilot could still require all of them. The exact scope cannot be priced until article/regulatory and effect-size uncertainty is resolved.

For scale only, one hypothetical follow-on package (not an approval budget) combines a 40–100-person expanded study ($350/800/1,800k), additional product/stability/scale work ($100/250/600k), additional nonclinical work ($100/300/900k), regulatory/data/IP/operations ($100/250/500k), and 25% contingency: **$812.5k / $2.0m / $4.75m** additional cash, roughly **18/30/48 additional months**. The sample size is not power-derived and high is not a ceiling. This sensitivity shows why no follow-on capital may mean **stop despite a positive pilot**, not automatic financing at a favorable valuation.

## Retained cash: explicit break-even tests

Let `C` be all ddiscov cash spent before a transaction, `P` the transaction's **cash payable at closing**, `f` ddiscov's net participation after other owners/rights-sharing, and `Q` remaining transaction/closeout costs. Retained cash is `f × P − Q`; cash profit is `f × P − Q − C`. Royalties that may arrive later, unfunded milestones and an increased equity valuation do not replenish cash now. These examples are before tax, inflation, financing and discounting; delay raises the economic hurdle.

At `C = $462k` and `Q = $50k`, the closing cash needed merely to recover principal is **$5.12m / $2.048m / $1.024m** at `f = 10% / 25% / 50%`. For an illustrative 20% cash return, it rises to **$6.044m / $2.4176m / $1.2088m**. The 20% target is an example, not a supplied user requirement. No buyer or payment of these sizes has been identified.

Alternatively, suppose failure recovers zero and the whole `C` is spent. Expected principal break-even requires successful net retained cash of `C/q`, where `q` is the probability of actually receiving that cash. For `C = $462k`, assumed `q = 25% / 50% / 75%` requires **$1.848m / $924k / $616k net** in the successful branch. These are sensitivities, **not estimated probabilities**. Stopping early on failure changes expected expenditure but does not prove a positive expected value. Clinical response, study interpretability, regulatory acceptability, licensable rights, financing and buyer payment are separate uncertain events; no validated joint probability exists here.

A partner-contribution route should therefore specify reimbursement or early cash triggers alongside contingent upside. A 10% interest in a narrow asset can be worth less to this user than a smaller, paid technical deliverable with no follow-on obligation. Conversely, selling substantial rights for a small early payment could sacrifice future value. Compare actual proposed economics when available, not nominal ownership percentages.

## What changes the decision

The strongest case to continue is an exact usable product with transferable reference/data and supply rights, a recruitable independent cohort, a credible clinically meaningful pain/function effect, and a partner pathway that can absorb follow-on capital while leaving worthwhile cash participation. The strongest case to stop is a narrow market plus substantial new CMC/safety work, uncertain recruiting, weak/irritant chronic benefit, or a data-only project with no retainable economic interest. None requires waiting for definitive failure in patients.

Public research still worth completing is bounded: inspect the original 2018/2021 registry PDFs and resolve their denominators against Wu if source materials permit; locate current aggregate R127C country/age data if publicly available; reconcile all product-specific trial results and patent families; and compare marginal commitments around $500k, $750k and $1m against alternative candidate packages. A registry-wide growth statistic, generic API price, orphan label or corporate partnership webpage cannot close these gaps. Do not presume high cash-recovery probability or a greater-than-reference Phase II probability from the present record.
