# Topical erlotinib: human evidence audit

Evidence cutoff: 8 November 2026; source retrieval completed 9 November 2026 UTC. This is a development assessment, not treatment advice. The exact candidate is a reproducible topical erlotinib product for painful, genetically defined keratin-associated plantar keratoderma, initially centered on **KRT16 p.Arg127Cys**. The structured companion is [human-studies.json](human-studies.json).

## Decision

**The human evidence warrants a tightly bounded replication and product-development program, but does not establish a high probability of Phase II success.** The strongest directly applicable observation remains two related adults receiving a locally compounded ointment, with salicylic acid and short randomized opposite-foot comparisons. The evidence is better than a purely mechanistic hypothesis; it is substantially weaker than an independently replicated controlled clinical effect.

The negative evidence is material. Two other published keratinopathy patients did not obtain adequate sustained control with a different 0.2% cream. A six-person industry PPK study reported only modest improvement. A separate seven-completer Darier study did not differentiate active treatment from vehicle and was discontinued for that indication. These are different populations/products and cannot be pooled as failures of the proposed KRT16 product. They do defeat the broader proposition that topical EGFR inhibition has already been reliably validated across hyperkeratotic diseases.

No defensible numerical Phase II-success probability can be estimated from these reports. Selection/publication bias, product differences, one-family clustering, uncontrolled extensions and incomplete outcome reporting prevent that calculation. A Bayesian prior informed by these observations remains a judgment, not an observed success rate.

## The Lee–Lowe signal, reconstructed

Primary sources: [2022 JAMA Dermatology report](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2021.5302), [full two-page author article hosted by PC Project](https://www.pachyonychia.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/LeeG2022Topical-Erlotinib.-pdf.pdf). Published online 5 January 2022; issue 1 February 2022. Treatment calendar dates are not supplied. The later [meeting abstract](https://doi.org/10.1111/ajd.14044) repeats the mother–son experience; it is not another cohort.

Two patients, **one pedigree**, both KRT16 c.379C>T, p.Arg127Cys. The mother was 66, the son 30. Four feet are not four independent patients. The following transcription separates reported facts from unresolved chronology.

| Patient / phase | Reported exposure and comparator | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Mother, baseline | Prior multiple topical treatments and acitretin; daily tapentadol | Pain 8–10/10; fissures restricted walking |
| Mother, first 6 weeks | 0.1% ointment daily versus placebo; randomized, double-blinded opposite feet; unblinded at week 6 | Minimal right/active-foot improvement |
| Mother, next reported 12 weeks | 0.1% plus 10% salicylic-acid cream twice weekly | Both feet improved; pain 4–7/10 |
| Mother, next 12 weeks | 1% versus placebo, with keratolytic; randomized, double-blinded opposite feet | Right/active foot improved; its pain 0–3/10 |
| Mother, another 28 weeks | 1% plus keratolytic, both feet | Pain-free soles, unrestricted walking, swimming reported |
| Son, first 12 weeks | 1% versus placebo, opposite feet, randomized and double-blinded; 10% salicylic acid prescribed but poorly used | Modest left/active-foot morphological improvement |
| Son, next reported 12 weeks | 1%, both feet; limited keratolytic use | Gradual bilateral improvement |

No adverse effects were reported over 60 weeks for the mother and 24 weeks for the son. The local pharmacy prepared the ointments; composition and applied mass are unspecified.

**Chronology cannot be made exact from the publication.** Mother photographs are labeled weeks 0, 6, 20, 32 and 60: the prose's first 6 plus next 12 weeks sums to 18, not 20. Son photographs are weeks 0, 12 and 28 although the prose describes 12 plus 12 weeks and 24-week safety. The later abstract uses 26–60 weeks. These are reporting discrepancies, not evidence of misconduct or grounds to discard the observation. Use the stated phase durations and flag the disagreement; do not invent missing visits or washouts.

The randomized contrasts carry more causal weight than the extensions, but important quantities remain unavailable:

- No numerical contemporaneous placebo-foot pain series, paired change estimate, uncertainty interval, prespecified clinically meaningful difference, responder definition, or patient-level daily dataset. The mother's 0–3 range is an active-foot outcome, **not** an active-minus-control effect. The son's pain is not quantified.
- No random-sequence method, allocation concealment, assessment of blinding, protocol, statistical plan, eligibility/selection denominator or explicit washout. The same active foot in the mother's two comparisons does not establish independent repeat randomization.
- The intermediate 0.1% plus keratolytic phase is not described with enough precision to reconstruct both feet's treatment assignments. The 1% phase explicitly has keratolytic plus active or placebo. Keep this ambiguity rather than treating the entire course as a single trial.
- Frequency is explicitly daily for the mother's initial 0.1% exposure; it is not independently specified for every later 1% phase. Twice-weekly salicylic acid is explicitly reported for the mother; the son's frequency is not restated.
- No verified discontinuation of tapentadol, standardized walking test, step-counter result, analgesic diary, quantified grooming/debridement schedule, adherence measurement, or off-treatment durability. Swimming is an encouraging patient-level observation during open treatment, not a controlled endpoint.
- No systemic PK, local target-exposure measurement, formal adverse-event table, laboratory monitoring description, or defined surveillance for skin fragility/infection. A report of no adverse effects in two people is not a safety rate with useful precision.

Inference: the treatment difference could reflect genuine local drug activity. Its magnitude, durability, independence from vehicle/keratolytic/grooming, generalizability beyond this family, and reproducibility using a manufactured product remain open. A time trend from 0.1% to 1% is not a randomized dose-response comparison.

## The human evidence that a favorable narrative can miss

### Published lower-concentration topical failures or partial responses

[Greco et al., Biomedicines, 3 April 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9028469/) describes two prospectively treated patients, expressly not a registered trial. They are distinct from Lee's family by ages and genotypes.

| Case | Topical exposure | What actually happened |
|---|---|---|
| 47-year-old man, KRT16 p.Asn125Ser | October 2019 switch from oral erlotinib to 0.2% oil-in-water cream, 1 g/foot twice daily | Plaque regrowth slowed but substantial pain returned; oral treatment resumed |
| 39-year-old man, KRT6A p.Asn172del | Starting 10 July 2019, 0.2% cream, 1 g twice daily on right foot; left untreated | At one month right pain 4/10 versus left 7/10; debridement remained necessary. Further improvement was insufficient; oral treatment began 3 February 2020 |

Blood erlotinib after topical treatment in the second case was undetectable; assay sensitivity was not supplied. This does not demonstrate absent absorption or adequate drug delivery to the target skin layer. Both subsequently benefited from oral EGFR-directed treatment, with tolerability limitations: dose-related fissures in the first and acneiform rash requiring interruption/reduction in the second.

Calling these cases simply “no effect” loses the partial right-foot response. Calling them successful topical validation loses the need to abandon that regimen. Low concentration is one plausible explanation for inadequate efficacy, not a demonstrated one. Concentration, vehicle, disease biology, baseline severity, concomitant care and exposure all differed from Lee. The separate [cream formulation/stability paper](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules27031070) establishes a preparation method, not clinical equivalence to Lee's ointment or a high-concentration product.

### Industry studies: distinguish three trials and one compassionate case

| Study lineage | Actual public denominator | Result and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| SGT-84-01, initiated January 2020 | Approximately 15 **planned**; six reported completed | Single-center, single-blind, vehicle-controlled PPK proof of concept. Launch population was punctate PPK type 1. June 2021 issuer reported modest improvement and favorable safety; no arm-level effects, full flow or analysis published in retrieved sources |
| SGT-210 healthy-volunteer maximal-use PK study, started December 2022, completed Q2 2023 | 12 participants, two concentrations | 28 days; 3.5%/5% ointment. Relevant product safety/exposure information, no diseased-skin efficacy evidence |
| SGT-210 Darier Phase 1b, initiated March 2024 | Seven completers reported November 2025; initial target/arm denominators not recovered | Recruitment limited by eligible Israeli patients. December 2025 unblinding showed no differentiation on efficacy assessments; development for Darier stopped |
| SGT-210 pediatric Olmsted compassionate use | One publicly described child | Issuer reports improved face/hand hyperkeratosis, pain and itch. Genotype, dose, comparator, full timing and peer-reviewed case dataset unavailable |

Primary lineage sources: [SGT-84-01 launch](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1684693/000117891320000017/exhibit_99-1.htm), [June 2021 pipeline update](https://ir.sol-gel.com/node/7826), [2022 SEC account](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1684693/000117891322001534/zk2227637.htm), [May 2023 MUSE report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1684693/000117891323001854/zk2329723.htm), [November 2025 seven-completer disclosure](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1684693/000117891325003921/exhibit_99-1.htm), [December 2025 unblinding](https://ir.sol-gel.com/node/9126), [2026 annual filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1684693/000117891326001552/zk2634571.htm).

These are issuer reports, not independently analyzed trial datasets. Later filings repeat earlier participants. One does not count each press release as a new study. The 2022/2025 filings place the old PPK report in Q3 2021 whereas the June 28, 2021 release already describes it; the earliest disclosure and later retrospective wording are retained separately.

The Darier result matters as a warning about mechanistic inference, vehicle response and recruitment; it does not prove lack of efficacy in KRT16 disease. Conversely, healthy-volunteer tolerance and a pediatric Olmsted anecdote do not offset it numerically. The proposed indication needs its own comparison.

The MUSE patent reports all concentrations below finite assay thresholds, not zero exposure. The documented parent-drug quantification limit is 97.73 ng/mL; the metabolite limit is 9.783 ng/mL. This is a substantially narrower statement than “no systemic absorption.” See the product assessment for the [patent's Example 13](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20230390291A1/en). Intact healthy skin, 28 days, product-specific exposure and lack of keratolytic co-treatment do not settle chronic application to fissured plantar lesions.

## Oral evidence: supportive, separately counted

[Basset, Marchal and Hovnanian, JID](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jid.2022.08.045), online 16 September 2022, February 2023 issue, has a [full article with supplement](https://www.jidonline.org/article/S0022202X2201898X/pdf). It treats **three**, not four, people: a 43-year-old man with KRT16 p.Met121Lys received 75 mg/day; a 52-year-old mother and 24-year-old son with KRT6A p.Leu468Pro received 50 mg/day. The fourth person supplied disease material but was untreated. Genotypes establish that these three are distinct from Greco's two and Lee's two.

Over 6–8 months, pain and functional measures improved without a control arm. Daily pain/activity records, a step counter, FFI, DLQI and Skindex-16 were used. Moderate acneiform lesions occurred in two treated people and required acne treatment. A 2022 symposium and ESDR abstract are earlier reports of this lineage, not additional cohorts.

Thus five identifiable patients across Greco and Basset received oral erlotinib for keratin-associated disease, including a related pair. This supports a pharmacological rationale and helps select measurements; it cannot establish the topical product's exposure, effect size or safety. FFI use in an uncontrolled series is also not validation of a foot-specific endpoint for a regulatory trial.

The [2026 BJD O16 abstract](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjd/ljag151.016) mentions anecdotal benefit in six PC patients and a pre/post-treatment KRT16 callus sample. It supplies neither a six-person clinical ledger nor sufficiently specified routes to count six additional topical patients. Its principal study is mechanistic. These anecdotes remain unlinked to the published clinical cases.

Oral Olmsted reports provide more distant support, including the original [Greco 2020 series](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2019.4126), [Zhang 2020 report](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2019.4141), a [2025 TRPV3 series](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40615090/), a [2026 MBTPS2 case](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41362860/), and a [2026 oral erlotinib–acitretin pediatric case](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.abd.2026.501313). Different genotypes, systemic exposure, combination therapy and possible repeated reporting make a pooled response rate inappropriate. The ledger marks papers whose full clinical tables were not recovered; titles alone are not positive outcome data.

The current primary [NCT06545695 registry record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06545695), updated 3 August 2026, lists **44 estimated adults**, not yet recruiting, estimated start 1 July 2027 and completion June 2031. It is an open-label **oral** Phase 1/2 keratinopathy study, with an eight-week baseline and dose escalation over 24 weeks. No results are posted. This is a future source of information and potential recruitment competition, not confirmation of present topical efficacy.

## What disease did Lee actually treat?

Use **KRT16 p.Arg127Cys-associated hereditary palmoplantar keratoderma** in the target definition. Lee calls it hereditary K16 PPK and discusses possible extension to conditions such as PC. KRT16 variants can occur in the PC spectrum and in focal non-epidermolytic PPK with limited nail involvement. The [variant record](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/clinvar/RCV000015705/) and [Wu genotype/phenotype study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8922998/) support that overlap. Do not infer complete classic PC diagnostic features merely from the gene, or imply that the family demonstrates efficacy in all five PC genes.

Wu's January 2020 genotype-plus-survey analysis contains 714 individuals, 61 with KRT16, including 13 p.Arg127Cys. This is **not a current recruitable ceiling**. Two official historical mutation tables report different denominators:

| Source and stated snapshot | KRT16 p.Arg127Cys individuals | Families | Meaning |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| [Official registry, January 2018](https://www.pachyonychia.org/upload/GRAPHS/IPCRRMutations.pdf) | 60 | 20 | Historical registry mutation count |
| Wu analysis, January 2020 data | 13 | Not reported for this variant | Genotype-plus-survey analytic sample |
| [Official registry, January 2021](https://www.pachyonychia.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/IPCRRMutations.pdf) | 88 | 33 | Later historical registry mutation count |

The January 2021 title and c.379C>T / R127C row were independently verified in indexed primary-PDF text. Its URL contains 2018, which is not the snapshot date. The older table was verified in indexed primary-PDF text by the market reviewer. Direct PDF retrieval failed or timed out; neither registry PDF was visually inspected. Wu requires genetic and survey data but does not provide a reconciliation flow explaining its smaller variant count. These overlapping historical populations must not be summed, counted as treated patients, or treated as currently eligible volunteers. None establishes current age, severity, geography, consent, competing enrollment or willingness to use a study product. Treat the discrepancy as a data-definition question, not as evidence of a disappearing population.

## Evidence required to change the decision

1. **Recover reproducibility:** exact Lee pharmacy composition, API/salt/strength basis, batch handling, doses, application instructions, salicylic-acid use, assessment dates and photographs. Obtain consented source records only in an authorized later diligence stage. No outreach occurred in this audit.
2. **Resolve actual treatment effects:** paired foot-pain observations, grooming/analgesic/activity records, randomization and blinding records, dropouts and selection denominator. If no underlying dataset exists, treat that as a limit and replicate prospectively.
3. **Bridge the product:** demonstrate stability, release and relevant diseased-skin delivery for the actual candidate; measure sufficiently sensitive systemic PK and local tolerability. Higher nominal concentration is not the bridge.
4. **Replicate outside the family:** a prospective vehicle-controlled comparison with standardized background care and meaningful pain/function assessment, independent clinical assessment where feasible, and explicit skin-fragility/fissure/infection monitoring. Use patients and pedigrees as biological units; account for correlation of feet.
5. **Recover industry negatives:** old PPK eligibility/genotypes, formulation, actual arm allocation, participant flow, outcomes and adverse events; Darier protocol/data and reasons for nonseparation. Without those data, retain uncertainty rather than treating “modest improvement” as controlled success.
6. **Predeclare the expansion rule:** p.Arg127Cys results may justify testing other KRT16 alleles or PC genes, but broad eligibility should not be smuggled in by a disease label. Phenotype and exposure may matter as much as genotype; test that explicitly.

## Search coverage and access limitations

Searched primary journal/PubMed/PMC records, article references and conference material, ClinicalTrials.gov directly, issuer and SEC archives, patent examples, and targeted indexed searches of ANZCTR/WHO/Israeli registry domains. Search terms combined erlotinib, topical/cream/ointment, KRT16, pachyonychia, palmoplantar keratoderma, Darier, Olmsted, SGT-210 and SGT-84-01, including failed/vehicle/placebo and recent-year variants. No additional independently described published topical KRT16 p.Arg127Cys clinical cohort was found.

ClinicalTrials.gov API searches for SGT-210 and SGT-84 returned zero; the keratoderma/Darier/Olmsted/PC erlotinib query returned NCT06545695. Twelve records returned by the broad erlotinib plus topical/ointment/gel search concerned oral anticancer treatment or topical management of EGFR-inhibitor toxicity, not topical erlotinib treatment of keratoderma. Lack of a US registry match does not mean the Israeli studies did not occur. A complete WHO or Israeli-registry export was not available, and search-index absence cannot establish global absence.

Lee and Basset full PDFs were recovered through indexed article copies even when direct URL retrieval failed. Greco and Wu full texts were read. Public issuer summaries lack raw trial tables. Some recent oral Olmsted papers were only bibliographically accessible. This is a broad, explicitly bounded evidence audit, not a claim to possess every unpublished case or worldwide trial record. Missing negative experience and incomplete reporting remain live risks.
