# Independent challenge review: topical erlotinib

Reviewed 9 November 2026 UTC by a separate reviewer after reading the target profile, human evidence, product, biology/safety, trial-design, rights and economics dossiers. No outreach or clinical work was performed. The user's approximately $500,000 reference is **not a hard budget ceiling**: the relevant question is the value and downside of additional capital, not whether a package crosses that number.

**Recommendation: retain conditional priority for a bounded product-and-replication program, but do not treat topical erlotinib as an established highest-return investment or a high-probability Phase II success.** The strongest exact-use observation is still one family. The program merits further work because that human signal is more informative than mechanism alone, while a local formulation could offer useful benefit. Its development value depends on an actual reproducible article, independent clinical benefit and a retainable economic interest. More capital can improve the study; it cannot establish those facts by itself.

## Findings that materially affect the decision

| Challenge | Assessment and required resolution |
|---|---|
| **The cheap product and the strongest efficacy evidence are not yet one asset.** | The access-enabled route credits a sponsor's existing product/dossier. Lee's favorable observation concerns an incompletely specified 1% ointment with keratolytic care. A sponsor's matching product means matching **its own dossier**, not automatic equivalence to Lee. Do not simultaneously inherit Lee efficacy, a different cream's stability and SGT-210 exposure. Name the chosen article and price the scientific bridge explicitly. |
| **Exact-variant restriction can itself overfit the evidence.** | R127C is a sensible replication anchor, not a validated predictive biomarker. Two relatives cannot distinguish an allele-specific response from shared modifiers, care or selection. Other genotypes can enter a separately justified exploratory cohort; pooling them to obtain enrollment or market size would conceal the very uncertainty under test. Requiring substantial bilateral pain may further reduce an allele group associated with relatively mild phenotypes. |
| **The model cannot answer the drug-success question.** | The illustrative 12-person, true-effect-2, SD-2.5 case gives a 67.5% chance of meeting its pilot advance rule under favorable assumptions; it is not a 67.5% probability that erlotinib works or passes Phase II. The same rule advances about 19% of modeled studies when the true effect is only 1 point, below its assumed 1.5-point target. At the 2-point effect, strong evidence above that target is only 9.1%. Independent families, dropout, interpretability and endpoint relevance matter more than Monte Carlo precision. |
| **A positive pilot and cash recovery remain separate outcomes.** | The central $462k access-enabled and $624k independent packages buy a decision pilot, before a major additional safety requirement. Neither price includes a guaranteed partner, adequate registration evidence or commercial scale. Going above $500k is not a kill criterion. Additional spend is justified only when its incremental evidence, control or retained economics are worth the additional exposure relative to alternatives. An inexpensive study that transfers nearly all useful value to another owner can be worse than a costlier controlled asset. |
| **The narrow market is not established by registry counts.** | Current adult severity, geography, contactability, enrollment competition, treatment persistence and paying access remain unknown. A larger historical registry reduces concern that only one family exists; it does not validate recruitment or revenue. A wider PC market requires additional clinical and product evidence. Break-even calculations are useful requirements, not evidence a buyer will pay them. |
| **The local therapeutic window is the biological gate.** | Lower pERK and thinner callus are not adequate success. Benefit must persist without greater fissuring, erosions, infection, repair impairment or treatment burden. The 2026 barrier experiments raise a relevant mechanism, but their experimental exposures do not show the proposed clinical product is unsafe. Equally, absence of reported adverse effects in two adults cannot qualify chronic use. |
| **The paired-foot design has an interpretation boundary.** | Loading changes, systemic exposure and cross-application can affect the control foot; irritation can reveal allocation. These problems require prespecified exposure, adherence and activity context. They cannot become reusable post hoc explanations that protect the hypothesis from every negative result. Whole-person function is supportive in a local paired study, not an independently randomized foot-level efficacy endpoint. |

The [rights review](ip-regulatory.md) correctly separates issued claims from broad patent descriptions and pending claims. A further material risk is competing same-drug/same-use orphan approval: changing erlotinib's route does not automatically avoid the issue. Conversely, a designation alone is not present market exclusivity. Research permission, a patent license, data-reference access, supply and commercial participation are distinct interests.

The finalized [economics sensitivity](market-economics.md) prices 19 randomized participants at approximately **$510k with transfer or $672k independently**, versus $462k/$624k for 15. Under the design's assumed independent 20% loss, increasing 15 to 19 raises the chance of at least 12 completers from 64.8% to 97.7%. This is a concrete reason to consider additional capital; it is not proof that 12 completers provide adequate precision, that loss is random, or that these participants can be recruited. The [clinical model](clinical-design.md) separately demonstrates how family dependence and attenuation reduce decision yield.

## Corrections and independent source checks

- **Registry denominator challenge resolved in interpretation, not recruitment:** the [January 2021 official mutation table](https://www.pachyonychia.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/IPCRRMutations.pdf) reports **88 R127C individuals in 33 families**, confirmed in the indexed primary PDF's K16 table and dated title. The older January 2018 table reports 60/20. [Wu's primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8922998/) independently confirms that its January 2020 genetic-plus-survey dataset contains KRT16 n=61 and R127C n=13. Thirteen is not a worldwide ceiling; 88 is not a current eligible cohort. Direct access to some registry PDFs was unreliable, so this verification used indexed primary-document content.
- **Exposure wording resolved:** independent inspection of [US20230390291A1, Example 13](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20230390291A1/en) confirms 12 healthy participants, 3.5%/5% ointments, approximately 15% body surface area and 28 days. The stated quantitation limits are 97.73 ng/mL erlotinib and 9.783 ng/mL OSI-420; results were below these limits and no PK analysis was performed. This bounds measured exposure; it does not establish zero absorption or a chronic diseased-skin safety margin.
- **False replication resolved:** four treated feet, repeated publications and mechanistic tissue samples are not additional independent responders. Oral cases and other topical products remain separate evidence. Recent favorable biology therefore has not displaced the central older evidence limitation.
- **Budget interpretation corrected:** approximately $500k is a capital reference, not a mandatory exclusion threshold. The old $350k technical estimate omitted meaningful scope; replacing it with complete staged costs is warranted. Crossing the reference amount alone does not justify demotion.

## Concrete stop or redesign criteria

1. **Before substantial product commitment:** stop the investment route if plausible product/data/commercial arrangements leave no useful retained interest or make reasonable cash-recovery scenarios depend entirely on unvalidated genotype expansion, extraordinary pricing or implausible ownership. Compare the incremental cost of independent control with the economics surrendered in an access deal. Unavailable original records stop an “exact replication” claim, not automatically all new-product development.
2. **At product and exposure qualification:** stop or reprice the chosen article if it cannot be reproduced and supplied for the intended study, or if credible target access cannot be obtained without unacceptable local/systemic exposure. A bounded new formulation can be considered on its own merits; repeated unbudgeted reformulation is not progress by definition.
3. **Before efficacy enrollment:** redesign or stop the proposed study if the accessible independent-patient/pedigree pool cannot support the prespecified precision and interpretation within a defensible cost and timeline. Do not fill a nominal sample by changing genotype, endpoint or biological unit after seeing results. Increasing enrollment can be economically preferable to an inexpensive uninformative result.
4. **After interpretable controlled evidence:** stop the tested product/use when the result excludes a prospectively justified worthwhile effect, or when skin injury and burden offset benefit. A signal confined to the originating family is insufficient replication. A delivery or measurement failure is inconclusive and receives no efficacy credit; additional spending requires a specific, testable correction and renewed value assessment.
5. **Before the next capital stage:** stop even after clinical benefit if incremental development costs, dilution, rights restrictions or attainable commercial scale destroy the retained-value case. Proceed above the initial capital reference when the opposite is true. Keep sunk costs out of that comparison.

The record now addresses the main shallow-diligence failure modes. What remains is uncertainty that public desk research cannot convert into evidence: the actual transferable article and rights, an independent interpretable response, a clinically useful local window and a financeable retained asset. Conditional priority is defensible; assurance of greater-than-reference Phase II success or profitable exit is not yet supported.

## Final synthesis review

The integrated [dossier summary](README.md), [current opportunities](../../../decisions/current-shortlist.md) and [selection policy](../../../decisions/selection-policy.md) were checked against the underlying workstreams after integration. No blocking inconsistency was found. The summary preserves the one-pedigree limitation, exact-product bridge, mixed counterevidence, distinct registry denominators, lack of calibrated success odds, cumulative cost scope and flexible capital reference. It does not present erlotinib as superior to untested alternatives or promote an unchanged strict $500k ranking.

The approximately 9% strong-threshold frequency is explicitly described as a stringent illustrative precision criterion, not a standard Phase II requirement or the probability the drug works. The capital discussion prices a larger enrollment reserve without implying that additional money creates patients, rights or commercial demand. This review supports the summary's conditional conclusion; it does not certify the cost assumptions or convert unresolved private evidence into established facts.
