# Comparative screening method

Set on 9 November 2026 UTC before integrating the asset assessments. The user's sequence is **screen widely, narrow to a handful, then consider inquiries**. This review therefore makes a provisional ranking using public evidence and explicit estimates. It does not require a quote to rank an opportunity, treat an unoffered transaction as available, or authorize outreach or spending.

## Decision unit and scope

Compare an exact drug/use, a hypothesized retained economic interest, and the next consequential development milestone. Distinguish technical feasibility, a meaningful clinical or functional result, and eventual cash recovery. The approximately $500,000 envelope constrains the development thesis; it is not a claim that the whole amount is affordable to lose or sufficient to finish Phase II. Unknown reserve, loss tolerance and horizon remain material to a later commitment.

The review covers the active opportunities, ten-team alternatives, additional retained Round 26 candidates and a targeted check of historically prominent candidates. It is not a fresh audit of every archived discovery record. The coverage dossier identifies the boundary and any exclusions. Historical rank, investigator familiarity, publication recency and the number of agents agreeing supply no independent scoring credit.

## Four judgment dimensions

Scores are ordinal **screening judgments**, not validated measurements, efficacy probabilities, expected returns or a valuation. Each has a low, central and high plausible assessment. Ranges capture uncertainty about the present evidence/package; they are not statistical confidence intervals and need not imply equally likely states.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **H: relevant benefit evidence** | No demonstrated relevant beneficial evidence | Relevant preclinical effect | Uncontrolled or inadequately controlled clinical signal | Prospective randomized/masked signal, or unusually credible causal comparison, with important limitations | Replicated or confirmatory controlled clinical benefit |
| **T: translation and execution** | No coherent viable route described | Multiple major unresolved bridges | Plausible route with substantial product/exposure/safety/design gaps | Substantial human product/exposure experience and a learnable clinical question | Well-characterized exact-use product/exposure and execution route |
| **K: capital fit** | No specified consequential package plausibly within the envelope | Meaningful next readout probably exceeds $500k; only a small contribution might fit | Consequential contribution/readout plausibly within $500k, with important overruns or continuation funding | Approximately $250k or less can plausibly answer a consequential question; remaining bridge explicit | Approximately $100k or less can answer it with a credible modest-capital handoff |
| **V: retained-value plausibility** | No coherent retained-value route described | Weak differentiation or generic/sponsor-owned participation thesis | Plausible differentiated product/data contribution | Strong, specific differentiated and protectable/useful contribution | Exceptionally strong documented attainable capture proposition |

H evaluates evidence *for benefit*, not simply study size or whether a trial was conducted. A negative randomized trial does not earn H3. Historical controls generally remain H2; a larger uncontrolled cohort can narrow uncertainty without becoming randomization. Biomarker correction, human cells and donor-organ experiments do not automatically become patient benefit. T uses the weakest important bridge rather than averaging away an essential missing step. Genetics cannot be counted a second time as observed clinical efficacy.

K's dollar anchors are guides, not automatic bins: a $150k assay with a poorly defined downstream decision can score below a $350k informative clinical package. V concerns a plausible route to retained value, not a named counterparty or an assumed paid contract. Missing offers widen K/V uncertainty and do not automatically make them zero.

## Ordering and sensitivity

The integrating lead uses the same explicit preference weights for every candidate:

| Perspective | H | T | K | V |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Balanced screening | 30% | 25% | 25% | 20% |
| Clinical confidence | 45% | 30% | 15% | 10% |
| Scarce-capital fit | 20% | 20% | 35% | 25% |

The weighted sum is a transparent way to expose judgment, not a fitted predictor. Ordinal arithmetic imposes a convenient spacing assumption; narrative evidence and sensitivity determine whether the ordering is useful. The weights are preference assumptions chosen for this screen, not empirical estimates of what causes success. Close scores and tied ranks remain provisional. The final handful can include a substantively different opportunity with a comparable score; any such selection must be explained rather than silently changing weights.

Three weightings expose objective sensitivity. One-candidate/one-axis substitutions to its recorded low or high value expose local judgment sensitivity while holding others central. Those rank ranges are limited stress tests, not exhaustive combinations or probabilities. Shared access, funding and safety dependencies mean uncertainty is correlated. Simultaneously favorable assumptions cannot be treated as independent evidence.

Before ranking actionable development theses, show separately: an already advanced clinical benchmark without a specified small-capital contribution; a result-dependent program whose completed controlled evidence must first be understood; and an exact claim with meaningful contrary efficacy evidence. These remain visible in the full matrix. A weighted score must not cancel convincing disconfirmation, an incoherent exact product/use, or a required unavailable route. Unknown commercial access alone is not a scientific failure.

For the primary small-capital shortlist, require central **K≥2** and no benchmark, high-burden or unresolved-claim hold. This is a conditional filter for a plausibly financeable next *technical* milestone, not proof of all-in affordability. Show the raw unfiltered ranks beside it so that a strong but larger-capital program cannot disappear. A changed K assessment can move a candidate across this boundary; the final synthesis names those reversals. Mixed evidence such as baricitinib's remains a visible caution even when it does not constitute an exact-claim hold.

`screening-model.json` records the judgments; `calculate.py` produces the complete Markdown/CSV comparison. Run it with `--check` to verify that saved outputs match the model. Competition ranks preserve ties. Generated decimals make the calculation reproducible but do not imply that a 0.05 difference is scientifically meaningful.

## Estimate discipline

Each dossier names the next technical milestone, low/base/high cost and elapsed time, included work, exclusions, basis and continuation. These are analyst planning allowances, not quotations. Costs for a small feasibility study are not a registration-enabling Phase II budget. Do not count a cheap preliminary dossier or assay as if it completed the consequential milestone.

Unpriced background rights, necessary source-data access, major new safety/manufacturing work and financing are explicitly additional. Accordingly the technical figures are not all-in investment exposure. High scenarios are plausible difficult executions, not maximum-loss bounds. Schedules generally begin after access and approvals. Dependence on existing infrastructure is stated, not treated as assured subsidy. Material excluded work or inability to obtain cooperation can defeat the small-capital thesis even where the technical base case fits.

The later investment decision still follows the [selection policy](../../../decisions/selection-policy.md): attainable interest, complete incremental economics, survivable exposure and comparison with preserving capital. This screen identifies which few packages deserve deeper public-source work first. It does not establish that any candidate is more likely than not to pass Phase II or return the investment.
