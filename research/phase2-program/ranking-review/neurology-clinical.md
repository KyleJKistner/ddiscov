# Neurology and existing clinical programs: comparative screen

Reviewed 9 November 2026 UTC by `/root/rank_neurology_clinical`, against `cc34bf2`. This is a desk screen under uncertainty. No inquiry, quote, protocol approval or investment offer has been obtained. The six packages below remain eligible for comparison; an unknown price is not treated as proof of unaffordability. Current rights and necessary product/data cooperation are nonetheless material dependencies.

## Judgment and estimate conventions

Scores are **low–central–high judgments**, not probabilities or measured quantities. H measures exact-use clinical benefit; T the weakest important product/exposure/safety/design bridge; K the fit of a consequential milestone and continuation with approximately $500,000; V the plausibility of differentiated retained value. A cheap hypothetical analysis contract does not earn a high K when it cannot change an important development decision. A successful historical Phase II is not a forecast we made.

Cost figures are **unquoted planning estimates in 2026 USD**, rounded deliberately. Stage A is a scoped product/data/regulatory feasibility package; Stage B is incremental development after A. The B estimates assume access to existing material and necessary source data on tolerable terms. They include scientific staff, project management, applicable site/product/measurement work and approximately 20–30% contingency. They exclude license/upfront consideration, royalties, institutional investment terms and commercialization. Those exclusions are unpriced—not zero—and can overwhelm the estimates. No estimate claims $500,000 completes a registration program. Low scenarios require existing infrastructure; high scenarios represent greater operational or product work, not an unlimited adverse case.

The following totals add A and B for the same scoped result described below, rather than comparing one candidate's feasibility work with another's clinical readout. They are arithmetic sums of rough assumptions, not more precise estimates. Elapsed-time sums assume sequential stages; prerequisites or recruitment delays can extend them.

## Comparative assessments

### Mavoglurant / adult cocaine use disorder

**Package:** an indication-program interest tied to a necessary clinical-development contribution, rather than ownership of an available generic. The exact-use randomized evidence is the strongest actionable drug-development signal in this subgroup after the already advanced azetukalner program.

The [primary EU results record](https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/trial/2017-000736-33/results), freshly read, reports 71 randomized, 68 treated and 65 in the primary analysis. The cocaine-use-day difference was −8.7 percentage points (95% CI −16.1 to −1.3); only 22/31 active and 32/37 placebo participants completed. The endpoint used observed days through discontinuation, making differential attrition important. **The study ended in December 2019 and results appeared in December 2020.** The [April 2025 paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40173260/) is the same cohort; its indexed abstract reports considerably less certainty for an effect exceeding ten percentage points than for any reduction. Full paper/source data were not recovered.

The [issuer's 2024 announcement](https://www.globenewswire.com/fr/news-release/2024/05/02/2874183/0/en/stalicla-initiates-u-s-phase-3-enabling-ddi-study-of-stp7-mavoglurant-to-treat-cocaine-use-disorder.html) identifies the STALICLA/NIDA development route and an enabling interaction study. Current primary registry retrieval for that study failed; completion or Phase III initiation is not assumed from third-party summaries. The [prior dossier](../teams/04-shelved-clinical-assets.md) preserves genuine failed fragile-X efficacy studies and psychiatric/hepatic safety issues, not merely financial shelving.

**Next development milestone:** a clinically meaningful placebo-controlled replication with objective use verification, prespecified missing-data sensitivity and acceptable retention/safety. Reanalysis alone can change the decision to replicate but is not replication. Stage A estimates **$40k/$80k/$150k; 3/5/9 months**. A standalone 100–150-participant replication is estimated **$2m/$4m/$8m; 18/24/36 months**, using several sites, frequent visits/urine verification, drug/placebo supply and monitoring. A ≤$500k contribution to a larger funded study may be useful, but its scope and retained interest are not yet established. A materially larger, robust effect or a necessary funded subpackage raises priority; attrition-sensitive efficacy, interaction problems or duplicating funded work lowers it.

### Levodopa/carbidopa / symptomatic movement impairment in MCT8 deficiency

**Package:** controlled adjunctive-function development with rights to a genuinely needed pediatric product or regulatory dataset. The claim is improved movement initiation and useful function during treatment; it is not correction of thyroid transport or global developmental rescue.

Two primary papers were refreshed in full. [Wilpert et al., March 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12089910/) describes eleven phenotyped males, with six systematically assessed after treatment. Some movement and fine-motor measures improved, but gross motor, cognition, language and CPCHILD did not significantly improve. [Bruschi et al., online October 2025; February 2026 issue](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13001712/) describes ten males but nine assessable treatment courses: seven broad improvements, eight reduced hypokinesia, and five of eight improved GMFM. Two GMFM values declined; functional classifications did not consistently improve and action dystonia increased. Neither series had a concurrent randomized control. Their participant linkage remains unresolved, so totals and responder fractions are not pooled.

**Next development milestone:** a clinician-designed, blinded, prospectively evaluated adjunctive study in approximately 12–20 independently recruited patients with stable background care, exposure/adherence checks, prespecified useful-function endpoints and burden assessment. A crossover or withdrawal design is only an option if carryover, ethical and clinical constraints permit. Stage A **$30k/$60k/$100k; 3/5/8 months**; Stage B **$200k/$400k/$800k; 12/18/30 months**. These assume an existing usable preparation and a small academic network; a new formulation, multicountry recruitment or sponsor-grade registration package raises cost.

This is more financially reachable than independent late-stage CNS development, but generic availability weakens capture. Do not invent a formulation deficit merely to create exclusivity. A differentiated product need or usable contracted/retained dataset would help; a result useful only for routine off-label care may benefit patients without repaying development capital. Failure of a relevant functional endpoint, unacceptable increased dystonia or lack of product differentiation lowers priority.

### Prolonged-release fampridine / FGF14-SCA27B

**Package:** a differentiated functional/product contribution alongside existing development. [Pellerin et al., March 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38507876/) supports a 4-aminopyridine ocular-motor signal. The [previous source extraction](../teams/05-rare-neurometabolic.md) identifies only four retrospectively genotyped participants in the earlier blinded study. That is not a prospective prolonged-release SCA27B functional trial. Fresh full-paper retrieval failed; the indexed primary record was refreshed.

[TREAT-FGF14, NCT07185347](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07185347) is an existing randomized study, planned n=70, started October 2025, using a week-12 functional-stage outcome. These details retain the prior direct API read because the current API failed and current public page exposed no data. The [official CTIS page](https://euclinicaltrials.eu/ctis-public/view/2024-520413-53-00) remained unreadable. Recruitment status is therefore not freshly resolved. No efficacy result is assumed. Existing dalfampridine experience helps product characterization but seizure/renal restrictions and the exact formulation/use bridge remain important.

**Next development milestone:** first use the ongoing trial's eventual functional evidence; an additional study is justified only by a specific unresolved claim. An independently assessed daily-function/gait and exposure substudy using existing infrastructure is an illustrative incremental package, but the reviewed public evidence does not establish a useful residual question beyond the n=70 trial. Stage A **$25k/$50k/$100k; 2/4/8 months**; such a nonduplicative Stage B **$150k/$300k/$650k; 9/15/24 months**. A fresh multicenter efficacy trial instead is estimated **$1m/$2m/$4m**, outside the small-contribution case. The $350k base A+B scenario therefore does not establish a currently meaningful affordable milestone; K is reduced to 1, with upside retained if a genuine residual need is identified. All estimates depend on permissions and actual trial feasibility; a substudy that can no longer be added is not an available opportunity.

Clinical attractiveness is moderate; generic capture and the existing program's ability to answer the same question lower capital priority. A clear functional signal plus a genuine formulation/delivery need raises it. Negative controlled function, seizure burden, or no remaining differentiated contribution lowers it.

### Saracatinib / adult fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva

**Package:** conditional participation in a program with completed but not fully disclosed controlled evidence. The [STOPFOP sponsor-consortium report](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/821600/reporting), refreshed, says 17 patients completed the randomized and open-label phases and entered extension. It reports no serious side effects during the randomized phase, but expressly defers efficacy conclusions pending analysis. Continued treatment and qualitative benefit language do not establish a controlled effect. The [results page](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/821600/results) still chiefly lists protocol and project deliverables. Its stale statement that no treatments are approved should not be inherited as a current comparator claim.

The direct disease rationale and prior human exposure support continued screening, but the relevant effect remains unknown. The [prior dossier](../teams/04-shelved-clinical-assets.md) retains negative Alzheimer efficacy and tolerability findings and the distinction between an academic molecule-access scheme and commercial rights.

**Next development milestone:** recover/analyze the completed randomized dataset before considering new efficacy enrollment. Stage A **$40k/$90k/$180k; 3/6/10 months** includes endpoint/imaging audit and regulatory/comparator mapping, assuming usable raw data. A subsequent 20–40-person multicenter confirmatory/replication package is estimated **$1m/$2.5m/$5m; 18/30/42 months**. A small analysis contribution may unlock an important result, but no useful ownership economics follow automatically. Unknown controlled results justify a **result-dependent** status, not an efficacy-failure label. Robust prevention of clinically consequential new ossification at tolerable exposure could move it sharply upward; a negative randomized result could remove the exact claim.

### Azetukalner / adjunctive adult focal seizures

**Package:** advanced-program participation benchmark. [The primary Phase IIb report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/10562989/) enrolled 325 treated patients (323 efficacy), with dose-dependent seizure reductions versus placebo. Its actual randomized period ran June 2019–August 2021; the paper appeared in 2023. [Xenon's September 2026 announcement](https://xenonpharma.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/xenon-announces-azetukalner-nda-submission-fda-focal-seizures) reports a separate positive Phase III program and NDA submission, not approval. The [prior dossier](../teams/09-precision-cns.md) retains the detailed Phase III denominators and adverse-event discontinuations. Fresh issuer text confirms a psychiatry-enrollment pause after neuropsychiatric-event review and says epilepsy studies were unaffected. That is relevant safety context, not an epilepsy clinical hold.

**Next development milestone:** no new independent Phase II is justified. A potentially useful contribution would address a specific remaining tolerability, adherence or clinically relevant outcomes question; none is yet identified as unmet by the sponsor. Stage A **$25k/$50k/$100k; 2/4/6 months** maps that question against existing evidence. A hypothesis-testing 60–100-person adjunctive study would be roughly **$1m/$2.5m/$5m; 15/24/36 months**. There is no basis to substitute an imaginary cheap analytics engagement for this exposure.

This is the strongest efficacy benchmark here and a weak currently specified entrepreneurial package: the sponsor owns an advanced, well-funded program. Public equity is a separate financial transaction. A genuinely necessary, remunerated small contribution would improve K/V; the absence of such a role keeps it outside the preferred new-development shortlist despite H4.

### GA002 / refractory unilateral mesial temporal-lobe epilepsy

**Package:** a sponsor-linked gene/procedure and activating-drug program. The [investigator institution's November 2026 account](https://www.cibr.ac.cn/detail/researchAchievementpaper/7e141d834d99406db7c00600ce933837) was refreshed and describes three people, large seizure reductions over an initial two-month interval and longer follow-up. It is the same cohort as the [new NEJM letter](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMc2606815), not independent replication. Full letter, protocol, individual trajectories and comprehensive safety tables remain unavailable in this pass. Larger uncontrolled percentage improvement cannot be compared directly with a placebo-controlled oral-drug effect.

**Next development milestone:** controlled, independently assessed benefit–risk in an expanded cohort, including cognition and prolonged safety, with vector/process comparability and procedural reproducibility. Turning off the activating drug does not remove the gene exposure. Stage A **$75k/$150k/$300k; 4/8/12 months** covers a serious clinical/product/regulatory assessment; Stage B **$3m/$7m/$15m; 24/36/48 months** for an illustrative 12–24-person sponsor-linked study with surgery, specialized product and prolonged follow-up. These are scope estimates, not a complete gene-therapy development budget. US/China development permissions reported by the institution were not independently regulator-verified.

The potential differentiated value is real, but a useful role for $500,000 remains particularly unclear. Complete credible safety/efficacy data, transferable product rights and sponsor-funded continuation could raise priority; vector/procedure hazards, unclear endpoint ascertainment or a need to recreate manufacturing lower it. The recent publication earns no recency bonus.

Clinical-evidence ordering is **azetukalner > mavoglurant > fampridine/levodopa > GA002 > presently disclosed saracatinib**; the last is a disclosure limit, not a forecast of a negative result. Under the central balanced weights, the calculated scores are **azetukalner 2.15, mavoglurant 2.10, levodopa and fampridine tied at 1.80, saracatinib 1.25, GA002 1.05**. These arithmetic outputs do not imply calibrated precision; access/capture assumptions can reverse the leading order. For an actively buildable small-capital development shortlist, **levodopa merits comparison with mavoglurant participation**; fampridine remains conditional on a genuinely incremental contribution, and azetukalner is retained as a benchmark/conditional participation route. No hard scientific stop was established for any of these exact uses. The most important next screening work is specific differentiation and realistic milestone design; inquiries are deferred until the portfolio is narrowed.
