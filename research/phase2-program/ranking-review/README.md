# Ranked screening review: 35 candidates

**Decision date: 9 November 2026 UTC / 8 November Los Angeles.** Six asset-review agents and a separate methodology reviewer completed this comparison, integrated by `/root`. It covers 35 exact-use packages, with explicit evidence, contrary findings, product and safety gaps, potential retained value, and development cost/time estimates. This is the current **screening** recommendation. No inquiries or paid work were performed.

## Recommendation

Narrow the next public-source diligence to **topical erlotinib, topical insulin, lisdexamfetamine/KCNMA1, APR-246 and enasidenib**. Retain **dornase and mavoglurant** as alternatives with larger randomized human datasets whose next meaningful programs currently need more capital or a specific smaller participation opportunity. Their endpoint, attrition and replication limitations remain important. Keep the full comparison visible rather than confusing a budget-constrained shortlist with a global ranking of drugs.

These five share a plausible path to a consequential technical milestone within approximately $500,000 under central assumptions. That is a screening hypothesis, not an investment recommendation. Their **all-in** exposure is not established. None has a demonstrated high probability of capital recovery or an asset-specific, calibrated probability of passing Phase II.

## What is—and is not—stable

The [method](method.md) separates human benefit (H), translation/execution (T), capital fit (K) and retained-value plausibility (V). It applies balanced, clinical-confidence and scarce-capital weights, then shows both raw ranks and a conditional K≥2 shortlist. Read the [complete matrix](screening-matrix.md) or [CSV](screening-matrix.csv) for all 35; the [model](screening-model.json) contains score ranges and flags.

At central judgments, erlotinib ranks first within the conditional small-capital set under all three weightings. The five above remain the leading group under balanced and clinical weights. Under capital weights, insulin shares the fifth-place boundary with **RM4404**, whose cheaper animal-function milestone remains far earlier than clinical proof. APR and enasidenib move up within that group when capital/capture receives more weight. This is preference sensitivity, not an observed probability of success.

The unfiltered balanced calculation places erlotinib first, the **azetukalner advanced-program benchmark** second, **dornase and mavoglurant** tied third, then insulin/lisdexamfetamine, then APR/enasidenib. That order is displayed precisely because the primary shortlist applies a capital constraint. Azetukalner has the strongest established clinical evidence here, but no defined necessary small contribution. Its historical success is not a new drug-discovery prediction.

One-axis changes within the recorded judgment ranges move erlotinib's raw rank from 1 to 6, APR's from 1 to 18, and enasidenib's from 2 to 23. Thus the apparent precision of any single list is weak. The ranges are limited local stress tests with other candidates held central; simultaneous changes can be larger. They are not confidence intervals. Shared funding/access uncertainty also prevents treating the handful as independent bets.

| Enasidenib | $150k | This must cover **all** excluded drug supply, rights and extra work. For the illustrative six-patient study it is at most $25k per patient over the whole study before other exclusions. |

Headroom is $500k minus a rough technical base cost, not an authorized allocation, reserve or loss limit. The precision-repurposing budgets exclude drug supply explicitly; the local and neurological packages include more product/supply work. Therefore enasidenib's $350k cannot be treated as equally complete to erlotinib's $350k. Under a supported/existing-supply scenario, the enasidenib milestone may fit. Under a purchased-research-supply scenario exceeding the $150k combined allowance, it does not: K must fall below the shortlist boundary. No free drug, insurance reimbursement, assistance eligibility or sponsor donation is assumed to exist. A definitive dose/duration and procurement budget is not yet available, so this is a transparent break-even scenario rather than an invented price.

Equivalent boundaries apply elsewhere: a meaningful ≤$500k dornase or mavoglurant contribution could admit those programs; an uninformative small sub-study does not. Alpelisib, trametinib and fampridine need an identifiable residual question beyond existing funded development before cheap hypothetical contributions earn capital-fit credit.

## Findings that changed the comparison

- **Coverage expanded:** the initial 23 packages omitted seven retained Round 26 candidates and five legacy clinical sentinels. Dornase and lisdexamfetamine were important challengers. The [coverage audit](process-and-coverage.md#coverage-audit-and-boundaries) identifies the 622-row historical register as broader than this bounded review; aliases, old screens and unreviewed reserves are not silently counted as fresh diligence.
- **APR:** no newly identified adverse APR result caused its current position. Its evidence design and product/capital uncertainties are now compared against the same standards as challengers. Useful ongoing control remains the claim; cure and off-treatment durability are not extra requirements.
- **Dornase:** an additional 58-person randomized report strengthens the need to inspect it, but its registered primary staining and OSDI analyses are within-group. The earlier pilot's between-arm symptom result was not significant. Cohort independence remains unresolved; neither “105 independent patients” nor confirmed comparative symptom benefit is claimed.
- **Baricitinib:** the Japanese adult CANDLE/NNS cohort adds mixed results and an efficacy-related ClinicalTrials.gov termination, alongside a conflicting EU premature-ending field. This reduces confidence relative to the earlier remission-focused account and warrants a separate caution.
- **AAT:** the relevant human evidence comes from donor lungs outside recipients. Its central meaningful donor-process package is approximately $350k, not merely the earlier $30–100k qualification stage. No paying customer is assumed.
- **Mavoglurant:** randomized evidence is valuable, but the 2025 article reports a trial completed in 2019. The illustrative independent replication totals approximately $4.08m; a small investor needs a different, genuinely useful contribution.
- **TTI-0102, timolol and regadenoson:** endpoint/analysis uncertainty or unfavorable controlled findings remain explicit holds. A favorable weighted sum cannot erase them.
