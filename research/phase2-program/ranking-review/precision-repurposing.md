# Precision repurposing: comparative screening review

Reviewed 9 November 2026 UTC (8 November Los Angeles) by `/root/rank_precision_repurposing`. Scope: five exact-use packages. This is a screening judgment using public evidence and explicit planning assumptions, not an offered transaction or an allocation. No inquiries occurred. Earlier evidence detail remains in [Team 01](../teams/01-genotype-repurposing.md), [Team 03](../teams/03-human-genetic-targets.md) and the linked historical dossiers.

## Comparative judgment

The central balanced score puts **enasidenib/D2HGA2 first**, **alpelisib/TEK and baricitinib/CANDLE tied next**, then **trametinib/RAS-HCM**, and **GBT601 plus PT2399/Leigh**. Baricitinib's numerical tie is subject to a separate adverse-evidence flag: newly recovered Japanese results show mixed outcomes and a documented efficacy-related termination, so it requires explanation before another broad efficacy program. Alpelisib has the more developed product/clinical pathway; enasidenib has a more distinctive small indication-development question but fewer patients and harder recruitment. Their order is not robust. Trametinib is clinically compelling, but an existing large funded study diminishes the incremental value of a new small sponsor. GBT601's late-treatment animal benefit is strong preclinical evidence but carries the largest translation burden here.

Scores below are **low / central / high judgments**, not confidence intervals or success probabilities. H = evidence for exact-use benefit (H1 can represent relevant preclinical evidence); T = product/exposure/safety and learnable clinical bridge; K = capital fit of a consequential milestone and continuation; V = plausible differentiated retained value. Missing commercial terms widen K/V rather than exclude every candidate. H3 requires unusually credible causal comparison; sample size alone does not qualify. Trametinib's H3 upper assessment is a favorable interpretation of the existing comparative data, not an assumed future positive trial.

With H/T/K/V weights of 30/25/25/20, central scores in table order are 1.80, 2.00, 1.55, 1.80 and 1.20 respectively. These differences do not support precise rank confidence. Clinical-first weights place alpelisib ahead centrally; interpreting trametinib's existing comparison as H3 would move it above both leaders. Capital-first weights favor enasidenib over alpelisib (2.00 versus 1.60). A documented differentiated role could reverse any of the first four; no hypothetical contract is counted as revenue. Baricitinib's K2 reflects the publicly documented unresolved exposure-versus-control tradeoff, not an offered contract; its adverse-evidence flag remains controlling for a broad repeat efficacy program.

## Enasidenib: narrow cardiac indication-development package

**Claim and evidence.** Oral enasidenib in germline IDH2 R140Q D2HGA2, initially focusing on established cardiac dysfunction rather than generalized neurological repair. [Geoerger 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-023-02382-9) reports two children with biochemical normalization, one with improved severe cardiomyopathy. [Gold 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38736636/) adds one distinguishable child with cardiac, seizure and developmental observations and aminotransferase/CK abnormalities requiring regimen adjustment. These support at least three distinguishable patients, not a controlled response rate. The two additional dermatological records remain incompletely deduplicated. The 2026 neonatal abstract remains metadata-only and adds no verified outcome. Main abstracts were refreshed; detailed trajectories retain the [Round 7 supplement/XML audit](../../rounds/round7/enasidenib_clinical.md).

**What could fail.** Metabolite suppression may not reverse established structural injury; severe cardiac improvement rests principally on one uncontrolled trajectory with concurrent care. Pediatric drug concentrations and a reliable exposure–benefit window remain incomplete. An infant delivery problem cannot be assumed merely from adult tablet labeling: a pediatric sprinkle precedent already exists. Rare recruitment can dominate time even for a small cohort.

**Development contribution.** A prospectively specified, approximately six-patient organ/exposure study with repeated baseline measurements, centrally masked cardiac review, linked drug/metabolite sampling and all-treated safety accounting. The sample is a costing scenario, not a power calculation or adequate confirmation by definition. Useful benefit with interpretable exposure supports a larger indication program; biochemical correction without worthwhile cardiac change at adequate exposure narrows or stops that claim.

**Retained value hypothesis.** An indication-data interest or a specific pediatric product improvement could be differentiated. Drug ownership, commercial supply and rights to use clinical data remain separate. Do not credit voucher revenue or assume a new formulation is patentable; the [earlier capture analysis](../../rounds/round15/enasidenib-product-capture.md) documents these constraints. If suitable recruitment or a differentiated interest is implausible even under favorable assumptions, rank below the local-product candidates.

## Alpelisib: TEK-specific benefit/exposure contribution

**Claim and evidence.** Oral alpelisib for symptomatic activating TEK venous malformations. [Sterba 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-37468-4) includes 13 TEK patients among 18, with nine TEK patients in the comparable monotherapy MRI subset; its detailed interpretation is carried from Team 03 after fresh PMC access failed. [Zerbib 2024](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11180659/fullTextXML), freshly retrieved, includes nine TEK patients among 25; symptoms improved and lesion reduction was reported, but abstract and Results disagree on median versus mean. No pooled responder denominator is justified. [Pithadia 2025/2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12859186/) adds a UCSF retrospective series of 41, containing four TEK patients. Its overall 92% clinical-improvement figure is not a TEK-specific response estimate. Imaging and symptoms disagreed in many evaluable patients, reinforcing the need for a patient-relevant endpoint. Main text and indexed Table 1 were inspected; supplement-level individual TEK response was not reanalyzed.

**What could fail.** Open-label selection, concomitant procedures and subjective response assessment can exaggerate benefit. Hyperglycemia and other systemic toxicities can offset sustained symptom gains. A developed oral product and supportive PROS experience reduce some product uncertainty without establishing efficacy in TEK-only disease.

**Development contribution.** A TEK-specific prospective symptom/exposure substudy of roughly ten patients, with blinded imaging review as supportive evidence and reproducible pain/function measurement. The [official NCT07543822 API](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT07543822), retrieved directly on the review date, records an already recruiting 61-person Phase II pathway study, updated 19 October 2026. The proposed substudy is valuable only if it adds an unresolved question, population or analysis beyond that program; copying its funded work has little incremental value. No such unfilled role was established in this screen, so the inexpensive overlay remains hypothetical and central K is 1, not 2. The technical cost below must not substitute for the cost of an independent clinical program.

**Retained value hypothesis.** A sponsor-compatible analysis, endpoint or exposure contribution with contracted rights; an independent generic indication thesis has weak capture. Tablets and granules already exist. Stronger causal symptom evidence could improve clinical rank, but an absent incremental role lowers financial rank even if the drug works.

## Trametinib: participate in a defined residual RAS-HCM question

**Claim and evidence.** Severe molecularly defined RASopathy-associated HCM. [Wolf 2024/2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11897442/), refreshed in full, compares 30 treated patients with 31 controls; surgery/transplant/death occurred in 5 versus 27. Five treated patients were previously reported. Historical eras, selection, prior surgery and clinician-dependent surgical decisions remain material confounding; unadjusted death/transplant alone was not significant. Four treated patients died; severe pulmonary/lymphatic disease may limit response. This is stronger than isolated cases but not randomized confirmation.

**What could fail.** Genotype, disease stage and pulmonary comorbidity may alter response. An existing pediatric oral solution does not settle infant single-agent exposure or cardiac/ocular safety. Recruitment and intensive cardiac follow-up are costly.

**Development contribution and capture.** An exposure–response or genotype/comorbidity analysis prospectively embedded in ongoing development could be a discrete contribution. [NIH's award](https://taggs.hhs.gov/Detail/AwardDetail?arg_AwardNum=UG3HL185610&arg_ProgOfficeCode=130) describes a 25-infant Phase III study with external controls. [Mount Sinai's 21 October 2026 announcement](https://www.mountsinai.org/about/newsroom/2026/mount-sinai-to-lead-national-trial-of-targeted-therapy-for-infants-with-noonan-syndrome-and-severe-hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy) describes combined recommended/awarded infrastructure exceeding $8.5 million across six years, with subsequent phases conditional. That is a real scope anchor, not an available subcontract or a per-patient price. An independent duplicate trial is a poor fit; no distinct unfilled role was established, so central K is 1. The $250k central overlay estimate below is hypothetical and excludes the host trial, whose resources cannot be presumed available to ddiscov. Clinically this can outrank enasidenib; attainable marginal value can reverse that order.

## Baricitinib: exposure/safety optimization under mixed evidence

**Claim and evidence.** Continued inflammatory disease control in genetically confirmed CANDLE/PRAAS. [JCI 2018](https://www.jci.org/articles/view/98814) reports remission in five of ten CANDLE patients; [2024 taper follow-up](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11420725/) reuses that cohort and is not independent replication. The newly identified [Japanese study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37087470/) and [final EU results](https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/trial/2025-000001-16/results) describe a distinguishable five-person adult NNS/CANDLE subgroup. Final primary diary change was −0.217 (SD 0.586), with no statistical analysis; physician assessment improved and four of five met the primary-period steroid-reduction criterion. Three of five had serious adverse events; there were no CANDLE deaths. Adults with Japanese NNS are not interchangeable with the pediatric NIH population.

The [official NCT04517253 API](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT04517253) records termination for efficacy/effectiveness reasons, whereas the EU page says the trial did not end prematurely. Both facts must remain visible; small uncontrolled improvement cannot erase the termination, nor does the termination prove every CANDLE use ineffective. The older publication and final registry are one study lineage. These are newly recovered results, not newly generated 2026 evidence.

**Development contribution and capture.** A small exposure/safety and validated symptom-measurement program could investigate whether worthwhile control can be retained at tolerable exposure. It must be justified by the mixed data before new intervention; indiscriminate dose reduction can cause flare. Infection, cytopenia and dose-dependent control are central, not generic label caveats. A sponsor-aligned analysis contract is conceivable, but a new broad CANDLE efficacy program presently has weaker marginal justification than the leading options. Clarifying the failed objective and defining a genuinely distinct responsive population could raise rank; another adequate negative study would lower it.

## GBT601 plus PT2399: strong animal signal, high translation burden

[Wang 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42853664/) reports that starting the combination in advanced Ndufs4-knockout disease extended median mouse survival from 62 to 158 days, versus 105 days with GBT601 alone. Late-treatment functional benefit is a substantive advantage over prophylactic animal rescue. The preprint and paper are one lineage. No exact combination human Leigh outcome is established.

**Development contribution.** A licensed preclinical package testing reproducible late-treatment benefit alongside exposure, oxygen-delivery physiology and tolerability of the exact combination. The clinical bridge is unusually difficult: both altering hemoglobin oxygen affinity and suppressing compensatory HIF-2 responses may impose systemic risk. An apparently successful mouse package still leaves substantial juvenile safety, manufacturing and human dose work.

The current [main osivelotor trial API](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT05431088) records recruiting Phase II/III sickle-cell development. Its [extension](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT05632354) was terminated because non-placebo-controlled data might not inform benefit/risk. Thus neither general asset discontinuation nor clean clinical safety can be inferred. Sickle-cell use is not a bridge already validated for Leigh disease. A two-party drug/IP contribution has plausible differentiation, but inaccessible product rights or an inadequate physiological safety window is a genuine stop condition.

## Planning estimates and downside

All amounts are **analyst assumptions in USD**, not quotes. They estimate technical execution through one defined readout after access is obtained. They include assumed site/assay work, data management, oversight and contingency; they exclude unpriced drug supply, licenses, regulatory-reference rights, major new manufacturing, routine care charged outside research and subsequent registration. Consequently they are not maximum cash commitments. Those exclusions can make even the low scenario unaffordable.

Low scenarios require accessible existing infrastructure and a narrower informative question. High scenarios include recruitment or assay redevelopment friction but are not worst-case limits. Separate short desk screening can cost less; it must not be presented as these development readouts. Each program can stop after a negative or uninterpretable result without assuming a sale. None has a documented payer for its output, and none currently establishes a high probability of returning the user's capital.
