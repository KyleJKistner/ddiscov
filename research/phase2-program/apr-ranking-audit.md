# APR-246: audit of the changed ranking

Audit: 8 November 2026, America/Los_Angeles (9 November UTC). Reviewed against program commit `53c0781`. Two separate reviewers, `/root/apr_history_audit` and `/root/ranking_bias_audit`, examined the evidence history and comparison method; the integrating lead checked their findings against the records below. This is a ranking audit, not a new clinical study, exhaustive literature review or completed investment underwriting.

## Decision and correction

The available record does **not substantiate demoting topical APR-246 for AEC erosions to a backup**. APR remains a leading unresolved development comparison alongside topical erlotinib for KRT16 p.Arg127Cys keratoderma. Their relative priority is open pending comparable evidence and attainable terms. The historical first position is not automatically restored as an investment recommendation.

The conversational summary conflated a route's lead, a commercial inquiry priority and an overall development ranking. Team 02 also applied an asymmetric condition: APR could compete if its commercial terms were materially better, although erlotinib's terms were unknown too. That condition is withdrawn. Neither candidate must defeat favorable terms merely assumed for the other.

## Matched local-development comparison

The [original APR paper](https://www.sindrome-eec.it/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/s41419-020-2223-8.pdf) and [complete two-page erlotinib letter](https://www.pachyonychia.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/LeeG2022Topical-Erlotinib.-pdf.pdf) were reread for this audit. The latter became accessible after the failed retrieval recorded in Team 02. Its text explicitly describes double-blinded randomized opposite-limb phases, consistent with the [Round 26 full-text extraction](../rounds/round26/local-product-screen.md). Neither publication supplies the underlying patient-level dataset for independent reanalysis.

| Decision dimension | APR-246 / persistent AEC erosions | Topical erlotinib / KRT16 p.Arg127Cys keratoderma |
|---|---|---|
| Proposed useful claim | Ongoing control of erosions, pain and treatment burden. | Ongoing improvement in pain, function and treatment burden. |
| Exact-use human signal | Two children; active-treated palm with contralateral vehicle in one, whole-scalp treatment in the other. Improved covering and reported symptom/burden measures during treatment. | Two people from one family; double-blinded randomized opposite-foot phases, with stronger response in the mother and more modest controlled response in the son; later uncontrolled extensions. |
| Causal and replication limits | Not reported as randomized or blinded; scalp lacked simultaneous control. No established independent clinical replication in the reviewed record. | Randomization and blinding are real design advantages. One pedigree, co-treatment and uncontrolled extensions constrain interpretation and generalization. |
| Product/exposure question | Transferable, stable finished article; relevant dermal exposure and safety for continued use. | Reproducible effective topical delivery; relevant exposure and safety; bridge between historical compounded product and any proposed sponsor product. |
| Evidence that must remain visible | Incomplete cornification and unmeasured barrier function limit broad repair claims. They do not by themselves refute useful ongoing control. | Different-genotype/preparation topical reports were inadequate or inconclusive; SGT-210 failed to separate from vehicle in Darier. These constrain generalization without being a failed trial of the exact KRT16 use. |
| Next comparable decision | Whether the attainable product and evidence package supports a feasible, informative next milestone. Select the clinical or functional evidence route for the actual uncertainty. | Whether the attainable product and evidence package supports a feasible, informative next milestone. Apply the same evidence-route criterion. |
| Economics | Current usable rights, complete staged scope, all-in cost, continuation funding and retained cash route unresolved. | The same items unresolved. A named developer is not an offered license, contract or payment. |

The same benefit standard applies to both: clinically useful sustained control during treatment can be valuable. Neither must demonstrate a cure or off-treatment durability unless that is the proposed claim. Neither earns a clinical safety conclusion from a tiny case series. Neither automatically requires another preclinical efficacy program; subsequent clinical confirmation needs an interpretable comparison in independently recruited patients, with the prior cohort and pedigree dependence preserved.

## Other comparisons and bias checks

[Enasidenib](teams/01-genotype-repurposing.md) has a particularly direct causal-genotype rationale; [mavoglurant](teams/04-shelved-clinical-assets.md) has randomized human evidence; [AAT](teams/08-organ-preservation.md) offers a possible product/process contribution. These are distinct advantages. They do not establish superiority to APR in an obtainable, priced development opportunity. In particular, a hypothetical paid AAT contract must not be counted as existing revenue.

The source work was substantive, but the aggregation was insufficient for an overall ranking. The briefs required one lead and one alternative per heterogeneous route, and some assignments named preferred leads. Coverage was unequal; no blinded cross-candidate ranking, common aggregation rule or calibrated candidate probability was documented. Thirty agents sharing studies are not thirty independent observations. Repository tests check the records and tools, not the scientific validity of an ordering.

A qualitative sensitivity check gives the same conclusion: removing recent-publication labels, sponsor-name familiarity and hypothetical contracts leaves legitimate differences in human evidence, but no demonstrated investment ordering. Preserving current access facts still helps choose whom to investigate. This is a logical check of the stated rationale, not a blinded experiment or quantitative robustness test. The audit identifies a plausible route for anchoring and recency effects; it cannot establish which bias caused the mistake or certify freedom from bias.

Future rank changes follow the [evidence standard](phase2-evidence-standard.md#changing-a-priority). Compare the same next decision and record the specific incremental evidence, remaining unknowns and reversal conditions before changing the active summary. Product access, patient-level evidence and real scoped terms can resolve this comparison; repeatedly recasting the existing literature cannot.
