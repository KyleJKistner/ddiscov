# Counterparty briefs and quote requests

Prepared 8 November 2026. These briefs are **drafts for review; nothing has been sent or submitted**. They identify questions and proposed structures, with no representation that a counterparty is interested or that ddiscov has a clinical operator. Review the [comparison and source limitations](README.md) and [source records](opportunity-packages.json) before using a brief.

**Deferred:** broad desk screening, an estimated comparison and a small shortlist come first. Retain these as background drafting material for a later inquiry stage; they are not the current work queue.

## Shared commercial scope

A useful response should identify an actual development need, the party authorized to transact, the permitted article/data use, a finite deliverable, who could execute it, the all-in price and the next cash event.

For a commissioned package, request objective acceptance criteria, payment for conforming work even when the scientific result is negative, payment/cancellation timing, capped obligations, treatment of overruns, and the allocation of background rights and new outputs. These are negotiating proposals. Assess counterparty credit and the route from project cash to permitted investor distributions.

For an option/license, request fees, field, territory, exclusivity, duration, termination, patent expenses, conversion economics, sublicensing and diligence obligations. Keep the option cost and later development commitments separate. A buyer or financeable handoff needs its own evidence.

A scope request should include materials, product documentation, operator/institution, data handling, relevant review/oversight, analysis, overhead and all exclusions. Any clinical work needs a qualified sponsor and operator with the actual product, access and applicable approvals established; this document requests no trial execution.

We are evaluating whether a bounded KRT16 Arg127Cys keratoderma evidence or feasibility contribution could address a current need in the SGT-210 program. Your October 2026 materials describe plans for small feasibility studies in additional indications.

The proposed first step would reconcile the exact article and existing evidence, identify a decision-relevant product/clinical gap, and determine whether an appropriately qualified operator could deliver a useful package. We recognize that the published KRT16 article and SGT-210 have distinct product evidence and that the Darier study did not establish active-versus-vehicle differentiation.

Could you indicate:

- Whether KRT16 is a current area of interest or an already-addressed program question.
- What specific output would change a development decision, and whether you would consider commissioning it or offering a defined participation arrangement.
- Which article, product documentation, data and permitted uses could be available.
- Whether a qualified operator/institution is already involved, and the scope needed for an all-in quote.
- What payment, acceptance, cancellation and output-rights structure could apply.

This is a request to establish fit and possible terms, not a request to begin a study.
