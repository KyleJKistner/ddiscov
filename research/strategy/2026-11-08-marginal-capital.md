# Strategy review: marginal capital across drug development

**Decision date:** 8 November 2026, America/Los_Angeles.

**Reviewed baseline:** [9f2ffa4](https://github.com/KyleJKistner/ddiscov/commit/9f2ffa46d87ff39e6d52ddd02f16f30a1ecd23c9), following the agent-context cleanup.

**Scope:** allocation strategy and working guidance, using inherited candidate evidence through Round 26 plus the external policy/method sources checked below.

## Decision

Organize ddiscov around **the financial value retained from the next bounded commitment**, across drug-development stages and transaction structures. Preserve the ability to continue after losses. Prefer stronger evidence and a higher credible chance of economic success when the financial opportunities are otherwise comparable.

The cleanup already established this objective. The remaining change is operational: compare an exact asset/use **together with its attainable economic interest, next deliverable and full exposure**. The same drug may offer several economically distinct commitments. Prices, scope, terms and later funding can reverse their order even when the biological evidence is unchanged.

The [current opportunities](../../decisions/current-shortlist.md) now present an unranked set of proposed packages. The [selection policy](../../decisions/selection-policy.md) supplies the comparison rules, and the [opportunity template](../../decisions/opportunity-template.md) captures a serious proposal. These are strategy changes; the candidate observations, denominators and scientific limitations retain their original evidence dates.

## How the broader approach works

### Start at the economically useful point

A new synthesis can be attractive if an obtainable interest and a finite result create enough incremental value. An existing medication can justify direct indication, formulation or clinical work when its evidence and article fit that route. A sponsor-controlled program can offer a license, field or territory arrangement, option, or bounded paid development contribution. Each structure has its own access, financing and retained-value question.

The following are proposals grounded in repository history, rather than available offers:

| Route | Existing starting point | First economically decisive question |
|---|---|---|
| Existing article and local clinical development | Topical erlotinib/KRT16; APR-246/AEC | What product/evidence contribution is attainable, what interest would ddiscov retain, and what scope reaches a useful clinical or partner decision? |
| Existing medication and new indication | Enasidenib/D2HGA2; levodopa/carbidopa/MCT8 | Who could pay for the incremental contribution, and what rights and remaining work make that route credible? |
| Early article and preclinical development | FD-2/ALS; BLU-3797/XLMTM; exact 8015-P2/MFN2-CMT2A | What exact article and economic interest are obtainable, and does the first tranche change a worthwhile later decision? |
| Formulation/manufacturing or process contribution | AAT/donor-lung preservation; mIGF-Gel/cornea | Can a defined product improvement or dataset earn compensation or retained participation after the remaining validation and adoption costs? |
| Participation in an existing program | GA002; GBT601 + PT2399/NDUFS4 Leigh | What discrete program need could ddiscov address, and at what terms and exposure could it participate? |

Candidate limitations remain in their linked dossiers. This review adds no new efficacy claim, accessible material, offered license or verified cost. A new transaction structure deserves assessment without implying a scientific promotion.

### Value decisions and realizable economics

The financial result depends on what the project can retain after later development, rights payments, royalties, sharing and dilution. Specify a plausible payer, licensee, buyer or downstream funding route and the evidence it would need. An increase in a financing valuation can matter to the value of an interest, while remaining distinct from cash available to the user.

An informative negative result can be valuable when it prevents a larger unproductive commitment. A technically successful result can have little economic value if it changes no decision, leaves no retainable interest or requires financing that cannot be obtained on workable terms. Value the continuation and stopping choices in each outcome, and carry scientific, transaction and financing dependencies through those branches.

Compare a tranche with the best feasible action available without it. For an already-held asset, its existing value belongs in that baseline; the tranche earns credit for the value its new information or deliverable creates or protects. This keeps a cheap study's apparent return tied to its actual incremental contribution.

A ratio of expected value to next-tranche cost is useful for comparison but incomplete. Show absolute net value, cash at risk, time, fixed costs and later commitments as well. Allocate a constrained budget across feasible packages and preserving cash. Record the assumptions that would reverse the choice; current evidence does not support a numerical portfolio ranking.

### Preserve the ability to continue

Treat total deployable capital, cumulative affordable loss, minimum reserve, per-commitment exposure and liquidity horizon as user inputs. Compare a failed project and plausible correlated failures with those limits. Select tranches with genuine stopping points and re-evaluate later expenditure after the result.

This implements the user's concern about personally bearing the sequence of outcomes. A favorable average across hypothetical repeated portfolios is insufficient when one plausible loss path would prevent further participation. The policy therefore uses survivability as a constraint and higher credible success as an additional preference among otherwise comparable financial opportunities. It assigns no invented probability, budget, Kelly fraction or allocation percentage.

## Scope of the resulting decision

The next useful work is to turn promising packages into comparable propositions: a finite deliverable, a realistic economic interest, an all-in scope, a path to payment or financeable handoff, and survivable exposure. The active page supplies a cross-stage starting set and broader sourcing instructions.

No paid development allocation is justified by this strategy revision alone. Current offers, costs, terms, financing and the user's capital limits remain unresolved. The repository records no outreach, experiment, purchase or new financial commitment from this review.
