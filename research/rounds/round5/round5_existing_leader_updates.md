# Round 5: further qualification of exact 8015-P2 and AAT

**Evidence date: 4 November 2026.** These are updates to two existing leaders. They add **zero new candidates** and **zero S-tier promotions**.

## Decision

Retain exact 8015-P2's independent human-function priority and AAT's smaller formulation/activity gate. The new evidence makes the next tests more concrete. It does not establish a new exact-compound human response or a durable ex-vivo-only transplant benefit.

## 1. Exact 8015-P2: a useful human model is not automatically a restoration assay

A newly reviewed independent 2026 JCI Insight study establishes **isogenic MFN2 R94Q/+ hESC-derived motor neurons**. Its cultures have mitochondrial-transport defects, but days 34–36 electrophysiology showed no overt mutant-versus-WT difference. Cells being electrically active is different from having a disease-linked functional deficit. Its patch-clamp total is 33 cells; the drug-transport comparison uses six WT and twelve variant neurons from three differentiations, not multiple human donors. The parental background is MShef11, with exact edited-clone identifiers disclosed. [Primary study](https://insight.jci.org/articles/view/200106)

This matters directly to P2's next proof. A transport-positive response in such a model is interpretable. A claim of restored electrical function is not interpretable until the relevant disease phenotype is present. Failure to improve a function that was normal at baseline would likewise not be evidence against P2.

The same study's ACY-738 intervention provides a useful **transport-positive comparator**, at 100 nM for 24 hours. Its fish swimming benefit should not be labeled delayed adult rescue: treatment began at eight days after fertilization, followed later by oral dosing. These results neither belong to exact P2 nor establish a mature human neuromuscular response. [Primary study](https://insight.jci.org/articles/view/200106)

A separate 2021 primary study supplies a better functional anchor: the patient-derived **MFN2 R94Q line showed increased network burst rate**, and isogenic correction restored the phenotype. The burst figure uses 9–16 wells from two differentiations; this is one MFN2 patient background within a multi-CMT study. Genetic correction before differentiation demonstrates disease attribution, not drug reversal after onset. [Van Lent et al., Brain](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/144/8/2471/6299386)

There is no necessary contradiction between these studies: patch-clamp properties and network bursting test different functions, and the backgrounds/protocols differ. The development implication is to select a stable, genetically attributable deficit and measure its useful consequences. A drug that merely suppresses a hyperactive network should not receive credit for repair if evoked transmission or muscle force also declines.

### A concrete independent allele challenge

CMTA's completed May 2026 Manor project, updated on 24 September, reports patient-derived neuronal models and use of an **R364W rat model**. Its current toolbox documents two CMT2A rat models, commercial/academic testing pathways and peripheral-nerve PK, behavior and electrophysiology capabilities. PsychoGenics also reports grip, gait and nerve-conduction characterization. These sources establish a public operating route beyond the P2 team's T105M models, while not demonstrating a P2 response or secured capacity. [CMTA project](https://cmtausa.org/research-projects/tiny-movers-big-clues-cmt2a/) · [Toolbox](https://cmtausa.org/preclinical-toolbox/) · [Provider characterization](https://psychogenics.com/resources/characterization-of-a-rat-model-of-cmt2a/)

The **USPTO official gazette** confirms grant **US 12,721,327 B2 on 1 September 2026**, assigned to CMTA and the University of Pennsylvania. Its first claim describes R364W/H361Y knock-in rats with progressive peripheral axon/Schwann-cell degeneration. This is a model right, separate from chemical-product patents. An operating licensed-service route is more accurate than a claim of unencumbered public stock. [Official grant entry](https://patentsgazette.uspto.gov/week35/OG/html/1550-1/US12721327-20260901.html)

### A formulation detail worth fixing before replication

The 2026 P2 rat methods describe a 250 mg/mL DMSO stock and a typical 10 mg/mL working solution in 30% hydroxypropyl-beta-cyclodextrin. The stated vehicle is 30% cyclodextrin, without explicit final-DMSO wording. Simple dilution implies **10/250 = 4% DMSO** in that typical active preparation. That is a conditional calculation, not proof that the control omitted DMSO or that every dose cohort used the same recipe. [Rat primary methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13513834/)

Independent work should specify identical final solvent conditions for active P2, less-active P1, parent mixture and vehicle, and verify compound identity/concentration. This is a bounded transfer question; it does not establish that solvent explains the existing animal results.

### A different biological comparator, still incompletely qualified

A 2026 PNAS primary abstract reports **AAV9-MFN2** and an **ER-targeting MFN1 isoform**, with benefit after symptom onset in a transgenic CMT2A model. This makes ER–mitochondria contact restoration a relevant orthogonal comparison. Complete construct, dose and denominator details were not retrieved here; PMC lists release on 16 December 2026. It is retained as comparator context, not counted as an eighth new lead or treated as exact-P2 evidence. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42301793/)

**Next deciding proof:** exact P2 restores stable, genetically attributable human transmission/force or qualified network function at measured free exposure, with healthy-culture controls and a second allele/background challenge. If only transport improves, the functional gate remains unmet. If T105M responds but another allele fails, narrow the indication instead of generalizing across CMT2A.

The initial PMC supplement retrieval returned challenge HTML. The subsequent [publisher download](https://www.mdpi.com/article/10.3390/ijms27167376/s1) succeeded: its 390,445-byte ZIP contains only an axon-size figure and the 87-assay off-target table, not animal-level efficacy data. The [independently checked panel audit](p2-off-target-audit.md) distinguishes MAO-A binding displacement from catalytic inhibition, hERG binding from electrical function, and a cross-assay concentration ratio from a clinical safety margin. No animal-level efficacy recalculation is claimed.

## 2. AAT: define the exposure and keep different routes separate

The existing CSL perfusion patent contains a useful unresolved discrepancy: pig methods describe **3 mg/mL added AAT**, while Example 8 reports **0.5 mg/mL human AAT antigen throughout 12 hours**, measured by ELISA. It also reports group differences in sodium, chloride and late calcium. The inspected text does not explain the nominal-versus-measured difference. [Experimental patent disclosure](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20190059362A1/en)

It would be unjustified to infer a particular loss mechanism, claim 83% adsorption, or assign this recipe to the 2026 human experiment. The product implication is narrower and useful: added mass, antigen recovery and functional NE/PR3 inhibition need separate measurements. A nominal mass concentration cannot serve as the exposure target until analytical recovery, active-protein stability and perfusate mass balance are reconciled. The observed electrolyte differences reinforce the value of a physically matched comparison; they do not prove that electrolytes caused the physiological benefit.

### Independent transplantation evidence supports biology, not the exact warm product

A newly reviewed 2021 independent mouse allograft study used **buffer-exchanged Respreeza/Prolastin**, administered to recipients before and after transplantation. Rejection histology improved at day 3. Approximately 40% of experiments had intraoperative death and were excluded; analyzed groups were ten AAT and five controls. The study is informative biological support, including an attempt to remove preparation-buffer differences, but does not demonstrate exact warm-EVLP equivalence, whole-enrollment survival benefit or durable graft gas transfer. [Primary study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12931-021-01890-x)

A 2025 retrospective SRTR/pharmacy study included **447 AAT-deficient lung recipients**; 109 had pretransplant augmentation and 32 continued afterward. Posttransplant augmentation was associated with improved survival estimates, while age, ischemic time and incomplete pharmacy capture constrain causal interpretation. This is systemic augmentation in deficient recipients, a different question from a finite ex-vivo pulse in injured donor lungs. Its patients cannot be pooled with the eight paired human EVLP donors as replication. [Primary recipient study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11935422/)

The 2026 eight-donor EVLP abstract and likely corresponding 2020 report remain one probable underlying dataset. This pass did not retrieve the full 2026 human formulation or verify the 2018 pig survival-abstract dataset, so it does not fill those gaps with assumptions. The prior round's active/inactive-AAT and protease-knockout evidence remains the better specific causal anchor.

**Next deciding proof:** qualify both NE and PR3 activity in the actual warm circuit, then establish persistent gas-transfer/compliance benefit against physically matched perfusate and a simpler NE-selective comparator during drug-free, recipient-like reperfusion. If ongoing recipient-systemic dosing is necessary, that is a different product/use proposition.
