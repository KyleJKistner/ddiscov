# HHD comparator decision after the Gupta version audit

**Round 13 · 4 November 2026 · existing program R6-NL-01.** This is a literature-based development decision, not an experimental result. The base publication is `f8d99f06f5a42e6cae0a65d183707917c6acb46d`.

## Decision

**Retain ruxolitinib 1.5% cream/HHD at rank 3 and 10 planning points. The Gupta model remains optional mechanistic support; CDN1163 is not qualified here as a complete-barrier or reliable cohesion-rescue positive control.** The final-publication author manuscript now documents an attachment assay and reports weak improvement with CDN1163. The supporting supplement was identified but its panels and legends could not be inspected. Neither an effect size nor an endpoint-specific replicate denominator is filled from that access gap. [F1, S1]

This is a narrower and more useful conclusion than treating all reported cellular rescue as interchangeable. The newly inspected text supports a model capability relevant to cohesion, but does not establish a sufficiently characterized pharmacological rescue benchmark for a negative ruxolitinib result. A failed comparison against an unqualified benchmark would be difficult to interpret. That is our development inference, not a conclusion stated by the authors.

## The decision that could have changed

Round 7 already rejected an assumption that restored spreading meant complete barrier repair. What remained uninspected was whether the final paper added direct functional cohesion evidence strong enough to change the comparator decision. A clear, reproducible rescue of mechanical cohesion would strengthen the case for using CDN1163 as an endpoint-specific reference in an optional model module. It would still not establish ruxolitinib efficacy or make the model a mandatory clinical gate.

The recovered manuscript instead reports baseline fragility and only weak improvement in attachment with CDN1163. This strengthens the reason to separate the endpoints and closes the inference that the paper supplies an established complete-barrier positive control. The magnitude, uncertainty and exact S4 comparisons remain unresolved. Failure to retrieve S4 is an access limit, not evidence that the figure contains no improvement. [F1: Cell Attachment Assay; Results on junctional phenotypes; Discussion; S1]

## Endpoint-specific interpretation

| Evidence or reference | Supported role at this evidence cut | Limit on a development decision |
|---|---|---|
| Untreated ATP2C1 mutant cultures in the added attachment assay | Main-text report of greater susceptibility to detachment; candidate model phenotype | A baseline deficit alone does not establish that a drug can repair it, or provide a quantitative assay acceptance limit |
| CDN1163 | Published cellular modifiability reference with endpoint-dependent effects; only weak attachment improvement reported | No qualified complete-barrier control; no reconstructed S4 effect size, significance, responder fraction or replicate count |
| hSPCA1 re-expression | Causal rescue reference for selected reported cellular phenotypes | Expression precedes harvesting and replating; it is not evidence of delayed repair after established barrier failure |
| Bisphenol perturbation | Disease-phenotype comparison reported in the paper | A worsening perturbation is not a recovery control and does not validate pharmacological reversibility |
| Second heterozygous clone | Main-text support for baseline expression/spreading phenotype in another clone | No claim that every drug-rescue endpoint was repeated in that clone; no independent patient background added |
| Ruxolitinib clinical proof | Existing controlled patient-benefit development priority | No ruxolitinib efficacy or safety result is supplied by this model paper |

The main-text attachment method uses an established monolayer and places treatment before the detachment challenge. That timing is materially different from demonstrating repair after induced tissue failure. This observation does not negate an effect on pre-existing mutant-cell defects; it limits the repair claim. No procedure is proposed or performed. [F1: Cell Attachment Assay and DNA transfection]

## What changes in the register

The current model entry now distinguishes a reported mechanical-cohesion phenotype from quantitative validation of cohesion rescue. The reported parent designation is N/TERT-2G, while the paper's N/TERT/Ker-CT terminology and actual stock authentication remain separate issues. The JAK inhibitor used during clone generation is recorded as historical model-construction context, with continuing endpoint exposure unknown. These two details were already present in the preprint and are extraction corrections, not new final-publication biology. [F1; P1]

Replication is recorded by unit: cultures from clonal lines, cells counted in image panels, and a second heterozygous clone supporting specified baseline phenotypes. The revised final statistics description is not evidence that a new patient cohort or an independent laboratory replicated the results. The companion audit also retains inconsistent insulin-receptor and Figure 7 genotype wording rather than silently resolving it. [F1; P1]

The clinical deciding proof, exact cream, useful pain/erosion/rescue/burden outcomes, exposure requirements and all-randomized/maintenance denominators remain unchanged. An immune-free model cannot rule out clinical benefit mediated through inflammation. No new mandatory assay, clinical protocol, numerical go/no-go threshold or material acquisition is authorized by this decision. [B1]

## Bounded completion and reopening conditions

The final-versus-preprint main-text comparison and comparator decision are complete. **Direct S1/S4 inspection and quantitative cohesion-rescue qualification are not complete.** Close the present retrieval pass. Reopen the specific evidence field only when the actual supplement or a corrected primary version becomes readable, or when a new primary study reports interpretable endpoint-specific rescue. A new generic abstract or another repetition of the same main-text statement is insufficient.

A useful future evidence package would identify the comparison groups, experimental units, clone/background coverage, endpoint magnitude and uncertainty, and the treatment-versus-injury timing. These are criteria for reading evidence, not an experimental work order. No authors were contacted and no underlying data were requested.

The BLU claim-mapping, zinc and SLC6A6 retrieval lanes remain closed. No new exact-use screen, molecule, treated-person record or S-tier promotion is counted. The 100-point queue remains unchanged.
