## Why this question was selected

The prior sprint closed BLU's one-family claim-mapping pass and retained a specific Gupta final-publication access lead. Zinc and SLC6A6 searches were already closed. Round 12's other sampled leading-program questions remained limited by inaccessible primary outcomes; those searches were not repeated here.

The HHD lead became actionable because the final manuscript added an attachment assay absent from the reviewed preprint. Strong endpoint-specific pharmacological rescue could have changed the optional comparator set. The opportunity was therefore a named development decision with accessible primary text, rather than another generic model search. Selection weighs decision importance against the chance that available evidence can resolve it; it is not a claim to have freshly re-ranked every unresolved portfolio question.

The bounded triage used two focused queries and three primary opens before selecting this lane. The main-text comparison and a finite attempt to read the author-linked supplement followed. The two versions are one project, and no inaccessible result is counted as verified.

## Development consequence

The model entry is more precise: it includes an author-reported functional cohesion deficit and a limited attachment response, rather than only spreading. That does not turn CDN1163 into a reliable rescue benchmark for rejecting a candidate. A baseline deficit, some modifiability and a qualified endpoint-specific positive control are different evidentiary steps.

No new ruxolitinib clinical efficacy or safety evidence is supplied. The exact article, clinical outcomes, exposure conditions, active-care comparison and all-randomized/maintenance denominators remain as previously specified. A negative immune-free model result cannot exclude an anti-inflammatory clinical effect. This sprint does not authorize execution of a model or clinical study.
