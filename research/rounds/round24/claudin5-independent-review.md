# Independent claim audit: CLDN5 sealing concept for diabetic macular edema

**Review date:** 8 November 2026. **Scope:** public literature and bounded public patent review of the proposed biparatopic CLDN5 sealing binder. This is a scientific and investment assessment, not an operational design, experimental plan, patentability opinion, or freedom-to-operate opinion. Source IDs T01–T05 are independent of the parent report’s C-series references.

## Assessment

**Retain this as an early discovery concept; the present evidence does not support promotion to an identified, validated asset.** The attachment contains a potentially useful direction—restoring a disease-associated endothelial barrier defect—but combines that direction with novelty, geometric, and translational claims that exceed the available evidence.

The strongest correction is factual: **the proposition that every engineered claudin binder opens a barrier is false.** Takigawa and colleagues reported an antibody directed to the extracellular region of CLDN2 that increased epithelial barrier integrity in a human intestinal model and attenuated TNF-associated loss of integrity. This is a direct binder-mediated tightening counterexample, published in 2017 [T01]. It weakens a family-wide novelty claim. It does not demonstrate that an antibody can stabilize barrier-forming CLDN5 through the proposed trans interaction, nor that the approach will work in the retina.

The narrower question—whether an adequately characterized, extracellular CLDN5 binder can produce clinically useful, durable sealing in DME—remains unresolved in this bounded review. No exact asset with that demonstrated mechanism was identified here. That statement must not be converted into a claim that no such asset, publication, or patent exists.

## What the CLDN2 counterexample does and does not establish

The primary abstract of **Creation of a Claudin-2 Binder and Its Tight Junction–Modulating Activity in a Human Intestinal Model** explicitly states that treatment of epithelial monolayers with the antibody increased barrier integrity [T01]. It also reports attenuation of TNF-associated barrier loss and an additive effect with an anti-TNF antibody. This is sufficient to refute a universal claim about all claudin-directed binders.

The important biological distinction is that CLDN2 is a pore-forming claudin. Reducing permeability through a pore-forming protein is not equivalent to stabilizing the intercellular assembly of barrier-forming CLDN5. The paper therefore provides **proof that binder-mediated tightening is possible within the family**, not proof of the attachment’s specific CLDN5 mechanism. The observed model was intestinal epithelium; neither retinal efficacy nor clinical efficacy follows. No in vivo colitis efficacy claim is made in this audit.

**Access limitation:** the primary abstract was available through indexed primary-source results. Full text was not verified; the parent independently encountered a full-text access restriction. Quantitative effect sizes, assay details, complete specificity data, and supplementary material were not audited and should not be inferred.

## Mechanistic and translational interpretation

The attachment’s cis/trans account should be presented as a hypothesis with several unproven premises. A binder must encounter the relevant extracellular surface in the native junctional state; its occupancy must favor a useful functional state; and that state must improve the disease-relevant barrier without creating a clinically important unwanted effect. These premises are not resolved merely by identifying a soluble or isolated structural binding pose. These are mechanistic inference limits; they are not direct evidence against the concept.

The same distinction applies to electrical resistance and tracer permeability. These can be informative barrier measurements, but their improvement does not by itself demonstrate less retinal fluid, improved vision, adequate duration of effect, or an advantage over contemporary DME therapy. Likewise, preservation of one physiological process in the CLDN1 EAE model is limited reassurance within that model, not a general demonstration that increasing barrier tightness preserves every required physiological function [T03].

The proposed falsification logic should therefore be rewritten at the level of the claim actually being tested. Opposite phenotypes for differently configured binders would strengthen evidence that molecular configuration affects barrier behavior. They would not uniquely prove trans crosslinking. Unfavorable results would reduce confidence in the tested implementation and its mechanistic prediction, rather than eliminate the entire target class. These are analytical conclusions, not reported experimental observations about this proposed asset.

## Bounded patent assessment

The public record includes **Claudin 5 antibody, and medicine containing said antibody**, published as US20200223915A1 and granted as US10882906B2, with the associated WO2018105560A1 family [T05]. The record lists a 7 December 2016 priority date. Its description concerns extracellular CLDN5 antibodies and applications including barrier control and facilitating passage across the blood–brain barrier.

This is relevant prior art because extracellular CLDN5 recognition and therapeutic barrier modulation are already populated areas. It prevents treating “an antibody binds extracellular CLDN5 to alter barrier function” as an unqualified new platform claim. The family’s opening and delivery emphasis is not evidence that the exact proposed biparatopic sealing mechanism has been disclosed or claimed. Conversely, the absence of an obvious sealing example in a bounded search is not evidence of freedom to operate.

**Access and scope:** public Google Patents metadata and description were reviewed. This was not a comprehensive search, a jurisdiction-specific claim analysis, a prosecution-history review, or an independent check of legal status. Public database labels such as active or granted should not substitute for official status review. No conclusion is offered about whether a particular future asset would infringe or be patentable. The parent separately reviewed US11078271B2; this audit does not represent that separate review as its own independent claim analysis.

## Implication for the candidate queue

The current evidence supports recording a differentiated **scientific hypothesis**, with explicit unresolved asset and mechanism questions. It does not support treating the attachment as an investment-ready molecule or as demonstrated first-in-class binder-mediated sealing.

The next decision should depend on whether existing evidence can establish an identified asset, direct CLDN5 functional stabilization in a relevant biological context, credible retinal access and preservation of required physiology, and a clinical benefit that remains valuable against the updated DME comparator landscape. Those are evidence requirements, not instructions to construct or test binders. The parent’s current clinical and economic assessment should determine the comparator standard; this independent review does not duplicate that pipeline work.
