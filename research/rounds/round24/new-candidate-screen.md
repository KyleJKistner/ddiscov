## R24-N01 — SGPL1 R222Q / pyridoxine / SPLIS

### Evidence and what it changes

The September 2026 JCI In-Press Preview reports neurological improvement and biochemical changes after pyridoxine in an individual with R222Q-variant SPLIS, accompanied by effects in patient-derived fibroblasts and recombinant enzyme. The paper also studies an R222Q mouse model. This supplies a more concrete translational hypothesis than a target-only association: the intervention is an existing substance, the variant is named, and an individual human observation is linked to biochemical evidence. [N01]

The human observation remains uncontrolled. The earlier preprint and the JCI publication are one study lineage, and should not be counted as independent replication. The current manuscript describes a second R222Q patient relative to earlier work. The older report’s full case inventory is not a treated or responding denominator, and no pooled response rate is calculated here. Neither this earlier-case context nor repeated publication provides a controlled efficacy estimate. [N01, N02, N07]

The mouse result needs a narrow reading. Mutant mice on pyridoxine-enriched chow lacked obvious phenotypes, whereas reduced pyridoxine availability produced enzyme inactivation, sphingolipid accumulation, and renal pathology. This supports cofactor sensitivity of this particular variant. It does **not** establish reversal of established human renal disease, prove general efficacy across SGPL1 genotypes, or quantify neurological benefit in patients. Diet-conditioned prevention can make a biologically real effect look more readily translatable than it is. [N01]

### Fit, capture, and next marginal dollar

This belongs beside the portfolio's genotype-specific rescue and repurposing ideas, rather than among platform delivery programs. The strongest initial desk question is whether the genotype restriction produces a sufficiently coherent clinical population and evidence path to support a differentiated asset. Pyridoxine itself is generic; a plausible biological effect does not establish defensible value capture. No exclusive asset, available license, clinical-development package, freedom-to-operate position, or commercial terms were established in this screen.

A bounded review of existing clinical observations, mutation-specific response, natural history, and public patent/ownership records could alter the investment decision without a development commitment. The time to such a **desk conclusion** is short; the time and capital to a credible prospective clinical milestone remain unestimated. Do not substitute the low acquisition cost of a substance for the cost of producing decision-grade evidence.

**Advance condition:** a reproducible genotype-defined clinical signal and a concrete, financeable capture route. **Stop or remain on watch:** responses are confined to cofactor deprivation, the human signal is not reproducible, the relevant population is too small or heterogeneous for an achievable milestone, or no capture route survives scrutiny. These are diligence conditions, not observed negative findings.

## R24-N02 — ATP1A3 prime editing / AHC

### Evidence and what it changes

The 2025 Cell study, *In vivo prime editing rescues alternating hemiplegia of childhood in mice*, reports AAV-mediated prime editing of the D801N and E815K disease variants in two mouse models, with molecular correction and phenotypic benefit. It is a specific therapeutic approach for two variants, not a general claim that ATP1A3 disorders are solved by editing. [N03, N04]

The primary timing limitation is decisive: **both mouse models received treatment at birth**. Later improvement in adult animals therefore follows neonatal intervention. It does not demonstrate that initiating treatment in older, already symptomatic patients reverses established disease. Developmental timing, distribution across relevant CNS cells, and differences between the modeled disease and heterogeneous human AHC all remain important uncertainties. The PubMed record and indexed PMC manuscript excerpts describe the same Cell paper and are one evidence family. Five variants corrected in cells are not five variants with demonstrated in-vivo efficacy. [N03, N04]

### Fit, capture, and next marginal dollar

This fits the genetic-neurology watchlist but does not displace existing candidates whose near-term evidence or access questions may be cheaper to resolve. A molecularly specific approach could support proprietary capture, but prime-editing platform rights, disease-specific claims, delivery rights, and ownership of the actual development package may be separate. The publication does not establish that any integrated asset is obtainable by this project.

A desk review can clarify which variants are addressable, whether a clinically realistic age-of-treatment proposition exists, and whether a single counterparty controls a licensable package. A clinical decision-changing milestone would require a substantial translational and manufacturing program; this screen provides no credible budget or calendar for it. No human efficacy or trial-ready package was verified.

**Advance condition:** an attainable rights package plus credible evidence that a feasible clinical treatment window and delivery strategy can produce a meaningful patient outcome. **Stop or remain on watch:** benefit depends on an impractical neonatal window, the necessary rights cannot be assembled on viable terms, or financing to the next decisive milestone is disproportionate to the obtainable value. These are prospective disconfirmers, not claims that the study has failed.

## R24-N03 — VS-X4 / STING inhibition / NGLY1 deficiency

### Evidence and what it changes

The 2025 JEM paper, *The STING pathway drives noninflammatory neurodegeneration in NGLY1 deficiency*, contains both genetic and pharmacological evidence. An inducible postnatal Ngly1 knockout model develops progressive neurological disease and Purkinje-cell loss. Genetic Sting1 deletion improves several disease measures and extends lifespan. Separately, the named orally active antagonist **VS-X4** mitigates neurological disease and preserves Purkinje cells. This is an exact pharmacological lead, rather than only a proposal to inhibit a pathway. [N05, N06]

The evidence boundaries matter. **Lifespan extension in the NGLY1 model belongs to genetic Sting1 deletion.** Pharmacological survival results elsewhere in the paper concern a different, Trex1-associated model; they must not be attributed to the NGLY1 drug arm. VS-X4 treatment began after weaning, before the later progressive phenotype. Delayed disease, improved composite neurological scores, and cell preservation support attenuation/prevention in that setting; they do not establish reversal of established neuronal loss. [N05, N06]

The inducible model also differs from congenital NGLY1 deficiency: it lacks the inflammatory phenotype seen in other loss-of-function models, and timing of gene loss is a possible explanation. NGLY1 loss affects more than STING signaling. Thus, a coherent pathway result does not establish that STING inhibition corrects the full multisystem human disorder. [N05]

### Fit, capture, and next marginal dollar

This is the most concrete small-molecule translational lead among the two purely preclinical additions. Oral exposure in the paper is helpful to the hypothesis but does not establish suitable human CNS exposure, long-term tolerability, a clinical formulation, or trial readiness. The research compound is named; this review does not establish qualified physical material or an integrated development package. Ownership, patents, obtainable rights, and freedom to operate also remain unresolved. The paper discloses Grace Science relationships and names US20240218396A1. The parent inspected that publication: it concerns NGLY1 nucleic-acid/AAV technology and lists Grace Science LLC, which does not establish ownership of VS-X4. An A1 publication is not treated here as proof of an issued grant. [N05, N08] No outreach or access negotiation was performed.

The first useful desk question is whether VS-X4 has a defined, obtainable asset owner and whether later public work closes the model and clinical-readiness gaps. A public evidence-and-rights answer may be inexpensive; a human disease-modification milestone remains unbudgeted and undated. No relative ranking should conceal those unknowns.

**Advance condition:** an identifiable licensable compound package and evidence supporting relevant human exposure and a plausible disease-modifying clinical proposition. **Stop or remain on watch:** the effect depends on the inducible model or preventive timing, STING blockade misses important disease mechanisms, tolerability or exposure defeats chronic treatment, or rights cannot be obtained on viable terms. No paid-development promotion is justified by the present record.
