# Barrier, ocular, airway and local-repair portfolio reset

> **Historical review snapshot.** Later findings and rankings are reconciled in the [current shortlist](../../../decisions/current-shortlist.md) and [round 4 review](../round4/README.md). Statements about activity or access describe this review period.

**Date:** 4 November 2026. **Snapshot:** `KyleJKistner/ddiscov`, commit `c241dc4c6cfa7cd5312a7237dda61a4d2af50c97`.

## Decision

**The best bounded proof in this domain is a retained, defined corneal-repair peptide, mIGF-Gel, tested against topical insulin and a clinical reference under realistic washout.** It is already in the repository, but the question should change from whether its owners might engage to whether its claimed retention advantage produces better durable epithelial repair than inexpensive obtainable treatment. That is a concrete development question that can be assessed publicly and then experimentally. It earns an **A-level proof shortlist**, not an S label today.

The next two useful independent lanes are **PslG-mediated, phenotype-selected antibiotic potentiation** and **direct desmosomal adhesion rescue delivered locally**. Both require a tightly bounded active-comparator experiment. **BPG10 physical cartilage replacement** is a fourth, mechanically interesting reserve. **SSP2518 for GUCY2C gain-of-function diarrhea** and **DAPT-derived MVID rescue** retain unusually relevant patient-model evidence, but their chemistry/exposure liabilities keep them behind those near-term proofs.

I would not keep searching within this lane merely until an S label can be awarded. The blood/ex vivo team's paired-human-lung AAT opportunity presently has a stronger direct human physiological foundation. mIGF should compete with that opportunity, BLU-3797 and CPTX on the merits; it should not inherit priority from being this agent's favorite.

No outreach was undertaken, and no research conclusion below depends on a reply from an owner. Publicly disclosed synthesis and product comparators are enough to define the research task. Patent scope, freedom to operate, territorial rights and willingness to license remain separate commercial questions; a public patent is neither permission nor evidence that a molecule cannot be studied or developed through a suitable route.

## The process problem exposed by this pass

### 1. Stop treating availability as one binary field

Four questions have been collapsed: whether the exact entity is publicly defined, whether a research quantity can be made or purchased, whether the correct biological model is accessible, and whether an eventual company can obtain commercially useful rights. A short disclosed peptide can satisfy the first two without an owner conversation. A catalog protein can satisfy the first two while lacking the biological equivalence needed for the proposed study. Neither observation alone settles commercial development.

The shortlist should rank the **value and tractability of the next proof**. The commercial file should then state the actual patent family, applicant, status source and unresolved jurisdictional scope. “Ask owner” should be reserved for information that cannot be resolved publicly, and should not replace a scientific decision.

### 2. Search for the comparator that could destroy the thesis

An intervention that beats PBS can still add little value. For mIGF, free IGF1 is not enough: topical insulin has an increasingly substantial clinical literature. For PslG, less crystal-violet biomass is not enough: dispersed viable bacteria can worsen infection. For BPG10, a higher local AFM modulus is not enough: whole-cartilage fluid support and friction can remain impaired. For desmosomal rescue, pretreatment of injected healthy skin is not enough: an established lesion and topical exposure are the intended product setting.

This reverses the incentive to add an anchoring domain or a second component merely because the component appears interesting. A combination belongs only after a factorial comparison shows a useful incremental function at a feasible exposure.

### 3. Current negative studies must update the whole family

On **30 October 2026**, Aer Therapeutics reported that fexlamose/MUC031/AER-01 failed its randomized 119-person COPD Phase 2a. There was no improvement in the reported spirometric outcomes or overall mucus-plug segment score; respiratory adverse events and discontinuations were more frequent on active treatment. This is a sponsor top-line report, not yet a peer-reviewed full dataset. It materially lowers confidence that strong ex vivo mucolysis alone predicts a fundable inhaled COPD product. It does not prove all mucus-clearing mechanisms are ineffective. [S1]

The pulmonary microlithiasis example is another instructive correction: the cited EDTA lavage stone-removal experiment was performed in **euthanized mice**. Chemical removal of microliths therefore cannot be described as safe restoration of living lung function. This is a biological-model problem, not an availability problem. [S2]

### 4. Preserve useful uncertainty without forcing a favorable grade

Use “proof shortlist,” “chemistry required,” “comparator only,” “current failure,” and “screen hold” as operational dispositions. A proof shortlist can contain a commercially useful idea with unresolved questions. An S label should mean that the current evidence and bounded proof jointly justify exceptional priority; it should not mean that licensing, efficacy or reimbursement are guaranteed.

## Deep comparison 1 — mIGF-Gel for persistent corneal epithelial defects

### What is actually supported

The 2026 study identifies a short, biotin-capped peptide containing **D-phenylalanine followed by YIGSSSR**. At 0.4 wt% it forms a transparent self-assembling gel. The paper reports epithelial repair in rats, including an acute debridement experiment with n=5: 48-hour closure was 94.10% with mIGF-Gel, 86.08% with IGF1 and 76.88% with PBS. A dexamethasone-suppressed delayed-healing model compared once-daily gel with three-times-daily IGF1. The paper also reports receptor-signaling activity, retention and repair-quality measurements. This establishes a formulation/repair hypothesis; it does not establish efficacy in human neurotrophic keratopathy. [S3]

**Important limits:** the acute model heals substantially without treatment; steroid-suppressed healing is not equivalent to chronic denervation, persistent infection, limbal stem-cell deficiency or severe ocular graft-versus-host disease. The accessible full-text excerpts confirmed acute-model n and exact chemistry, but this pass did not independently extract every delayed-model dose, animal denominator or blinding detail from the supplement. Those are explicit items for the independent challenge, not assumed favorable facts.

### Exact material and public commercial mapping

There is no need to wait for a proprietary code name to be explained: the peptide sequence, D stereochemistry, assembly behavior and analytical controls are public. The material should be independently synthesized and verified against the disclosed chemical identity. A vendor's generic “IGF peptide” would not be equivalent.

Nankai University is the listed applicant/assignee for **CN121949581A**, published 1 May 2026, and **CN113321706B**, granted 7 January 2022. The earlier disclosure already includes closely related self-assembling sequences. These records make the ownership/claim issue concrete. Google Patents' status labels are not a legal opinion, and no global freedom-to-operate conclusion is drawn here. The original sequence cannot simply be represented as a newly invented asset. [S4, S5]

### Why insulin is the decisive comparator

A recent multicenter retrospective series included 57 eyes and reported higher treatment success in neurotrophic keratopathy than in dry eye or ocular GVHD; 20/28 persistent epithelial defects of varied etiologies closed. This is uncontrolled evidence, but it makes an inexpensive active comparator unavoidable. Separately, a randomized observer-blinded insulin-versus-autologous-serum trial is listed as ongoing with 234 planned participants. Public insulin formulation stability studies also show that vehicle choice materially changes stability. [S6–S8]

Thus the proposed product is **a reproducible, less-frequent-dose epithelial repair formulation with durable functional benefit**, not simply “an IGF-mimetic gel.” A strong outcome could be clinically useful even if closure is not dramatically faster: equivalent repair with substantially less dosing burden, reliable shelf stability and no optical penalty may be valuable. That value remains a hypothesis to test.

### Bounded proof that deserves consideration

First establish two independent peptide lots that match identity, assembly, transparency, degradation behavior and sterility-compatible processing. Then use a blinded, paired-donor corneal epithelial repair comparison under repeated tear-like washout. The model should reproduce a persistent repair limitation, not simply a scratch in continuously supplemented cell culture. Pair closure with barrier integrity, epithelial adhesion, optical clarity and cell survival. Where innervation cannot be represented ex vivo, use a subsequent defined denervation model rather than claiming nerve recovery from epithelial markers.

The essential arms are the exact mIGF material, an appropriate physical gel control, topical insulin in a validated vehicle, free IGF1, and a clinical reference where the model can faithfully measure its function. A scrambled peptide that fails to form the same material is an imperfect physical-control arm; it cannot isolate signaling from retention. Use dose/exposure and dosing-frequency comparisons that distinguish superiority from simply supplying more active material.

**Advance criterion, proposed rather than literature-derived:** durable superiority to the insulin reference on closure/barrier restoration after washout, or reproducible noninferior repair with a compelling reduction in dosing and acceptable optical performance. Merely beating PBS, outperforming once-daily free IGF1, or increasing a receptor-phosphorylation marker would not pass. Stop if optical haze, epithelial fragility or inconsistent assembly consumes the supposed practical advantage.

**Funding interpretation:** this is a credible bounded proof package. It is not yet a uniquely dominant venture asset. Human-active comparator failure is a cheap and informative kill; success could support a defined product specification and a focused commercial claim discussion using already identified patent families. No email is needed to decide which result would be valuable.

## Deep comparison 2 — PslG plus an antibiotic, selected by matrix phenotype

### Evidence that supports a new lead

This is genuinely new to the reviewed inventory. A 2026 study tested 14 early CF clinical Pseudomonas isolates. Nine were disrupted by PslGh and three by PelAh. PslGh improved antibiotic-associated biofilm effects in 24/42 strain–drug pairs, versus 5/42 for PelAh; the largest reported adjunctive effect was with tobramycin. Matched enzyme/substrate phenotype mattered, and combining the enzymes did not automatically improve the result. PslGh remained more stable than PelAh in sputum-containing conditions. Most screening readouts were biomass; two representative strains received deeper live-cell/confocal assessment. [S9]

The entity is sufficiently defined to reproduce: the soluble **PslG 31–442** region is publicly characterized, and a 2020 paper describes protease-resistance variants. Any comparison must name the chosen sequence and tag, and must not substitute a variant's trypsin resistance for demonstrated stability against the protease mixture in a patient's sputum. [S10, S11]

### Why this did not earn S

The earlier pulmonary mouse work coadministered enzymes with infection, so it was not treatment of long-established chronic infection. Enzyme combinations alone did not reduce lung bacterial burden; PslGh–PelAh was associated with more bacteremia. A ciprofloxacin combination improved bacterial burden, whereas other tested enzyme/antibiotic combinations did not. This makes “dissolves biofilm” an inadequate benefit claim and shows why the antibiotic must be integral to the proof. [S12]

The 2026 clinical-isolate data are useful but modest; they do not establish sterilization, improved mucociliary transport or better lung function. The older PslG/polysaccharide patent literature and SickKids' public technology page identify multiple relevant families, including US2017/0216410 and US20210138044. A WIPO publication labeled ceased does not establish that every national claim has expired. [S13–S15]

### Better development thesis and decisive test

Start with **matrix-selected Pseudomonas infection with a locally delivered antibiotic**, not a universal inhaled “biofilm dissolver.” A topical established-wound model may permit a cleaner early product test than chronic CF airway delivery; indication selection should follow comparative tissue results, not convenience alone.

In chronic clinical isolates, quantify viable bacteria in both residual biofilm and dispersed effluent after treatment. Compare antibiotic alone, PslG alone, combination, and catalytically inactive protein. Include physiologic fluid/protease exposure and a tissue viability/barrier readout. The useful result is greater bacterial clearance at the same antibiotic exposure without increasing surviving dissemination. A Psl-responsive subgroup can be a valid product population if its assay is practical and the effect is large enough.

A prespecified meaningful reduction in viable burden, maintained after washout and replicated across independent isolates, is preferable to optimizing a statistically significant small biomass change. If the combination merely disperses viable bacteria, stop. If the phenotype selection predicts substantial added killing, continue to a post-establishment tissue model and repeat-dose local safety. This is **A/B-level exploratory proof**, not the leading portfolio asset today.

## Deep comparison 3 — Local desmosomal adhesion rescue

The repository contains two different opportunities: a desmoglein-binding tandem peptide and apremilast-mediated stabilization of adhesion. They should not be merged into a single “desmosome repair” efficacy claim.

The apremilast primary study used healthy human skin injected with **100 µM apremilast before pathogenic IgG**, alongside cultured cells and mouse experiments. It supports direct adhesion biology but does not establish that an oral or topical clinical formulation reaches that concentration at the relevant site after established injury. A subsequent mechanistic study identifies Epac1 dependence. Clinical reports now include both apparent responses and a three-patient nonresponse series; these are uncontrolled observations. [S16–S18]

The tandem peptide is a distinct, disclosed approach to stabilizing desmoglein interaction. The original study included topical activity in mice. It remains attractive for an exact-material comparison, but it should not inherit the apremilast human-skin evidence. [S19]

### Materially improved lead: local PDE4 product versus exact peptide

An approved topical PDE4 formulation supplies a concrete exposure/delivery comparator. **Topical roflumilast for a defined adhesion-defect indication** is a new or materially improved hypothesis relative to simply administering apremilast. It is not yet demonstrated in autoimmune pemphigus by the retrieved primary tissue study. A public 2025 patent application specifically includes topical roflumilast treatment of pemphigus and related cohesion disorders, so this is not an unclaimed empty field. [S20]

The bounded proof should use human skin or oral mucosal epithelium after pathogenic-IgG injury has begun. Match the intended indication: mucosal-predominant PV, cutaneous PV, Hailey–Hailey and Darier disease have different biology. Measure mechanical cohesion and barrier recovery, map actual tissue exposure, and include a relevant standard therapy. The peptide, topical PDE4 comparator and vehicle should be distinguished from preventive pretreatment arms.

**Advance:** clinically deliverable local exposure improves post-injury cohesion without impairing epithelial repair, preferably with a meaningful advantage over the active topical comparator. **Stop:** benefit requires injected supraphysiologic exposure or disappears once the lesion is established. This is a reasonably inexpensive decisive proof, but current evidence does not justify calling it S-tier.

## Deep comparison 4 — BPG10 and cartilage mechanics

BPG10 is a disclosed aggrecan mimic using a small polyacrylic-acid core with chondroitin-sulfate side chains. The human study used four normal and four OA donors and found localization and increased pericellular/territorial micromechanics. The synthesis uses publicly named commercial inputs. Earlier animal work cited in the paper supports matrix infiltration and retention, but the human experiment does not show restoration of whole-joint performance. [S21]

This is valuable because its mechanism is physical, the human tissue has established disease and the material is reproducible without discovery of a new molecular target. The limitation is equally concrete: increasing local modulus around a chondrocyte may be useful, neutral or even harmful under real load; it is not itself regeneration. A living-tissue mechanics experiment can answer more than another descriptive localization study.

The next comparison should measure bulk compressive recovery, fluid load support, friction, cell survival and catabolic response after repeated loading and washout in paired early-OA cartilage. Include vehicle, equivalent free chondroitin, a suitable HA reference and a well-defined lubricin comparator. **BPG10 plus lubricin** is a new combination hypothesis only if BPG10 repairs a bulk mechanical deficit and lubricin independently repairs a friction deficit. The factorial experiment must show that the combination adds value; do not invent a dual product before that.

Current disposition is **B-level reserve**, with a high-information physical experiment. This may be an attractive small translational project, but it does not outrank direct paired-human-organ physiological rescue merely because OA has a large market.

## Patient-organoid opportunities retained, with explicit liabilities

**MVID / DAPT.** The JCI study derived enteroids from two patients and demonstrated defective sodium/proton exchange, preserved chloride secretion and rescue of epithelial differentiation and exchange by DAPT. Crofelemer also reduced secretory responses. This is unusually relevant patient-model physiology. Yet gamma-secretase inhibition changes cell lineages and other pathways, making chronic use in infants a qualitatively different task from adding DAPT to differentiation medium. [S22] The useful next product question is a local, exposure-controlled rescue that restores absorption without creating a worse lineage or barrier defect. Repeating the original enteroid result alone does not solve that problem.

**GUCY2C / SSP2518.** Three distinct patient organoid lines showed reduced elevated cGMP and chloride secretion. The article explicitly mentions a potential cardiac-rhythm liability; the reference was oral communication, so the quantitative safety margin is not public. Independent receptor work shows that mouse versus human GC-C pharmacology differs, and the inhibitor's effect maps to the human intracellular cyclase domain. [S23, S24] A gut-restricted product therefore still needs cell entry and a systemic exposure margin; simply making it completely membrane-impermeant could destroy the intended action. A paired transport/cardiac-exposure proof is a better next step than either rejecting the lead for lack of a license or promoting current SSP2518 as a ready pediatric drug.

**Divertin.** This has strong mechanism and human-biopsy relevance, but the original investigators and subsequent SBIR record explicitly distinguish it as a tool from a lead requiring improved chemistry. Public evidence already settles that distinction. [S25, S26] It belongs in a medicinal-chemistry lane, not a shortlist of nearly developable existing drugs.

## Current-source checks that matter for ongoing prioritization

* **Netherton is not an empty field.** Azitra's filing describes ATR12-351, an engineered S. epidermidis delivering LEKTI D6; BioCryst describes BCX17725, a KLK5 inhibitor in clinical development. These are comparator/program facts, not reasons to assume no other formulation can be developed. [S35, S36]
* **Natural nuclease variants face a clinical comparator.** Protalix previously tested actin-resistant alidornase in a small, open-label switchover CF study. Its public release cannot establish randomized superiority to dornase. A retrieved 2026 review confusingly calls PRX110 an AAT product in one passage; do not repeat that identity error. Use primary issuer/trial records. [S37]
* **A clear chemical identity is not a ready medicine.** The same distinction applies to SSP2518, Divertin, SFTI peptides, PslG and mIGF. Their next experiments should resolve one commercially decisive scientific uncertainty, not produce another positive marker figure.
* **Generic-drug clinical experience changes the expected value of a prototype.** Insulin is an essential mIGF benchmark; apremilast's mixed clinical experience limits an easy desmosomal-rescue narrative; a negative losartan series prevents upgrading a convenient repurposing lead from case reports alone.

## Recommended allocation of the next research round

1. **Independent challenge mIGF against the strongest portfolio competitors.** Extract delayed-model timing, denominator, blinding, exact formulations and all comparator exposures from the paper/supplement. Determine whether the apparent dosing advantage survives equal delivered bioactivity and an insulin reference. Inspect the old/new Nankai claims together.
2. **Retain one genuinely new lane: PslG phenotype-selected combination.** Its decisive outcome is viable burden and dissemination after established infection. Stop if the apparent benefit is primarily biomass staining.
3. **Retain one low-cost barrier lane: post-injury local desmosomal rescue.** First verify that a topical product can attain the exposure and restore mechanical cohesion. Keep the peptide and PDE4 route separate.
4. **Hold BPG10 for a human mechanics comparison.** Do not add a delivery domain or lubricin until the separate function is demonstrated.
5. **Use the patient-organoid leads as a chemistry pipeline.** SSP2518, DAPT and Divertin are worth knowing about, but none should be called a near-ready product merely because the target mechanism is compelling.

The outcome of this pass is therefore a concrete proof shortlist and several falsifiable new leads, accompanied by two material downgrades. It is not an invitation to contact owners, nor a claim that this domain must contain the final winner.
