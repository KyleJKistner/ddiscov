# Independent challenge: BLU3797 for MTM1-related myotubular myopathy

> **Historical review snapshot.** Later findings and rankings are reconciled in the [current shortlist](../../../decisions/current-shortlist.md) and [round 4 review](../round4/README.md). Statements about activity or access describe this review period.

**Evidence cut: 4 November 2026. Reviewer: blood/ex vivo workstream, independently challenging the neuro/metabolic recommendation.** No outreach, purchases, or repository mutations were performed. This memo concerns a research funding decision, not treatment of patients.

## Decision

**Yes: fund a bounded human muscle function and exposure-window experiment.** I find no demonstrated biological or competitive fact that makes that experiment futile. The most valuable result would be recovery of established human muscle weakness at an exposure compatible with limited neural target inhibition and acceptable hepatobiliary function. That is a substantial, transferable development question, and it has not been answered by the mouse publication.

My ranking is **A+ for an immediate, staged proof program, with a credible path to S-tier if the human functional and exposure-window result is convincing**. I would not describe BLU3797 itself as an already demonstrated S-tier development asset. The separation matters: there is enough causal evidence to fund the next decision, while human force, chronic exposure, and ownership of a finished product remain unresolved.

For a new independent development effort, I would prioritize this bounded BLU3797 experiment over simply repeating AAT lung perfusion. AAT has stronger direct human-organ evidence and easier manufactured-protein access. BLU3797 offers a larger still-unanswered, product-defining question. A genuinely differentiated AAT experiment remains fundable, but must establish durable repair after washout or another contribution beyond the existing CSL-associated perfusion work.

## What survived the challenge

### The target has more than one favorable publication

The 2023 genetic study showed normal muscle force with **homozygous** kinase-dead PIK3C2B in Mtm1-null mice; heterozygotes had incomplete survival benefit and did not normalize tibialis anterior force or atrophy. Force groups contained 3–7 mice. BIN1 myopathy did not respond. This supports kinase inhibition in MTM1 disease while warning that mild inhibition may be inadequate. [R2]

More compelling for reversibility, the independent 2016 study induced Pik3c2b deletion after mice were symptomatic: the late-start group began at P30, included seven animals for survival, and survived beyond 100 days. Four late-start animals tested at P100 had grip strength close to wild type. This is whole-protein genetic deletion, not exposure-controlled BLU3797 treatment, but it makes a purely irreversible developmental lesion less likely. [R3]

A separate 2026 mechanistic study links MTM1 loss to abnormal lysosomal phosphoinositides and Rag–mTORC1 regulation, includes patient-derived myotubes, and finds that PI3KC2β reduction improves differentiation in its edited muscle-cell system. It supplies mechanistic convergence, not evidence that BLU3797 restores human contractile force. [R4]

### The drug effect is credible, but the clinical analogy is narrower than the headline

In the BLU3797 paper, oral treatment began at P22 in an unusually severe mixed-background Mtm1 model. Main groups had 5–7 mice; survival and wire hanging improved with exposure. Every-48-hour treatment maintained 28-day survival at 100 mg/kg but only 20% survival at 50 mg/kg. In the supplement, a five-day course begun at P25 also helped, then function declined after withdrawal. The publication uses plasma PK and serum-corrected cellular potency, without establishing free muscle/brain exposure. Its C2C12 work selected one competent knockout clone; neither cellular fusion nor wire hanging directly measures human specific force. The methods do not clearly document severity-stratified randomization or blinded functional scoring. [R1]

**Interpretation:** the results are strong enough to justify independent human testing. They do not establish recovery of chronically weak, mature patient muscle. P25 initiation is late relative to this model's humane endpoint, but still occurs during rapid juvenile growth, and survival to P25 may select a different starting population. The genetic late-rescue evidence reduces that concern without eliminating it. A new program should not spend its first major tranche reproducing the same wire-hang design.

I inspected the public supplemental PDF, including the late-treatment figure and its caption. The supporting numerical workbook is publicly linked, but the web retrieval tool did not parse its XLSX format; I have not represented its animal-level values as independently reanalyzed.

## Two development constraints that must become early experimental variables

### Neural target inhibition is a real on-target issue

Human heterozygous PIK3C2B variants were associated with focal epilepsy, and functional experiments supported loss of kinase function, abnormal neuronal mTORC1 signaling, hyperexcitability, and increased induced-seizure susceptibility in mice. This is not proof that a reversible drug will cause seizures at its useful muscle exposure. It is a direct reason to measure the relevant tissue window. Conversely, the absence of gross illness in kinase-dead mice is not a negative EEG or developmental neurotoxicity study. [R5]

The particularly useful inference is the conjunction of two observations: partial genetic reduction was insufficient for normal mouse muscle force, while heterozygous disruption carried a neuronal risk signal. The program should therefore optimize **muscle target engagement with CNS sparing**, rather than assume a low systemic dose solves both problems. A highly selective kinase inhibitor can still fail this tissue-selectivity requirement.

A human neuronal-network assay alone cannot establish pediatric safety, nor can a low total brain/plasma ratio. The first package needs unbound exposure estimates, target engagement, and a functional neuronal readout. If the exact molecule has poor separation, it can remain a useful research tool while a peripheral successor becomes a separately justified chemistry project. That successor must not inherit BLU3797's efficacy score automatically.

### Hepatobiliary susceptibility is not limited to AAV therapy

The primary Unite-CNM report documents hepatic and hematological tolerability concerns that stopped DYN101, a DNM2 antisense program, before sustained potentially therapeutic dosing. This does not establish toxicity of PIK3C2B inhibition, but it argues against treating hepatobiliary risk as exclusively an AAV-vector problem. [R6] A 2026 patient-led study analyzed 219 participants across MTM/CNM conditions; 36% of affected male myotubular patients reported abnormal liver tests, with substantial gaps in testing. These are self-reported, incompletely monitored data rather than a prospective incidence estimate. [R7]

The practical implication is an early liver-function screen at muscle-effective exposure, especially bile transport/canalicular function and repeated exposure. Healthy hepatocyte viability alone is too weak. A disease-background hepatic model would be valuable where it can be qualified; its absence should be stated rather than compensated for with a claim that normal mouse growth proves safety.

## A newly concrete human experiment: publicly offered XLMTM force tissue

The strongest practical addition from this challenge is **Curi Bio's CB-SKM-XLMTM**. The company announced an off-the-shelf XLMTM contractility model on **14 October 2026**. Its public catalog identifies a male, fibroblast-derived patient line with **MTM1 exon 12 c.1388T>C, p.L463S**, and explicitly labels it a **standalone patient line**. Its product page reports reduced force and greater fatigue, including roughly 2.5–5-fold lower twitch force than healthy controls in 96-well tissues. These are vendor data, not an independent demonstration of an isogenic disease effect or of drug rescue. [R8–R10]

This is materially better than proposing an unspecified human-muscle platform. It gives a named model, a genotype, a force readout, and an ordinary commercial acquisition route. It does **not** verify current stock, a delivery date, access terms, or three independent patient backgrounds. The product page uses a quotation workflow. No inquiry was sent.

BLU3797 itself is catalog-listed as Probechem **PC-26519**, with 50/100/250-mg quotation options and claimed analytical purity. This is a material lead, not verified inventory or a lot-specific certificate. [R11] The published chemistry supplies a route for independent manufacture; the synthesis and structure, rather than the product name alone, should define the test article. [R1]

## The exact proof I would fund

The work should be a **two-release package**, with model qualification and tissue exposure examined before expanding donor count or moving to a large animal efficacy study. The protocol below is a proposed development design, not work already completed.

### Release 1: a qualified, post-differentiation human-force test

Use the catalog-defined patient muscle line as an initial, clearly single-background experiment. Include a genetic MTM1 correction or restoration control in the same background, plus a healthy reference; do not label the unrelated healthy reference “isogenic.” Verify the variant and residual MTM1 expression/activity so that a missense model is not silently treated as a complete knockout. Add a complete-loss MTM1 model in a separate background when available or engineered as a prespecified extension.

Qualify repeatable contractile impairment across separate differentiation/casting batches **before** drug assignment. Confirm that the MTM1 restoration control can improve the relevant phenotype. If the platform does not show a stable, rescuable deficit, stop and report model failure; a negative BLU3797 result in that situation would be uninterpretable.

Randomize developed tissues after their baseline force deficit has been recorded, with blinded analysis and balanced baseline force across arms. Compare exact BLU3797 across a measured concentration range with vehicle, orthogonal PIK3C2B reduction, and MTM1 restoration. BLU2720 is a lower-potency comparator, not an assumed inactive negative control. Use its own engagement curve if it is included.

The decision-driving readouts should be electrically evoked force normalized to contractile cross-section, total force, fatigue and recovery, relaxation, calcium handling, and injury. Measure tissue size and viable muscle content so that added mass cannot masquerade as improved muscle quality. Improvement that only occurs when drug is given during initial differentiation would narrow the development proposition to early intervention; it would not establish restoration of established disease.

Measure free medium exposure and intracellular target engagement alongside function. A common measured exposure scale is essential for comparing muscle, neuronal, and hepatic assays. Confirm the compound's identity and stereochemistry before interpreting either success or failure. Two independently characterized lots would help exclude a supplier-specific contaminant or misidentification.

The experimental units are independent donor backgrounds and differentiation batches, with individual tissues nested within them. Twenty wells from one donor are not twenty patient replicates. A positive first background earns expansion; it does not establish broad human efficacy.

### Release 2: replication and a usable tissue window

Replicate the force result across additional donor backgrounds, ideally spanning a complete-loss allele and residual-function alleles. Add parallel human neuronal network testing with an orthogonal target perturbation, and a hepatobiliary functional assay. A small, focused distribution study measuring unbound muscle and brain concentrations can connect these systems; another large survival study is not the first requirement.

Prespecify the meaningful rescue margin after blinded model qualification, using the MTM1-restored dynamic range and assay variability. Require a consistent exposure–response and more than a statistically detectable change. A credible result should improve force and fatigue together without evidence that injury or loss of tissue explains the apparent change.

**Advance the exact molecule** if established human muscle dysfunction improves reproducibly, the active exposure is attainable, and neural/hepatic findings support a usable separation. **Advance the target but replace the molecule** if orthogonal inhibition works in human muscle while BLU3797 has an unfavorable brain distribution or compound-specific toxicity. **Stop this development direction** if engaged PIK3C2B inhibition repeatedly fails to improve force in qualified, MTM1-rescuable human models, or if the required inhibition is inseparable from a relevant on-target injury.

This produces useful data even if a license to BLU3797 is never obtained: a qualified human functional assay, a target-engagement/force relationship, allelic boundaries, a neural constraint, and a concrete design target for any successor. Its value would be much lower if the output were only more nuclei per myotube or another survival curve without tissue exposure.

## Commercial and intellectual-property position

The discovery publication is funded by Blueprint Medicines, now a Sanofi subsidiary, with company authors. It is not evidence of an abandoned or available asset. [R1] The first-team search did not identify a named BLU3797 clinical program; I treat that as a search result, not proof of corporate intent.

I found **Stemline US20220202824A1 / WO2020056269A1**, concerning treatment of centronuclear myopathy with a different named thiazolopyridinone compound. The retrieved US claims are molecule-specific; they are not a verified monopoly over every PIK3C2B inhibitor. Google Patents displays the US application as abandoned and the European counterpart as withdrawn. Those are useful public facts with aggregator caveats, not a freedom-to-operate opinion. [R12]

No exact BLU3797 claim family was established in this limited name/target search. A structure and inventor-based claim review is a defined diligence task. It should run alongside the proof package and before a product transaction. A supplier listing does not grant patent rights, and failure to find a patent does not imply absence of one. Equally, the existence of earlier target-related filings is not a sufficient reason to halt a well-specified scientific proof.

The positive commercial thesis is conditional but substantive: an orally deliverable, tissue-selective modifier could offer a reversible and titratable way to treat a serious genetic muscle disease. Human force and a tissue exposure window would be partner-useful evidence for the existing molecule or a differentiated successor. I found no already demonstrated PIK3C2B clinical product that makes that result redundant.

## Direct comparison with AAT ex vivo lung perfusion

| Decision axis | BLU3797 / PIK3C2B | AAT / donor-lung EVLP |
|---|---|---|
| Closest relevant human evidence | A defined patient muscle platform is commercially listed; drug force rescue remains unshown | Paired declined human lungs already show functional physiology benefit |
| Defined material | Exact published chemistry plus quote-listed research compound | Approved plasma-derived AAT product; EVLP formulation still requires qualification |
| Most valuable unanswered question | Does human force recover at a sustainable tissue exposure? | Does benefit survive washout and improve transplant durability beyond optimized perfusion? |
| Principal development risk | Deep muscle target inhibition may conflict with neural or hepatobiliary tolerance | Perfusion physiology may not translate into a durable transplant benefit; protein/oncotic controls matter |
| Incremental independent contribution | Potentially large: human function, allelic range, tissue window | Potentially useful but must exceed existing human perfusion dataset and sponsor-associated claims |
| My funding decision | **Yes, staged human-force/exposure proof first** | **Yes only for a differentiated washout/durability or dose-qualification proof** |

The AAT entries draw on the source-audited companion `round2_blood_exvivo.md`, particularly its human paired experiment, exact Zemaira perfusate precedent, CSL-assigned claims, and current clinical program record. I am not treating AAT's rights as an automatic disqualifier. I am distinguishing greater existing biological maturity from greater opportunity to create new, decisive development information.
