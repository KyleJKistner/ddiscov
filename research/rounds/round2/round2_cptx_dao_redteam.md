# Independent challenge: recombinant CPTX for spinal injury and engineered DAO for histamine-mediated shock

> **Historical review snapshot.** Later findings and rankings are reconciled in the [current shortlist](../../../decisions/current-shortlist.md) and [round 4 review](../round4/README.md). Statements about activity or access describe this review period.

**Evidence cut: 4 November 2026.** Prepared as an independent challenge to the second portfolio screen. No outreach, purchasing, repository mutation or owner-dependent gate was used. Grades below describe the value of a bounded research proof, not a probability of clinical approval.

## Decision

**Retain CPTX as an A-level candidate for a staged independent proof. Retain engineered DAO as a conditional B-level adjunct opportunity. Neither presently earns S-tier.** Both have sufficiently defined molecular starting points to make useful work concrete without an owner conversation. CPTX offers the stronger differentiated development question: can a brief, spatially controlled intervention produce durable, useful circuit function after an established injury without inducing harmful excitation? DAO offers a clean biochemical mechanism, but its strongest recent efficacy experiment is unusually favorable to a histamine-degrading enzyme and does not establish the broader anaphylaxis proposition.

The CPTX challenge also corrects an overly pessimistic reading of the literature: **small delayed-contusion experiments exist in the public patent**. They are not independent peer-reviewed confirmation, but the question is no longer whether anyone has tried delayed contusion. Conversely, adding rehabilitation or calling the treatment delayed is not, by itself, a new invention: those directions are already disclosed.

| Decision dimension | CPTX | Engineered DAO |
|---|---|---|
| Exact intervention | Sequence-defined recombinant synaptic organizer; keep injected protein distinct from cells expressing it | Reduced-heparin-binding recombinant human enzyme; keep the free enzyme distinct from Fc fusion, native DAO and oral supplements |
| Best reason to continue | Functional repair signal, a reproducible starting construct, and an identifiable missing safety/function bridge | Strong substrate removal linked to physiological improvement; tractable enzyme engineering |
| Main inference to reject | Protein disappearance means circuit effects are reversible or safe | A histamine challenge demonstrates superiority in antigen-triggered human anaphylaxis |
| Valuable first package | Qualified batches followed by an independent delayed-contusion study with coordinated function and excitation safety | Human mediator-mixture functional gate, then antigen-triggered adjunct rescue with a clinically relevant route |
| New platform necessary for this proof? | No; use the disclosed local route before attempting route simplification | No for an injectable proof; a rapid outpatient intramuscular product would be a separate delivery problem |
| What could earn a higher grade? | Replicated durable benefit with a usable dose/distribution window and no sustained sensory/autonomic harm | Benefit beyond appropriate standard rescue in a mixed-mediator setting, with peripheral-IV exposure and manufacturing comparability |

## 1. CPTX: what the evidence actually covers

### Published protein experiments

The 2020 Science study used local intraspinal protein administration. Figure 6 includes immediate hemisection, one-week delayed hemisection, and immediate 70-kdyn contusion; functional groups had nine mice. The delayed cohort was selected at BMS 1.5. At least two independent investigators assessed BMS blinded to treatment. Allocation randomization was not verified in the retrieved methods. Histology used 16 slices from eight mice. ChABC and Cbln1 were comparators: delayed ChABC was less effective, whereas acute contusion favored the CPTX–ChABC combination particularly early. Function was followed for six weeks; immunoblot signal was undetectable by day seven. The reported local dose included 0.5 µL at 1 µg/µL. CPTX localized near spared glutamatergic structures. In separate hippocampal experiments it enhanced excitatory transmission, including in wild-type tissue. I found no systematic SCI pain, spasticity or autonomic safety assessment in the retrieved primary sections. These are functional animal results, not merely synapse counts, but they do not establish a human delivery window. [C1]

### The patent adds relevant delayed-contusion experiments

US20230192810A1 discloses a 326-residue precursor in SEQ ID NO. 2: a secretion signal, human Cbln1-derived neurexin-binding segment, engineered GCN4 trimerization element, human NPTX1-derived receptor-binding segment and His tag. HEK production, purification and oligomer characterization are described. Figure 5F reports two-week treatment after 70-kdyn contusion in C57BL/6 mice, n=4–5. Figure 5G reports four-week treatment after 90-kdyn contusion, C57BL/6 n=4, with a recovery tendency; Figure 5H uses ICR mice, n=2–3. Methods describe blinded assessors, excluding animals with BMS above zero on day three, and analyzing footfalls only when animals walked at least 70 times in three minutes. Later dose schedules differ from the Science experiment. Rehabilitation and combination treatment are expressly contemplated. The displayed assignee history includes Keio, Aichi and subsequently Fujita; displayed legal status is not a formal rights opinion. [C2]

These examples increase the value of replication, but their tiny groups and disclosure setting substantially limit confidence. They do not support an assertion that delayed, heterogeneous clinical SCI has already been de-risked. Nor should the day-three neurological selection criterion be recast as proof of anatomical cord transection: the project still depends on a recruitable substrate.

### The cell-therapy follow-on is not replication of the injected product

The 2024 study transplanted CPTX-expressing human iPSC-derived neural progenitors into rats. At 14 weeks, BBB means were 7.97 for PBS, 10.36 for control progenitors and 11.57 for CPTX progenitors; the respective groups had 15, 14 and 15 animals. Both cell groups differed from PBS, but CPTX versus control cells had p=0.58. Stride length did not differ between the two cell groups either. Other integration and conduction findings can support biological plausibility, but the paper does not demonstrate an incremental locomotor benefit on every endpoint, and its cells create a different exposure, persistence and manufacturing product. [C3]

## 2. CPTX: strongest challenges and how to resolve them

### A favorable locomotor score is not sufficient

My main concern is whether the intervention improves the organization of useful activity rather than simply increasing excitability. The next experiment should distinguish voluntary task performance, appropriate conduction and nonproductive motor output. A larger BMS change without coherent stepping, appropriate recruitment and preserved sensory function would be an ambiguous result.

A second concern is selection. Narrow baseline selection can make a legitimate mechanistic experiment easier to interpret. It also prevents the result from carrying the burden of a broader treatment claim. Stratification is preferable for the development study: specify the clinically relevant incomplete-injury range, randomize within baseline strata, and retain all randomized animals in the primary analysis. Walking-only analyses should remain explicitly conditional secondary outcomes, because the ability to enter that analysis is itself affected by treatment.

A third concern is persistence. The time at which an assay no longer detects protein does not determine the time at which an induced synapse disappears. Persistent benefit is the attraction, and persistent inappropriate connectivity would be the corresponding liability. A short exposure can therefore be advantageous without being a built-in reversal mechanism.

The model also needs an honest indication. A useful near-term hypothesis is repair of recruitable circuitry in incomplete injury. A claim to rebuild an anatomically disconnected chronic cord would demand a different evidence package. Conflating those goals would make a bounded experiment look inadequate merely because it cannot solve a separate, much harder problem.

### A useful proof does not require a delivery invention

A sequence-defined local protein is sufficient for the first decision. Recreating a qualified research article and delivering it by the disclosed route is a production and experimental execution task. It is not necessary to add a hydrogel, vector, cell graft or systemic carrier before asking whether the underlying effect survives independent scrutiny.

For eventual development, local distribution and procedure burden still matter. A mouse parenchymal injection cannot be assumed to predict intrathecal exposure or coverage of a larger lesion. The correct sequence is to earn a therapeutic window with the known route, measure tissue distribution and then decide whether a practical clinical procedure can reproduce it. Route simplification should carry its own comparability milestone.

The same discipline applies to manufacture. A tag-removed molecule or different expression/glycosylation process can be a sensible product optimization, but its equivalence should be tested rather than inferred. Start with the public research configuration and create a reference standard; do not silently move to a successor while calling the outcome a replication.

### Staged independent experiment

**Gate 1: define and qualify the material.** Produce two independent batches against the public sequence. Confirm identity, oligomer distribution, aggregation, both binding activities and a functional cell assay. Use a mechanism-impaired control that preserves the relevant physical characteristics as far as feasible. Document activity per unit mass rather than relying on an intact band or a binding result alone. This is a finite CMC and biology gate; stop if qualified material cannot reproduce the expected activity.

**Gate 2: delayed incomplete contusion with fixed rehabilitation.** Make the initial experiment manageable: vehicle and CPTX on the same standardized rehabilitation background, with a justified mechanism control and an active ChABC benchmark if the powered design permits it. Do not launch a sprawling dose-by-rehabilitation-by-combination matrix. Allocation concealment, blinded ratings, prespecified exclusions, baseline strata and animal-level analysis should be explicit. Choose sample size from a qualified model's variance and a meaningful functional target, not from the small published group size.

Treat after the initial deficit has stabilized for the chosen subacute model. Make one coordinated functional endpoint primary, with conduction and an independent locomotor task as supportive evidence. Quantify the exposed tissue volume and protein levels with an assay whose detection limit is stated. Follow behavior beyond measurable exposure. Include sensory hypersensitivity, abnormal spontaneous activity, spasticity-like behavior and autonomic measures appropriate to the model. These should be prespecified, rather than inferred from the absence of a gross adverse event.

**Go:** reproducible improvement in useful coordinated function, consistent with physiological recruitment, across qualified batches and baseline strata, at an exposure that does not produce persistent pathological excitation. **Stop or redesign:** an effect confined to an acute/preselected subgroup; synaptic-marker changes without function; generalized excitability without coordinated improvement; persistent pain or autonomic cost; or a very narrow exposure/distribution margin.

The new asset would be the **reliable function-and-safety package**, followed by a procedure-compatible product specification. It would not be an independently novel claim to CPTX, delayed treatment or rehabilitation. Public prior art can establish what not to invent again while leaving scientific development decisions fully actionable. No assertion about licensing willingness is needed to rank this proof.

## 3. DAO: an impressive narrow experiment and an overstated broad inference

### Exact recent efficacy evidence

The 2026 guinea-pig study used subcutaneous histamine, not antigen exposure, in anesthetized females. Engineered rhDAO was tested at 2, 4 and 8 mg/kg, both prophylactically and after challenge. Although the abstract describes IV use, methods specify infusion through the carotid arterial catheter; adrenaline was one IM injection at 0.005 or 0.01 mg/kg. Therapeutic dosing began after heart rate exceeded 250/min for one minute, whereas shock additionally required a sustained MAP decrease. Thus treatment need not have begun after established shock. Histamine controls had 5/12 deaths, each adrenaline group 6/10; DAO's mortality analysis pooled prophylactic and therapeutic doses. The highest dose prevented shock in its groups; only one DAO-treated animal died overall. Observation ended at 60 minutes. The main text identifies a mutated heparin-binding motif but does not fully specify the lot sequence. Its cited earlier guinea-pig experiment used continuous histamine infusion; its antigen precedent used older plant-derived DAO. [D1]

The biochemical starting point is more explicit in the 2021 engineering study: **rhDAO-R568S/R571T** reduced heparin-related binding/clearance while retaining histamine-degrading activity. An Fc format was also examined, and should remain a separate molecule. [D2] Follow-on work identifies Asn168 glycosylation as important for slow clearance. [D3] Sequence identity alone is therefore an incomplete product specification.

I did not identify a public therapeutic antigen-challenge efficacy experiment with this exact reduced-heparin-binding recombinant construct. That is a bounded search conclusion, not proof that unpublished experiments do not exist.

### Why the broad claim does not follow

The experiment creates a strong prior for enzymatic control of circulating histamine. It creates a much weaker prior for control of the complete mediator response to an allergen. Human observational work associates anaphylaxis severity with multiple mediator patterns; in one 41-patient study, histamine was elevated in 70% of grade-three reactions and did not show the same severity association as platelet-activating factor. That does not prove DAO would fail, but it directly limits the inference from substrate challenge to the entire clinical syndrome. [D4]

There are two independent comparison problems. First, a route that immediately exposes the circulation to an enzyme cannot automatically be interpreted against a single muscle injection as a comparison of intrinsic therapeutic superiority. Second, preventing progression after a tachycardia trigger is a different claim from reversing an already established hemodynamic collapse. Neither observation invalidates the study's physiological results; both narrow what a new program should claim and test.

I would not overstate a possible nitric-oxide failure either. A 2025 primary study found irreversible enzyme inhibition in vitro, but did not demonstrate DAO inhibition in the tested in-vivo models, including passive anaphylaxis. That paper is not an antigen-challenge therapeutic rescue experiment with engineered DAO. [D5] It motivates measuring activity under the intended conditions rather than assuming either universal inhibition or universal robustness.

The short observation window also cannot carry a prevention-of-biphasic-reactions claim. Any such proposition needs measured active-enzyme exposure and its own functional follow-up. Likewise, extrapolating a rodent half-life to several days in humans is a development hypothesis, not established human pharmacokinetics.

## 4. DAO: the bounded proof worth considering

**First qualify the actual enzyme.** Use a defined human sequence and mutant, a reproducible mammalian production process, glycan and oligomer characterization, active-enzyme concentration and histamine-degradation kinetics in the relevant biological matrix. The 2026 lot should not be described as fully reconstructed from the main article alone. Its supplemental DOCX was linked publicly but could not be parsed in this review; the separately published double mutant remains a concrete, testable starting article.

**Use an inexpensive mixed-mediator human-function gate before another large animal study.** Apply material from allergen-activated human mast-cell/basophil systems to a qualified vascular-barrier or other relevant functional assay. Test active and inactive enzyme, measure histamine elimination, and determine how much functional injury remains. This deliberately tests the gap between removing the nominated substrate and restoring the desired function. A strong biochemical effect with little functional protection would narrow the indication or lower priority before substantial spending.

**If that passes, test adjunct rescue after an actual antigen challenge.** The development comparison should add DAO to an appropriate rescue regimen versus that regimen alone, use a peripheral-IV route relevant to the intended initial product, and prespecify a hemodynamic trigger. Randomize and blind where practical; include all treated animals in the primary outcome; extend the observation period enough to distinguish a brief delay from sustained recovery. Record active enzyme and histamine alongside physiological outcomes. A stand-alone arm might inform mechanism but should not define a claim that replaces standard care.

The attractive first indication would be a monitored setting in which vascular access and patient selection are realistic. That is a project hypothesis, not a validated market selection. An outpatient intramuscular product must solve a separate absorption and timing problem; it should not inherit the evidence for directly circulating enzyme.

Public patents already disclose engineered DAO for histamine-associated disease; the displayed EP3927817 family includes a 2025 B1 publication and identifies the Vienna universities. [D6] The independent contribution would be reliable mixed-mediator efficacy, a practical exposure window and a controlled manufacturing specification. A study that merely repeats histamine depletion would add too little. A later claim-specific commercial review is distinct from the question of whether the next proof is scientifically worth funding.

## 5. Implications for the shortlist and process

**CPTX survives independent challenge with a narrower, more valuable task.** It has enough exact-intervention evidence to justify a qualified independent study, while the key uncertainty is experimentally addressable without first constructing a delivery platform. The portfolio should retain it as a serious alternative to the lead muscle and ex-vivo programs, not promote it on the strength of a narrative about permanent repair.

**DAO remains a reserve.** It is materially more concrete than an unnamed scavenger concept, but its decisive experiment is still a translation from a single-mediator challenge to a clinically relevant mediator mixture. That is worth a small first gate if resources permit. It is not yet the portfolio's strongest development package.

Two process changes follow. First, the evidence unit must stay attached to **construct, route, injury model, timing and comparator**; otherwise a patent example, a cell therapy or a challenge model can silently fill a gap it did not test. Second, a good shortlist entry must name the result that would kill it. For CPTX that is failure of coordinated delayed rescue or persistent unwanted excitation. For DAO it is failure to improve function despite removing histamine, or failure to add benefit to an appropriate rescue comparator.
