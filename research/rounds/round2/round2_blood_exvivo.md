# Round 2: blood, vascular, matrix and ex vivo drug opportunities

> **Historical review snapshot.** Later findings and rankings are reconciled in the [current shortlist](../../../decisions/current-shortlist.md) and [round 4 review](../round4/README.md). Statements about activity or access describe this review period.

Date: 4 November 2026. Repository snapshot: `c241dc4c6cfa7cd5312a7237dda61a4d2af50c97`. This is an internal research memo, with explicit separation of source observations and proposed work. No outreach, transactions, or remote edits were performed.

## Decision

**The best fresh development thesis in this branch is an alpha-1-antitrypsin (AAT) perfusion additive for repairing injured donor lungs.** It has a defined, manufactured component; an accessible treatment compartment; paired human organ functional evidence; and a useful decisive next experiment. I would advance it to the final cross-review and fund the preparation of that experiment. I would call it an **S-tier proof-program contender**, not an independently owned, clinically validated S-tier asset. A new project must contribute a durable, lower-dose, reproducible organ-repair product; simply repeating “AAT improves EVLP” adds too little.

The strongest alternative new lead is **plasminogen replenishment plus alteplase during kidney perfusion**. This explicitly fixes a missing substrate in an acellular perfusion system and clears a visible pathological obstruction in human kidneys. The current evidence is smaller than its headline “39 kidneys” implies: the central paired efficacy experiment involved three donor pairs. That makes it a good controlled replication opportunity rather than a proven organ-rescue product.

The most interesting existing physical-repair family remains **cartilage charge replacement**, especially PSS and BPG10 considered as different products with different endpoints. BPG10 has human tissue data, but changes a local pericellular modulus; PSS has bulk permeability recovery, but in experimentally depleted animal cartilage. Combining those facts into a claim that a retained PSS-PNIPAM or BPG10 product restores whole human joints would be an evidence error. A comparative human explant study could resolve this cheaply relative to organ transplantation work.

Haptoglobin for aSAH is biologically serious and has unusually strong target-to-function evidence, but an active CSL888 development effort is visible publicly. Taurine for cold platelets is an easy experiment with a weakened commercial thesis: the October 2026 CHIPS trial established noninferiority of unmodified cold platelets stored for up to 21 days. The product now has to improve on that standard.

## 1. AAT for donor-lung repair: the leading fresh thesis

### What the evidence actually says

The 2026 human report studied eight declined double-lung blocks, with left/right lungs randomized to AAT or control on separate circuits for 12 hours. Oxygenation and compliance improved; edema/perfusate loss fell. This is human organ physiology, not cytokines alone. The independent biological unit is the donor, not the lung side, hourly sample, or biopsy. The same eight-donor experiment appears to have been presented in 2020; these publications should count as **one dataset**, pending any contrary provenance. [A1, A2]

The accessible 2020 abstract gives small pressure/resistance differences (approximately −0.4 mmHg pulmonary pressure and −30.4 dyn·s·cm−5 vascular resistance), while the full oxygenation/compliance effect sizes are not exposed in the retrieved abstract. Do not turn statistical significance into a claimed transplantability effect. The 2026 full text was inaccessible through the available retrieval route. [A2]

The exact published pig EVLP reference used **Zemaira 3 mg/mL in acellular Steen solution**, six pig lung blocks per arm, after 24 hours of cold storage followed by 12 hours of EVLP. Function and edema improved. A separate randomized, blinded transplantation experiment used recipient intravenous AAT, 240 mg/kg, versus albumin, five animals per arm, and followed reperfusion for four hours. That supports the mechanism after transplantation, but it is not proof that an ex vivo-only, washed-out treatment produces durable graft benefit. A 2018 three-day survival abstract exists, but its detailed outcomes were not independently verified here. [A3, A4, A10]

A public patent supplies additional exact human pilot information: five split donor blocks, AAT stated at **10 mg/mL**. It must not be represented as the confirmed dose of all eight lungs in the 2026 article. Its circuit-volume text contains an apparent 1.5 mL typo, so that number should not be used operationally. The pilot includes heterogeneity between contralateral lungs; pairing reduces donor confounding but does not eliminate asymmetric injury. [A5]

### Why there is a concrete product path

Zemaira is an FDA-approved plasma-derived A1PI product, supplied in 1 g, 4 g and 5 g vials. There is no need to discover a protein sequence or await an academic owner to establish experimental tractability. Its approved use is systemic augmentation in AAT deficiency, not EVLP. Its label limits the reconstituted administration period to three hours and says not to mix it with other medicinal products. A 12-hour, warm, recirculating perfusion application therefore needs its **own** stability, compatibility and functional potency package. That is a legitimate product-development task, not a paper obstacle. [A6]

Public rights are real and specific: US20190059362A1 is assigned to CSL Behring and addresses AAT-containing organ perfusion; related AU/KR grants are listed. The US status shown by the public aggregator is pending, not a legal freedom-to-operate determination. An approved UHN trial of Zemaira to prevent primary graft dysfunction after lung transplantation is also listed in Health Canada's portal, protocol 23-5745, control 294199, “not yet recruiting” at the displayed update. That is evidence the field has moved toward clinical development, not proof that an ex vivo product is commercially spoken for in every jurisdiction or indication. [A5, A7]

The reasonable commercial thesis is a **validated repair additive/kit for a defined EVLP workflow**, with controlled active concentration, established compatibility, a response criterion identifying salvageable lungs, and demonstrated benefit after washout and transplantation. Existing AAT manufacturers, perfusion companies and transplant service providers are plausible eventual counterparties. That is an inference from the component and use case; no partnership is asserted. Public evidence supports the scientific investment decision today. Commercial rights would matter before commercialization; no email is necessary to decide whether the proof is worth doing.

### The decisive experiment that adds value

First use a blinded, donor-paired human lung experiment comparing current optimized perfusion with a predefined low-dose AAT strategy. Match protein/excipient load, oncotic pressure and electrolyte changes. Do not use “extra protein” as an unbalanced control. The active comparison should include the higher pilot concentration, so a lower-dose product must retain the functional benefit. AAT protease-inhibitory activity—not antigen concentration alone—should be measured throughout the circuit, together with aggregates/adsorption and a washout mass balance.

The primary product question is whether AAT produces **sustained usable lung function after removal of free perfusate drug**. Predefine gas exchange, compliance, permeability/fluid balance and a blinded clinical acceptance rubric. Treat multiple time points as repeated observations within each donor. Follow with an ex vivo-only treatment versus control in a survival transplant model spanning the primary-graft-dysfunction window. Systemic peri-reperfusion AAT can be a mechanistic comparator, not a substitute for validating the intended product route.

Suggested decision thresholds are planning choices, not published facts: advancement requires a consistent donor-level improvement in oxygenation/compliance, reduced fluid accumulation, persistence after washout, and no worsening after transplantation. A result confined to circuit measurements, erased by physiologically corrected perfusate or albumin matching, or requiring continuous systemic exposure would kill this particular ex vivo product thesis.

Why these controls matter: a recent porcine study found that merely correcting perfusate composition improved EVLP physiology without a post-transplant oxygenation benefit. The regadenoson randomized donor-lung trial also found no increase in lung use or reduction in graft dysfunction despite its preclinical rationale. These are specific reasons to require transplantation durability. [A8, A9]

**Assessment:** strongest candidate for a serious proof budget in this branch; existing product supply established; public rights dependency known; no claim that the current evidence establishes a separately ownable S-tier company.

## 2. Plasminogen plus alteplase for human kidney microvascular plugs

The lesion is unusually concrete. Prolonged cold storage was associated with epithelial fibrinogen accumulation, followed by intravascular release, red-cell aggregation and plugging during reperfusion. The acellular perfusate lacked plasminogen. Giving tPA without supplying its substrate was therefore a mechanistically incomplete intervention. In the human kidney study, the combination cleared obstructions better than either component control. The overall 39-organ program contains distinct mechanistic and treatment cohorts; the paired viability comparison was **three donors, six kidneys**, not 39 independent efficacy observations. Urine output in that paired cohort did not meet conventional significance (P=.1582), even though injury markers and obstruction measures favored treatment. [K1]

Both components have clinical manufacturing precedents: alteplase and plasma-derived human plasminogen (Ryplazim). Ryplazim's FDA label confirms the protein and formulation; it does not establish that substituting it into the paper's protocol is automatically equivalent. Activity, activation kinetics, excipients and final circuit composition still need qualification. [K2]

A small earlier randomized clinical trial of tPA during hypothermic machine perfusion did not show significant graft-function benefit. It is not a test of the later normothermic, substrate-replenished combination, but it prevents treating “thrombolysis improves kidneys” as established. [K3]

**Proposed product:** a timed two-component reperfusion additive with a washout step for kidneys demonstrating high-resistance microvascular obstruction. **Decisive proof:** randomized contralateral kidneys, with combination, component and standard-perfusion comparisons distributed across independent donor blocks; clearance of a freely filtered tracer, perfusion uniformity, cortical oxygenation, tubular injury and residual fibrinolytic activity. Urine volume alone is insufficient. A subsequent transplant study must show better graft function without bleeding or microvascular damage.

**Kill criterion:** impressive histology without improved filtration/perfusion after washout. **Grade:** A for a controllable comparative experiment, below AAT because true functional recovery is less established.

## 3. Cartilage charge replacement: keep the exact products separate

BPG10 is an approximately 180 kDa chondroitin-sulfate brush on an approximately 10 kDa poly(acrylic acid) core. Its preparation is public and uses obtainable inputs. In four normal and four OA human donor samples, BPG10 increased pericellular micromodulus; the OA donor-average estimate rose from about 71 to 140 kPa. Hundreds of AFM indents per donor are technical observations, not hundreds of people. The bulk interterritorial domain did not show the same effect. This supports local matrix engineering, not proven whole-cartilage load recovery or analgesia. [C1]

PSS homopolymer restored hydraulic permeability in GAG-depleted cartilage in a different study. PSS-PNIPAM was evaluated for transport and retention in another study. Neither result automatically transfers to the other construct. PSS also has a nondegradable polymer backbone, so repeated-dose tissue disposition and joint tolerance matter; a short live/dead assay cannot resolve them. [C2, C3]

**Proposed fundable comparison:** in adult human OA osteochondral samples, compare BPG10, PSS, an explicitly specified PSS-PNIPAM construct, chondroitin sulfate and vehicle at matched delivered charge where feasible. Measure bulk biphasic mechanics, fluid pressurization, friction and wear after cyclic loading and repeated washout. Local AFM is a mechanistic secondary endpoint. Quantify spatial distribution and distinguish simple filling/coating from retained mechanical recovery.

A materially improved new concept is **BPG10 plus full-length recombinant PRG4**: pericellular charge support plus boundary lubrication. This is a hypothesis, not demonstrated synergy. Test both components and combination; a combination is justified only if it adds function beyond the better single component without antagonizing penetration or surface attachment. Current lubricin-inspired polymers are already entering first-human evaluation, so “an injectable lubricant” alone is not distinctive. [C4]

**Grade:** A− for a human mechanics proof; no S claim until useful bulk function survives washout/loading.

## 4. Haptoglobin for aSAH: good science, an active development landscape

Human aSAH CSF and vessel experiments, together with sheep work, support a physical mechanism: Hb-Hp complexes restrict hemoglobin movement into vulnerable vessel/tissue compartments and preserve vascular signaling. The awake-sheep study randomized 12 animals to Hb or Hb-Hp over three days and found less neurological deterioration with Hp. However, Hb was co-administered with scavenger; this is not a delayed rescue of an established human hemorrhage. [H1, H2]

The actual CSL888 product is plasma-derived haptoglobin; a 2026 clinical-pharmacology poster describes first-human dose modeling based on nonhuman-primate ICV exposures. HAPTO is a CSF distribution/pharmacokinetic study using vancomycin as a tracer, **not a completed efficacy trial of haptoglobin**. A 2026 organotypic brain-slice study supports complementary Hp and hemopexin actions, but does not establish that their combination is better than Hp in living patients. [H3, H4, H5]

**Useful differentiation:** delayed treatment after established clot lysis, clinically relevant compartmental distribution, and Hb/heme-guided dosing with functional neurovascular/neuronal outcomes. Existing L047 already includes Hp removal or Hp/Hpx, so merely suggesting two scavengers is not a new lead. A validated delayed-rescue window would be valuable. This program remains below AAT for a new independent proof effort because the most decisive remaining experiment is closely aligned with a visible advanced sponsor program.

## 5. Platelet storage: update the product requirement

The taurine study used ten unique donors and measured platelet function under flow. That makes it better than a metabolomics association. But a 2026 phase 3 trial analyzed 989 transfused patients and found unmodified cold platelets stored up to 21 days noninferior for cardiac-surgery bleeding; the cold arm had more reexploration. Simply demonstrating 21-day clotting again is insufficient differentiation. [P1, P2]

There is a specific new combination hypothesis: **reversible calcium chelation during storage plus taurine**, followed by verified restoration of calcium-dependent activation before use. EGTA pretreatment limited cold-triggered activation/clearance and improved bleeding outcomes in mouse studies. This addresses survival/activation while taurine addresses metabolic function. Their combination is untested. A candidate must preserve recovery and useful hemostasis after prolonged storage, against modern cold storage, not merely improve a storage biomarker. [P3]

The 2024 human crossover study adds a useful boundary: 14-day cold platelets generated more thrombin but reversed aspirin-dependent platelet inhibition less effectively than seven-day room-temperature platelets. “More procoagulant” and “better platelet replacement” are different product claims. [P4]

**Grade:** taurine alone B after the updated comparator; the chelation/taurine factorial experiment A− for learnability, not demonstrated clinical value.

MAM's timing matters: it was protective when given at reperfusion, not before ischemia in the renal study. A 2026 cardiac study showed loss of malonate protection under a more metabolically complex insulin-containing condition. This is a useful design constraint for realistic perfusates, not grounds to assume all reperfusion biology is wrong. [N1, N2]

DiNAC is chemically and pharmacologically distinct from NAC. At 2 mM it markedly reduced white-clot burden in the reported flow experiment, whereas red fibrinous clots remained resistant. The first product should therefore be framed around a demonstrable clot phenotype and achievable local exposure. The related NAC-Safety trial was stopped after two fatal hemorrhages among 12 patients; that does not prove DiNAC has the same risk, but it decisively rejects borrowing a “NAC is familiar, therefore thrombolytic augmentation is safe” argument. [N3, N4, N5]

The new DNase/plasminogen concept is mechanistically different from adding more tPA: it attempts to unlock tPA already in a resistant clot. However, the 34-patient NETS-TARGET pilot did not improve its recanalization endpoint, and the retrieved 330-patient EXTEND-IA DNase abstract did not yet report final efficacy results. Those clinical facts outweigh a confident claim based solely on ex vivo lysis. [N6, N7, N8]

## Existing families that should move down, and why

- **rLOX tendon fixation:** the principal positive mechanical result was in developing chick tendon; adult human repaired tendon, suture pullout and adhesion are not established. Do not re-label fetal tissue crosslinking as surgical repair.
- **Tf2 lymphatic pumping:** a valuable target-level result, but the retrieved therapeutic study is a preprint. An independent synthetic-Tf2 paper found NaV1.9 activity, pain and swelling not explained by the nominal target. This is a precise medicinal-chemistry problem; a local depot does not automatically fix locally induced swelling.
- **ABO organ conversion:** initial antigen erasure is real; a human decedent kidney developed antibody-mediated lesions beginning around day 3 with antigen re-expression. A durable product must solve rebound or bridge to accommodation. No inference of long-term compatibility from early avoidance of hyperacute rejection.
- **Liver defatting:** triglyceride mobilization is not equivalent to organ rescue. A 2026 discarded-human-liver comparison reported viability criteria in 3/9 treated versus 2/3 controls despite tissue TG reduction. The relevant modern comparator is the already recruiting DeFat strategy using clinically applicable agents and lipid filtration.
- **Ibuprofen-assisted dialysis:** 2026 crossover data support enhanced toxin clearance and rule out the tested loop-diuretic shortcuts. Clinical outcome improvement and repeated NSAID exposure remain separate questions. A clearance signal is enough for a focused delivery experiment, not an S-tier renal-outcomes claim.
- **PNH RBC-tethered DAF:** the missing terminal regulator and normal-RBC antigen sink matter. An exact construct must improve clinically relevant complement protection over current proximal/terminal suppression without impairing red-cell mechanics.
- **ADAMTS7:** human target support does not establish selectivity or adult-intervention safety of the available dual-ADAMTS7/12 tools.
- **Formulation backlog:** preserve promising engineering measurements, but require a specified product and present-day active comparator before promoting a franchise-level question to a drug lead.
