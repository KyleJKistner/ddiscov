# Round 2 — neuromuscular, neurologic, metabolic and renal opportunities

> **Historical review snapshot.** Later findings and rankings are reconciled in the [current shortlist](../../../decisions/current-shortlist.md) and [round 4 review](../round4/README.md). Statements about activity or access describe this review period.

**Evidence cut: 4 November 2026.** This is a development-opportunity assessment, not treatment guidance. The unit being ranked is an exact intervention plus an indication, delivery context, comparator and decisive experiment. The latest HJ-02/icerguastat/ODAD1 shortlist did not determine this search.

## Decision

**BLU3797-mediated PIK3C2B inhibition in MTM1-related myotubular myopathy is the strongest newly found proof-funding opportunity in this lane. CPTX synaptic repair is the strongest recovered physical-repair concept.** Neither deserves an assertion of clinical certainty. Both deserve an independent challenge and a bounded experiment that could turn an attractive publication into an investable asset.

I would first fund the BLU3797 human-muscle and tissue-exposure proof, provided its first safety/identity stage is passed. I would reserve a separate, smaller initial work package for reproducing CPTX protein activity and testing whether its functional benefit survives a clinically relevant delayed-treatment design. That is a substantially better allocation than continuing to rank the same three recent candidates by how easy it might be to obtain an owner response.

There are other strong mechanisms here. A new piclidenoson/Lowe paper is particularly instructive: the public commercial route is unusually explicit, yet a large cell-to-human exposure gap remains. Conversely, BLU3797 has a catalog-listed research article and a published synthesis even though commercial rights are unresolved. These are different questions and must remain different fields.

### What was covered

The companion `round2_neuro_metabolic_coverage.json` maps **308 relevant or overlapping records** to the root portfolio inventory: 160 historical topics, 30 component proposals, 29 functional comparisons, 11 broad-screen entries, 64 later screen rows and 14 formulation comparisons. These are **records, not 308 unique drugs**. Eight existing records received a fresh primary-source check in this lane; additional newly generated concepts were independently searched. The remainder received traceable registry/atlas triage, not a fictitious claim of exhaustive paper-level validation.

The review included the full historical registry and later-screen index, the neuromuscular/metabolism/kidney atlases, and the relevant marketed-formulation comparisons. Renal organ-perfusion overlaps were passed to the blood/ex vivo agent. No outreach or external transactions were performed.

## 1. Repaired decision process

The earlier workflow repeatedly promoted a broad biological idea and only later asked whether its exact form could be tested. That produces recurring availability disappointments. A second problem is that a successful comparator, carrier, cargo or animal model can silently supply confidence to an untested combination. A third is allowing commercial uncertainty to stop a scientifically useful investigation before the experiment has even been specified.

For this round I used these separate questions:

| Question | Evidence that moves it forward | Evidence that does not answer it |
|---|---|---|
| Is the lesion causal and reversible? | Genetic correction, mechanism-specific rescue, reversal after dysfunction appears | Association, expression normalization alone |
| Does the exact intervention restore a relevant function? | Force, conduction, renal uptake, coordinated movement or substrate clearance linked to injury | Protein abundance, myelin shape or pathway markers alone |
| Can this exact test article be made or obtained? | Catalog identity; full sequence; reproducible synthesis; available control construct | A paper about a related molecule; an unspecified proprietary platform |
| Is effective exposure plausible in the needed compartment? | Unbound tissue exposure and exposure–response; practical local delivery | Total plasma concentration versus a protein-free assay; “crosses the BBB” without dose |
| What could a new project contribute? | A missing human functional bridge, a delivery improvement, a defensible therapeutic window | Repeating a funded program's introductory experiments |
| Is there a commercial route? | Public patents, current assignees, sponsor disclosures and active program scope | Lack of search results interpreted as freedom to operate |
| Is the next proof worth its cost? | A prespecified result that changes development priority | An open-ended list of more assays or requests for information |

A named owner's unknown willingness to license is a commercial uncertainty. It is not a reason to exclude a reproducible proof from a shortlist. A visible owned program can be scientifically excellent and an existing-company investment opportunity; it can also be a poor basis for launching a duplicative independent product. No outreach is needed to make that distinction.

## 2. Priority one: exact BLU3797 for MTM1-related XLMTM

### Published evidence, tightly bounded

Shearer et al. disclose a stereodefined PIK3C2B inhibitor, BLU3797, with biochemical IC50 28 nM and cellular NanoBRET IC50 68.2 nM. A less potent analog, BLU2720, and PIK3C2B knockdown support mechanism assignment. The cellular work used a selected MTM1-null C2C12 clone, not human patient muscle. Treatment of severely affected male Mtm1-null mice began at P22; a small P25-start experiment began near the usual humane endpoint. Survival and wire-hang improved, and withdrawal caused functional decline. The 28-day intermittent-dose experiment reported all high-dose animals surviving versus 20% at the lower dose; main groups had 5–7 animals. These are animals, not muscle fibers. This is short-term late rescue in a rapidly developing juvenile model, not demonstrated recovery in chronically ventilated human muscle. The paper provides chemical structure, synthesis and supporting numerical data. [S1]

An independent genetic study found that disabling PIK3C2B kinase activity rescued force and survival in Mtm1-deficient mice, but not the tested Bin1 myopathy. That strengthens target specificity while defining an indication boundary. [S2]

### Why this is a plausible high-value project

The first-principles idea is appealing: rather than transporting a large missing intracellular phosphatase into every muscle, reduce an opposing lipid-kinase activity enough to rebalance the affected trafficking system. It has an exposure-linked small-molecule demonstration, mechanistically related genetic support, and a serious disease in which a functional improvement would matter. The project does not need to start by inventing a delivery platform.

The scarce information is now **whether the molecular correction generates useful contractile force in mature human MTM1-deficient muscle at a tolerable tissue exposure**. A positive answer would have more development value than another report of myotube nuclei, p62 normalization or wire hanging. A negative answer would save a large animal or gene-delivery program from being pursued on the wrong endpoint.

### Exact test article and material route

The paper names BLU3797 as `(S)-N-(5-(2-(1-cyclopropylethyl)-7-(cyclopropylmethoxy)-1-oxoisoindolin-5-yl)-4-methylthiazol-2-yl)acetamide`; use the structure in Figure 1 and synthetic characterization as the identity authority, not a loosely named PI3K inhibitor. The published supporting chemistry is a concrete custom-synthesis route. [S1]

Probechem publicly lists **PC-26519, BLU3797**, including identity and the original reference. This is evidence of a catalog-listed research material, not verified stock, delivery, purity of a particular lot, or clinical-grade supply. [S3] A first test can therefore be specified with two independently verified lots, or one supplier lot plus a commissioned identity-confirmed batch. Identity should include stereochemical purity, LC-MS/NMR, residual impurities and assay potency before biological conclusions are drawn.

The **proposed improvement** is not to relabel BLU3797 as a new compound. It is a muscle-exposure-defined development package, potentially followed by a more peripherally restricted successor only if the exact molecule shows unwanted CNS exposure. No successor has yet been made or validated.

### The strongest falsifier

PIK3C2B loss-of-function variants have been linked to human focal epilepsy, with neuronal hyperexcitability and seizure susceptibility in experimental models. [S4] High kinome selectivity therefore does not remove the main safety question: on-target inhibition can be harmful in the wrong tissue.

A sustainable muscle therapeutic window must be demonstrated, not assumed from the absence of obvious mouse toxicity. The relevant question is whether muscle target engagement sufficient for force rescue can coexist with acceptably low neural exposure and preserved electrophysiology. A compound that needs near-complete systemic target suppression and substantially reaches the CNS could fail despite being chemically selective.

The other major falsifier is a developmental-model artifact. Improvement in forming young mouse myotubes may not repair the excitation–contraction apparatus of older patient muscle. Treatment should start after a prespecified functional deficit in a matured human model. Simply adding drug during differentiation would not resolve this uncertainty.

### Concrete proof to fund

**Stage 1 — identity and exposure window.** Confirm the exact molecule, its free fraction in the assay medium, intracellular target engagement, metabolic stability and basic muscle/brain distribution. Test neuronal network activity and an orthogonal PIK3C2B perturbation alongside the compound. Use BLU2720 as a potency comparator, not an assumed inactive negative control. Its concentration must be chosen with its own target engagement measured.

**Stage 2 — human muscle function.** Use at least three independent human donor backgrounds, with matched MTM1-deficient and corrected/isogenic controls; two clones per genotype are technical safeguards, not extra human donors. Start treatment after a reproducible force deficit has appeared. Measure electrically evoked specific force, fatigability, relaxation, calcium handling and myofiber injury together with target engagement. Include vehicle, exact BLU3797, genetic PIK3C2B reduction and MTM1 restoration as distinct controls. Normalize force to cross-sectional area and report total force as well, because improved bulk could masquerade as improved specific function.

Prespecify a developmental go criterion such as a reproducible improvement in the force deficit across all three donor backgrounds, a coherent exposure–response, and no corresponding pathological firing in neuronal controls at a substantial exposure margin. A numerical effect-size threshold should be set after a blinded model-qualification run establishes dynamic range; assigning a universal 20% threshold now would be false precision.

**Stage 3 — independent in vivo confirmation only after Stage 2 passes.** In an established symptomatic model, make direct diaphragm or other respiratory-muscle force the primary physiological endpoint, with ventilation measures, locomotion and survival supporting it. Randomize by baseline disease severity and analyze every randomized animal. Add unbound muscle and brain concentrations and EEG rather than relying on plasma drug or gross behavior. A second, slower-onset model would distinguish a broadly reversible muscle process from a narrow weaning-age rescue.

**Planning cost, not quotations:** approximately **$150,000–250,000** for the identity/exposure and qualified human-function package over roughly four to six months. This assumes an existing muscle-engineering platform, three donor backgrounds, outsourced analytical/DMPK work and ordinary research-grade compound. A subsequent independent animal/respiratory-force package could add **$180,000–300,000**. These ranges exclude licensing, GLP toxicology, GMP manufacture and a clinical trial. The first tranche should stop early if there is no human force phenotype or no usable neural/muscle exposure separation.

### Public program position

The discovery group is Blueprint Medicines, now a Sanofi company. Its publicly listed clinical trials concentrate on mastocytosis; this search did not locate a named BLU3797 clinical program. [S5] That is evidence of public visibility, not proof the asset is abandoned, available for license, or patent-free. The material and experiment are concrete enough to rank now. A later transaction would be a distinct decision after a useful proof and a claim-specific rights review.

## 3. Priority two: CPTX as a transient spinal synaptic bridge

### Evidence and exact construct

CPTX joins a cerebellin-1 neurexin-binding segment, trimerization element and neuronal-pentraxin-1 AMPA-binding segment. The 2020 study found improved mouse SCI locomotion after local protein administration; delayed treatment at one week followed hemisection, whereas the contusion experiment used immediate dosing. Functional groups had nine mice. The delayed cohort was selected for a specific baseline BMS score, which limits generalization. Histological quantification used 16 slices from eight mice, not 16 independent animals. Protein was undetectable at seven days while functional follow-up continued to six weeks. ChABC and Cbln1 provided relevant comparators. [S6]

US20230192810A1 identifies the exact fusion protein in SEQ ID NO. 2 and its nucleic acid in SEQ ID NO. 1. Its listed original assignees are Keio/Aichi Medical University, with Keio/Fujita Health University currently displayed. Patent status and displayed assignee fields require formal verification before a commercial conclusion. [S7]

A 2024 follow-on study used **CPTX-expressing human iPSC neural progenitors** in rats and reported functional integration. It is additional support for the organizer concept, but is a cell therapy and does not establish dose, safety or manufacture of injected recombinant CPTX. [S8]

### Development interpretation

This is unusually close to a molecular prosthetic: the component supplies a missing physical connection between surfaces that remain present. The useful initial indication is therefore a lesion with spared, recruitable circuitry. The proposal is not to claim axonal regeneration across a complete chronic transection.

The brief exposure is attractive. In principle, a local pulse could induce a connection that normal activity and rehabilitation maintain. That would reduce the need for sustained gene expression. It also creates a testable development hypothesis: transient organization plus task-specific activity might yield a more controllable product than indefinite synapse-promoting expression.

That same mechanism creates the main risk. More excitatory connectivity is not automatically better circuitry. A development proof must show coordinated useful activity rather than merely more synaptic puncta, and look for pain, spasticity, autonomic disturbance and inappropriate network synchrony.

### Obtainability and proposed new contribution

Recombinant protein production is described in the primary methods, with HEK-based expression and public construct architecture. [S6] The sequence-defined object can be commissioned and compared with the original binding/activity specification. The cited generic Addgene backbone is not itself proof that the complete CPTX plasmid is distributed. A tagged research construct, tag-removed derivative, sustained-release formulation and cell-expressed CPTX must each retain separate identity and evidence fields.

**Proposed new concept:** an exposure-limited local recombinant CPTX pulse paired with a standardized rehabilitation window for **subacute or established incomplete contusion injury**. This is an untested product/regimen hypothesis, not a claim of new chemistry or existing efficacy. The original paper's CPTX–ChABC combination is already prior art; it should be a comparator, not presented as our invention.

### Decisive proof

First qualify two protein batches for oligomerization, dual binding and functional synapse induction, with binding-impaired controls. Then use a blinded contusion study in which treatment begins after stable baseline function has been documented. Stratify rather than selecting only a favorable baseline score. Include vehicle plus rehabilitation, CPTX alone, CPTX plus the same rehabilitation, and an activity/binding control. Measure coordinated locomotion and evoked conduction alongside sensory and autonomic function. Count mice as the experimental unit; repeated steps, synapses and sections are nested measurements.

A compelling result would be a reproducible, clinically meaningful functional gain after the protein has cleared, accompanied by appropriate task-related conduction and no sustained pathological excitation. Failure of delayed contusion rescue, activity-independent generalized excitation, or benefit confined to selected acute injuries would lower the priority substantially.

**Planning cost:** about **$90,000–150,000** for production, analytical qualification and a functional cellular/ex vivo gate, then **$180,000–300,000** for a well-controlled delayed-injury study. An intrathecal route should not inherit efficacy from parenchymal injection; route simplification is a separate distribution experiment. This is a genuine development burden, but a bounded one.

## 4. Other deep checks

### A. Cognate tRNA add-back in dominant aminoacyl-tRNA-synthetase neuropathy

The August 2026 YARS1 paper provides an unusually clean mechanism-specific experiment: neonatal AAV9-U6-tRNATyr rescued motor performance, nerve conduction and motor-axon caliber at six months, whereas tRNAGly and wild-type TyrRS did not. Functional groups had 19–27 mice; the model was homozygous Yars1 E196K, and delivery was neonatal ICV. It demonstrates prevention, not post-onset reversal in heterozygous adult patients. Both intron-containing and intronless cognate constructs were tested. [S9]

The 2025 GARS1 paper strengthens the broader sequestration mechanism and argues against simply assuming dominant-negative enzyme loss. [S10] This weakens the rationale for the historical proposal to neutralize only extracellular mutant GlyRS unless that proposal independently restores relevant intracellular function.

**Development decision:** scientifically high priority; independent new-product differentiation is narrower than it initially appears. XtRNA Bio is publicly developing the platform. Radboud explicitly says post-symptom efficacy is under investigation; NLC publicly describes a €5 million financing objective. [S11, S12] This is a visible fundable venture route, not an access mystery.

**Meaningful new proof:** test the exact cognate cassette after established conduction loss, with adult-relevant delivery and an exposure/processing limit. Keep GARS1 and YARS1 as related programs, not independent corroborations of one exact product. The key functional endpoint is recovery or stabilization of nerve function beyond an untreated natural-history trajectory, not tRNA abundance. Monitor aberrant tRNA processing and global translation. A commercially commissioned AAV and engineered human neuron/Schwann-cell systems are a concrete early route; a donor's permission to provide a unique cell line need not be the first gate.

**Planning cost:** $100,000–180,000 for cassette identity, human cellular functional comparison and basic dose window; a properly powered adult in vivo reversal study adds substantial time and roughly $200,000–400,000. This is a strong existing-program investment/partner benchmark. It is not an unclaimed platform.

### B. Vanillic acid plus CoQ10 for selected primary CoQ deficiency

The 2025 combination paper reports better survival and motor function in Coq9 R239X mice with CoQ10 plus vanillic acid than either alone; the combination survival group had six mice, versus five CoQ10-only, fourteen vanillic-acid-only and twenty untreated mutants. Treatment began at one month using 0.5% CoQ10 and 1% vanillic acid in chow. Human experiments were COQ7-deficient fibroblasts, not human COQ9 neurons. The proposed complementarity is quinone supplementation plus reduced DMQ burden/neuroinflammation, not simple restoration of the missing COQ9 protein. [S13]

This is a plausible inexpensive **proof program**, especially compared with adding a novel mitochondrial transporter to a protein. The components are chemically defined and readily synthesizable. It is not yet an S-tier clinical candidate because genotype, tissue exposure, high dietary dose and the difference between mouse survival and established human neurologic function remain material.

**Meaningful new contribution:** use the optimized existing CoQ10 comparator and test whether vanillic acid adds function in a genetically defined human neural or renal system at measured exposures. The factorial design must include each component. Measure respiration coupled to ATP, stress resistance and cell-type function, not merely total quinone or oxygen consumption. A change in precursor pool can look biochemically favorable while reducing residual native synthesis in a different allele, so genotypes should not be pooled.

**Planning cost:** $75,000–140,000 for a genotype-defined human proof and analytical exposure work. A generic food-additive description is not evidence that gram-scale chronic therapeutic exposure is safe. A new formulation should be earned by a measured exposure problem, rather than added speculatively as the company's entire value proposition.

### C. Piclidenoson/IB-MECA in Lowe syndrome: excellent process benchmark, exposed translational gap

The paper published on 4 November 2026 links A3-receptor/PLC activation to correction of OCRL-related phosphoinositide mislocalization and reports receptor-mediated uptake rescue in patient proximal-tubule cells, OCRL-null kidney organoids and humanized mice. Organoid treatment was **10 µM for seven days**. The kidney organoids were engineered, not multiple independent patient organoid donors. Hundreds of cells or organoids do not substitute for donor-level replication. [S14]

A public sponsor protocol reports steady-state total Cmax **58.14 ng/mL at 4 mg twice daily**, with higher dosing less well tolerated. [S15] Using the public molecular identity and approximate molecular weight 510.3, this is about **0.114 µM**, roughly **88-fold below 10 µM**. This calculation compares total nominal concentrations; it is not a complete unbound kidney PK model. It is a strong reason to demand such a model. The disparity is not eliminated by calling the compound clinically safe.

Can-Fite's 2 November SEC-filed release states that it holds development, commercialization and out-license rights through Telethon, and describes a five-adult, six-month Phase 2 study. [S16] Thus the commercial position is public, while the pharmacological bridge remains an open scientific question.

**Development decision:** do not launch a duplicate program on this evidence. The useful contribution would be an exposure-matched human proximal-tubule proof and a tissue-concentration explanation. If lower clinically plausible exposures restore uptake over longer treatment, the thesis improves. If only suprapharmacological exposure works, the exact oral proposition should be downgraded despite active clinical development. A kidney-targeted successor would be a new chemical project and must not inherit the oral drug's clinical safety.

**Planning cost for the discriminating proof:** $50,000–100,000, assuming an established kidney-cell platform. Renal uptake is more directly functional than endosomal morphology, but improved uptake still does not establish long-term preservation of GFR or neurological benefit.

### Evidence and boundaries for the additional concepts

**NM-N06:** The ADCK4/COQ8B primary study showed renal prevention in podocyte-knockout mice and mitochondrial rescue in human knockout cells with 2,4-DHB. [S24] A 2026 observational Japanese series reported improvement with CoQ10 itself in 12 patients, 11 with COQ8B variants; only seven had available 12-month data. [S25] Therefore the useful new claim is **incremental benefit over an effective existing comparator**, not “a therapy for an untreated disease.” Genotype-specific rescue and barrier function should be tested before developing a new oral product. The COQ6 and COQ7 positions in the synthetic pathway differ; a beneficial precursor in one genotype cannot be generalized by its name.

**NM-N07:** The original CMT1A rat study used a defined soy-phospholipid material, approximately 55% phosphatidylcholine and 20% phosphatidylethanolamine, and reported improved myelin and neuropathic function. [S26] The Göttingen clinical service publicly lists CMT-LIPID as in preparation. [S27] Thus generic lecithin is neither an invented treatment nor an unclaimed clinical field. The independent research opportunity is a composition/exposure/nerve-function product specification. It is lower priority than BLU3797 because a clear proprietary and functional increment has not yet been demonstrated.

**NM-N08:** GCN2iB improved motor behavior and conduction when started at disease onset in Gars-mutant mice; CMAP improvement did not reach significance. [S28] The 2026 specificity study shows that GCN2iB also inhibits ZAK, and prior work identifies activation of GCN2 at low concentrations. [S29] This is a plausible new mechanistic lead that **failed promotion as a clean drug candidate during this same search**. A successor must separate pathway effects and avoid a harmful low-exposure region. The exact research chemical remains useful as a control, but it is not an S-tier asset.

**NM-N09:** Affinity removal itself has primary experimental precedent in the patient-CSF/artery work. [S21] Our proposed contribution is the closed, pressure-controlled extracorporeal device specification and a quantitatively validated removal capacity, not discovery of haptoglobin binding. Repeated samples from one patient's drain are not independent patients. Start with a paired pre/post assay across approximately 8–12 independent patient samples, include a blank cartridge and soluble-Hp comparator, and model rebound from continuing erythrolysis. An in vitro cartridge cannot establish safe intracranial circulation; that is a later engineering and animal question.

**NM-N10:** The peer-reviewed March 2026 Cell study adds small- and large-mammal evidence to the earlier screen and reports six individually treated Leigh patients. [S30] These are uncontrolled observations. A useful development proposal must prespecify genotype, baseline crisis rate, blinded motor assessment and treatment exposure. Sildenafil is a credible repurposing research program; it is not automatically a differentiated commercial product. A crossover design would be inappropriate if effects have long carryover or crises are too infrequent, so the endpoint and natural history must determine design.

## 7. How to spend the next research effort

1. **Independent challenge of BLU3797.** Inspect the supporting data and compare the result against PIK3C2B human genetics. Decide whether the human-force and neural-window package could change a serious financing decision. The question is not whether another mouse figure could be generated.
2. **CPTX replication feasibility.** Verify the exact fusion/oligomer and specify a delayed contusion plus activity study with a real circuit-function readout. Decide whether a practical delivery route can reach spared gray matter without uncontrolled spread.
3. **Keep a small inexpensive metabolic track.** Use the CoQ combination's factorial proof to test whether a readily obtainable two-component product can produce a functional increment. It offers a cheap negative answer if the human/PK bridge fails.
4. **Treat visible corporate programs as benchmarks or investment options.** XtRNA and Can-Fite provide public evidence of active development, not a reason to demand emails or pretend there is no route. Their existence changes what a new project needs to contribute.

A project earns the highest tier when the biological thesis, exact test article, exposure window and next experiment fit together so well that the result would be valuable regardless of whether it is positive or negative. In this lane, **BLU3797 is closest to that standard for a funded proof**. The most important uncertainty is the human-muscle/neural therapeutic window. CPTX remains a compelling recovered alternative, with a harder delivery and circuit-safety problem. Neither should be promoted by suppressing those decisive questions.
