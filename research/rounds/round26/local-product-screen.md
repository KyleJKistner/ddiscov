# Round 26 — new local and bounded-product screen

**Decision as of 8 November 2026 (America/Los_Angeles):** retain four new exact-use records. Topical erlotinib for **KRT16 p.Arg127Cys hereditary palmoplantar keratoderma** is this lane's first conditional information opportunity. Ophthalmic dornase alfa is a clinical-evidence reserve constrained by an announced exclusive license and an inconclusive symptom comparison. Amlexanox for selected COL7A1 nonsense RDEB remains a preclinical reserve. Timolol for diabetic foot ulcers is a hold pending reconciliation of the controlled trial reports. None is an S-tier promotion, a priced development commitment or an authorization to spend.

This is a new-candidate screen against frozen commit `2ac9dafe2c45aa7d1baf1499a440fde013cece28`, not another reassessment of the existing portfolio. It follows the [selection policy](../../../decisions/selection-policy.md). The [ledger](local-candidate-ledger.json) contains the exact uses, denominators, decisions and rejected scouts; the [source register](local-sources.json) and [retrieval log](local-retrieval-log.json) preserve access scope and unsuccessful routes.

**A new lead can displace an incumbent's next information task.** Assume equal all-in cost and time, obtainable evidence, an adequate starting article and reusable output or economic rights. Under those assumptions, erlotinib's existing blinded human pain comparison makes a bounded clinical-proof-definition and product-feasibility package preferable to an APR, RM or BLU qualification-only substitute that cannot yet answer useful function. That preference could justify placing erlotinib ahead of those narrower information scopes. It does not establish superiority to their original, appropriately scoped useful-function opportunities. No current comparable quote exists on either side; historical incumbent envelopes are not current offers.

The proposition is narrower than “local delivery is cheap.” Erlotinib still needs a credible product and exposure interpretation, a useful comparison on appropriate background care, a feasible evidence route and a retained contribution. A new formulation is not mandatory if the existing presentation is adequate; conversely, a generic ingredient does not establish a transferable clinical product. A package that only repeats the two-patient story or yields nonexclusive prescribing advice has much less private investment value. Dornase has stronger randomized ocular-sign evidence but an already announced license and uncertain symptom benefit; timolol's controlled evidence is less persuasive for the intended healing/recurrence claim. The existing source passes need not be reopened merely to preserve an old queue.

Cash or waiting remains the uncommitted alternative. No numeric EV, probability, price, all-in development cost or time-to-market is invented. An actual comparison must include the initial deliverable, later clinical/product costs, financing still needed, delay and what value the user could retain. Scientific usefulness, a differentiated therapeutic and a financeable company are separate judgments.

## 1. Erlotinib / KRT16 p.Arg127Cys palmoplantar keratoderma

### What the primary report actually supports

The full two-page JAMA Dermatology letter describes a mother and son with **KRT16 c.379C>T, p.Arg127Cys**. These are **two people in one pedigree**, not independent genotype replications. The initial lower-strength topical comparison in the mother gave little improvement. The later erlotinib ointment plus keratolytic comparison showed greater clinical and pain improvement than vehicle plus the same co-treatment. The mother reported pain falling from 8–10 to 0–3 in the treated foot. Later treatment of both feet was uncontrolled and accompanied by being pain-free and able to walk freely and swim. The son showed more modest improvement during his blinded comparison, with poor keratolytic adherence, followed by improvement in an uncontrolled extension. No adverse effects were reported during the described follow-up, which does not establish uncommon-event or systemic-exposure safety. [R26-L01](https://www.pachyonychia.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/LeeG2022Topical-Erlotinib.-pdf.pdf)

The useful claim is **ongoing reduction in painful fissuring and improved mobility**, not genetic correction, permanent repair or a drug-free cure. The co-treatment and formulation are attached to the result. Neither separate feet nor successive phases create extra patients, and the uncontrolled extension cannot be treated as a randomized mobility endpoint. The paper establishes an extemporaneously compounded starting presentation; it does not establish a current physical lot, transferable stability package, manufacturing qualification or quote. [R26-L01](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamadermatology/fullarticle/2787624)

A separate full-text report described two other patients and found topical erlotinib cream inconclusive in one and without significant improvement in the other. Oral EGFR-inhibitor benefits and adverse effects in that report belong to a different route. The topical formulation, concentration and genotype context differ from the KRT16 p.Arg127Cys family. This is consequential contrary evidence about delivery and generality, not proof that the exact combination failed or evidence of four topical responders. [R26-L02](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9059/10/4/841)

### Current contribution and next decision

Sol-Gel's August 2026 issuer release says it continues to advance topical EGFR-inhibitor program SGT-210. It does not identify an available license for this family's compounded article or report a new exact-use controlled result. The field is therefore not assumed vacant. Earlier repo uses of erlotinib for Darier disease, TRPV3-Olmsted syndrome and FAP are separate exact uses, not fresh clinical evidence for this one. [R26-L03](https://ir.sol-gel.com/news-releases/news-release-details/sol-gel-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results-and); R26-L00.

The next bounded decision is whether a **defined clinical-development proof** can be specified with an adequate article, a practical vehicle/keratolytic and care comparison, clinically meaningful pain or mobility outcomes, interpretable exposure/safety and a usable contribution. An existing human signal does not require an automatic return to an animal rescue experiment. Article or exposure qualification may be informative, but its price must not be labeled the price of establishing clinical benefit.

Advance to comparing an actual scoped opportunity if those elements and output rights can be made concrete. Hold or rescope if the article cannot support a useful clinical comparison, the safety or adherence burden offsets the intended benefit, or the only result available is an unowned replication of off-label care. A new primary controlled result, usable product evidence or concrete rights/access fact could reopen the source pass; another generic EGFR rationale cannot.

## 2. Ophthalmic dornase alfa / severe tear-deficient dry eye

The full phase I/II pilot paper concerns **0.1% recombinant DNase I**, based on the existing dornase alfa product, used ophthalmically. It is not evidence for Neutrolis's later proprietary L304 article. Forty-seven participants were randomized (25 DNase, 22 vehicle), and 41 completed (21 and 20). The population included Sjögren, non-Sjögren tear-deficient disease and ocular graft-versus-host disease; subgroup counts do not establish subgroup efficacy. Two eyes and repeated measurements do not create additional independent people. [R26-L04](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6504128/)

At eight weeks, corneal staining and mucoid-strand comparisons favored DNase. The between-arm OSDI symptom-change comparison was **−20.75 versus −8.43, p=0.078**. A significant within-DNase symptom change is not a significant controlled symptom result. There was no corresponding general restoration of tear production. The paper's safety table uses 25 DNase and 21 vehicle recipients, distinct from the 47 randomized and 41 completers; the short observation does not settle rare or long-term ocular risks. Attrition and multiple exploratory outcomes limit confidence. [R26-L04](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6504128/#i2164-2591-8-3-10-t07)

The current official registry lists **corneal staining and OSDI as primary outcomes**, both at eight weeks. It reports actual enrollment 47, completion on **11 October 2017**, results first posted **5 November 2019**, and last update **18 January 2020**. Its status is **terminated**, with the sponsor's stated reason being cessation of sponsorship because of slow accrual. That is an execution and timing warning; it is not a stated safety or efficacy termination. A terminated registry record can still contain the published pilot's outcomes, and it is not a new replication. [R26-L06](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02193490)

Neutrolis announced an **exclusive University of Illinois Chicago license for topical ocular DNase use** in December 2024 and described its proprietary L304 program. The announcement is primary issuer evidence of a claimed license, not the agreement, a current territorial claim analysis or offered terms. It also mentions other clinical work that was not independently audited here; no additional people or efficacy outcomes are pooled. [R26-L05](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/neutrolis-licenses-university-illinois-chicago-130000008.html)

This is a reserve for a **partnership, usable-data or differentiated-product opportunity**. Its smallest valuable next information package would reconcile the symptom result, endpoint status and phenotype, then establish a distinct obtainable contribution in light of the announced license. Existing human exposure makes a clinical/product qualification question plausible without a mandatory model reset. It does not justify simply copying an assumed unencumbered eye-drop product. No such license, quote or partnership was sought.

## 3. Amlexanox / selected COL7A1 nonsense RDEB

The full 2017 primary paper studied patient-derived keratinocytes/fibroblasts and organotypic skin. Material came from **14 patients and represented 12 premature-stop mutations**; the reported **8 of 12** refers to responsive mutation contexts, not clinical responders. Primary, immortalized and carcinoma-derived backgrounds are not interchangeable independent treatments. Full-length type VII collagen expression, secretion and dermal–epidermal junction localization support biological plausibility. They do not demonstrate adequate mechanical anchoring, reduced blistering or wound healing in treated people. Nonresponding alleles and background dependence remain material limitations. [R26-L07](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022202X17315506)

An identifiable trial, **CTRI/2021/04/033024**, is a remaining clinical-information gap. A secondary mirror last updated 20 April 2021 describes a planned 15-person, open-label study and says not yet recruiting; that is neither a current primary status check nor 15 treated people. A search-index snippet from the EB Research Network gave a different closed/terminated label, but the retrieved page did not expose the corresponding row. No primary CTRI body or published human outcomes were recovered in the bounded pass. The status conflict remains unresolved instead of being turned into a negative efficacy result. [R26-L08](https://trial.medpath.com/clinical-trial/88abd748b1f68a60/ctri/2021/04/033024-amlexanox-wound-healing-collagen7-dystrophic-epidermolysis-bullosa); [R26-L09](https://www.eb-researchnetwork.org/clinical-translation/clinical-trials-database/).

Retain a genotype-specific reserve. First resolve the actual clinical program's status and usable results if obtainable through authorized public access; a new project should not duplicate unknown existing work. If that yields no clinical evidence, the relevant next proof is whether the exact article restores useful skin function in a qualified disease context, with a practical comparator and a viable local product. Protein localization alone must not release clinical-development funding. Higher-concentration cell-growth effects do not establish a clinical therapeutic window; historical oral-ulcer use does not qualify a skin product. No operational protocol is proposed.

Novelty is limited: the repo already screened **amlexanox/PAX6 nonsense aniridia**, and external Screen L includes “amlexanox” in a root scout list without use detail. That does not prove prior RDEB coverage, but it leaves prior-scout uncertainty visible. This is a new exact-use review within the inspected corpus, not discovery of a new molecule. R26-L00.

## 4. Timolol / diabetic foot-ulcer closure and healed-barrier integrity

The official BAART-DFU record reports **48 randomized participants**, 21 timolol and 27 placebo, both with standard care. Its primary outcome is **complete closure at 12 weeks: 8/21 versus 7/27**. The record's later time-to-closure window is 31 weeks. Results were first posted **25 April 2025**, with the last update **8 May 2025**. The registry, conference report and later barrier paper belong to this program, not three independent clinical trials. [R26-L11](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03282981)

The 2025 conference report describes its primary endpoint as week 14, reports the early result at week 15, and uses week 30 for later follow-up. These **12 versus 14/15** and **31 versus 30** week differences remain explicit. It also prints 7/27 as 33%, which does not match the stated count. Its early comparison is nonsignificant (p=0.77); later unadjusted and adjusted time-to-event results differ. They cannot be exchanged to claim a clearly positive prespecified primary trial. The report's 108 initially enrolled/screened people do not become 108 randomized recipients. [R26-L12](https://www.hmpgloballearningnetwork.com/node/337711)

The 2025 full paper selects **20 of 28 healers**, ten per treatment group, for healed-site barrier measurements. This post-randomization selection can bias causal interpretation. Five repeat readings at a site, serial visits and hundreds of measurement observations do not turn 20 people into hundreds of independent participants. Lower transepidermal water loss supports a barrier-measure signal; **there was no significant recurrence difference over one year**. The mostly older male cohort and varying follow-up denominators limit generality. The repository cover describes an earlier conference item, while the embedded full article carries DOI **10.1159/000545357**; these are not additional cohorts. [R26-L10](https://escholarship.org/content/qt85h0f5w9/qt85h0f5w9.pdf)

Hold paid promotion for healing or recurrence prevention. Reconcile the primary endpoint, timing, analysis population and missing observations before treating adjusted time-to-event or post-hoc barrier findings as a new development thesis. The study leaves uncertainty, rather than proving zero biological activity; a further mechanistic assay would not resolve the central controlled-clinical discrepancy. No formulation, price or commercial exclusivity advantage was established.

## Rejected and already-covered scouts

- **Glafenine/SLC4A11 corneal endothelial disease:** cell-based trafficking and water-flux rescue in transfected systems, not patient corneal pump function or vision. The article itself discusses serious historical systemic toxicity. A usable ocular article and safety window remain a product-development project, so it is not retained as a lower-cost ready proof. [R26-L13](https://iovs.arvojournals.org/article.aspx?articleid=2474986)
- **Pirfenidone gel / pediatric burn hypertrophic scars:** a 2012 abstract describes 33 treated children and 30 pressure-therapy controls with scar-score improvement. Full methods were not recovered; randomization, blinding and functional mobility benefit are not assumed. Unpaywall supplied no indexed OA location, and a current brand-page retrieval was unsuitable to verify product availability. Prior external reports screened pirfenidone for urethral stricture, a distinct use. This limited package does not beat the retained local leads. [R26-L14](https://doi.org/10.1097/SAP.0b013e31821b6d08); R26-L16.
- **Tideglusib / pulp capping:** the full 2025 comparison used 56 teeth in only two dogs; teeth do not supply 56 independent animals. Against MTA, the reported hard-tissue comparison was nonsignificant and inflammatory/structural findings were unfavorable. The small clustered study does not establish universal target failure, but it weakens a ready low-cost superiority thesis. Earlier external reports encountered the compound for DM1, not this exact dental use. [R26-L15](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12903-025-06546-6)
- Prior exact uses—losartan/corneal fibrosis, RGN-259/corneal repair, lufepirsen/persistent corneal epithelial defects, MG53/cornea, alphaCT1/chronic wounds, and KLK5/7/Netherton—were excluded without reopening their screens. R26-L00.

## Access and novelty boundaries

Firecrawl supplied the principal discovery and primary-text retrieval route; paper-search/read tools were also tried. Unpaywall was used for DOI access gaps. For the erlotinib letter, timolol paper and pirfenidone paper, the queried records reported closed status with no best OA location and no OA locations. The first two nevertheless had readable lawful public copies at the PC Project and University of California repository. Unpaywall is location metadata, not proof that no legal copy exists; no authentication or paywall bypass was used. Publisher gateways, abstract-only access, empty paper passages and unreliable page contents remain labeled in the source register.

The bounded newness audit covered all **424 baseline files** and complete indexed text of **13 recovered external reports**. The baseline extraction had no read errors. **Screens N–Q and any other uninspected historical work remain unresolved.** Known molecules were not excluded merely because they had a different prior use; no claim of global novelty, patentability or FTO is made. Current rights statements remain separate from available commercial terms. No people were contacted and no purchases, recruitment, experiments, simulations or material receipt occurred.
