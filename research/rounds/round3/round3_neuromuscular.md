# Round 3: neuromuscular and metabolic candidate decisions

> **Historical review snapshot.** Later findings and rankings are reconciled in the [current shortlist](../../../decisions/current-shortlist.md) and [round 4 review](../round4/README.md). Statements about activity or access describe this review period.

**Evidence cut: 4 November 2026.** This is a delta review of the prior portfolio, plus nine additional named candidate screens. Research only: no outreach, purchases, experiments or remote repository changes. The companion JSON contains the structured evidence and source registry.

## Decision

**Keep BLU3797 as the first neuromuscular proof investment and add two serious new candidates: exact 8015-P2 for MFN2-CMT2A, and human translation of Pip8b2-conjugated DNM2 exon-skipping PMO.** Both new directions have useful function after established disease in animals. Neither has established the human functional and exposure window needed for an S-tier designation.

The most useful progress on BLU is practical: there are publicly specified alternative MTM1 patient-cell starting materials and a public target-engagement construct. That reduces dependence on one supplier model or an investigator's discretionary access. It does not add a human drug-response result. Equally useful, scrutiny narrowed overly broad claims about a complete synthetic route and a readily transferable assay.

A third, lower-priority functional reserve is topical XIB4035. It comes from older post-onset work, not this year's publication cycle. Six other candidates were retained as explicit screens with hold, deprioritize or benchmark decisions. Their presence in the ledger is evidence of screening, not an attempt to fill a shortlist.

## 1. BLU3797: what actually changed

### A broader, defined human-model route

Coriell **GM06813** is a cataloged MTM1 fibroblast line with an exon-8 frameshift; **GM24509** is a different cataloged genotype, **c.1180G>C, p.D394H**. The Sarepta/Murdoch patent explicitly describes reprogramming these fibroblasts at iXCell and generating immature myotubes. Its cell experiments measure DNM2 suppression, not force. These sources establish starting materials and a disclosed route, not ready qualified muscle tissues or validated independent drug responders. [NM-S04–06]

This complements the previously identified Curi **p.L463S** force model. A defensible initial program can therefore specify residual-function and frameshift backgrounds without making an investigator email the scientific gate. The work still includes line authentication, mature tissue formation, stable deficit qualification and a genetic correction/restoration control. The catalog genotypes must remain attached to results; three backgrounds cannot be replaced by three clones of one line. [NM-S35]

The key proposed decision is whether the model is rescuable **after** a stable deficit. If genetic restoration cannot recover force, a negative BLU result would not cleanly falsify the target. If restoration works but engaged PIK3C2B reduction does not, the target thesis becomes substantially weaker.

### Chemistry, engagement and selectivity are more specific

The BLU article discloses the S stereoisomer, analytical characterization and key final synthetic steps from an advanced intermediate. It does not, in the material inspected, establish an independently reproduced end-to-end route. Its live-cell panel reports 68.2 nM PIK3C2B potency but omits cellular PIK3C2A and PIK3CB curves. The published engagement assay uses an in-house tracer. No unbound muscle/brain exposure or exposure-linked chronic neural margin was located. [NM-S01–02]

Addgene now publicly lists **NanoLuc-PIK3C2B vector 238834**. This supplies a defined construct, not the missing tracer or a validated transfer of the original assay. It changes the implementation problem from an unspecified proprietary assay to a tractable public-component assay-development task. [NM-S03]

The proposed first release should establish authenticated material, assay performance and a common free-concentration scale before expanding efficacy. Include the missing cellular selectivity comparisons because a single-concentration binding panel cannot establish the functional selectivity needed at sustained muscle engagement. If the assay cannot be rebuilt with an independently characterized tracer, use a validated orthogonal engagement/target-perturbation approach and explicitly avoid claiming numerical equivalence to the original NanoBRET values.

Do not use PITCOIN chemistry as though it were a matched C2β inhibitor: its relevant selectivity is centered on C2α. PI-701 also lacks the necessary established selectivity to serve as a clean orthogonal C2β control. Genetic PIK3C2B reduction is the stronger initial control. [NM-S08–09]

### Genetic neural risk is a constraint, not a deterministic prediction

The primary epilepsy study explicitly discusses incomplete penetrance, an unaffected transmitting father and a tested variant retaining kinase activity. It also supports neuronal consequences of heterozygous loss. Those observations justify a functional neural exposure comparison; they do not provide a drug concentration threshold or make seizures inevitable with reversible inhibition. [NM-S07]

**Advance exact BLU** only after reproducible post-deficit human force and fatigue benefit, engagement concordance and a usable neural/hepatic separation. **Advance the target but replace the compound** only if orthogonal inhibition works while BLU fails on its own distribution or chemistry. A peripheral successor would be a separately scored project. **Stop** if qualified, MTM1-restorable tissue repeatedly fails despite target engagement, or if useful inhibition and relevant injury cannot be separated.

This review increases operational confidence. It does not justify raising BLU's biological maturity beyond the prior bounded-proof decision.

## 2. 8015-P2: the best additional neuromuscular lead

### The exact-article chain matters

The 2020 work's four-patient neuronal experiments used **Chimera C**, whereas its animal reversal used **MiM111**. The 2024 human T105M neuronal experiments used the **8015 mixture**. Exact **8015-P2** was tested in older knock-in mice, and then in older knock-in rats in 2026. Human data from the family cannot be relabeled exact-P2 evidence. [NM-S11–12]

The rat paper reports randomization and blinded testing. Its dose cohorts contain four rats per dose and seven WT references; the highest-dose cohort stopped once values appeared normal. An acute mitochondrial-motility experiment used a substantially higher dose than the chronic functional regimen. Treat the endpoint-specific denominators and exposure differences as part of the evidence, not footnotes. [NM-S10]

There is real reason to pursue this: a repair hypothesis with established disease and organ-level function is more valuable than another image-based mitochondrial screen. There is also a concentrated failure mode: apparently broad replication may mostly represent one allele and one research network. The next experiment should deliberately reduce that concentration.

### Public material and active development

The 2024 article provides synthesis/chiral-separation information defining P2. A custom-chemistry brief must attach the correct chiral reference; generic piperine or an unspecified 8015 mixture is not acceptable. The mixture's protein-binding and brain-partition numbers cannot be assigned to P2. P2's enterohepatic behavior is a reason to measure human-relevant free exposure rather than simply celebrate a longer mouse profile. [NM-S11]

An FDA orphan listing and existing mitofusin patent families show an active external development context. The orphan listing does not identify P2 as a cleared clinical candidate, and a ceased PCT listing does not erase a granted national family member. This is compatible with independent scientific proof, but it defeats a story that the family is an unowned abandoned opportunity. [NM-S13–14]

### Deciding proof, proposed rather than already demonstrated

Fund a staged **exact-P2 human neuromuscular function** study. Establish a reproducible deficit before exposure. Use T105M plus another pathogenic MFN2 background, with genetic correction controls. Compare P2 with the parent mixture, less-active P1 and vehicle. Prioritize evoked motor-neuron-to-muscle transmission, force, fatigue and direct-muscle-stimulated force. This separates improved axonal/NMJ function from a direct muscle effect or nonspecific growth.

Track mitochondrial transport and morphology as mechanistic readouts, not the final decision. Measure free drug and compare pulse versus sustained exposure, because the family papers do not establish which profile is necessary in human tissue. Add healthy neuronal and cardiac functional measurements at the same free exposures; increased fusion by itself is not proof that mitochondrial quality control remains healthy.

A convincing result would be a material ranking change: exact P2 restores useful human function after onset, across more than one variant/background, at a coherent exposure. A T105M-restricted response would still be useful but supports a narrower development claim. Stop or redesign if only the mixture works, only morphology changes, or active exposure compromises healthy function. An independent fixed-design animal replication becomes valuable after that question is answered; repeating the original figure design first would resolve less.

## 3. DNM2 PPMO: function survives a treatment-free interval, but translation is a new product choice

The 2025 Brain study specifies a murine exon-6 PMO and Pip8b2 conjugate. Late treatment produced a specific-force increase from 11.2 to 15.6 mN/mg, assessed one month after the last dose; the late disease arms had five control and four treated animals. Fibre size did not improve. The mechanism should be described as exon skipping with reduced DNM2 protein: total transcript was not substantially reduced in vivo, so demonstrated nonsense-mediated decay is the wrong claim. [NM-S15]

This is the second new lead worth a focused translation package. Force improvement with no late fibre-size correction is particularly useful to the question of muscle quality versus simply enlarging tissue. However, the precise human oligo is a development choice, not an automatic substitution of human letters into the mouse sequence.

The separate 2025 patent discloses human-targeting sequences and immature patient-myotube knockdown. Its PPMO numbering is local to that document; a patent “PPMO6” must not be assumed to be the Brain mouse PPMO6. Neither the patent nor the mouse force result establishes human contractility rescue with a selected human article. [NM-S06]

The proposed package should first select a sequence from reproducible public disclosure, authenticate the complete conjugate and identify the actual splice product. Check for a persistent truncated protein instead of assuming that a frameshift guarantees transcript destruction. In mature DNM2-mutant human muscle, compare post-deficit force/fatigue with a non-targeting conjugate, unconjugated PMO and orthogonal DNM2 reduction at a matched degree of suppression.

The differentiation question is **muscle-selective, durable useful function at tolerable exposure**. Short mouse findings are insufficient to dismiss the earlier DYN101 clinical tolerability problem, but a different chemistry and tissue distribution may address it. That possibility is testable. Do not expand the claim to MTM1 or BIN1 disease without a separate qualified functional model. Advance only when the human article and force window are established; stop if protein reduction has no functional value or if the required suppression overlaps conjugate/off-muscle toxicity.

## 4. XIB4035: an older functional reserve worth preserving

The 2014 study contains a genuine late-start sensory experiment, not just a prevention arm. It does not establish regeneration of epidermal fibres, and benefit recurred/waned with continued treatment/withdrawal. That makes the initial proposition restoration or maintenance of impaired sensory signaling, with a separate bar for a durable regeneration claim. [NM-S19]

A catalog route exists, but the supplier's product and SDS identifiers conflict. The right response is analytical reconciliation of structure and salt form, not a conclusion that the name proves stock or that an email determines scientific promise. The report and JSON retain both source documents. [NM-S20–22]

A comparatively narrow proof would use a human sensory-neuron/skin system with an established deficit, measure stimulus-evoked signals, require a plausible endogenous ligand context, and document local exposure. RET dependence would help distinguish the claimed mechanism from generic excitability. If protective sensation is impaired or the apparent benefit is merely altered nociceptive threshold, the intended restoration product has failed even if a behavioral number moves favorably.

## 5. Why the other screens did not earn immediate investment

**CKD504:** The human GARS1 study supports an assay/mechanism lead, but neurons were treated through maturation and WT firing increased. Public primary materials did not resolve the exact code-to-structure mapping. This fails the immediate exact-article gate; a sponsor reply should not be the sole reason to rank it highly. Preserve it as a comparator and revisit only after an independent material definition and mature functional question exist. [NM-S16–18]

**NV354:** The newer complex-I paper has functional promise but is prevention-dominant and does not extend lifespan. A post-onset human energy-demand test could be valuable; the existing evidence does not support promoting it over the two stronger reversal candidates. [NM-S23]

**Surlorian:** Its six-person phase1 study includes seven dosing episodes because one participant reenrolled. An active randomized phase2 began dosing in October2026. The program is an important benchmark, not an overlooked independent asset simply because it was absent from this inventory. [NM-S24–25]

**Ignaseclant:** The sponsor's 81-person CMT trial missed its primary endpoint despite selected secondary positives. Retain that negative when evaluating new CMT functional endpoints, while keeping symptomatic transmission enhancement separate from axonal repair. [NM-S26–27]

**Decitabine:** The recessive-RYR1 mouse functional result is worth recording. The chronic-use thesis still needs a meaningful separation from the compound's marrow and DNA liabilities. Approval for oncology does not supply that separation; neither does a low nominal mouse dose. [NM-S28–30]

**ARD-1676:** Public chemistry and a catalog route are stronger than for CKD504. The SBMA work nevertheless stops at target engagement and transcriptional correction rather than useful function. This is a good example of material availability being necessary but insufficient. [NM-S31–33]

## Portfolio implication

The revised process is producing better decisions because it binds a candidate to its exact material, disease state, function and deciding proof. It also makes rejection informative. CKD504 exposes an identity problem; NV354 exposes a timing problem; ARD-1676 exposes a functional-evidence gap; the clinical benchmarks expose differentiation requirements.

The next useful milestone is not another larger list of positive mechanisms. It is an independently reproducible human-function/exposure result for BLU or P2, or successful human translation of the DNM2 conjugate. These have explicit ways to fail and produce transferable knowledge if they do. None earns S-tier by literature volume, recency, patent status or convenience of supply.
