# Independent challenge: FXT-001 / UAMC-3203 in donor-organ perfusion

> **Historical review snapshot.** Later findings and rankings are reconciled in the [current shortlist](../../../decisions/current-shortlist.md) and [round 4 review](../round4/README.md). Statements about activity or access describe this review period.

**Reviewer:** neuromuscular workstream, independent of the organ recommendation. **Evidence cut:** 4 November 2026. Primary abstract, publisher summary, investigator institutional sources and exact supplier forms checked. The large author PDF was not retried, and its full methods are not represented as inspected.

## Decision

**A credible organ-preservation lead, but the reviewed public evidence does not establish a delayed-repair or post-transplant benefit.** An early-perfusion preservation product can be valuable without reversing pre-existing injury. The immediate task is to decide whether a new experiment adds a differentiated product claim beyond an active external program, not to discard preservation because it is prevention.

## What the primary source supports

The Cell paper reports an early, transient lipid-peroxidation signal in human liver transplantation; protection in porcine liver/lung perfusion; and preserved viability in split-perfused declined human lungs while controls deteriorate. The abstract separately identifies FXT-002/003 as optimized analogues. It supplies no human-donor count, exact perfusate formulation, delayed-start comparison or recipient outcome. Those details cannot be reconstructed from the title or transferred from the analogue experiments. [F1]

The investigator university describes reduced lung fluid accumulation and retained elasticity, and an observational analysis of 116 liver recipients. Those recipients are not 116 drug-treated transplant patients. The institution's broad promotional statement that FXT-001 is suitable for clinical use is not regulatory clearance or demonstrated clinical safety. [F2]

**Decision-changing boundary:** “treated declined lungs deteriorated less during perfusion” and “drug restored a deteriorating lung after injury was established” require different experimental timelines. Only the former is established by the retrieved abstract. If the organ team has primary full-method timing, preserve that specific source; this check does not independently verify it. The relevant human biological n remains unknown in this independent abstract-only assessment.

## Exact article and active development

MCE explicitly distinguishes **UAMC-3203/FXT-001 free base, HY-112909, CAS2271358-64-4**, from **hydrochloride, HY-112909A, CAS2271358-65-5**. This supports a public research-material route but does not identify which form, vehicle or lot produced the human-organ result. Any proposed perfusate must specify active-moiety equivalents, salt, vehicle, solubility and circuit stability. A catalog purity claim is not a received-lot authentication result. [F3–F4]

The university's current spin-off page identifies **FiriX Therapeutics** and names **FXT-001 as its lead**. The article also discloses founders and related patents. It would therefore be wrong to pitch this as an inactive, unowned opportunity based on catalog access or the absence of a clinical-trial result. These facts do not prevent an independent scientific test; they increase the need for an incremental proposition. [F1, F5]

## The proof that would change my decision

Two useful questions should be kept separate:

1. **Preservation product:** Does a specified, authenticated FXT-001 formulation preserve donor-organ performance beyond optimized perfusion, with durable benefit after drug washout and acceptable residual recipient exposure? This should include the strongest relevant active comparator, not simply worsening vehicle lungs.
2. **Repair product:** After a prespecified physiological injury threshold is documented, does delayed FXT-001 addition reverse dysfunction? It needs a delayed-start arm and recovery relative to its own injured baseline. Failure here would narrow the claim to preservation rather than automatically invalidate an early-treatment product.

For either, organ-level gas exchange, compliance, vascular behavior and edema/weight should be interpreted together. A favorable oxidative marker alone is insufficient. Keep donor-pair counts separate from repeated perfusion measurements and do not assign optimized-analogue PK or safety to FXT-001. A later transplantation experiment would address durability under a recipient environment; ex vivo stability does not answer that question.

**Advance** a bounded, differentiated formulation/timing/washout proof if the full primary methods and exact article can be specified. **Hold S-tier promotion** while post-implant translation, exact test form and the independent value proposition remain unresolved. **Stop a duplicate program pitch** if it only repeats the already public early-perfusion result. None of these scientific decisions requires outreach.
