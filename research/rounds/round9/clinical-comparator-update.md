# Round 9 clinical comparator update

Evidence cut: **4 November 2026**. Comparator baseline: recovered Round 8, commit `a7f10cd8b84bc2ffe6a4cf0160f29e9ab443dad3`, particularly `research/rounds/round8/clinical_challenge.md` and the Round 7 clinical/product dossiers. Public-source research only; no contact, purchase, experiment, or clinical action.

## Decision

**Keep enasidenib as an organ-specific, finite development priority, with vorasidenib a required neurological comparator. The public record does not identify which drug provides the better tolerable unbound exposure at IDH2-R140Q in the normal infant brain.** A newly reviewed regulatory assessment makes a parent-only vorasidenib comparison incomplete: an accumulating active metabolite is missing from the exposure model and has an unresolved brain-distribution bridge. This is a new qualification constraint, not evidence that vorasidenib fails or that enasidenib wins.

Retain the Round 8 enasidenib and HHD allocation recommendations of **15 and 10 of 100 research-planning units**, respectively. No new result here supports an S-tier designation or displacement of BLU3797. These are allocation judgments, not calibrated success probabilities, committed expenditure, or authorization for studies.

## 1. New evidence for this dossier: the vorasidenib metabolite/model gap

The **21 May 2025 TGA AusPAR** reports active metabolite **AGI-69460**, mean molar **predose** metabolite:parent ratio **1.173 at day 1, cycle 10**, and reported human-plasma mean fraction unbound **13.0 ± 1.07%**. Animal metabolite brain penetration was not assessed, the metabolite was excluded from population PK modeling, and that model contained **one observed patient under 18**. These are new to the recovered dossiers. [S1, printed PDF pages 13, 16–18]

Do not treat that ratio as an AUC ratio or sum parent and metabolite concentrations into an R140Q-equivalent exposure. The missing metabolite potency and tissue measurements prevent that calculation. The adolescent observation count does not establish infant PK or safety.

## 2. What the comparator evidence actually identifies

The following facts are **already covered or supporting context**, not additional Round 9 discoveries.

| Question | Evidence and exact denominator | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Does vorasidenib engage the relevant mutation? | FDA cellular 2-HG IC50: **14 ± 1.1 nM**, TF-1 IDH2-R140Q, assay N=3; **7.1 ± 3 nM**, U87MG IDH2-R140Q, N=7. [S2] | Yes, in those assay systems. Nominal assay concentration is not a clinical free-brain threshold. |
| Is there a demonstrated wild-type margin? | FDA review reports time-dependent wild-type IDH1/2 cellular inhibition around **400 nM**. [S2] | This cannot be combined with nominal mutant IC50s and total tissue concentrations to declare a pediatric therapeutic window. |
| Is human brain target engagement demonstrated? | Perioperative study: **49 patients, all with IDH1-R132H nonenhancing glioma**. Vorasidenib 50 mg/day reduced tumor 2-HG **92.6%**, 95% credible interval **76.1–97.6%**, relative to untreated comparison. The 49 are the full study population, not the denominator of this one dose group. [S3] | Strong tumor pharmacodynamic evidence. Different mutation, tissue state, age distribution, and endpoint from normal infant IDH2-R140Q disease. |
| Does total distribution establish infant free exposure? | Current label: parent binding **97%**, brain-tumor:plasma concentration ratio **1.6**, terminal half-life about **10 days**. [S4] | Neither unbound normal-brain partition nor infant exposure is supplied by these measurements. |
| Is enasidenib already clinically bridged? | Round 7 identified five outcome records across three papers, with unresolved independence of the skin-case records. The major severe-cardiomyopathy recovery signal is concentrated in **one child**, with concomitant cardiac care; other cardiac observations have different starting severity. [S5; controlling Round 7 lineage] | Exact disease and organ function give enasidenib a valuable but uncontrolled cardiac anchor. Five records are not five verified independent organ rescues. |

No treatment-attributable neurological effect size is identifiable from the available enasidenib records after separating maturation, seizure control, improved circulation, rehabilitation, and observer expectation. Peripheral D-2-HG reduction does not itself locate target engagement in neurons. Conversely, vorasidenib's glioma target engagement cannot be treated as normal infant IDH2-R140Q clinical benefit.

**Unresolved fields stay null:** infant parent and active-metabolite unbound plasma exposure; normal infant-brain unbound partition; time-matched R140Q target engagement; AGI-69460 exact-R140Q potency under comparable assay conditions; combined parent/metabolite mutant versus wild-type margin; and head-to-head organ-function effects. Missing information is not zero exposure, zero potency, or demonstrated inferiority.

## 3. Formulation, organs, and actual development context

Round 7 already established clinical use and public disclosure of an enasidenib pediatric sprinkle, existing formulation claims, ESMART activity, and a formulation crossover study. A proposal to invent the first pediatric enasidenib formulation would therefore be inaccurate. Actual present supply, a usable infant administration specification, and freedom to operate remain separate unresolved questions. [S6–S8; Round 7 product dossier]

Vorasidenib's approved tablets and glioma development program establish an available adult/adolescent article and substantial oncology experience. They do not supply an infant formulation or a normal-brain D2HGA2 efficacy dataset. The label's hepatic risks and the existing FDA wild-type/cardiac findings remain part of comparator assessment; the metabolite gap does not create a new adverse-event finding. [S2, S4]

The bounded current search did not yield a verified new D2HGA2 trial outcome or comparator trial that changes this conclusion. This is not an assertion that no trial exists. The inaccessible five-week enasidenib report remains metadata-only for outcome purposes; its age in a title is not a newly verified neonatal response. The existing formulation and oncology registry records are not counted as D2HGA2 efficacy trials.

## 4. Ruxolitinib/HHD: no verified new decision-changing delta

The current screen returned the **same** nine-patient multiomics preprint, PMID **42282850**, and the same single-case record, PMID **42222696**, already explicitly recorded in Round 7. The former remains unpooled: regimen, concentration, duration, patient overlap, recurrence, and safety denominators were not newly recovered. A PubMed listing does not turn the nine patients into an independently audited response cohort. Blocked/limited retrieval was not filled using secondary descriptions. [S9, S10; Round 7 clinical dossier]

**KB111/HALITE-1 is also not new:** the prior product dossier already incorporated NCT07717346 and the sponsor's **2 November 2026** report of five enrolled patients, without an efficacy outcome. Repeating earlier planned enrollment or slides would move the evidence backwards. [S11]

No newly verified controlled HHD effect, maintenance denominator, or procedure-comparator result was recovered. Preserve Round 8's useful-control and treatment-burden question. The five exact-strength reports and the nine-patient preprint must not be pooled into a 14-patient responder numerator. The existing Botox RCT's seven patients and the pilot's 26 HHD plus four Darier participants remain separate comparator datasets; nothing in this pass changes those denominators or resolves their limitations.

## 5. Finite next action and stopping rule

Within enasidenib's existing planning allocation, complete **one public-document exposure ledger** for the parent and each human active metabolite of both drugs. Require the assay's exact variant and conditions, nominal versus unbound concentration, species, age, normal versus tumor tissue, sampling time, and exposure statistic. Add a single primary-source check for a newly posted D2HGA2 outcome or publicly specified usable pediatric formulation. This is document analysis, not an instruction to acquire material, contact investigators, treat anyone, or run a study.

If matched free-brain and R140Q metabolite data remain unavailable after that bounded review, close the neurological superiority comparison as **not identifiable from public evidence**. Carry the cardiac clinical-development question forward separately; do not fill the gap with a PBPK point estimate presented as observed exposure, or order experimental work as an automatic next step. HHD remains at its existing comparator/endpoint qualification gate until a new controlled outcome or auditable cohort appears.
