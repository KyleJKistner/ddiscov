# SLIT2/EP300 scar prevention: a useful new lead, not a demonstrated winner

> **Historical review snapshot.** Later findings and rankings are reconciled in the [current shortlist](../../../decisions/current-shortlist.md) and [round 4 review](../round4/README.md). Statements about activity or access describe this review period.

Review date: 3 November 2026 (America/Los_Angeles). Working memo for the lead agent; no outreach or purchases performed. This supplements the HJ-02 diligence memo.

## Decision

Stanford S23-520 merits an availability-first owner inquiry and examination of the complete data package. It does not yet justify replacing an overpromoted A-tier asset with another overpromoted A-tier asset. The public package establishes an interesting scar-prevention pathway in mice and an identifiable institutional counterparty. It does not establish treated adult-human wound efficacy, porcine efficacy, a clinical product, exclusive commercial availability or superiority to verteporfin.

Separate the protein and small-molecule branches in the candidate registry. A defined human SLIT2 construct and a defined CBP/EP300 inhibitor have different manufacturing, safety, patent and delivery questions. Treating them as one candidate obscures the actual development work.

## What the public package supports

Stanford's owner listing identifies the project as preclinical. It describes one local application immediately after mouse wounding, reduced scar-like architecture, regeneration of skin appendages and no delay of closure. The listing is a positive access signal, not owner confirmation of unencumbered exclusive rights. It links published application WO2026015476. Its assertion of “No competing technology” should not be carried into an investment memo: the same page links Stanford's verteporfin scar-prevention technology S19-318.

Source: https://techfinder.stanford.edu/technology/manipulating-slit2-robo2-eid1-ep300-pathway-overcome-fibrosis (PDF https://techfinder.stanford.edu/print/pdf/node/34224).

The primary Cell publication is Chen et al., “Fibroblasts of disparate developmental origins harbor anatomically variant scarring potential,” DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2025.12.014, published in 2026. Its mouse experiments connect anatomic fibroblast origin with a SLIT2–ROBO2–EID1–EP300 axis. Genetic perturbation and drug treatment strengthen the biological argument. Genetic triangulation does not automatically make the administered chemical selective or the protein a ready human product.

Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41576949/ ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0092867425014278 ; https://gurtnerlab.arizona.edu/news/chen-et-al-publishes-paper-cell . Full publisher text was not directly retrievable in this session; indexed primary text and the associated application were inspected. PMC12984043 has a stated 2027 public-availability date.

## Exact product and experiment issues

The patent identifies local I-CBP112 treatment at wounding, and an R&D SLIT2 reagent described as aa26–900. Its human evidence includes expression and immunohistochemistry of face/back skin and scars, with n=3 biological replicates/group in the relevant figure. This is observational human relevance, not drug-treated adult-human wound efficacy. A mouse scATAC experiment pools six mice per condition; the sequenced cells must not be counted as independently randomized animals. The application reports fibroblast-specific Ep300 deletion and recognizes that I-CBP112 inhibits CREBBP as well as EP300. Neither “porcine” nor “tensile” was found in the application inspected. Dose units are inconsistently rendered in the searchable text and should be reconciled against the original protocol before budgeting or replication. The application is published, not a granted exclusionary right.

Source: https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2026015476A1/en (; see paragraphs 0225, 0227, 0234, 0261–0264, 0281–0283 and Figure 27 legend). These limitations describe the inspected public record; they do not prove that additional unpublished studies are absent.

### The protein is not yet defined for human development

R&D product 5444-SL matches the described aa26–900 reagent: CHO-derived **mouse** Slit2 Gln26–Gln900 with a C-terminal 10-His tag. R&D's listed **human** product 8616-SL is HEK293-derived Gln26–Val1118 with a C-terminal 6-His tag. Both are research reagents; the human catalog product is not the same sequence fragment as the mouse reagent. The match to 5444-SL is an inference from supplier and residue range, to be confirmed with the authors.

Sources: https://www.bio-techne.com/p/proteins-enzymes/recombinant-mouse-slit2-aa-26-900-protein-cf_5444-sl (); https://www.rndsystems.com/products/recombinant-human-slit2-aa-26-1118-protein-cf_8616-sl (); https://resources.rndsystems.com/pdfs/datasheets/8616-sl.pdf.

The practical implication is a material bridge experiment, not an automatic rejection. Select a human construct and show its relevant potency and wound phenotype. A vendor page clears neither this bridge nor supply scale, clinical formulation, manufacturing comparability or commercial rights. Obtain an actual lot/quote only after product identity and use are clear.

### The small molecule is a probe with dual target activity

The Structural Genomics Consortium calls I-CBP112 a CREBBP/EP300 bromodomain chemical probe and points to research vendors. The developer page reports limited cellular cytotoxicity testing, not a wound-specific therapeutic safety package. It should not be described as a selective EP300 clinical asset. A broad patent list of other inhibitors also does not transfer clinical experience to the exact probe tested here.

Source: https://www.thesgc.org/chemical-probes/i-cbp112 (), citing Picaud et al. 2015, DOI 10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-15-0236.

## Verteporfin is a substantive comparator

The 2025 Science Translational Medicine study reports one-time immediate treatment in red Duroc pig wounds and a human neonatal foreskin xenograft model in mice. These are useful translational steps, although the xenograft is not an adult clinical result. SLIT2's differentiation must be tested against this existing work rather than inferred from the novelty of its pathway.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39970235/ ; https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/inhibiting-mechanotransduction-prevents-scarring-and-yields-regen/ (/4). DOI 10.1126/scitranslmed.adt6387.

An official EMA CTIS record additionally lists **SCARFREE-001**, a Phase 2 dose-finding/proof-of-concept study of intradermal verteporfin for prevention of surgical scars, sponsored by Odense University Hospital. The indexed official record shows Denmark authorization on 13 May 2026 and recruitment pending; later third-party mirrors show a different recruitment status. Safely describe this as a registered/authorized clinical competitor, without claiming current enrollment or efficacy. ClinicalTrials.gov identifier NCT07488988 was found, but the directly inspected page did not return readable trial detail.

Sources: https://euclinicaltrials.eu/ctis-public/view/2025-525083-14-00?lang=en (); https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07488988 . No outcome results were identified.

## What would make the opportunity financeable?

First, an attainable option/license covering a specific product and field, confirmation of inventor involvement, and complete access to raw biological and safety data. A technology-transfer listing starts this conversation; it cannot finish it.

Second, define the human product and show a reproducible response in relevant human adult wound tissue with an orthogonal measure of tissue quality. The relevant inference is that lower collagen or normal-looking architecture alone does not establish mechanical strength, durable barrier function or acceptable healing under clinical conditions. The initial use should remain prevention at a new wound; immediate dosing evidence does not establish remodeling of established scars.

Third, select a clinically meaningful advantage worth pursuing and test it against vehicle/standard care and an active comparator. Possible questions include wound strength, durable scar quality, local tolerability, a workable treatment window and manufacturability. These are proposed decision criteria, not demonstrated advantages of SLIT2. A later independent porcine study would be valuable if it addresses the remaining uncertainty with the actual intended product; it should follow identity, access and basic human-tissue feasibility.

Fund a bounded result only when success would belong to an accessible asset and change a financing decision. Do not force a numerical valuation or invented probability of success from the current mouse data. There is no vendor/owner quote supporting a project budget yet.
