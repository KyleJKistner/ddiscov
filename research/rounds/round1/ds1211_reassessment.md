# DS-1211 / TNAP inhibition for PXE: availability-first reassessment

> **Historical review snapshot.** Later findings and rankings are reconciled in the [current shortlist](../../../decisions/current-shortlist.md) and [round 4 review](../round4/README.md). Statements about activity or access describe this review period.

Date: 3 November 2026 (client timezone). Follow-up to the icerguastat diligence. Repository: KyleJKistner/ddiscov, c241dc4c6cfa7cd5312a7237dda61a4d2af50c97.

## Decision

**Keep only as a tightly bounded clinical-asset diligence prospect. Do not promote it to an obviously fundable experimental startup.** This candidate has more direct human pharmacology than the presumed top-three preclinical opportunities, but public evidence does not establish human functional efficacy or an available transaction. The next useful work is inspection of existing data and rights, not another generic mouse calcification experiment.

The repository already correctly distinguishes DS-1211 from SBI-425, identifies the 65-participant safety/PD/PK study, and records Daiichi's 2025 discontinuation. The additional diligence below narrows what “efficacy” can responsibly mean and updates the competing PPi-restoration landscape through October 2026.

## 1. Ownership and willingness are separate questions

Sanford Burnham Prebys says that its TNAP program was licensed to Daiichi Sankyo in 2015; the associated phase 2 announcement describes SBP's discovery work and Daiichi's clinical development. [D1] Daiichi disclosed discontinuation of DS-1211 in October 2025. Its stated reason was a longer development timeline and weaker competitiveness, while describing safety and efficacy favorably. [D2]

That combination is consistent with a possible asset-recycling opportunity, but it does not establish one. Fresh searches found no affirmative current outlicensing offer, completed license reversion or new sponsor for DS-1211. This is a search result, not proof that confidential discussions or contractual reversion rights do not exist. Both the present Daiichi control position and any upstream SBP rights need documentary confirmation.

**Access status:** identifiable historical licensor and clinical sponsor; sponsor discontinued development; current rights package and willingness to transact unconfirmed. A discontinued program is not necessarily an available program.

An instructive process problem is that an SBP partnering page, crawled recently, still calls DS-1211 a phase 1 program, despite linking a phase 2 announcement. [D3] Retrieval date cannot substitute for the underlying evidence date.

## 2. Human data show pharmacology, not established clinical benefit

The phase 1 primary publication reports 56 single-dose and 40 multiple-dose participants. Drug exposure correlated with inhibition of alkaline phosphatase and increases in its substrates, including PPi. Multiple-dose treatment lasted ten days. This supports human target-pathway engagement and short-term tolerability; it cannot settle chronic skeletal safety or whether vascular/ocular disease will improve. The time profile matters: transient post-dose effects can be missed by trough-only comparisons. [D4]

The primary EU results record for DS1211-A-U201/NCT05569252 reports 65 PXE participants receiving low, middle or high doses (16 per group) or placebo (17), for 12 weeks. Reported outcomes concern safety, PD and PK; the public record does not demonstrate improved calcification, walking or vision. Predose day-84 mean PPi percentage changes (SD) were 23.48 (78.04), 81.77 (313.08), 24.27 (97.87), and 4.28 (41.45), respectively, with descriptive rather than inferential analyses. The between-group pattern is noisy and non-monotonic. One treatment-related adverse event led to discontinuation; no deaths were reported. [D5]

**Interpretation:** these summaries do not establish a sustained, dose-dependent therapeutic effect in PXE. They also do not prove that DS-1211 lacks useful pharmacology: baseline-dependent percentage changes, outliers, peak-versus-trough effects and exposure differences require individual data. Daiichi's investor phrase about efficacy may encompass pharmacodynamic or unpublished evidence. It should not be upgraded to demonstrated patient benefit from the public record alone.

The publicly filed protocol contains substantial redactions. It excludes several pre-existing mineral/bone conditions and specifies monitoring relevant to hypophosphatasia and renal toxicity. [D6] That supports requesting the complete long-term safety rationale, rather than treating normal short-term tolerability as resolution of a mechanism-based therapeutic-window question.

## 3. The 2026 comparator update changes both competition and evidentiary standards

BioMarin announced on 18 May 2026 that ENERGY 3, a randomized study of 27 children with ENPP1 deficiency, increased plasma PPi with BMN 401 (formerly INZ-701) but did not improve the radiographic rickets co-primary outcome. Secondary rickets and growth measures showed no positive trends. [D7] BioMarin then discontinued the program and moved to close all ongoing trials, including expanded access, on 30 July 2026. [D8] Its 12 October 2026 patient update reiterated the absence of clinical or skeletal improvement after 52 weeks. [D9]

**Inference, with an important boundary:** enzyme replacement in ENPP1 deficiency and rickets is different from TNAP inhibition in ABCC6-associated PXE and vascular/ocular disease. This result does not falsify DS-1211 or predict an identical result. It does show why raising circulating PPi cannot automatically be treated as proof of clinical benefit. It removes a prominent active competing program while making a PPi-only investment thesis harder to defend.

Etidronate remains a relevant human comparator. The investigator-authored TEMP-PREVENT protocol describes a 24-month randomized PXE study, with last enrollment in December 2024, final follow-up expected in December 2026 and results planned for 2027. Its primary endpoint is arterial calcification. [D10] A DS-1211 program needs a credible advantage over that path and a strategy that remains worthwhile after those results arrive.

## 4. The first investment milestone should be a data-room decision

The highest-value deliverable is a short, independent integrated review under a defined evaluation agreement. It should answer six questions before funding new wet-lab work:

1. **What exactly can be acquired?** Identify current patent ownership, SBP contract obligations, sublicensing/reversion provisions, territory/field scope, regulatory-file reference rights, existing inventory and manufacturing access. Obtain actual owner willingness and indicative economics.
2. **Why did Daiichi stop?** Review the contemporaneous internal decision and regulatory feedback. Determine which timeline and competitive assumptions drove termination, and whether any have materially changed. A smaller company's lower overhead does not by itself shorten biological progression or required trial follow-up.
3. **Was human exposure sufficient?** Reconstruct individual concentration–ALP–PPi time courses, including trough and peak, baseline variation, adherence and assay quality. Establish whether there is a reproducible exposure window that plausibly affects the relevant tissue process without impairing physiologic mineralization.
4. **What efficacy evidence exists beyond PD?** Request all imaging, clinical, exploratory and nonclinical results, including negative findings. Clearly separate prevention of new mineral deposition from removal of established deposits and from meaningful function.
5. **What is the registration path?** Have an appropriate clinical/regulatory team define population, acceptable endpoints, study length, recruitment feasibility and meaningful effect size. PPi normalization alone should not be assumed adequate.
6. **What is the differentiated product?** Specify intended advantage in efficacy, safety, delivery or patient selection compared with etidronate and other available care. Relate that advantage to an achievable trial and finance plan.

This review could make a defensible transaction concrete. It cannot responsibly be replaced by a numerical score attached to a published compound.

## Go and stop criteria

**Proceed to negotiated development planning only if** the owner makes a relevant package accessible on plausible terms, full human data support a coherent exposure/PD and safety rationale, and an independent clinical team identifies a feasible differentiated efficacy study. These are conditional criteria, not findings already satisfied.

**Stop or hold** if current rights cannot be transferred/licensed, the original timeline disadvantage remains decisive, chronic safety requires an unaffordable reset, individual data do not support useful pharmacology, or the proposed next result would still leave the same clinical decision unanswered. A cheap preclinical study that cannot change one of these conclusions has low option value.
