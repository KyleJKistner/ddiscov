# Icerguastat / IFB-088 / Sephin1: independent diligence

> **Historical review snapshot.** Later findings and rankings are reconciled in the [current shortlist](../../../decisions/current-shortlist.md) and [round 4 review](../round4/README.md). Statements about activity or access describe this review period.
Date: 3 November 2026 (client timezone). Repo reviewed: KyleJKistner/ddiscov, c241dc4c6cfa7cd5312a7237dda61a4d2af50c97.

## Decision
**Keep as an owner-led licensing/partnering prospect; do not call it a startup-ready A-tier experimental opportunity.** The public evidence supports genuine pharmacology and partially improved disease phenotypes. The central unmet gate is a commercially useful role that Enemsa will actually license or co-develop. An independent laboratory study alone does not give its funder rights to this drug or create a new therapeutic asset.

The curated repository already states important limitations and leaves portfolio-wide priority open. My criticism is therefore of the promotion/decision process, not an assertion that its public summary claims approval-ready efficacy.

## Primary evidence and interpretation

### 1. An active company has secured the relevant disease rights
UChicago announced on 30 July 2026 that Enemsa received exclusive rights to the US10,905,663 patent family for demyelinating disorders, including CMT. Consideration includes equity, maintenance fees, milestones and royalties. This is affirmative evidence of an existing controlled program, not an orphaned academic asset. Enemsa's current pipeline explicitly presents icerguastat ALS and CMT assets as available for licensing or partnership, but no territory, field split, price, sublicense power or accepted contribution is disclosed. Sources I1–I3.

**Implication:** Classify access as “identified exclusive licensee publicly soliciting partners; transaction scope unconfirmed.” A reagent purchase or collaboration invitation cannot advance this to “commercial access secured.” The July transaction means current ownership diligence matters even for a recently assembled literature review.

### 2. The mouse result is real, but the magnitude and timing limit the inference
Bai et al. treated MpzR98C/+ mice beginning PND30, with disease already present, for five months. The functional cohort sizes were 15–27 animals per condition, evaluators were blinded, and grip, rotarod and conduction improved. Morphology involved four animals per condition. The paper reports statistical improvement, not normalization. Group sizes were designed for an effect of 1.25 SD; outlier screening used Grubbs' test. These are genuine strengths and limitations, rather than a reason to dismiss the study. Source I4.

The repo's approximate plotted motor-conduction means are 14.5 to 16.4 m/s, against approximately 32 m/s in healthy mice. **Calculated from those approximate means:** +1.9 m/s is about 13% improvement over diseased baseline and about 11% closure of the healthy–diseased gap. These are not raw-data reanalyses. A large remaining physiologic deficit persists even when the treatment difference is statistically significant. Adult late-treatment reversal, durable benefit after stopping, and human MPZ conduction benefit remain separate propositions.

The earlier S63del model contains a transgene plus two WT copies; R98C is a heterozygous endogenous knock-in. Their degree of rescue cannot be pooled as if they were the same genetic lesion. Source I4. The S63del protein-localization observation in transfected non-neural cells is not proof of human nerve repair.

### 3. Clinical exposure experience is valuable; efficacy remains indication-specific
The 2025 sponsor report on P288ALS describes 51 randomized bulbar-onset ALS patients, 41 with six-month observations, and an exploratory study primarily designed for safety. Intent-to-treat efficacy was described as a trend; the reported significant ALSFRS slope difference was in 40 per-protocol patients (-0.95 versus -1.42 points/month). Source I5. The registry identifies safety as primary and efficacy scales as secondary, and does not convert this into CMT evidence. Source I6.

The sponsor's current site describes an accepted Brain paper, but I did not locate an accessible published version with full analyses during this review. Source I7. I therefore do not upgrade press-release efficacy to an independently scrutinized definitive finding. Full CSR, prespecified SAP, missing-data handling, exposure distributions, serious adverse-event narratives and long-term toxicology should be reviewed in the owner's data room.

**Implication:** Human dosing experience reduces first-in-human uncertainty. It does not show that the needed unbound concentration reaches human Schwann cells, that chronic pediatric treatment is acceptable, or that relevant MPZ subtypes respond. A “clinical-stage” label can wrongly make the proposed new CMT assay appear nearly de-risked.

### 4. Material existence is ahead of assay qualification
NYSCF's March 2026 list includes AG0009-02-MR (MPZ R98C), AG0009-02-MCS (isogenic control), and AG0019-01-MR (R98C). The two mutant entries share age/sex/ancestry descriptors; independent donor identity must be established before counting them as independent biological replication. The listing invites requests, without establishing present inventory or commercial terms. Source I8.

The all-human nerve-on-chip proof-of-concept reported roughly 5% myelination and 0.13–0.28 m/s conduction; neuronal–Schwann-cell cocultures were slower than neurons alone in that assay (n=3). Source I9. This is not a claim that newer platforms cannot work. It shows why “measures conduction” is insufficient evidence that a system measures disease-dependent myelin repair.

A 2025 preprint describes a scalable platform using iPSC sensory neurons and primary human Schwann cells, tested with vincristine-induced injury. Source I10. This is useful operator/platform evidence, but a healthy-cell neurotoxicity assay does not establish a reproducible R98C Schwann-cell disease phenotype or response to icerguastat.

**Implication:** The proposed experiment includes cell differentiation, co-culture, maturation and disease-model qualification risk. It should not be costed or scheduled like a routine repeat assay. Twenty-four RNA samples from one donor background remain one donor background.

### 5. Mechanism should be tested, not inherited from a sponsor label
Independent primary experiments found Sephin1 protection-related effects without inhibition of PPP1R15A-mediated eIF2α dephosphorylation and even in cells lacking the proposed pathway component. Source I11. Other groups and the sponsor favor ISR-related activity and additional targets. This is a mechanistic dispute, not proof the phenotype is false.

**Implication:** Do not use one downstream stress marker as proof of direct target engagement. A functional benefit at an achievable exposure is the essential readout; mechanistic attribution should use orthogonal perturbations appropriate to the validated human model.

## Minimum package before paying for a large new study

1. **Commercial gate:** Enemsa identifies in writing a precise CMT field, geography or co-development role it will consider, and the decision that an outside result would change. Verify ability to grant that role under upstream licenses.
2. **Information gate:** Access full nonclinical/clinical package, current patent-family schedule and assignments, relevant drug substance/formulation specifications, stability, available inventory, and whether a substantively identical human study is already completed or planned.
3. **Model gate:** A qualified operator shows baseline R98C-versus-corrected-control conduction deficit, measurable myelination, reproducibility across independent batches, and a prespecified assay performance threshold before treatment efficacy is scored.
4. **Biological replication gate:** Confirm independent donors; use multiple backgrounds where the biological question requires inference beyond one patient. An isogenic control is valuable for causality but does not replace donor replication.
5. **Exposure gate:** Measured unbound medium exposure is related to actual human unbound PK and, ideally, relevant peripheral-nerve distribution. No promotion based solely on a nominal culture dose or total plasma Cmax.
6. **Value gate:** Agree beforehand what minimum functional gain, absence of neuronal toxicity, and consistency across backgrounds would justify the next clinical-development decision. Ownership and permitted transfer of data must match that use.

## Specific stop rules

- No defined rights-bearing or paid sponsor role: stop independent asset-development spend; maintain only a BD prospect.
- Model does not reproduce a genotype-related conduction deficit or meaningful myelin response: stop efficacy experiment and label as assay-development work.
- Benefit restricted to unattainable free exposure or accompanied by neuronal toxicity: stop this clinical translation path.
- Only transcriptional/structural improvement without functional gain: do not label rescue or move to A tier.
- Owner already has equivalent results and no remaining decision: stop duplicative work.
- Economics require trial financing far beyond the user's intended first milestone: do not reframe a clinical-stage licensing transaction as a cheap preclinical project.

## Budget interpretation

The repository explicitly calls its $9,000–10,200 academic / $11,940–14,580 industry numbers a two-service subtotal for RNA sequencing and specimen preparation. That is fair as written. It is **not** a study quote. The principal uncertainties—cell access, differentiation, assay development, exposure bioanalysis, electrophysiology, operator labor, failed batches, rights and repeat cohorts—sit outside it.

A transparent planning envelope can be built as an assumption, not a quote: one specialist full-time-equivalent for 4–6 months at an assumed fully loaded $180,000–250,000/year implies $60,000–125,000 labor; $20,000–50,000 assumed non-labor model/material costs; $15,000–30,000 assumed analytical services; plus 20–30% contingency. This gives approximately **$115,000–265,000** before licensing fees, additional donors and major platform capital. An established CRO could price differently. The decision should be based on a milestone quote with explicit qualification criteria, not this illustrative envelope. No available source establishes that even this scope is sufficient for an investable CMT program.
