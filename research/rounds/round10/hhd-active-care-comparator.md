# Round 10 — Ruxolitinib/HHD against an explicit active-care pathway

**Evidence cut: 4 November 2026. Existing candidate: R6-NL-01.** This is an active-comparator refinement. Naltrexone/HHD is not counted as a new candidate or as new ruxolitinib efficacy.

## Decision

**Retain ruxolitinib's controlled clinical-proof priority and ten planning-effort points.** Add oral naltrexone-based care to the explicit comparator map, alongside sweat-control procedures and other protocolized care. The evidence does not establish naltrexone as universally superior, a mandatory treatment for every participant, or a dose-escalation strategy.

The new question for a differentiated cream is more specific: can it provide useful lesion/pain control with an attractive total burden compared with the actual alternative care pathway? A topical-versus-oral preference is a product hypothesis, not an observed comparative benefit.

## Primary observations newly audited

Cao et al. reported three biopsy-confirmed patients, all women, with differing regimens and cointerventions. [H10-S01]

| Case | Historical treatment context | Reported outcome and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4.5 mg nightly plus topical rescue/maintenance | Approximately 95% clearance at 18 months; add-on treatment prevents monotherapy attribution |
| 2 | 12.5 mg from quartered 50-mg tablets, then 4.5 mg after interruption | Initial clearance, flare after stopping, slower improvement on restart; no randomized dose comparison |
| 3 | 50 mg initially, later multiple changing adjuncts | No initial improvement; partial control after additional drugs, with rescue interventions |

The report's optimistic dose-escalation language is not adopted as a validated regimen or safety conclusion. More drug did not consistently produce better control, and within-person treatment sequences have time, cointervention and flare-selection confounding.

A separate 2026 report describes a 65-year-old man with biopsy-confirmed HHD and pain limiting walking. Oral naltrexone was increased from 2.5 to 5 mg; the authors estimated 80% improvement at six weeks and described control beyond two years. Infection-associated flares and concurrent care remained relevant; no validated severity or quality-of-life instrument was used. The same case was presented at BAD in 2025. [H10-S02]

That conference presentation is not another patient. Author-estimated percentage improvement is not a measured change in the proposed erosion-area or rescue-free-day endpoint. The four described people from these two reports are a selected case set, not an all-treated denominator, and are not pooled with ruxolitinib cases.

## Consequences for the existing proof plan

The [Round 7 controlled-proof design](../round7/rux_hhd_development_proof.md) and [Round 8 maintenance/comparator requirements](../round8/clinical_challenge.md) remain the basis. This update makes three decisions explicit:

1. **Specify the alternative care pathway.** A matched vehicle comparison addresses an active-ingredient question. An add-on comparison estimates benefit over the same recorded background care. A claim to replace an oral or procedural treatment ultimately requires that relevant comparison.
2. **Record and stabilize important cointerventions when clinically appropriate.** Oral naltrexone use, sweat-control measures, infection treatment, topical rescue and recent procedures must be visible. Their presence is neither automatically forbidden nor silently attributed to the study cream.
3. **Judge total useful control.** Retain all randomized patients, rescue use, maintenance entry and missing follow-up. Lesion improvement after adding rescue therapy is not attributable to one ingredient without analysis. An oral daily regimen, topical application burden and procedural visits cannot be ranked using uncontrolled photographs.

The early proof need not become an oversized multi-arm comparison. Select the comparison that answers the declared claim; later expansion requires its own justification. No head-to-head efficacy or cost-effectiveness estimate is available from these cases.

## Article, dose and mechanism limits

A quartered marketed tablet, a lower-dose preparation and a topical ruxolitinib cream are different articles. Historical tablet access does not establish current low-dose content uniformity, an authenticated lot or the cost of a specified product. Reports of tolerability are not a chronic safety margin.

The current evidence can support useful symptomatic control without demonstrating SPCA1 correction. The proposed opioid/immune mechanisms of naltrexone and IFN/JAK mechanism of ruxolitinib do not establish restoration of mutant ATP2C1-dependent adhesion or a durable drug-free cure.

The nine-patient Ceccacci preprint remains abstract-level in this sprint: the primary PMC, bioRxiv full-text and Europe PMC routes did not yield an auditable clinical table. Its patients are not pooled into a response rate. A 2026 naltrexone review title was also located, but no primary dose-response dataset was recovered from it; it supplies no quantitative result here.
