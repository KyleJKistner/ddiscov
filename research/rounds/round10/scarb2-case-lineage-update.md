# Round 10 — SCARB2/miglustat case-lineage update

**Evidence cut: 4 November 2026. Existing candidate: R9-NL-01.** This adds a historical treated person to the retrieved evidence set; it does not add a candidate or claim a complete treatment census.

## Decision and new evidence

**Retain the conditional clinical-research reserve below lisdexamfetamine, with no independent asset promotion.** The additional observation strengthens the record of possible useful function after severe disability while preserving the directly contrary cases and the absence of controlled efficacy.

Chaves et al. described two sisters, but **only patient 2 received miglustat**. At age 24, after profound disability, she regained sitting, oral feeding and some speech during treatment; the report describes benefit for almost two years before death from pneumonia at 26. Both sisters previously received imiglucerase without improvement. [SC10-S01]

That is **one additional miglustat-treated person**, not two. The sibling who received enzyme replacement only is not a miglustat nonresponder or randomized control. The exact SCARB2 allele was not recovered from the primary passages available in this bounded review and remains null. The report describes C1q nephropathy; it does not establish renal repair.

The retrieved set is now **five treated people across four primary reports**. These are heterogeneous observations with different variants, disease stages, cointerventions and outcomes. Neither three favorable narratives out of five nor any other pooled fraction estimates the probability of benefit. The two adverse trajectories do not, by themselves, prove drug causation in a progressive disease.

Round 9 explicitly counted a bounded retrieved set of four, not a complete census. That historical count remains unchanged in its original files; this update expands current coverage.

## Interpretation and product consequence

The new case matters because the reported gain involved daily function after severe impairment, rather than an isolated biochemical change. However, it lacks concurrent control, independently masked functional assessment and a standardized response endpoint. Survival benefit cannot be inferred; the later death cannot be assigned to miglustat without supporting evidence.

Prior enzyme-replacement failure and later substrate-reduction improvement involve different interventions. They do not constitute a randomized mechanistic experiment. No dose-normalized brain exposure, target-engagement measurement or common authenticated historical formulation was established.

The current development question remains whether a reproducible, tolerable functional benefit exists across a clearly defined treated population. Renal exposure, weight/GI burden, background medications and complete discontinuation records remain material. The new report does not solve the independent product-contribution question or displace the leading programs.

The next useful public evidence is a complete case/variant/exposure ledger or a controlled outcome, not another pooled responder percentage. Current authorization covers document analysis only.
