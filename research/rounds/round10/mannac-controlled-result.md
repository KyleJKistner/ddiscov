# Round 10 — ManNAc/GNE controlled-result disposition

**Evidence cut: 4 November 2026. Existing inventory record: historical-topics:T060.** This is a current decision update, not a new candidate or a newly published clinical result.

## Decision

**Hold new independent ManNAc development for an undifferentiated GNE muscle-strength claim. Retain the program as a controlled-evidence benchmark.** A new formulation or subgroup thesis would need primary evidence explaining why it should outperform the tested approach; an earlier biochemical signal is insufficient. This does not reject every intervention in the sialic-acid pathway.

The original inventory already linked the AAN abstract. Its review was marked repository_registry with fresh_primary_review=false. This sprint reads the primary report and adds a current disposition while preserving that historical record.

## Verified result and denominators

The investigator abstract for NN109/MAGiNE reports **54 treated participants: 37 ManNAc and 17 erythritol placebo**, randomized 2:1. The historical regimen was 4 g three times daily for at least 24 months. The strength endpoint used maximum voluntary isometric contraction/QMA with the GNE progression model. No significant primary benefit was demonstrated; the per-protocol direction favored ManNAc without significance. Secondary/exploratory results were also negative. The active arm began weaker in several muscle groups. Flatulence and ophthalmic adverse events increased, without counts in the abstract. [M10-S01]

The registry identifies **NCT04231266, phase 2**, actual enrollment 54. Its older narrative still describes a planned total of 51. Planned enrollment, treated counts and an endpoint-analysis denominator are different quantities. The retrieved registry snapshot was last updated June 2025; its administrative status cannot override the later investigator result. [M10-S02]

The AAN page and the Neurology supplement are the **same trial report**, not independent replications. The journal explicitly labels the content as an abstract that was not reviewed by its editors. No full primary analysis report, endpoint confidence interval, exact p value, arm-specific attrition, genotype-response table or detailed safety table was recovered.

## What this changes

The current evidence does not support presenting ManNAc as an unfilled, clinically de-risked muscle-repair opportunity. The controlled functional result receives more decision weight than uncontrolled biochemical or historical-comparator signals. No numerical effect bound or posterior success probability is manufactured from the word “negative.”

Baseline imbalance remains a real limitation. It cannot be turned into a favorable adjusted effect without the actual model, data and uncertainty. The abstract does not establish that the trial adequately excluded every clinically meaningful effect, that target engagement failed, or that a specific variant subgroup benefited. Likewise, a nonsignificant per-protocol direction does not rescue the primary claim.

ManNAc, aceneuramic acid, and other pathway interventions remain distinct articles. This update neither transfers this outcome to another molecule nor proposes a new dose, treatment, experiment or subgroup enrollment.

## Reopening rule

Reconsider the exact article only if a public full report or dataset supplies a credible, prespecified functional effect, a demonstrable article/exposure problem that a defined replacement can solve, or a prospectively testable differentiated claim grounded in actual data. Additional biomarker-only evidence does not meet that rule. Repeated searches of the same abstract are closed for this sprint.
