# Round 6 — six new exact-use screens

Verification date: 4 November 2026. This is a research work product for integration into ddiscov, based on the 103-file snapshot at main `44fe86f9b5d22914ce366bcef9f6c3667b1e14b2`. Dispositions below are proposed; they do not independently change the current shortlist. No outreach, purchases, clinical treatment, or experimental execution occurred.

## Decision

**Topical ruxolitinib for Hailey–Hailey disease is the strongest new candidate for a bounded clinical proof. ABX-1431 for AP4B1 deficiency merits a smaller, explicitly developmental functional-validation project.** Three other screens show that meaningful human improvement is discoverable, but their immediate value is clinical research or a comparator, with a less clear new-product contribution. Erythromycin/DM1 illustrates why a positive mechanistic abstract should not outrank the clinical functional results.

None is presently an S-tier company asset. This does not prevent funding a sharply defined experiment. The next funding decision should concern the value of the next proof, with product ownership and differentiation evaluated separately.

## R6-NL-01 — topical ruxolitinib / Hailey–Hailey disease

**Exact proposition.** Ruxolitinib 1.5% cream applied locally to established refractory Hailey–Hailey lesions. The disease is associated with ATP2C1; an individual sequence-confirmed genotype was not recovered for the clinical case. This is a local lesion-control and re-epithelialization thesis, with pain, recurrence, treatment burden, and quality of life as useful outcomes. It is not a claim that JAK inhibition repairs the causal mutation.

**Primary evidence and denominator.** Jobe et al. published a peer-reviewed case on 13 March 2026: one 77-year-old patient, prior therapies stopped, twice-daily 1.5% cream, rapid pain relief and reported resolution within three days. Use became intermittent because of cost; remissions lasted weeks, followed by milder flares. Images at three months do not establish three months of uninterrupted dosing or remission. The comparator is the patient's prior state, without a concurrent blinded control. The publication and its PubMed figure captions describe the same patient, not separate replication. [H1]

Ceccacci et al.'s 4 June 2026 preprint abstract reports nine refractory patients with re-epithelialization and reduced pain, itching, oozing, and inflammation. **Only the abstract was verified.** Full treatment schedules, individual genotypes, background therapies, cohort overlap with case reports, and exact response denominators could not be audited. Do not sum this nine-patient report with published cases into a supposedly independent response rate. Molecular IFN signatures and a clinical response do not themselves quantify mechanical barrier strength. [H2]

**Article and availability.** The current US label defines a manufactured 1.5% cream, 15 mg ruxolitinib per gram, with marketed packaging. This substantially reduces article/formulation uncertainty. It does not establish HHD approval, a received lot, distribution in every jurisdiction, or commercial rights for a new developer. Exposure in atopic dermatitis or vitiligo is not proof of the systemic window across eroded intertriginous HHD skin. [H3]

**Current competition.** The 2 November 2026 Krystal filing says KB111's HALITE-1 enrollment is complete: **five patients**, weekly dosing for 12 weeks ongoing, interim results expected before year-end. Older anticipated enrollment of seven is superseded for the current enrolled count. This is an active, different ATP2C1-directed program; its evidence must not be transferred to ruxolitinib. [H4]

**Proposed next proof.** A controlled matched-lesion study can test whether the cream increases closure and lesion-free time, reduces pain and rescue treatment, and sustains useful control with tolerable exposure. Where anatomically appropriate, contralateral lesions can control patient-level variation; background care and intercurrent infection must be recorded. A direct barrier measure may strengthen mechanism, but clinical closure and recurrence already matter to patients. Permanent off-treatment cure is not a prerequisite for a useful maintenance product.

**Advance / stop.** Advance if a prespecified useful difference from vehicle/background care replicates across independent patients, including recurrence and exposure. Stop the new-product thesis if there is no meaningful advantage in efficacy, tolerability, treatment burden, or another intended product benefit over appropriate existing care, if the effect disappears after background-treatment control, or if the effective local regimen produces an unacceptable systemic/local window. Separately ask what a new developer adds beyond access to an existing cream: an HHD-specific controlled dataset, a differentiated delivery/regimen, or a formal indication path. That contribution is unresolved, rather than automatically defeated by the existence of a marketed ingredient.

## R6-NL-02 — manganese sulfate / SLC39A8-related ataxia

**Exact proposition.** Oral manganese sulfate monohydrate replacement for low-manganese SLC39A8 disease, with gait/coordination and daily independence as functional outcomes. In the 2025 adult case, the exact article is reported as the monohydrate. Reported salt mass must not be silently converted into elemental manganese mass.

**Primary evidence.** Portela et al. describe a 34-year-old man with homozygous c.610G>T, p.Gly204Cys, low serum manganese, and roughly 12 years of progressive cerebellar ataxia. Monohydrated manganese sulfate was increased from a reported 10 mg/day toward 60 mg/day. Gait, coordination, and independence improved, with baseline/nine-month video. After approximately a year, inadvertent withdrawal was followed by deterioration, with improvement on reintroduction. This within-person sequence strengthens the causal hypothesis but is not a blinded effect estimate. Cognition was an impression rather than a measured endpoint. [M1]

Park et al.'s earlier two-patient report found biochemical correction and motor/hearing/neurological improvements; its different MnSO4 dose convention must not be pooled as an equivalent regimen. A January 2026 Bulgarian pediatric case reports low manganese and new motor milestones during oral manganese sulfate treatment. Those reports support the direction of benefit, without establishing a uniform effect across variants or ages. The pediatric abstract does not supply an independently audited exact formulation/dosing comparison here. [M2, M3]

**Availability and value.** The chemical identity is reconstructable without an author response. A specific qualified oral formulation, released lot, and prescribing/manufacturing route were not verified. This is not equivalent to the entire treatment being unavailable. The major opportunity is better diagnosis, formulation/monitoring, and prospective measurement; a defensible proprietary product has not been identified. Replacement is credible only in the appropriate deficiency context, with manganese exposure and toxicity monitored, not as a general ataxia treatment.

**Proposed next proof and decision.** A prospective genotype- and baseline-stratified functional cohort could quantify gait/ataxia, independence, biochemical response, and exposure. The strongest next question is how much function remains recoverable after established deficit, and what predicts response. Advance a grant-oriented clinical study if the formulation and monitoring can support interpretable prospective measurements. Do not label a commodity replacement as S-tier venture material without a real differentiated product contribution. Do not deliberately withdraw clinically beneficial replacement merely to recreate the published inadvertent interruption.

## R6-NL-03 — nicotinamide / NAXE deficiency

**Exact proposition.** Nicotinamide, also called niacinamide, for NAXE-associated encephalopathy. It is a distinct article from nicotinic acid, nicotinamide riboside, and the previously screened NAXD combination. A broad “vitamin B3” label would erase a material identity difference.

**Primary evidence.** Xu et al., published 23 January 2026, retrospectively studied nine patients: **eight NAXE and one NAXD**. Six NAXE patients received nicotinamide 180–500 mg/day, starting 0.08–3.5 years after onset. Five improved in ataxia/tone over three to six months; median NAXE follow-up was 3.76 years. Patient 7 remained unconscious and continuously ventilated despite treatment: survival is not useful motor rescue. Four of six treated NAXE patients also received acute short-term vitamin cocktails; subsequent treatment was nicotinamide alone. One patient required dose reduction for flushing/rash. The cohort was enriched for p.Lys245Gln. [N1]

The paper's pooled 59-patient literature analysis is not a new independent cohort or randomized survival comparison. The NAXD biochemical experiments and an earlier published NAXD patient's long-term follow-up must not be relabeled NAXE drug-response evidence. Postmortem diagnosis among untreated patients creates severe selection and survival biases. [N1]

An independent 2024 NAXE case had only transient stabilization and ultimately died despite high-dose niacin therapy. Its publication abstract establishes the negative outcome; the abstract does not independently settle the exact dispensed vitamin species. It therefore limits claims about the general NAD-precursor rescue concept rather than constituting an exact nicotinamide failure comparison. A full-text chemical-identity audit is still needed before pooling this case with the 2026 nicotinamide cohort. [N2]

**Availability, next proof, and disposition.** Nicotinamide's identity is defined, but a specific trial-quality lot/formulation was not qualified. A prospective early-versus-established-deficit natural-history-anchored study could assess mobility, consciousness, respiratory dependence, episode severity, and durable daily function, with the precise species and concomitant treatments recorded. Advance that clinical-research question if it produces interpretable genotype/severity-stratified functional evidence. Stop a universal reversal claim now: a severe functional nonresponder is already visible. A simple supplement-to-orphan-product narrative is insufficient for a proprietary company thesis; improved formulation, crisis delivery, or another actual product contribution would have to be demonstrated rather than assumed.

## R6-NL-04 — fluoxetine / slow-channel congenital myasthenic syndrome

**Exact proposition.** Fluoxetine for a genetically and functionally established slow-channel acetylcholine-receptor syndrome. This is not a general congenital-myasthenia treatment; different mechanisms must not inherit the signal.

**Human function and mechanistic link.** Harper, Fukodome, and Engel's May 2003 report treated two quinidine-allergic slow-channel patients for three years, reporting marked quantitative strength and EMG improvement. Receptor open bursts in transfected cells shortened at 5 μM fluoxetine and approached normal at 10 μM. Reported clinical serum concentrations of 8–11 μM are **combined fluoxetine plus norfluoxetine**, not free parent drug or direct evidence of an intrinsic therapeutic margin. The reported 80–120 mg/day regimen is historical research exposure, not a prescribing recommendation. [F1]

A 2006 follow-up case reported improved strength/endurance and withdrawal from ventilatory support. A 2022 prospective open-label Thai family study, with CHRNA1 p.Gly153Ala reported in its source, supports improvement in strength/endurance but variable respiratory response. The family is not multiple independent genotype replications. Exact family participant counts and all variant-specific doses were not recoverable from the verified abstracts and are not invented here. [F2, F3]

**Availability and development contribution.** Current official labeling confirms marketed oral fluoxetine hydrochloride capsules. Commercial manufacture of the molecule is defined; no physical lot or commercial clearance for a new indication was established. [F4] This is a particularly useful positive-control benchmark: an intervention can improve longstanding neuromuscular function when it corrects the channel defect, yet still be a well-known repurposing approach rather than a fresh drug-discovery asset.

**Advance / stop.** A new project needs a concrete deficiency in current therapy, such as a demonstrated genotype-specific window or formulation problem. Measure repetitive-stimulation behavior, strength/endurance, and respiratory function against appropriate existing management. Advance only if the proposed product solves a meaningful unmet limitation. Stop novelty-based promotion based solely on rediscovering the old response, or if the molecular subtype is not slow-channel gain of function.

## R6-NL-05 — ABX-1431 / AP4B1-SPG47

**Exact article.** ABX-1431 is Lu AG06466, now listed as elcubragistat; an irreversible covalent MGLL inhibitor. Davies et al. used MedChemExpress **HY-117632**, prepared in DMSO. The current manufacturer catalog retains this exact identifier and lists research use only. These aliases are one article, not three independent leads. A catalog page supplies a procurement route, not a received lot, verified assay concentration, clinical supply, or rights. [A1, A2]

**Biological unit, timing, and effect.** The 25 February 2022 paper assessed neurite outgrowth in **two AP4B1 patient-derived iPSC backgrounds** against unaffected sex-matched heterozygous parents. A third patient line used elsewhere does not make three drug-response donors. Day-six neurons were replated and treated; imaging began four hours after plating, then every three hours to 25 hours. There is no verified three-hour delayed drug start or demonstration of mature established impairment before treatment. At 10/50 nM, neurite length/branching improved; 100 nM was partial and 500 nM ineffective. The main panel's 18 images came from two biological replicates, not 18 donors. This is structural developmental rescue, not demonstrated electrical connectivity, mature axon maintenance, or patient motor improvement. [A1]

**Orthogonal evidence and limits.** Reduced brain 2-AG in AP-4-deficient mice supports the proposed pathway, but those measurements are not an in-vivo drug-treated motor-rescue experiment. The parent comparisons are not isogenic controls. Covalent occupancy and the nonmonotonic response require time-dependent target-engagement measurements rather than a single nominal concentration comparison. [A1]

The separate 49-adult Tourette randomized trial of Lu AG06466 did not establish efficacy; the week-eight tic difference favored placebo. This is not AP4B1 evidence against the target, but it prevents borrowing successful clinical efficacy from the same article. More practically, the trial reports dose reductions in 14/23 treated patients versus 3/26 placebo and attention/fatigue events. A generally tolerable adult exposure cannot simply be transferred to chronic pediatric AP-4 treatment. [A3]

**Proposed next proof.** First qualify the exact lot and occupancy/time course in independent AP4B1 lines, using corrected controls and the known parent-line phenotype. Ask whether improved outgrowth creates useful connectivity or sustained axon function, and test a delayed-treatment arm after an independently measured deficit. Early developmental preservation remains a valid product proposition if that is the supported window. Advance a bounded proof if useful function replicates at exposure compatible with a measured occupancy and tolerability window. Stop if the effect is imaging-only, nonspecific developmental acceleration, dependent on one line, or absent at a plausible chronic window. A preclinical validation project can be fundable before human AP4 efficacy is known.

Public sources identify prior Abide/Lundbeck clinical development; no AP4B1-specific active clinical program was verified in this search. This is a bounded search result, not evidence that rights are free or that the article is abandoned. [A3]

## R6-NL-06 — erythromycin / adult DM1

**Exact proposition.** Oral erythromycin as the study article **MYD-0124** to reduce toxic expanded-DMPK-RNA effects in adult DM1. Trial identity is defined; substitution with an arbitrary erythromycin salt or marketed formulation has not been bridged.

**Clinical result controls the screen.** The phase 2 trial published 26 December 2023 randomized 30 ambulatory adults: placebo six, 500 mg/day twelve, and 800 mg/day twelve, for 24 weeks. Safety/tolerability was primary. Six-minute walk, strength, myotonia, and other clinical outcomes did not establish a useful between-group benefit. All six prespecified splice biomarkers lacked significant between-group differences; two of five additional post-hoc markers had nominal positive pooled-drug comparisons. Biopsies were assigned to half the participants, not all 30. CK change was not conventionally significant. Those results support further investigation, not a confirmed clinical restoration claim. [E1]

The official jRCT record corroborates completed enrollment and the absence of significant efficacy differences. Its baseline sex counts contain an apparent transcription inconsistency, so the publication, not that field, should govern demographic extraction. The safety result arose in a selected trial that excluded major cardiac/ECG and other abnormalities; it does not establish an unrestricted DM1 safety window. [E2]

**Availability and rights.** A 2023 AMED report describes audited manufacturing and trial supply of MYD-0124 by a Mylan EPD facility. That is historical clinical manufacturing evidence, not present sponsor stock. The paper discloses Osaka University patents JP6460552B2, US10500223B2, and EP3323419B1. These are identified claims to examine, not a freedom-to-operate opinion. [E1, E3] Targeted current searches recovered the completed phase 2 record but did not verify a current later-phase registration. An article's stated future trial intention is not current enrollment.

**Disposition.** Hold a new independent erythromycin/DM1 program. Reopen if a specific exposure/formulation improvement produces a prespecified useful functional effect, or a new controlled clinical result changes the conclusion. The next project must explain why it can overcome the functional uncertainty and offer something beyond the existing university program. A low-powered trial does not prove zero benefit; it does defeat the claim that the published trial already demonstrated compelling restoration.

## What this changes about the search

The more productive query is “exact intervention, existing deficit, measured function, controlled exposure, and a development contribution,” rather than “novel molecule plus an optimistic disease review.” This round found human improvement in several rare conditions. It also separated that humanitarian value from proprietary value, retained one useful developmental rescue without calling it adult reversal, and rejected a mechanistic-positive clinical-functional overclaim.

A primary abstract can justify a narrowly scoped follow-up while remaining an abstract. Defined research supply and a marketed topical formulation are concrete ways to start; absence of an author reply is not a scientific veto. Conversely, a public drug name cannot substitute for a qualified lot, a disease-specific exposure window, or evidence that a new project changes treatment.
