# Round 6: the intended BLU3797 population, endpoints and liver context

**Evidence cut: 4 November 2026.** This extends the round-5 review and both same-day continuations. It adds a newly published adult dataset, checks original investigator communications and official trial records, and specifies consequences for the proposed proof. No new patient experiment or clinical efficacy estimate is represented.

## Decision

**Keep BLU3797 first for a staged functional proof, and define the intended population before choosing a surrogate or clinical endpoint.** An older-patient opportunity is plausible, but the available adults differ from the severely affected infant population. A small proof can merit funding while the later clinical-product case remains unresolved. Liver qualification belongs beside muscle function early in that proof; it is not evidence that BLU itself has caused liver injury.

## 1. A concrete adult human anchor

Prikhodko et al., published online **14 August 2026**, report screening/natural-history data for **26** Unite-CNM patients: 15 DNM2 and 11 MTM1. Fourteen entered the intervention. Eight had prior NatHis observations; eighteen had 1–3 run-in observations. This is the same cohort as the 2025 hepatic report, not independent replication. [R6-CT01]

The MTM1 subgroup's pooled median age of 52 obscures its sex composition:

| Public Table 1 subgroup | Number | Derived median age |
|---|---:|---:|
| MTM1 males | 6 | 22 years |
| MTM1 females | 5 | 57 years |
| Combined MTM1 | 11 | 52 years |

The [arithmetic audit](round6_clinical_translation_audit.json) preserves the reported ages. These are descriptions of selected participants, not population demographics. The paper reports relatively stable measured function over available follow-up; distal MFM32 was less affected than other domains, and male MTM1 respiratory measurements were consistently impaired. [R6-CT01]

**Development consequence:** choose an endpoint with both an observable deficit and room to improve in the selected population. A short stabilization claim is difficult to interpret when the comparator trajectory is already stable. Conversely, the absence of rapid natural decline does not preclude useful recovery. Respiratory or proximal functional improvement may be more informative than a near-ceiling distal score; the existing data do not establish a clinical minimum useful change or sample size for BLU.

Include genotype, residual MTM1 function, sex, severity and disease duration in the translational argument. A p.L463S engineered tissue, a p.E48* isogenic system, a young male null mouse and a manifesting adult female are distinct evidence units. An adult-onset experimental loss model can isolate mature-muscle biology but cannot reproduce decades of congenital disease merely by changing the induction date.

## 2. A blood marker cannot be presumed to report muscle response

In the adult dataset, DNM2 differences depend on compartment and subgroup: muscle elevation was significant for the DNM2 subgroup, while circulating DNM2 was reduced in both disease groups. A nonsignificant small MTM1 muscle subgroup does not establish absence of an effect. The figure-caption combined/subgroup means also fail a weighted-mean consistency check; the audit records the issue without inventing corrected data. [R6-CT01]

**Development consequence:** define separately what a measurement establishes: drug exposure, target engagement, downstream muscle biology or useful function. A disease-associated blood marker is not automatically a pharmacodynamic marker, and a pharmacodynamic marker is not automatically a surrogate for function. Any proposed circulating marker needs a paired exposure–muscle–function relationship before it can substitute for a tissue readout. This strengthens the existing conclusion from the BLU withdrawal experiment without pretending that DNM2 is BLU's direct target.

## 3. The hepatic warning is disease-context evidence across distinct articles

The 2026 patient-led liver registry analyzed **219** participants. Its abstract reports abnormal liver tests in **36% of affected MTM1 males**, while **30% of all participants had never had liver blood testing**. This patient-reported, incompletely monitored population is not comparable to a uniformly screened prospective infant cohort. Its percentage should not be substituted for the INCEPTUS broad hepatic composite or used to estimate BLU toxicity or trial ineligibility. [R6-CT02]

The existing DYN101 hepatic report states that abnormalities resolved after treatment stopped, but tolerability prevented dosing sufficient for the intended benefit. It concerns a DNM2-targeting antisense article, not PIK3C2B inhibition. The official DYN101 record specifies nonrandomized, sequential, open-label allocation; the word “randomized” in descriptive papers must not convert it into a randomized efficacy trial. [R6-CT03, R6-CT04]

The original TAM4MTM investigator transcript supplies more detail than the earlier halt letter: investigators described **seven participants completing** the trial and **two hepatic serious adverse events**. One involved laboratory abnormalities followed by successful re-exposure; the other involved symptomatic cholestasis and hospitalization. Efficacy analysis was incomplete at that December 2024 presentation. Investigators also described an exact-formulation regulatory-documentation barrier that prevented opening US sites, despite use of a familiar generic medicine. [R6-CT05]

A fresh official registry check still lists **six actual participants** and **no posted TAM4MTM results**, last updated September 2024. The six-versus-seven discrepancy is retained; neither denominator is silently replaced. Registry locations include both countries and planned US sites, so the discrepancy cannot be explained by an assumed national split. No definitive completed efficacy report was recovered in this search. [R6-CT06]

**Development consequence:** familiarity with a molecule in other populations does not settle susceptibility in MTM1 disease. Select an exact formulation and a disease-relevant hepatic assay, then connect any observed harm to the exposure needed for muscle benefit. These cross-article observations justify early qualification. They neither demonstrate BLU harm nor establish that all modalities share one toxic mechanism.

## 4. Changes to the next proof

| Question | More precise decision rule |
|---|---|
| Who is the intended beneficiary? | Start with an explicit established-disease population. Keep older males, manifesting females and severe infants separate until evidence supports pooling. |
| What would count as useful function? | Measure the deficit relevant to that population. Preserve force and fatigue in human tissues; require direct respiratory evidence before claiming respiratory rescue from limb hanging. |
| Does stability establish benefit? | Compare against a contemporaneous or qualified trajectory. Stable untreated function requires improvement or a separately demonstrated longer-term preservation advantage. |
| Can a cheap blood test replace tissue/function? | Only after a validated relationship in the selected context. Compartment discordance makes a one-step substitution unjustified. |
| Is an oral product inherently easier to execute? | Formulation, administration and regulatory-document access remain actual product work. They are not reasons to require an author reply before scientific selection. |
| What invalidates the exact article? | Failure at adequately engaged exposure in a modifiable human model, or demonstrated relevant-tissue harm overlapping benefit. An unknown exposure relationship remains unresolved rather than proven safe or unsafe. |

These are proposed criteria, not a clinical protocol. The evidence does not support inventing an adult response probability, a market size, a clinical meaningful-change threshold, a human dose or a priced development budget.
