# Round 6 — Enasidenib and PPCS reserve challenge

**Verified:** 4 November 2026. **Repository baseline:** `44fe86f9b5d22914ce366bcef9f6c3667b1e14b2`. **Scope:** two existing intervention/use pairs; no new lead count. This record challenges the current shortlist using newly recovered primary detail, rather than counting another review of the same studies as replication. It does not authorize outreach or experimental execution.

## Decision changes

Neither finding supports S-tier promotion by itself. Enasidenib has the stronger direct human organ-recovery evidence; PPCS offers an informative, bounded precursor-selection question. Prior human randomization is not a prerequisite for funding either of those early milestones. Their next proofs must establish an added contribution, not merely reproduce a known formulation or normalize a biomarker.

## 1. Enasidenib: pediatric sprinkle use is already documented

### Exact article and patient lineage

The 2023 *Nature Medicine* main article remains subscription-limited in the retrieved page. Its **public Supplementary Information** was downloaded, extracted and the critical dose/cardiac pages visually checked. That attachment changes the assessment without requiring access to the paid article. [E1–E2]

| Evidence unit | Genotype and treatment | What can be counted |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 patient 1 | De novo heterozygous **IDH2 p.Arg140Gln**; treatment began February 2019, age **7 years 1 month**. Supplement says enasidenib **“capsules” 50 mg/day**. Follow-up 3.9 years. | One individual with severe established dilated cardiomyopathy and longitudinal improvement. |
| 2023 patient 2 | De novo heterozygous **p.Arg140Gln**; October 2021, age **8 years**. **Sprinkle 60 mg/day for three months**, then **“capsules” 50 mg/day**. Follow-up 14 months. | Actual use of a pediatric delivery form in the exact disease; baseline systolic function was normal. This is not a second severe-DCM rescue. |
| 2024 Gold report | Separate previously reviewed patient, treatment age 3 years 11 months. | Retained independent report; this sprint does not recount it as new. Its hepatic/exposure limitation still governs the decision. [E3] |
| Roux et al., online November 2024 / issue February 2025 | Two children with p.Arg140Gln and granulomatous skin disease; cutaneous resolution accompanied metabolite improvement. | Two reported cutaneous observations, not two additional cardiac rescues. Institutional/authorship lineage appears separate from the original reports, but a complete individual-level cross-publication census is not established. [E4] |
| 2026 five-week-old infant abstract | Title, authors, DOI and publication metadata verified; outcome/dose text not retrieved. | **Zero additional efficacy observations** from this source. [E5] |

The supplement's word **“capsules”** is preserved. It must not be silently converted to the marketed tablet dosage form or treated as a released compounding recipe. The attachment does not supply the complete excipient, batch, salt/base dose or infant stability specification. The demonstrated clinical sprinkle use nevertheless directly defeats the implication that only whole adult tablets have ever been available for this disease. [E2]

### Established cardiac dysfunction improved; it did not normalize

The severe child's LVEF was **20–25%** at baseline, **35–40% at six months**, and **40–45% at eight months**. The first listed furosemide reduction occurred at eight months. At 46 months, LVEF was again **40–45%**, with LVIDd 44–47 mm versus 57–60 mm at baseline. The record also contains fluctuations: LVEF 36% at 20 months and a later decline during carvedilol adjustment. [E2, Supplementary Table 1]

This is a more persuasive established-disease functional association than metabolite suppression alone: the early improvement preceded the first listed heart-failure medication de-escalation. It remains one uncontrolled trajectory with concurrent captopril, carvedilol, furosemide and spironolactone; later medication changes complicate durability attribution. “Cardiac normalization” would overstate it. Patient 2 had fractional shortening of 34% at baseline, 32% at six months and 40% at twelve months, so that patient cannot be used as independent recovery from impaired systolic function. [E2]

The 2024 case continues to provide a concrete tolerability boundary: 50 mg daily was associated with ALT 118 IU/L; reduction to three times weekly maintained biochemical control with improvement in laboratory abnormalities, while twice-weekly treatment allowed metabolite return. This prior evidence is not erased by discovering another formulation. [E3; retained from round 5]

This is an occupied clinical repurposing path. That is positive evidence that the treatment is practicable, while making a “first pediatric formulation” or “first disease-treatment program” thesis untenable. Public information does not resolve licensing, current lot supply, a neonatal product specification or a clean new-entrant business model.

### The infant report remains a precise evidence gap

The newly indexed report is *Use of enasidenib in a 5-week-old male with type II D-2-hydroxyglutaric aciduria*, DOI **10.1016/j.ymgme.2026.110094**, *Molecular Genetics and Metabolism* 148(1):110094. Crossref carries publisher-deposited title/authors/May 2026 metadata but no abstract. The publisher article was inaccessible; the publisher text endpoint returned a site-unavailable page. The SIMD poster route required registration/login and was not pursued. No dose, formulation, functional result, adverse event or overlap conclusion is inferred from the title. [E5, E13]

The dermatology article does not close this gap: one child first presented at eight months, then developed further manifestations over the next year before the enasidenib response described in the paper. Eight months is not an audited treatment-start age. The skin cases also lack a quantitative cardiac treatment trajectory in the inspected primary text. [E4]

### Fundable next milestone

**Recommendation:** retain as a genotype-specific human rescue lead and explicitly consider a staged infant-exposure/organ-function qualification project. The demonstrated variant is R140Q; other R140 variants require their own susceptibility support.

The first investment should buy a defined answer: can an exact, reproducible pediatric article maintain mutant-metabolite control within a tolerable exposure range, and does that exposure restore a qualified cardiac deficit? A commercially usable engineered R140Q/isogenic human cardiac route is a plausible independent starting route, not an already qualified model. Treat after a measurable deficit; distinguish 2-HG suppression, force/rhythm recovery and liver liabilities. Existing clinical sprinkle/tablet experience is the benchmark. Model maturation and a functional positive control after the same deficit must be addressed before interpreting failure as an irreversible disease mechanism.

**Advance:** reproducible exact-article delivery/exposure; a useful cardiac functional effect after established dysfunction across independent backgrounds; a plausible chronic tolerability margin; and an incremental product or clinical-evidence proposition beyond AcSé-ESMART. **Hold/stop the proposed article:** precise dosing remains irreproducible, adequate suppression repeatedly requires harmful exposure, or qualified target engagement produces no useful function in a model demonstrably capable of recovery. A clear scientific proof can merit disease-focused funding even if it does not justify a standalone venture. No author reply is needed to make this selection.

## 2. PPCS: a human oral downstream-precursor comparator exists

### What remains unchanged

Round 5 already established the mismatch between “pantethine” and Sigma 16702's current **(R)-pantetheine** identity; unmeasured redox speciation; two patient cardiac backgrounds; stabilization rather than significant force increase in the patches; and the unresolved oral-to-myocardium exposure bridge. It also reviewed PPanSH salvage and mouse oral evidence. None is a new finding here. The original PPCS clinical/cell families must not be multiplied by their later publications, and the separately verified infant remains one separate clinical report. [P1–P2; prior audits]

The material new information is a **completed human oral 4′-phosphopantetheine program with actual posted results**, not merely an attractive related metabolite.

### Exact article, cohort and formulation

**CoA-Z / NCT04182763** studied formulated **4′-phosphopantetheine (4′-PPT, PPanSH)** in PKAN, a PANK2 disease. It is chemically distinct from pantethine and pantetheine. The protocol describes a **1 mg/mL fruit-flavored semi-liquid gel/syrup** administered orally or through a gastrostomy tube; GMP base substance was to be formulated through the OHSU/OSU Food Innovation Center route. Daily double-blind assignments were **20, 15 or 5 mg/m²**, versus placebo; the later open-label dose was 15 mg/m². These are documented study regimens, not a proposed PPCS regimen. [P3–P4]

There were **77 total participants: 65 randomized** (15/17/17/16 across high/mid/low/placebo) and **12 directly enrolled into open label**. Sixty-three randomized participants completed the six-month double-blind phase. The study completed January 2025; results were posted **29 October 2025**. The direct-open-label cohort must not enter the randomized denominator, and later follow-up of randomized participants is not another independent trial. [P3]

The protocol intentionally evaluated safety, tolerability and exploratory blood CoASY pharmacodynamics while developing a medical food. It was not designed as a PPCS cardiac trial, nor as conventional maximum-tolerated-dose finding. Thus absent cardiac outcomes are a limit on transfer, not evidence that the investigators failed their stated objective. [P4]

### Posted results qualify optimistic program language

| Posted outcome | High / mid / low / placebo | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Early CoASY mRNA ratio-to-baseline, geometric least-squares means | **1.31 / 1.13 / 1.04 / 1.07** | Posted ordered-dose trend **p=.105** against a stated two-sided **p<.10** threshold. This is not strong confirmatory dose-response evidence. |
| Six-month CoASY ratios | **1.07 / .97 / 1.02 / .96** | Ordered trend **p=.55**; no persuasive separation shown. |
| Adjusted treatment-emergent adverse-event counts per person | **1.15 / 1.38 / 1.14 / .86** | Joint test **p=.50**; lack of significance is not established equivalence or proof of no risk. |
| All-cause serious adverse events during double blind | **8/15 / 7/17 / 5/17 / 3/16** affected; one death in the middle-dose arm | Severe underlying disease and all-cause collection preclude assigning these events to treatment from this table alone. Avoid a blanket “no adverse events” claim. |

The registry reports **90% confidence intervals**. For the early biomarker row, the high dose is 1.05–1.57 and placebo .98–1.16; for six months, .87–1.28 and .83–1.09. The early row is titled “1 month +3 days after first dose,” but its model explicitly includes time as one month versus three days; do not convert this label into a single day-33 assessment. [P3]

There is a consequential **protocol/results analysis mismatch**. Protocol p30 planned an initial pooled-active-versus-placebo contrast at approximately three days, and a six-month expression pattern with lower values at higher active doses. Posted results instead show an early ordered-dose trend and describe a six-month no-difference hypothesis. The inspected registry does not present the originally stated pooled contrast. This is an unresolved reporting/analysis-concordance issue, not a misconduct conclusion and not a clean prespecified clinical-efficacy failure. It prevents treating an older program-page claim of dose-dependent biomarker benefit as conclusive. [P3–P4]

Ten participants' open-label periods were shortened by product shortage during the pandemic, according to posted limitations. Supply disruption and treatment toxicity must remain distinct. The protocol and registry provide formulation/development evidence; they do not give a current released manufacturing package, infant cardiac exposure, a PPCS functional endpoint or proof that this precursor is the best PPCS article. [P3–P4]

### Current development path and differentiation

The investigator program's **18 March 2026** update describes a pending food-additive process and manufacturing/distribution work with Ody Sibon for the United States and Netherlands. The Federal Register separately confirms filing of **FAP 5A4842** by the Spoonbill Foundation for 4′-phosphopantetheine as a nutrient in medical foods. A filing notice is not approval of the additive, a drug approval or a PPCS indication. No later final approval was located in this sprint's primary-source search; that is not an exhaustive legal-clearance conclusion. [P5–P6]

The opportunity therefore cannot be framed as inventing the first oral CoA-bypass formulation. It can be framed as selecting and qualifying an exact precursor/formulation for **PPCS cardiac function**, including whether a useful preservation indication is more realistic than reversal. PPanSH's human formulation history reduces uncertainty about the feasibility of making and administering a downstream precursor. It does not identify which species from oral pantethine caused the PPCS observations or transfer PKAN safety/PD into PPCS benefit.

### Fundable next milestone

**Recommendation:** preserve R5-C01 as a serious reserve and make the first funded step a **defined-species comparison**, rather than declaring oral pantethine exposure-qualified. Evaluate authenticated PanSH, PanSSPan and PPanSH as separate articles, with their actual matrix species and relevant products tracked. Include the published PPCS phenotypic benchmark and the CoA-Z development article as comparators without assuming either is the correct product.

The deciding result is useful force/fatigue or preservation after a qualified deficit in independent, commercially usable PPCS backgrounds at exposure that can plausibly be delivered. CoA restoration alone is insufficient; force increase and prevention of further loss are separate outcomes. Genetic controls and a post-deficit functional positive control are required to interpret a negative experiment. Existing patient-line restrictions remain as documented in round 5; an engineered permissible background is a route to qualify, not a ready model.

**Advance:** one exact species/product produces a repeatable, useful functional effect, with coherent active-species exposure and acceptable rhythm/mitochondrial behavior. **Stop or redirect the exact article:** activity only appears in an assay-specific redox mixture that cannot be delivered, exposure is implausible, or metabolite correction yields no useful function in a demonstrably responsive model. If only preservation is reproduced, assess that product on its own value instead of calling it reversal. These are appropriate early funding questions; an already positive PPCS randomized human trial is not a necessary entry criterion.
