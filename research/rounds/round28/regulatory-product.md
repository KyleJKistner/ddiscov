# Nifedipine–lidocaine: regulatory, product and IP feasibility

**Reviewed 9 November 2026. Exact starting product:** nifedipine 0.3% plus **lidocaine hydrochloride** 1.5%, rectal cream, for chronic anal fissure. This is a drug-development and transaction screen. Cost figures below are analyst planning assumptions; no supplier, owner, regulator or investigator was contacted. Source IDs resolve in [regulatory-evidence.json](regulatory-evidence.json).

## Decision

**The useful next step is a bounded assessment of an existing product and a specific territory or economic interest. A fresh US formulation program needs a separate thesis.** Commercial product quality, historical clinical activity and some systemic-exposure evidence already exist. More evidence that the combination can be manufactured or that healthy volunteers tolerate it would have limited incremental value unless it closes an identified regulatory or buyer requirement.

The evidence supports three distinct possibilities:

1. **A licensed mature-product territory or supply interest.** Obtain access to an existing dossier, manufacturing chain and a commercially useful territory. France and Canada demonstrate actual approvals; they also demonstrate that attractive territories may already have operators. Existing approvals do not establish that another territory is available or profitable. [R01–R12]
2. **A contractual interest in an existing developer's next milestone.** Steel/Fizurex provides an identifiable US development program. Any investment, co-development, royalty or distribution participation would require an actual opportunity and negotiated economics; none is established here. [R15–R16]
3. **An independent differentiated product.** A new delivery system, dose, claim or access advantage could be valuable, but requires a product-specific comparison against Antrolin, existing compounds and Fizurex. A generic aspiration to improve stability or convenience is insufficient: Antrolin already has a long approved shelf life and later delivery patents address those features. [R01, R32–R36]

The previously screened single package should therefore be replaced by stage-specific work. No new human trial is selected by this review.

## 2. US route: possible abbreviated reliance, substantial remaining obligations

### 505(b)(2) is an application pathway, not recognition of Antrolin

Section 505(b)(2) can use relevant information the applicant did not generate and for which it lacks a right of reference, including appropriate published literature and FDA findings for listed drugs. The sponsor must identify what it relies on and establish the scientific connection to its own formulation, route, dose and indication. Foreign approval does not itself make Antrolin a US reference listed drug. A complete product-quality package and evidence supporting the proposed changes remain necessary. The governing regulation is 21 CFR 314.54; FDA's May 2019 pathway guidance remains final, while the August 2026 replacement is explicitly **draft**. [R17–R19]

For this combination, the residual questions include local delivery and dose reproducibility, systemic exposure at the intended site and duration, local safety, the intended claim, and each component's contribution. These are gap-analysis questions, not a predetermined list of mandatory new trials.

### Fixed-combination contribution is a material issue

Under 21 CFR 300.50, each component must contribute to the claimed effects and its amount, frequency and duration must be appropriate for a population needing concurrent therapy. A study against lidocaine plus hydrocortisone does not, by itself, establish what lidocaine adds to nifedipine alone. Conversely, requiring lidocaine to heal tissue would miss its proposed anesthetic contribution: the claim and evidence must fit together. A factorial or component-comparison design is one possible solution, but this review does not assert FDA will require a particular four-arm study. [R20]

**Lidocaine alone is a different regulatory product.** FDA's OTC anorectal monograph M015 includes lidocaine at **2–5%** subject to its ingredient, formulation, route and labeling conditions. M015 permits products suitable for topical or intrarectal administration but individual provisions apply. It does not list nifedipine as an active ingredient. Antrolin's 1.5% lidocaine-hydrochloride combination and a fissure-healing claim cannot inherit monograph status from lidocaine alone. Prescription anesthetic approvals likewise do not validate this fixed combination. [R21]

### The endpoint precedent is pain, not automatic healing approval

RECTIV's current US indication is moderate-to-severe pain associated with chronic anal fissure. Its label describes a three-week placebo-controlled study with pain change over days 14–18; the treatment difference was approximately **7 mm on a 100 mm VAS**, confidence interval −13.6 to −0.4 mm. That is a precedent for a defined symptomatic claim, not approval of the nifedipine combination or acceptance of a healing endpoint. [R22]

The FDA medical review is a useful caution about execution: REC-C-001 initially missed the prespecified analysis, received a complete-response letter, and was ultimately supported through revised missing-data analysis following agency discussions. It also discusses manufacturing-site comparability and reference-drug exposure bridging. This history argues for an agreed estimand, rescue-treatment rules, missing-data plan and final commercial-product bridge before funding a pivotal study. It is not a license to rescue an unsuccessful study retrospectively. [R23]

### Foreign evidence and exclusivity

Foreign studies can support US applications when applicable to US practice/population and credibly conducted and verifiable. Rules address GCP, investigators, product identification, ethics and FDA validation; the applicability of current requirements to historical reports needs a regulatory assessment. Published literature may remain useful without a full foreign-study submission, but an old paper and foreign label are not interchangeable with a complete auditable dossier. [R24–R25]

Three-year exclusivity can attach where new human investigations, other than bioavailability studies, were conducted or sponsored by the applicant and essential to approval. It protects relevant conditions of approval against specified abbreviated applications; it is not a new patent on nifedipine or lidocaine, and a PK-only exercise does not automatically earn it. A nonexclusive purchase of a completed study does not automatically make the buyer a qualifying predecessor in interest. Both components are old active moieties, so a new combination should not be modeled as receiving new-chemical-entity exclusivity. Treat exclusivity as a conditional scenario, not guaranteed commercial protection. [R26]

## 4. Product quality and PK: the useful uncertainty is narrower than “can we make it?”

The AIFA RCP supports a conventional aluminum-tube product with **three-year unopened stability, 30 days after opening and storage at or below 25 °C**. It also describes 12 healthy volunteers with undetectable nifedipine and minimal unquantifiable lidocaine in two. Those are existing product facts, not proof that every cream base, applicator or packaged dose behaves the same. [R01]

The Canadian monograph states nifedipine is light-sensitive and may become a pharmacologically inactive derivative, requiring light protection. It discusses systemic absorption on damaged anorectal mucosa: nifedipine in a few patients and quantifiable lidocaine in all, below the described CNS-toxicity threshold. A new systemic-exposure study must address what the existing evidence fails to answer—such as changed delivery, dose, excipients or repeated maximal use—rather than merely repeat healthy-volunteer PK. [R09]

A product-specific CMC diligence plan should examine:

- Provenance and transferability of methods, specifications, stability reports and batch records; API and lidocaine salt form; drug-substance and product impurity control.
- A stability-indicating assay for both actives, degradants, light protection during manufacture/use, and proposed container closure and in-use period.
- Uniformity and delivered dose, rheology/pH and appropriate release/permeation comparisons, including the relevance and limitations of any in-vitro assay to anorectal tissue.
- Microbial quality and preservative performance, container compatibility, local excipient tolerance, and reproducibility of the proposed supplier/process.

These are proposed diligence questions, not claims that the existing product failed them. An unchanged, licensed product may allow substantial reuse. A changed applicator or vehicle can create new comparability and safety work.

Clinical source reconciliation also has value: the Canadian monograph labels P001/98 as a three-week/day 21 pivotal study, whereas the published Perrotti/NIHR account emphasizes six weeks/day 42. Obtain the protocol, clinical study report and labeling rationale before representing the historical dossier as fully reconciled. The difference is not resolved by this desk review. [R09, R12]

## 5. Competition and patent map

### Steel is an active competitor, not an approved substitute

Steel's own site describes Fizurex as a single-use nifedipine/lidocaine wipe. Altasciences' **30 June 2026** release states a pivotal GLP toxicology study was completed with a favorable profile, with an IND planned for Q3 2026 and first-human studies planned for 2027. These are sponsor/CRO statements, not publicly audited raw data or FDA decisions. As of this review, no primary confirmation of IND submission/clearance was found; targeted ClinicalTrials.gov queries did not identify Fizurex or an exact Steel lead-sponsor record. Those negative searches do not prove an IND does not exist. Fizurex's exact marketed-intent strengths were not verified from the sponsor page. [R15–R16, R40]

**Rights and access are separate explicit variables.** No observed option price, license upfront, royalty, minimum purchase commitment or inventory requirement was found. Use `all-in next-stage cost = selected work package + negotiated access/rights cash + noncancellable commitments + relevant overhead`, with an unknown access input rather than zero. Compare price sensitivities and retained investor economics before major work. A large fee can erase the thesis even when technical reuse is easy. Royalties are contractual revenue deductions; equity dilution changes the ownership share. Model those separately, including any downstream investor distribution restriction. Company license receipts do not automatically reach an investor.

## 7. Concrete next milestone and stopping rules

The next reviewable deliverable is a **territory/product/rights gap memo** with one lead case and a reserve case. It should name the product/label, existing operator and known approvals; map a plausible dossier owner and supply chain; identify a lawful route and required missing evidence; estimate territory-level demand and net contribution after supply, promotion and obligations; and show exactly which interest could be acquired or financed. A US developer participation case can be a separate comparator.

The decision can then be made under uncertainty:

- **Continue** when a plausible unfilled commercial need, access path and bounded value-changing task survive reasonable cost and price sensitivities. Exact quotes are not required to screen; later commitments require appropriate terms.
- **Revise the package** when the molecule is useful but the selected territory is allocated, the route needs a different dossier or formulation, or investor cash depends on an unmodeled launch/financing obligation.
- **Stop the independent experiment** when it mainly recreates existing stability/PK evidence, lacks a buyer-relevant differentiation, requires a later capital program with no plausible handoff, or is less valuable than preserving cash for another opportunity.

No numerical probability of regulatory approval, paid transaction or principal recovery is inferred from product authorization abroad, a patent publication, a successful toxicology announcement or these planning cost bands.
