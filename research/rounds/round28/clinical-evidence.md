# Nifedipine/lidocaine for anal fissure: independent clinical audit

**Candidate:** `PROPOSED-PKG-NIF-LIDO-CAF`

**Evidence checked:** 9 November 2026. Targeted desk review; no outreach or experimental work.

## Decision

**Continue this as a clinically credible product/dossier opportunity, with a narrower first milestone.** Nifedipine has a meaningful clinical signal across independent studies. The incremental value of adding lidocaine, the durability of benefit, and the transferability of a particular manufactured article are less established. The useful next evidence package is an exact-product and clinical-dossier gap analysis; a new human trial or generic PK repeat should not be assumed necessary.

On clinical grounds, the candidate remains plausible for deeper review. The integrated Round 28 decision places it in the territorial/partnered reserve after considering the commercial and regulatory evidence; clinical plausibility alone does not establish first investment rank, a defensible price premium, or high-probability recovery of capital. The financial comparison must incorporate attainable territory, product differentiation, the actual reliance/registration route and competing development.

## What each component contributes

The existing comparisons chiefly test **adding or substituting nifedipine**, not whether adding lidocaine to nifedipine improves outcomes. The 1999 acute-fissure trial used nifedipine alone. The 2002 chronic trial included the same lidocaine strength in both arms. Kujur's later trial added a calcium-channel blocker to background lidocaine. These designs support nifedipine activity; they cannot estimate lidocaine's additional benefit over nifedipine alone. [CL01, CL02, CL08]

No randomized, same-strength chronic-fissure comparison of nifedipine/lidocaine against nifedipine alone was retrieved in this targeted search. That is a retrieval limit, not proof that none exists. A local-anesthetic rationale is plausible, but early pain relief, adherence, healing and sustained benefit require separate claims. A marketed combination is not by itself evidence that both components are necessary.

France's HAS independently reached a related concern: its 2023 assessment considered lidocaine's benefit unestablished, pain relief insufficiently characterized and recurrence uncertain. It gave a favorable reimbursement opinion but low clinical benefit and **ASMR V/no demonstrated added clinical value**. That is an HTA judgment in its assessed indication, not a conclusion that nifedipine has no activity or a prediction of a US decision. [CL14]

## Clinical evidence, with boundaries preserved

| Evidence unit | Observation | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Antropoli 1999, acute, randomized/blinded/multicenter, n=283 | Nifedipine 0.2% alone: 95% remission at 21 days, versus 50% with lidocaine 1%/hydrocortisone 1%. | Supports the nifedipine component; different population and article. [CL01] |
| Perrotti 2002, chronic, randomized/blinded, n=110 | Nifedipine 0.3%/lidocaine 1.5%: 94.5% healing versus 16.4% with lidocaine 1.5%/hydrocortisone 1%; publication endpoint day 42. | Large signal, but one small trial with a debatable comparator and no nifedipine-alone arm. [CL02] |
| Golfam 2010, chronic | Nifedipine 0.5% alone: 42/60 healed versus 6/50 with lidocaine 2%. Full methods show 120 allocated, then 10 excluded. | Independent monotherapy support; complete-case bias and a different strength. [CL06] |
| Kujur 2020, chronic, open-label | Nifedipine 0.3% cream **and** lidocaine 2% cream: 28/30 healed; diltiazem plus lidocaine 26/30; lidocaine alone 11/30 at four weeks. | Steroid-free support; 100 allocated/90 analyzed. Coadministration is not verified identical fixed-combination product. [CL08] |
| Kumar 2026, randomized/blinded, n=110 | Mixed acute/chronic/acute-on-chronic; nifedipine 0.3% plus **lidocaine 2%**, healing 70.9%; diltiazem 2% plus lidocaine 2%, 67.3%. | Useful independent evidence, but not exact 1.5% lidocaine replication or chronic-only proof. Similar results do not establish formal equivalence. [CL10] |
| Vardhani 2026, retrospective, n=431 | Exact nominal 0.3%/1.5% strength; 133/141 chronic patients healed by eight weeks. | Selected, uncontrolled complete-record series; no recurrence evidence and no established vehicle equivalence. [CL11] |

### The 2002 control requires precision

Both arms contained lidocaine; nifedipine replaced hydrocortisone. This prevents clean isolation of the effects of nifedipine versus an inert vehicle. It is reasonable to question clinical relevance of the comparator, as HAS does. It is **not** justified to assert that the entire result was caused by a steroid impairing healing. In a different 1986 first-episode acute trial, healing with hydrocortisone was 82.4%, compared with 60% for lignocaine and 87% for baths/bran. That older result does not validate the chronic comparator, but it prevents an overconfident mechanistic dismissal. [CL02, CL12, CL14]

The newer steroid-free chronic comparisons mean the biological case no longer rests solely on the 2002 contrast. Their weaknesses still matter: open or limited blinding, post-allocation exclusions, heterogeneous definitions, differing strengths/vehicles and small samples. No pooled efficacy estimate is defensible from this targeted extraction.

### A material public-record discrepancy

The Canadian monograph identifies pivotal study **P001/98**, n=110, and reports 52/55 versus 9/55 at **day 21**, counting complete or almost-complete healing. The Perrotti publication abstract describes the same percentages at **day 42** after six weeks. Matching sample, arms and response counts suggest the same study lineage, but that is an inference pending the protocol/CSR. Count it once. Reconcile treatment duration, endpoint timing and healing definition before relying on the package. [CL02, CL15]

Canada's initial authorization was 30 May 2024; the reviewed monograph was revised 13 June 2025. It is a regulatory precedent for its specified chronic-primary-fissure population, not a newly independent efficacy cohort or evidence of transfer rights. [CL15]

## Durability and the size of useful benefit

The impressive short-term response should not become a durable-cure assumption. Perrotti reports 3/52 recurrences within a year **after unblinding**. In the independent Ezri/Susmallian chronic trial, nifedipine healing was 89% versus 58% with glyceryl trinitrate, but recurrence was 42% with nifedipine; pain improvement did not differ between arms. Golfam reported 11/42 recurrences at 12 months. These studies differ in article, follow-up and outcome definitions; their recurrence rates are not interchangeable. [CL02, CL03, CL06]

For acute disease, the 2023 nifedipine-alone versus diltiazem study found 41/53 versus 27/50 healed at eight weeks. It supports monotherapy activity in another cohort, while its recurrence denominators and pain-duration units have reporting inconsistencies. Acute spontaneous/supportive improvement and chronic refractory disease must remain separate evidence units. [CL09]

The 2026 blinded comparison used a pain/bleeding/quality-of-life composite and reported improvement within both groups. An active-active comparison without vehicle does not isolate the treatment effect from background improvement. Its abstract does not provide a verified chronic-only result or longer-term durability. [CL10]

## Safety and exposure: existing evidence is useful, not absolute

Two exact-strength PK reports already exist:

- **12 healthy volunteers, repeated exposure for seven days:** sporadic drug signals were below assay quantification limits; no serious events or significant hemodynamic/ECG changes were reported. This is small healthy-mucosa evidence. [CL04]
- **24 patients after hemorrhoidectomy, one application:** lidocaine was measurable in all; median peak 73.6 ng/mL, with three peaks above 1000 ng/mL. Nifedipine was detected in five, at 5.9–18.8 ng/mL. No serious acute events or significant mean hemodynamic changes were reported. The authors specifically identified repeated-dose damaged-mucosa exposure as an unresolved question. [CL05]

Thus “no systemic absorption” is incorrect. The healthy repeated-dose study and injured-tissue single-dose study answer different questions. Same strengths alone do not establish equivalent exposure for a new vehicle, applicator or regimen. The Canadian label carries a methemoglobinemia warning and cardiovascular contraindications; the absence of serious events in small trials cannot establish broad safety. [CL15]

The larger postoperative efficacy trial is also relevant counterevidence. In 270 randomized patients, the combination did not improve its primary time-to-rescue-analgesia endpoint: 6.3 versus 7 hours, p=.25. A secondary six-hour pain signal does not turn this into a successful fissure trial; fissure patients were excluded. Its reported day 7 p < .054 does not establish conventional p < .05 significance. Bracco funded the study and employed three authors. [CL07]

## Product quantity and the economic model

The Canadian label specifies approximately 2.5–3 g twice daily for three weeks. This implies 105–126 g, or 3.5–4.2 nominal 30 g tubes; dispensing whole tubes would require four to five. This is a calculation from the labeled regimen, not observed purchasing or a treatment recommendation. A one-tube retail price is not a full-course price. Dispensing, adherence and course-price effects on uptake remain unknown; a future wipe or differently dosed compounded article needs its own quantity calculation. [CL15]

## Replication and bias audit

There are independent supportive studies, so “only one human study” is too broad. However, independent studies of nifedipine, different lidocaine strengths, coadministered creams, acute disease and postoperative pain are not independent replications of the exact chronic-fissure product claim.

The 1999/2002/2006/2009/2010 Italian reports share key investigators; they include distinct cohorts but should not be treated as five independent research lineages. The 2026 retrospective series excludes missing records and important patient groups and has two manufacturer-employed authors. Its within-person improvement cannot resolve component attribution or uncommon harm. [CL01, CL02, CL04, CL05, CL07, CL11]

The 2023 SIUCP guideline recommends calcium-channel blockers or nitrates for typical hypertonic chronic fissure at 1B, while its specific 0.3%/1.5% combination recommendation is weaker, 2B. This supports clinical plausibility while preserving uncertainty about this exact article. [CL13]

## Lowest credible next evidence milestone

**Proposed deliverable:** an exact-article and claim map that selects among an existing-product dossier route, a changed delivery/formulation route and nifedipine alone. This is decision-changing if it identifies a small, valuable remaining bridge—or establishes that a much larger efficacy program is unavoidable.

1. Reconcile P001/98 with the publication and labels. Map prespecified endpoints, analysis population, follow-up and the role of lidocaine.
2. Match the contemplated product to each evidence source: strength, vehicle, delivered dose, local application, stability and exposure. Distinguish biological support from usable product evidence.
3. Compare the intended benefit with current alternatives and emerging products. Choose the exact claim before designing a test.
4. Map the remaining clinical gap to a target jurisdiction. Full source-data access and rights of reliance remain separate diligence questions.
5. Commission an experiment only if it resolves that gap. Pharmaceutical comparability/delivery work can precede human work for a changed article. A component-benefit trial could be necessary for a new combination claim; it is not automatically the next tranche. Existing evidence may already support a narrower reliance route.

A focused desk package could take roughly **2/4/8 weeks** under low/base/high analyst planning assumptions, excluding unavailable sponsor records, regulator waiting time and experiments. This is not a vendor quote. The currently completed public audit is the first part of that package.

**Clinical assessment:** retain biological/product plausibility; confidence in nifedipine activity is moderate, confidence in incremental lidocaine benefit and durable response is low. Spend the next analytical effort on article/claim selection and the existing dossier. Raise or lower investment rank only after integrating the commercial and regulatory findings, rather than translating clinical healing percentages into a probability of investment return.
