# Nifedipine/lidocaine: commercial and territory audit

**As of 9 November 2026 UTC. Desk work before inquiries. No external contact or investment.**

## Decision

**Downgrade the generic $325,000 bridge from first-place to an incumbent-specific territory/program reserve.** The combination is a mature commercial product in several countries, and US development already has a direct competitor. Its clinical familiarity is useful, but a new investor must buy or create a specific commercially valuable interest. A small-country royalty alone is unlikely to repay a substantial bridge package. A US interest can support an upside case, but it requires a funded approval program, competitive product/data rights, payer acceptance and substantially more than a modest tolerability or dossier-transfer study.

This corrects a limitation of Round 27: its first-place placement did not identify Fizurex or adequately map the existing international commercial footprint. The right response is a lower rank and a narrower transaction hypothesis, not treating missing offers as a universal screening gate.

## 2. Demand and willingness to pay

The best retrieved population-based primary anchor is a regional US managed-care study from 2005–2011: diagnosed annual incidence 1.1/1,000 person-years,1,243 cases, and 14.2% with a coded visit at least 12 weeks later. That last number is a follow-up definition, not true chronic prevalence. The study was Ventrus-funded; its old treatment/fill patterns should not be mechanically transferred to 2026. [LC19]

An Italian 17-center study of 22,016 patients provides more recent practice context: medical therapy was common in chronic disease and nifedipine/lidocaine already widely used. Specialist selection prevents national population extrapolation. [LC20] ASCRS accepts topical calcium-channel blockers in chronic fissure care; that supports a clinical use case, not a premium for an unspecified new presentation. [LC21]

The commercial pool is therefore **diagnosed eligible cases × actual filling × product capture × courses × packs**. The model uses a deliberately uncertain 20–50% adult chronic/refractory eligibility fraction and does not add a lifetime-prevalence backlog to incident cases. Repeat courses are modeled separately for the same captured patients. Recurrence does not create a new unique patient or guarantee another purchase.

Price competition is concrete. PHC advertises 0.5% nifedipine/3% lidocaine 30 g for $45; Uptown lists 0.5%/5%30 g for $54.99 with Texas-only delivery. Both are different compounded articles, and neither price is manufacturer revenue. [LC14–15] Cosette and Acrux/TruPharma provide approved generic nitroglycerin competition. Historical issuer disclosures put the nitroglycerin 0.4% US market at about $21–23m, with an August 2024 reference of 47,000 packs. Those channel-market numbers exclude much compounding and are not audited net sales or a 2026 forecast. [LC16–18]

There is a plausible quality/access need, but it requires evidence. A2013 Ventrus-supported study found potency/uniformity problems in diltiazem from a small one-city pharmacy sample; it cannot establish current nifedipine/lidocaine defect rates. [LC22] Fizurex already targets standardization and convenience. A new package must solve something more specific than “manufacture the familiar combination.”

The most direct payer challenge comes from France: HAS judged Nifexine's medical benefit low and found no demonstrated added benefit; it questioned the comparator, pain/recurrence evidence and the demonstrated value of lidocaine. That is not proof the product is ineffective, but it is strong contrary evidence against assuming a premium for the combination. [LC05]

## 3. What actual historical transactions show

The Ventrus/SLA transaction covered **two topical GI assets** in the US, Canada and Mexico. The initial $250k fee plus $50k reimbursement was already included in the $1.087876m PBS assignment consideration: adding both would double-count. Equity, management and clinical obligations were separate. The 2013 note records $41.5k monthly payments per managed program, $4.2m in diltiazem clinical reimbursement, $1m enrollment milestones and $400k for the final report. The earlier prospectus describes mid-to-upper single-digit royalties and an improvement-license-back. These are historical stage-specific obligations, not current Antrolin terms. [LC23–24]

The much larger Rectiv deal was for an **already FDA-approved product**: Aptalis paid $20m upfront, with $20m at first commercial sale, up to $40m sales milestones, double-digit sales royalties and separate supply economics. Its $139.2m accounting fair value included contingent obligations and royalties; it was not cash upfront and cannot price a small new bridge. [LC27]

VEN307 subsequently failed the primary pain comparison in its second pivotal trial. The license was terminated after FDA feedback and a strategy change. [LC25–26] The combination of a legitimate clinical use, manufacturing quality argument and a real territory license did not remove pivotal-study risk.

## 4. The modeled economic interests

All numerical scenarios below are analyst assumptions. They describe dated branches under uncertainty, not market forecasts or offers. **The investor owns 100% of the isolated vehicle throughout.** A retained royalty spread is not a fractional equity stake.

**Royalty vehicle:** a downstream commercializer pays 8%/12%/15% of its net product sales to the vehicle; the vehicle passes 6%/7%/8% upstream, retaining 2%/5%/7%. Those are assumed contract terms informed only in scale by historical licensing. The downstream party funds trials, manufacturing, inventory, selling and commercial launch. The vehicle still incurs annual administration and a small launch/legal interface cost. An actual licensor may require a different supply or royalty structure; no free ownership is assumed.

**Direct territory vehicle:** the vehicle receives net product sales and bears the supply and operating costs. For a small-territory supplied product, the upstream owner's economics are already inside the supply-price assumption and are not deducted again as a royalty. The US direct case separately includes underlying license royalty and a $7m/$12m/$25m favorable/base/adverse later-development allowance before application fees. These are conditional scopes with explicit work components, not regulator-mandated programs or guaranteed approval budgets. The direct US case is a capital-needs benchmark, not an executable $325k plan.

**Existing local operator:** a separate small-territory route assumes contracted access to existing infrastructure, retaining 100% ownership of the territory vehicle. Annual vehicle administration is $5,000; incremental operator fixed costs are $10,000/$7,500/$5,000 in adverse/base/favorable cases, with setup of $25,000/$20,000/$15,000. Supply and variable selling still apply. This tests whether marginal costs make a useful difference; it does not assume an established operator or an offered contract.

**Financial boundary:** the prior $325k total package is paid in year 1 in every case. It assumes access, legal, data and bounded development fit inside that package; there is no verified entry price. Any necessary fee beyond the package increases contributed capital. Subsequent administration/launch/development cash calls are shown separately. If continuation is unfunded, stop after the bounded package; the modeled later calls are not commitments. No distributions are recycled. A 25% planning tax/withholding reserve is applied to positive annual cash, with no benefit assumed from earlier losses. All remaining cash is distributed to the 100% owner. No terminal business value, royalty tail, inventory recovery or speculative buyer upfront is counted.

**Dose-to-units correction:** the exact Canadian cream label specifies approximately 2.5–3 g twice daily for 3 weeks, implying 105–126 g and 4–5 whole 30 g packs for full supply. [LC28] An earlier preliminary model used 1–2 packs without this anchor; the main model is corrected. The adverse 2-pack case explicitly means interrupted purchase, not a complete course. Fill assumptions were lowered from 50/65/80% to 35/50/65% to reflect a price/adherence stress, but there is no measured elasticity. A separate half-fill stress below holds all other inputs fixed.

Catchments are modeled denominators, not census counts or available territories. At the assumed US pack prices, a purchased episode produces manufacturer net receipts of $100/$500/$1,000; these are not patient out-of-pocket prices. The US premium cases therefore need substantial payer acceptance. This is a mature-cream-equivalent model, **not a unit-economic model for the Fizurex wipe**. The favorable 219,375 packs far exceeds the cited historical nitroglycerin 47,000-pack market; it requires expansion or capture from compounding. No such expansion is forecast.

Foreign launch is assumed in years 4/3/2, with respective ramps 0.25→0.60; 0.25→0.60→1.00; and 0.50→0.85→1.00→1.00. The US royalty case assumes participation in an existing program: no launch within five years for downside, 25% of steady sales in year 5 for base, and 50% in year 4 then 100% in year 5 for favorable. The independent US program instead launches at 50% in year 5 only in its favorable case; base and adverse have no sales within five years. These are conditional timing stresses, not expected regulatory dates.

Annual models net launch costs against receipts within the same year. They therefore do **not** establish peak liquidity or total gross capital calls under actual payment dates. Prelaunch setup, inventory, minimum orders and customer collection could require additional cash before sales receipts arrive. These results are not binding capital caps. The input conventions make this boundary explicit.

Without the favorable fee waiver, modeled five-year contributions rise to **$12.02m**, while distributions remain **$6.85m**. This is a fee sensitivity using the current rate, not a future fee prediction.

### A longer horizon can change principal recovery

The US base royalty ramp reaches 60% in year 6 and full sales in year 7. No new development, terminal value or extra territory is added. Ongoing rights, sales and the ability to continue are assumed; they are not verified. Thus these are conditional continuation calculations, not a forecast.

Both can recover nominal capital under these continuation assumptions while failing the illustrative 10% annual hurdle. The distinction between principal recovery, positive nominal return and adequate return for time and risk matters. Neither horizon supplies a probability of achieving the scenario.

For the five-year favorable shared-operator case, discounting the modeled net operating cash at 10% reduces the supported initial package from approximately $207k to **$144k**, before any probability haircut. After the hypothetical $75k non-entry work, only approximately **$69k** remains for entry at that illustrative return hurdle. The $75k allowance is a narrow-scope sensitivity, below the regulatory report’s $100k–400k territory technical package. At $100k of other initial work, the same discounted entry headroom is about **$44k**; at $150k, it is already negative. A smaller useful package could therefore be worth comparing, but a full territorial filing cannot be assigned the narrower allowance without justification. The original $325k proposal is not the right starting commitment by default.

## 5. Is a $325k bridge worth funding?

Not as a generic transfer/PK/tolerability project. The original nominal screen at p = 55% and q = 25% required $2.36m of net investor cash on the paying branch. Here p means a useful bounded-package result conditional on access; q means investor cash within the analyst five-year horizon conditional on that result and modeled rights. Neither is a healing rate or approval probability. Holding p constant while using a more cautious commercial q of 10–15% raises that hurdle to $3.94–5.91m, before optional later capital. These are judgmental sensitivity inputs, not calibrated principal-recovery probabilities.

The territorial royalty calculations show why a modest clinical project can be scientifically successful and financially inadequate. At a 5% retained spread, even $1m in annual commercializer net sales generates only $50k before administration, taxes and timing. A launch late in year 5 leaves little time to recoup; a royalty tail may have value but has not paid principal within the comparison horizon. An acquisition or paid option could accelerate cash, but no transaction price is assumed.

**Positive-case conditions:** a still-attainable, materially sized territory with a usable dossier; existing distribution or independently evidenced orders; enough retained royalty spread or operating contribution after the underlying owner is paid; subsequent development financed without uncapped owner commitments; and a clearly consequential regulatory or product gap. A sponsor-paid contract can instead earn margin from conforming delivery, including negative scientific findings, but requires a separate acceptance/collection model.

**Next desk work:** compare the existing Neopharmed/Flynn/Seaford/Steel chains and known registration dossiers, isolate one still-useful contribution, and calculate the largest entry fee or smallest retained economics the actual territory can support. Only then should an inquiry test that narrow hypothesis. There is no reason to request generic licensing quotes for every country or commission a new formulation study first.
