# Round 28: deep nifedipine/lidocaine review

> **Priority update:** The [reconciled current shortlist](../../../decisions/current-shortlist.md) governs new work. This record retains its dated evidence, scope, estimates and conclusions; its internal priorities are historical.

**Reviewed 9 November 2026 UTC. Candidate: `PROPOSED-PKG-NIF-LIDO-CAF`.** This focused review follows the Round 27 broad screen. The starting article is **nifedipine 0.3% plus lidocaine hydrochloride 1.5% rectal cream for chronic anal fissure**, represented by Antrolin and related authorized territorial products. Findings about other strengths, separate creams, acute fissure and postoperative pain retain those boundaries.

## Decision

**Move the undifferentiated product-bridge proposal from first desk priority to a territorial/partnered reserve.** The clinical case is credible. The investment case needs a narrower source of incremental value than another demonstration that the combination works, can be manufactured or has limited systemic absorption.

Three developments change the original economic package:

1. **Much of the proposed technical value already exists.** There is an authorized stable product, historical clinical data and healthy-volunteer and injured-mucosa PK. Repeating those functions does not create a comparably valuable new asset unless it closes an identified product-specific gap.
2. **Commercial access and competition are more constrained than the broad screen captured.** Neopharmed reports global Antrolin commercial rights; France and Canada have authorized marketed products; the UK has a named developer; Steel is actively developing a US nifedipine/lidocaine wipe. This does not mean every territory or participation interest is unavailable, but a generic vacant-market thesis is insufficient.
3. **A small territory can offer modest technical risk and weak retained cash simultaneously.** Low observed European pricing and inexpensive compounded alternatives challenge the royalty model. Direct operation retains more contribution while adding launch, inventory, local operating and collection obligations. A US program offers a larger commercial opportunity with a much larger development and financing task.

These are evidence and estimated-economics conclusions, not an insistence on receiving offers before ranking. The remaining three Round 27 priorities—APR-246, topical erlotinib and CBD3063—retain their relative unresolved order; this review does not establish that their own economics are attractive or appoint a new fourth finalist. See [current opportunities](../../../decisions/current-shortlist.md).

## What the clinical evidence does—and does not—support

| Question | Best current interpretation | Decision consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Does topical nifedipine have useful clinical activity? | Moderate confidence. Multiple controlled studies support short-term healing, although populations, articles and study quality vary. | Existing-drug biology is a real advantage over a speculative mechanism. |
| Does lidocaine add meaningful benefit to nifedipine in the exact chronic-fissure product? | Unresolved. No direct same-strength combination-versus-nifedipine-alone randomized comparison was retrieved. | Do not translate combination healing into proven component benefit or a price premium. |
| Is the often-cited 94.5% a durable success probability? | No. It is a short-term outcome in one small study with a questionable comparator; recurrence evidence differs markedly across reports. | Keep healing, pain, recurrence, regulatory success and investor cash separate. |
| Is a new generic PK or stability program the obvious next step? | No. Exact-strength PK and a stable commercial article already exist. | Choose a target product, claim and jurisdiction, then identify the remaining gap. |

The 1999 multicenter acute-fissure trial used **nifedipine alone**: 95% remission at 21 days versus 50% with lidocaine/hydrocortisone. The 2002 chronic study reported **94.5% versus 16.4% healing** at six weeks, but both arms contained lidocaine 1.5%; nifedipine replaced hydrocortisone. The latter comparison cannot identify what adding lidocaine to nifedipine contributes. It is also unjustified to assert that hydrocortisone caused the entire effect. [Clinical sources CL01–CL02, CL12](clinical-evidence.json).

Independent steroid-free evidence helps preserve the biological case. Kujur's 2020 study reported healing in 28/30 receiving nifedipine plus separate lidocaine cream, 26/30 receiving diltiazem plus lidocaine, and 11/30 receiving lidocaine alone. It was open-label, excluded ten of 100 allocated patients from analysis, and did not verify equivalence to the fixed 0.3%/1.5% product. The 2026 blinded comparison reported 70.9% versus 67.3% healing with nifedipine versus diltiazem, both with **2% lidocaine**, in mixed acute/chronic disease. Similar observed results do not prove formal equivalence. [CL08, CL10](clinical-evidence.json).

The exact-strength retrospective 2026 series is additional supportive evidence, with 133/141 chronic patients healed by eight weeks. Its uncontrolled complete-record design, manufacturer affiliations and missing recurrence assessment prevent treating it as independent randomized confirmation. The larger postoperative trial is material counterevidence for a different claim: 270 randomized patients and no significant improvement in the primary time-to-rescue-analgesia endpoint. Neither population should be silently merged with chronic fissure. [CL07, CL11](clinical-evidence.json).

France's [HAS assessment](https://www.has-sante.fr/jcms/p_3410031/fr/nifexine-nifedipine/lidocaine-chlorhydrate-douleurs-associees-a-la-fissure-anale-chronique) gave a favorable reimbursement opinion but **low clinical benefit and ASMR V/no demonstrated added clinical value**. It cited the small single-center pivotal trial, debatable comparator, unestablished lidocaine contribution and limited pain/recurrence evidence. This is a payer assessment, not a finding that nifedipine is inactive or a prediction of a US regulatory decision.

### A pivotal-record discrepancy needs reconciliation

The Canadian product monograph identifies **P001/98**, n=110, with 52/55 versus 9/55 complete or almost-complete healing at **day 21**. The Perrotti publication describes the matching percentages at **day 42** after six weeks. The matching arms, sample and counts suggest the same study lineage; this is an inference pending the protocol and clinical study report. These could be different visits and healing definitions within the same cohort rather than an error. Count the cohort once and leave the relationship unresolved. No sponsor records were accessed. [CL02, CL15](clinical-evidence.json).

### Absorption has been characterized, with limits

The exact-strength product has a repeated seven-day study in **12 healthy volunteers** and a single-application study in **24 patients after hemorrhoidectomy**. In the latter, lidocaine was measurable in all patients, with three peaks above 1000 ng/mL; no serious acute events or significant mean hemodynamic changes were reported. These are not evidence of zero absorption or a blanket safety waiver for a changed vehicle, dose, applicator or repeated use on damaged tissue. [CL04–CL05](clinical-evidence.json).

The [clinical audit](clinical-evidence.md) preserves designs, denominators, attrition, source access limits, recurrence findings and overlapping research lineages. It does not pool heterogeneous studies into a spurious single efficacy probability.

## Select the route before selecting the experiment

**Existing-product transfer or territorial registration** may use an accessible dossier and supply chain. France provides an actual mutual-recognition precedent; Canada shows another authorization. Their existence reduces some uncertainty but does not authorize a new applicant or jurisdiction. The selected product, source-data rights, legal basis, local requirements and market economics have to fit together.

For a UK recognition hypothesis, current MHRA rules add an important limit: reference approvals older than five years are ineligible. The Italian and French original approvals exceed that window. Canada's 2024 approval meets Route B timing only; the same product, qualifying standalone assessment, appropriate rights and unredacted reports remain necessary. This is not automatic UK eligibility. [R31](regulatory-evidence.json).

**An independent US program** requires a separate development and financing thesis. 505(b)(2) can support reliance on relevant prior evidence, but does not recognize a foreign approval automatically or remove the scientific bridge to the proposed product. Fixed-combination rules require each component to contribute to the claimed effects. Lidocaine's proposed pain benefit need not be a healing effect, but must be established appropriately. A particular factorial design is not assumed mandatory. [FDA pathway guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/124848/download); [21 CFR 300.50](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-300/subpart-B/section-300.50).

**A delivery change** needs an actual advantage. Published Steel claims concern a fibrous wipe and do not establish that ordinary cream infringes; another family concerns exact-strength micro-enemas. Old combination disclosures weaken a basic-composition novelty thesis. The detailed preliminary claim map preserves current-status verification limits and makes no freedom-to-operate conclusion. Inventing a different applicator does not by itself establish protectability, clinical benefit or willingness to pay. [R32–R37](regulatory-evidence.json).

The stage-specific cost ladder in the [regulatory report](regulatory-product.md#6-a-bounded-cost-ladder) replaces the single generic bridge estimate. An existing-product territory's **technical/regulatory package is modeled at $100k–400k**, conditional on accessible files, unchanged product/manufacturer and no new efficacy study, **excluding rights, inventory, launch and working capital**. If new human development is needed, the work moves into a different cost and duration class. These are bottom-up analyst planning bands, not quotations or settled regulatory requirements.

## What retained cash could look like

The commercial question is narrower than population prevalence. A regional US managed-care study found 1.1 diagnosed fissures per 1,000 person-years; it does not establish a current national chronic-fissure market. The model separately estimates adult chronic/refractory eligibility, filling, product capture, repeat courses and packs. Its 10 million non-US and 300 million US treatment catchments are illustrative denominators, not offered territories or current census estimates. [Commercial source LC19](commercial-evidence.json).

Observable prices challenge a premium assumption. France lists **€9.95 per 30 g tube before dispensing fees**; Albania's official CIF list records approximately **€7.06 per pack**. US pharmacies advertise different-strength compounded 30 g products at **$45 and $54.99**. These are different price concepts and products, not manufacturer net receipts or guaranteed prices in another market. [LC04, LC07, LC14–LC15](commercial-evidence.json).

**One tube is not automatically one course.** The Canadian cream label implies four to five 30 g tubes for complete three-week supply, depending on dose. The model uses two for an explicitly interrupted-purchase downside, four at the lower labeled dose for base, and five at the upper dose for favorable. It reduces assumed filling and separately halves uptake as a sensitivity. The US cream model is not a unit-economic model for Fizurex wipes. [Clinical unit check](clinical-evidence.json); [LC28](commercial-evidence.json).

### Five-year conditional scenarios

All figures are **analyst assumptions**, not forecasts, offers or probability-weighted values. The starting package is the historical $325k screening benchmark, not an instruction to spend that amount. Later cash calls are included on an annual net basis. The investor owns **100% of the dedicated vehicle**; upstream royalties, supply costs and local operating costs remain separate. A royalty case assumes the downstream developer funds all later development and launch. Positive cash receives a 25% planning tax/withholding reserve; no speculative exit payment, terminal value or tax-loss credit is included.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Small territory, royalty / base | $418,120 | $0 | −$418,120 |
| Small territory, existing operator / base | $376,218 | $0 | −$376,218 |
| Small territory, existing operator / favorable | $325,000 | $206,781 | −$118,219 |
| Existing US program, royalty / base | $395,445 | $0 | −$395,445 |
| Existing US program, royalty / favorable | $385,000 | $3,372,656 | $2,987,656 |

Annual netting does not establish peak liquidity: setup, inventory and collections can require money before receipts arrive. These are neither binding spending caps nor all possible future obligations. Later calls are optional continuation decisions; a funding-unavailable branch can stop instead, with the initial work potentially lost.

The favorable small-territory operator case assumes approximately **$219k annual net product sales**, a 45% contribution before operator/admin fixed costs, and launch in year 2. It supports approximately **$207k total initial spending for nominal five-year principal recovery**, before a probability adjustment. At an illustrative 10% annual return hurdle, the supported initial amount falls to approximately **$144k**. If other initial legal/regulatory/data work consumes an assumed $75k, only about **$69k** remains for entry at that hurdle. Using $100k of other initial work—the lower end of the regulatory territory-package band—leaves approximately **$44k** for entry; at $150k, that discounted headroom is negative. The $75k allowance is a narrower scope sensitivity, not the budget for a complete territorial filing. These are calculated ceilings, not offers or valuations.

That case is fragile: halving the filled-purchase fraction reduces the nominal supported initial package to approximately **$83k**, leaving only about **$8k** for entry after the same $75k of other work. Existing infrastructure improves the marginal economics, but does not make a six-figure entry fee automatically attractive.

The favorable US royalty case assumes **219,375 30 g packs and $43.875m annual manufacturer net sales**, a retained 7% royalty spread, launch in year 4 and separately funded development. These are demanding assumptions: the cited historical approved nitroglycerin market was approximately $21–23m in channel sales and around 47,000 packs. The measures and products differ, but the scale check shows that the favorable case needs significant expansion or capture from compounding, not merely a small share of today's approved-product niche. [LC16–LC18](commercial-evidence.json).

An independent US program has a different clock and cost. The corrected favorable case assumes **$7m of later development before application fees**, a qualifying fee waiver and launch in year 5. Base and adverse assume larger programs and later launch. The initial bridge, rights, launch and working capital are separate. The full cost/fee assumptions are in the [commercial report](commercial-economics.md#independent-us-cost-and-timing-reconciliation); they are hypothetical programs, not asserted FDA requirements.

### Time changes the answer, but does not resolve the risk

Five years is a comparison convention, not an investor deadline. With ten years, no new development, continued rights and unchanged prices, the favorable small-territory operator case distributes about **$539k** against $325k contributed; the base US royalty case distributes about **$747k** against approximately $395k contributed. With 10% annual price erosion from year 6, those distributions fall to about **$442k** and **$519k**, respectively. All four cases remain below an illustrative 10% annual return hurdle at the initial modeled price.

Thus a longer hold can recover nominal principal under specified conditions without establishing either attractive time-adjusted returns or a high probability of recovery. The [cash-flow output](commercial-cashflows.json) contains all 15 main scenarios, half-uptake stresses, ten-year continuations and the US fee-waiver sensitivity. The [commercial report](commercial-economics.md) explains the assumptions and historical deal comparisons.

## What would move this opportunity back up?

| Specific thesis | Evidence or estimate that could reverse the downgrade |
|---|---|
| Territory with an established operator | An identifiable coverage or supply gap, feasible dossier route, adequate net contribution and a capital schedule whose conservative economics survive meaningful delay and price pressure. |
| Retained interest in an existing program | A material economic interest earned by a useful bounded contribution; remaining capital and commercialization supplied by a credible other party; actual investor distributions modeled separately from financing valuations. |
| Better product | A measurable convenience, delivery, exposure or clinical improvement that matters to users or payers, can be protected or otherwise captured, and is worth the added development. |
| Nifedipine alone | Avoids assuming lidocaine's incremental value, but becomes a distinct article, label, evidence and commercial comparison. It is not automatically cheaper to approve or easier to protect. |

The next desk work should prioritize the **territory/channel and retained-cash case**, then map the exact product/dossier gap. Do not select a new stability study, healthy-volunteer PK repeat or human efficacy trial merely because those are familiar development tasks. A specific decision-changing gap can justify one; the current evidence does not make any of them the default.
