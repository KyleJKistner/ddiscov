# Round 16 — APR-246/AEC clinical evidence and claim audit

Evidence cut: 4 November 2026 (client date). Candidate: `R4-L02`. Scope: original human report, later primary conference reports, outcome/site lineage, and the appropriate next clinical claim. This completed public-source audit informs the [marginal-dollar decision](marginal-dollar-decision.md). All later records are reconciled to the original cohort; no new treatment was performed.

## Decision-changing result

The evidence supports retaining a **useful ongoing-treatment wound-control question**. The historical Round 5 requirement that benefit persist after withdrawal is too restrictive for that intended product. Drug-free persistence should govern a durable off-treatment repair claim, not every useful treatment. Likewise, perfect cornification is not a universal entry requirement. A product can be valuable if it reduces the burden of chronic erosions during tolerable continued use, with tissue integrity and safety adequate for that use.

This does not establish that APR-246 achieves those outcomes causally or safely. The reported clinical experience remains two children. The available human observations are stronger than a differentiation-marker result, while still lacking an independently measured barrier effect, reliable causal effect size, exposure margin or prospective all-treated response estimate. A new cell/animal efficacy experiment is not a mandatory reset before all clinical-development consideration; an article, exposure or tissue study should answer a consequential question rather than repeat the existing marker phenotype.

## Consequences of the source reconciliation

**Do not assign the reported daily active amount to the palm alone.** The later site description leaves its distribution across treated surfaces unresolved. It does not settle cream mass, application area, dose per unit area, adherence, drug delivery or the historical dose-transfer ambiguity.

**Do not manufacture a 6-to-2 pain response.** The original numerical baseline belongs to P2, whereas the later numerical follow-up belongs to P1. Painkiller cessation is meaningful reported burden information, but a global patient outcome cannot be attributed to the paired-palm contrast, especially when another site also received active treatment. No numerical patient-level change or comparator-adjusted pain estimate is supported.

**Do not count the abstracts as two more treated children or independent replication.** Ages, investigators, phenotype, sites and the distinctive outcome schedule strongly support overlap. Formal public patient linkage is not demonstrated, and the genotype conflict remains. Conservative accounting therefore adds zero unique patients and zero independent clinical cohorts. Later publication dates do not extend follow-up beyond the original report.

**Do not turn descriptive tolerance into a measured chronic safety window.** The abstract has no structured adverse-event count, ascertainment schedule, laboratory/exposure report or formal safety denominator. It should be represented as an author report in two cases. The original short mouse irritation observation was not displayed and cannot establish long-term pediatric tolerance.

## What is measured, inferred and still absent

The human observations concern coverage, symptoms and care burden in established lesions. Clinical incomplete cornification and unrescued cell cornification markers are cautionary, but neither is a quantitative human barrier assay. No transepidermal water loss, permeability, mechanical-strength, fragility, infection-rate or standardized lesion-area trajectory was recovered. No treated-versus-vehicle numeric contrast or blinded/randomized assessment is reported. Baseline lesion comparability, wound-care standardization, adherence, interruptions, withdrawal, rechallenge and comprehensive long-term surveillance remain unestablished.

The cell exposure began before induced differentiation. Its changes in markers and unchanged aggregation therefore do not show delayed repair of an already defective mature tissue, nor prove a unique mechanism. The biopsied nonerosive sites could heal normally; the anatomical lesion context matters. These are model qualifications, not a reason to erase the human observations or demand a perfect model before selecting a bounded next development question.

The finite intended claim should be **clinically useful control of persistent erosions during the specified regimen**, with a credible comparison to appropriate ongoing care and a tolerable local/systemic exposure profile. Meaningful barrier or mechanical readouts can support that claim and reject cosmetic coverage, but an unnormalized histological marker alone should not veto an otherwise demonstrated patient benefit. A regenerative/off-treatment claim needs additional evidence and should be separately labelled.
