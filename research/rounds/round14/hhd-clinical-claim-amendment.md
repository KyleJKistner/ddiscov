# Ruxolitinib / HHD: controlled HS evidence and the claims it can support

**Round 14 · 4 November 2026 · Existing program R6-NL-01.** This is a completed literature-based decision amendment. It neither executes nor authorizes a clinical study.

## Decision

**Retain rank 3, 10 planning points, and the existing controlled clinical-proof priority for ruxolitinib 1.5% cream in Hailey–Hailey disease (HHD).** The newly audited randomized study concerns hidradenitis suppurativa (HS), a different disease. Its positive lesion-count primary result supports activity of this formulation in that studied setting. Its symptoms, attrition and phase-specific safety results prevent broadening the claim to proven pain relief, durable control or a defined HHD exposure margin.

The substantive amendment is an explicit endpoint-to-claim map, supported by recovered official result denominators and uncertainty. The [Round 7 development proof](../round7/rux_hhd_development_proof.md) already required controlled evidence, patient benefit, all-randomized accounting and interpretable maintenance. Those requirements are retained, not presented as new discoveries. The [Round 10 comparator decision](../round10/hhd-active-care-comparator.md) and [Round 13 optional-model decision](../round13/hhd-comparator-decision.md) remain intact.

## Why this bounded question was selected

The closed BLU claim-mapping, zinc, SLC6A6 and HHD supplement searches were excluded. A short comparison of remaining leading-program questions found no new actionable primary delta in the available DYN101/PIK3C2B and enasidenib sources. The exact marketed cream had accessible randomized HS evidence with an unresolved connection between lesion improvement and patient benefit. That question could change what counts as a successful HHD development claim without requiring new materials, patient contact or new data generation. This is a bounded value-of-information choice, not a claim that every unresolved question was exhaustively reranked.

## What the official results resolve

NCT05635838 randomized 69 people: 34 to ruxolitinib 1.5% cream and 35 to vehicle during a 16-week double-blind period. The official results identify 24/32 completers and 10/3 noncompleters. Five active and one vehicle participant were lost to follow-up; two active and no vehicle participants discontinued for adverse events. Other reasons are retained in the [trial evidence audit](rux-hs-trial-evidence.md). These are reported counts, not numbers reconstructed from percentages. [Official results](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT05635838), participantFlowModule.

## Missing data and analysis: recovered rules, remaining limits

The official SAP describes an all-randomized primary population including week-16 missingness and discontinuation, a repeated-measures model with treatment, baseline AN category, visit and treatment-by-visit terms, and an unstructured covariance matrix. Its primary two-sided alpha is .10, and the planned sample of approximately 60 was not derived from a power calculation. The reported P=.0215 is also below .05, so the result must not be dismissed as merely passing a .10 threshold. No HHD power or effect threshold follows from this plan. [SAP](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/38/NCT05635838/SAP_001.pdf), printed pages 10–11 and 24–25.

The registry outcome descriptions also refer to MMRM, but the structured statistical-method field says ANCOVA. Preserve that discrepancy. Neither the SAP nor the table label establishes the complete implemented participant contribution, missing-at-random justification, handling of categorical nonresponse, or robustness to informative attrition. The SAP is dated 12 September 2023; the registry records its upload on 4 October 2024. Those dates do not verify pre-lock finalization or prospective public posting. Some SAP text is visibly redacted; no redacted material was recovered. [Official results](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT05635838) and [SAP](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/38/NCT05635838/SAP_001.pdf).

For weekly pain and itch, the visible SAP uses the seven daily scores before a visit and makes the visit value missing when at least four entries are missing. Thus at least four daily entries are needed for a weekly value. This recovered rule can explain why symptom observation counts need separate reporting, but it does not prove which participants became missing for that reason or why the arms differ. It is a studied HS rule, not an automatically validated HHD diary rule. [SAP](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/38/NCT05635838/SAP_001.pdf), printed pages 20–21.

## Safety and maintenance scope

During the DBVC period the registry reports any TEAE in 13/34 active and 15/35 vehicle participants, and grade 3 or higher TEAEs in 0/34 and 2/35. During the extension, the corresponding any-TEAE counts are 8/24 continuing-active and 15/32 vehicle-to-active participants; grade 3 or higher counts are 0/24 and 3/32. Both extension groups received active cream. These are treatment-emergent events, not a demonstration of drug causality. Grade 3 or higher severity and serious-event status are separate classifications. The registry's safety windows include follow-up: up to week 16 plus 30 days for DBVC and weeks 17–32 plus 30 days for the extension. [Official results](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT05635838), outcomes 7–10.

The adverse-event module separately reports serious events in 2/66 people ever exposed to active treatment and 1/35 exposed to vehicle. People who crossed over contribute to both exposure groups. These figures cannot be treated as independent randomized arms or compared as if they shared the same exposure duration. The journal abstract's broad no-grade-3-or-higher phrasing and the sponsor's no-serious-active-TEAE statement must not override the registry's period- and group-specific data. The complete journal paper and supplement were not read, so this audit does not adjudicate their full safety presentation. [Official results](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT05635838), adverseEventsModule; [primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41248765/); [sponsor summary](https://investor.incyte.com/news-releases/news-release-details/incyte-announces-new-data-phase-2-study-evaluating-ruxolitinib).

## Completion and next reopening condition

This sprint completes a public-source audit and a decision amendment. It resolves trial counts, period-specific safety and several uncertainty fields; it does not complete a patient-level reanalysis, verify the implemented analysis code, read the full journal supplement or produce new clinical evidence. The public HS trial, sponsor release, registry and article are one cohort, not four studies. No new HHD-treated person is added.

Close this retrieval pass. Reopen the narrow question for a substantive primary HS readout, a correction that resolves the model/population discrepancy, or newly available primary HHD evidence that can change the intended benefit, comparator or exposure decision. The [prospective HS phase 3 design](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06958211?tab=researcher) and [sponsor forecast of a Q4 2026 readout](https://incytecorp.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/incyte-reports-third-quarter-2026-financial-results-and-provides) are context, not completed outcomes or an HHD validation. No monitoring automation or execution task is created. The next sprint should choose another decision-relevant question unless one of those new evidence conditions occurs.

No outreach, purchases, recruitment, experiments, simulations or material receipt occurred. The work comprises primary-document reading, evidence reconciliation, deterministic integrity checks and repository publication.
