# Round 8 clinical-leader challenge: enasidenib and topical ruxolitinib

Evidence cut: 4 November 2026. Baseline: `f49a937206cbbf18e6c8f89f2dcb31eb704e9cd7`. This is a public-evidence decision memo, not an authorization to acquire material or conduct a study. No outreach, discussion, purchase, or experiment occurred.

## Decisions that change the next project

**Retain enasidenib and ruxolitinib as finite clinical-development proof priorities, but make their comparator and product questions more demanding.** Enasidenib retains the stronger exact-disease organ-function anchor. Vorasidenib must now be included in any claim that an enasidenib product is the best way to address the neurological component of IDH2-R140Q D2HGA2. Ruxolitinib must demonstrate useful control beyond specified contemporary care; avoiding an invasive sweat-control procedure or reducing rescue burden is a testable contribution, whereas uncontrolled lesion clearance alone is insufficient.

Neither finding displaces BLU3797's initial qualification project. Neither drug becomes an exclusive asset because its approved formulation can be obtained through ordinary clinical channels. The same requirement applies to BLU: an exact reproducible article and persuasive biology are not an established right to commercialize it.

## Enasidenib: the competitive question is now organ-specific

Round 7's clinical case lineage and source corrections remain controlling. The severe-cardiomyopathy signal is concentrated in one original child with concomitant heart-failure care, while an independent child had milder abnormalities. The additional skin records and inaccessible neonatal report cannot be treated as additional independent cardiac or neurological successes. [Prior clinical dossier](../round7/enasidenib_clinical.md)

### A genuine R140Q comparator, with important limitations

Vorasidenib is not merely an IDH1-glioma analogy. Its discovery program explicitly tested **IDH2-R140Q homodimers and engineered R140Q cells**. The FDA review reports 2-HG cellular IC50 values of **14 ± 1.1 nM in TF-1 R140Q cells (reported assay N=3)** and **7.1 ± 3 nM in U87MG R140Q cells (reported assay N=7)**. Inhibition of wild-type IDH1/2 cellular activity was approximately 400 nM and strengthened with time. These assay-specific nominal values do not establish clinical selectivity margins. The review also records one monkey cardiomyopathy/heart-failure death at **105-fold human AUC**; that is a high-exposure liability signal, not evidence of comparable clinical cardiac harm. [Discovery paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7025383/) · [FDA review, pharmacology and toxicology](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2024/218784Orig1s000MultidisciplineR.pdf)

The June 2026 label specifies oral **10/40-mg tablets containing the hemicitric-acid hemihydrate co-crystal**. It reports 97% protein binding, a **brain-tumor:plasma concentration ratio of 1.6**, a ten-day terminal half-life, and material liver toxicity. Use below age 12 is unestablished. Tumor distribution is neither normal infant-brain unbound exposure nor D2HGA2 CNS rescue. The label does not supply an infant formulation. [Current label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=31405fee-55b7-4857-987e-2724ee76be84&type=display)

**Inference:** vorasidenib raises the opportunity cost of making a broad enasidenib neurological claim. It does not justify replacing the disease-matched enasidenib cardiac observation with a tumor PD result. A stronger CNS-distribution hypothesis and a weaker exact-disease safety/functional bridge can coexist.

### Split three hypotheses before allocating further effort

| Proposed claim | What the next result must discriminate | Appropriate comparator and endpoint | Advance or stop decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Improve established cardiomyopathy | Drug-associated improvement beyond changing loading conditions, concomitant care and regression after an unusually severe baseline | Stable, fully recorded clinical care; longitudinal age/body-size-aware echocardiographic function, chamber dimensions and clinically meaningful support burden. Enasidenib exposure and chiral D-2-HG are linked explanatory measures. | Continue article/exposure qualification now. Confirm organ benefit only if the functional trajectory is credible after those alternatives are addressed; D-2-HG suppression alone cannot pass. |
| Improve established neurological disability | Acquired skill, seizure or daily-function benefit beyond maturation, therapy intensity and observer expectation | A prespecified individual functional trajectory with masked assessment where feasible; PK/PD and any CNS proxy remain separate. Vorasidenib is a mechanism/exposure comparator, not proven D2HGA2 standard care. | Do not market cardiac observations as CNS rescue. Advance a neurological claim only with meaningful function and a plausible tolerable CNS exposure bridge. |
| Prevent neonatal injury | Prevention of future damage, which cannot be inferred from an older child's improvement | An age-specific development plan and interpretable natural-history framework; the five-week report adds no outcome evidence until primary content is available. | Keep a separate hypothesis. No neonatal efficacy or safety promotion from adult labels, a patent formulation, or tumor PK. |

A newly constructed cardiac model is useful only if it resolves a choice such as mutant/WT selectivity, competing inhibitors, or a clinical exposure limit. It should not become a ritual prerequisite for the already justified pediatric article/exposure project. Conversely, reproducing suppression in a cancer line would settle neither cardiac performance nor developmental benefit.

### The product contribution has to be explicit before larger investment

A pediatric sprinkle disclosure already exists and was used clinically. Therefore “make a pediatric formulation” is too vague. A fundable qualification package should name **one improvement**—reliable administration in a presently unsupported setting, reduced dosing/measurement burden, a better exposure distribution, or a prospectively interpretable organ outcome—and demonstrate how the current article fails that requirement. The specific proposed formulation must be assessed against the existing composition/process claims; no patent-absence or freedom-to-operate conclusion was reached. [Round 7 formulation and claims analysis](../round7/enasidenib_product.md)

If reliable administration and useful organ control are already achievable with an existing article, a prospective clinical dataset can still merit grant or clinical-research funding. It does not automatically create a new commercial product. A CNS-oriented product thesis additionally needs a reason to prefer it to vorasidenib at relevant free exposure and tolerability, rather than an assumption that all R140Q inhibitors have the same tissue distribution.

### A newly verified negative precedent changes evidence discipline

The original inventory already contains epalrestat/PMM2-CDG (`historical-topics:T173`). Its official randomized-trial record now says **terminated due to futility**, actual enrollment **42**. The public API has `hasResults: false`; August/September 2026 submission tracking is not a posted result table. No treatment-arm effect, safety difference, or specific failed endpoint can be reconstructed from that status. [Official trial/API](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT04925960)

This does **not** lower enasidenib's drug-specific probability by an invented amount. It provides a concrete reason to refuse the general inference that a convincing metabolic change plus a favorable early case guarantees controlled clinical benefit. Enasidenib's next milestone must retain organ function as the decision variable.

## Ruxolitinib: a better challenge than another uncontrolled responder

The five exact-strength reports remain useful hypothesis-generating observations, with mixed induction/background/maintenance contexts. The nine-patient preprint remains unpooled. Round 7's oral LX3305 placebo response and ruxolitinib exposure-tail findings remain relevant. [Clinical dossier](../round7/rux_hhd_clinical.md) · [Controlled-proof plan](../round7/rux_hhd_development_proof.md)

### Sweat control provides a concrete active benchmark

A 2023 double-blind onabotulinumtoxin-A study reported **seven patients, four initially active and three initially placebo, with six completing**. Its later outcomes include reinjection, so they cannot be read as a clean persistent randomized contrast. One initially placebo-treated patient maintained clearance for six months. [Primary abstract; publisher full text blocked in this sprint](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37026885/)

A separate prospective pilot enrolled **26 HHD and four Darier patients**, using intradermal Botox. **Eleven HHD patients relapsed and left efficacy follow-up** when additional treatment was needed: 11/30 is 36.7% of the mixed cohort, while 11/26 is 42.3% of the HHD subset. Only 19/30 remained at six months; favorable later symptom means cannot be interpreted as all-enrolled durable control. The repeated injections/visits, discomfort and relapse leave room for a useful alternative. [Primary full text and tables](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7893874/)

This is a practical comparator for sweat-exacerbated flexural HHD, not proof that botulinum toxin is universally best or that it defeats ruxolitinib. It strengthens the claim to test: **meaningful, sustained, tolerable control with less total treatment burden**.

### Make the design answer the proposed product claim

The most economical initial design remains a patient-randomized, concurrent comparison of the specified **marketed 1.5% cream plus protocolized background care** against the same care, with masked clinical assessment. Background infection management, sweating/friction measures, rescue thresholds and recent procedural treatment must be prespecified. One should not intentionally leave an untreated infection or withhold necessary rescue to create a larger apparent drug effect.

If the intended contribution is an alternative to procedures in sweat-driven disease, then a subsequent pragmatic comparison against a predefined onabotulinumtoxin-A care pathway becomes necessary. A vehicle comparison answers a narrower active-ingredient question; it cannot by itself establish superiority to a clinically effective procedure. A six-month maintenance question cannot be settled by an eight-week induction photograph.

Choose one primary patient-relevant estimand before enrollment. A candidate is the **proportion of all randomized follow-up days without activity-limiting painful erosions and without rescue escalation**, supported by standardized lesion scoring and patient-reported pain. This is a proposed development endpoint, not a validated HHD instrument. Its definition and meaningful difference require qualification before a trial-size calculation. Retain all randomized patients in the primary denominator; count rescue and missing follow-up explicitly. Report maintenance outcomes both among initial responders and among all randomized patients, without treating them as equivalent.

The strongest alternatives to a drug effect are spontaneous flare resolution, vehicle/barrier care, infection control, ongoing systemic therapy and observer-selected lesion photography. Concurrency and standardized background care address more of these alternatives than a sixth unblinded case. The molecular explanation—anti-inflammatory relief versus restoration of mutant ATP2C1-dependent adhesion—does not have to be settled to establish useful symptomatic control. It must be settled before advertising durable repair after drug withdrawal.

### What would justify advancing, redirecting or stopping

- **Advance clinical confirmation:** credible improvement in the prespecified useful-control outcome, with tolerable measured exposure and no unacceptable burden tradeoff. A meaningful steroid/rescue-sparing or procedure-avoiding benefit can qualify even without genetic repair.
- **Redirect the claim:** short-term suppression without durable control supports an ongoing-treatment product, if the total burden remains attractive. It does not support disease correction.
- **Stop this independent product thesis:** no meaningful advantage over the chosen care pathway, or required exposure/burden negates the benefit. A positive active-versus-vehicle result alone is insufficient if an easier current treatment accomplishes the same goal.

The formulation/composition claims and branded franchise identified in Round 7 remain live commercial constraints. An independently valuable clinical result may be fundable without exclusivity; a standalone therapeutic asset requires a distinct, executable product contribution. There is no need to seek an owner response to reach that scientific decision. [Public product/claims analysis](../round7/rux_hhd_product.md)
