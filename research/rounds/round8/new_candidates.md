## R8-NL-01 — Fluoxetine in KCNC1 gain-of-function disorder

**Article, genotype and route.** The 2026 trial used oral fluoxetine powder mixed with medical corn starch in a capsule opened onto yogurt; its API salt, manufacturer and lot are not verified here. The participant carried de novo heterozygous `NM_001112741.2:c.1300G>C`, p.Val434Leu. A prior uncontrolled child had c.1273G>A, p.Val425Met. These variants and seizure phenotypes remain separate; neither supports using a Kv3.1 inhibitor in loss-of-function MEAK. [F1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13360919/) · [F2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37203213/)

**Evidence and biological units.** The 40-week trial randomized blinded transition times within a fixed placebo–fluoxetine–placebo order. One child's serial observations gave a time-adjusted primary Early Motor Questionnaire effect of **6.61 points, 95% credible interval −0.53 to 14.78**. The interval includes no treatment benefit. Purposeful hand movement and other secondary observations were suggestive; they do not multiply the participant count or rescue the primary uncertainty. NCT06341127 describes this same trial. [F1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13360919/) · [F3](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06341127)

**Contrary evidence and interpretation.** Maturation, caregiver reporting, fixed treatment order, slow elimination and carryover complicate developmental inference. The sequential higher dose does not provide a randomized dose-response comparison. EEG excitation increased without observed clinical seizures; withdrawal-period irritability occurred. The older V425M work used transiently transfected CHO cells, not a demonstrated patient-derived neuronal model. [F1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13360919/) · [F2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37203213/)

**Material, model and exposure.** A described pharmacy preparation and ordinary heterologous channel assays provide routes for future independent work. Exact article/stability qualification and individual parent/metabolite exposure remain missing. A clinical dose or EEG shift cannot establish brain Kv3.1 occupancy. Mechanistic comparison should include wild-type/mutant coexpression at measured free concentrations; positive modulators for loss-of-function KCNC1 answer an opposite functional question.

**Independent exposure and chemistry challenge.** A primary 2024 investigator poster reports a fluoxetine IC50 of **12.3 µM** in V434L CHO-K1 thallium-flux validation, not patch clamp or patient neurons. Its later primary investigator summary says neither fluoxetine nor norfluoxetine met the screen's 90% inhibition hit rule; this does not exclude partial modulation. The 2026 VU0521426/VU426 paper offers a state-dependent mechanistic comparator, but broad Kv-channel inhibition and low-concentration hyperpolarizing gating shifts prevent a selective-drug claim. Different assay potencies must not be converted into a clinical superiority ratio. This comparator is not a seventh screened clinical lead. [F4](https://www.metrionbiosciences.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KCNC1_SLAS-Europe-2024_poster_final.pdf) · [F5](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/ajpcell.00239.2026) · [F6](https://metrionbiosciences.com/hts-ion-channel-drug-repurposing-kcnc1/)

**Contribution, next proof and stop rule.** The immediate contribution would be better variant-specific evidence, a credible exposure bridge, or a more selective article. Generic fluoxetine availability does not create exclusivity. First determine whether the claimed channel mechanism is plausible at clinical exposure; then predefine a meaningful developmental outcome, independent masked assessment, stable cointerventions and carryover handling. Reproducible useful function and tolerability advance the clinical-research case. Failure of exposure plausibility stops the *precision Kv3.1* claim even if a nonspecific symptomatic effect remains possible. Persistent trivial/null benefit stops expansion of this product thesis. No automatic treatment-withdrawal requirement is proposed.

## R8-NL-02 — Lisdexamfetamine in KCNMA1-N999S PNKD

**Article, genotype and route.** The six-case series involved heterozygous KCNMA1-N999S; two also carried R1128W designated benign by the authors. Capsules, chewable tablets and combinations were used, so there is no single historical formulation/lot. A prospective article could specify marketed lisdexamfetamine dimesylate. N999S/Asn1053Ser is a transcript alias. A 2025 Asp789Glu case and earlier N536H/dextroamphetamine case are different genotype/article records, not additional N999S replications. [L1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8810426/) · [L2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40812354/)

**Evidence and biological units.** All six patients were **already known to the authors as responders before selection**. Retrospective parent estimates described large reductions in frequent daily episodes; three had no reported episodes during the medication-effect window. This is meaningful apparent function, but it is not a six-of-six response estimate for an unselected treated population. Reported onset/duration are caregiver observations, not measured patient PK. [L1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8810426/)

**Contrary evidence and interpretation.** Mood/aggression, appetite and sleep effects constrained dose or coverage. Neither lisdexamfetamine nor dextroamphetamine directly altered BK-channel activity in the reported heterologous work; relief does not establish channel correction. One patient started at nine and was twenty at assessment, while others were children. The July 2026 label does not recommend use below six because of higher exposure and adverse effects; the younger published cases do not establish approval or a safe regimen for that age. [L1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8810426/) · [L3](https://www.dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=da3148af-f9b6-4725-adae-3595b4c7dc4b)

**Material, model and exposure.** A marketed article and prospective video-based event assessment are public routes for later regulated clinical research; a proprietary cell line is not necessary. New BK target assays would not by themselves explain an indirect stimulant effect. An exact dextroamphetamine comparator could distinguish prodrug coverage/burden if clinically appropriate; it is not assumed equivalent. A bounded ClinicalTrials.gov KCNMA1 query returned a natural-history registry and an unrelated healthy-volunteer emodepside study, not a dedicated lisdexamfetamine PNKD trial. This is not proof that no program exists anywhere.

**Contribution, next proof and stop rule.** This is the strongest new **clinical-evidence** question in this set: prospectively enroll eligible genotype-defined patients, record nonresponders, independently adjudicate events and quantify interrupted daily activity. Separate attention/mood changes from PNKD benefit and preserve growth, appetite, sleep and cardiovascular tradeoffs. Advance if useful PNKD control generalizes beyond selected responders with acceptable burden. Stop a broad product claim if the effect is confined to selected cases, is only an attention benefit, or requires unacceptable coverage-related harms. The obvious value is evidence and a better coverage/burden solution; no exclusive asset has been identified.

## R8-NL-03 — N-acetyl-L-leucine in GM2 gangliosidoses

**Article, genotype and route.** IB1001/N-acetyl-L-leucine/levacetylleucine is the single L-enantiomer, distinct from N-acetyl-DL-leucine and N-acetyl-D-leucine. The trial used oral powder suspended in ORA-Blend in genetically confirmed Tay-Sachs or Sandhoff disease, ages six to fifty-five. Individual HEXA/HEXB variants were not fully re-extracted. Current Aqneursa granules are a defined marketed article, but the formulation bridge is not proven by the common active name. [N1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9990862/) · [N3](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/219132s002lbl.pdf)

**Evidence and biological units.** The open-label six-week treatment/six-week washout study used masked central video raters: **30 enrolled, 29 modified intention-to-treat, 27 washout completers**. The CI-CS contrast was 0.71 ± 2.09, with a 90% Hodges–Lehmann interval of 0–1.50. Paper p=.039 differs from the posted registry's one-sided Wilcoxon p=.044; these are one cohort, not confirmation. Registry reclassification counts **16/29 improved, 12 worsened, one unchanged** on treatment; washout counts use 27. [N1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9990862/) · [N2](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT03759665)

**Contrary evidence and interpretation.** No aggregate improvement was demonstrated on objective walk/peg measures or total SCAFI; only seventeen could perform the walk and twenty-three the peg task at the relevant visits. Treatment assignment lacked randomization or concurrent placebo; the paper calls the evidence Class IV. Short symptomatic change cannot establish disease modification or infantile-GM2 benefit. Five attributed adverse events occurred in three patients. [N1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9990862/)

**Material, model, exposure and comparator.** The marketed granule article and functional assessments supply a practical route. Neither normal infant CNS exposure nor individual GM2 target engagement was established here. Any controlled proof should prespecify ambulatory/nonambulatory strata, suitable tasks, missingness and optimized supportive care. The NPC and A-T trials are not GM2 controls, and an extension of IB1001-202 does not create a new independent cohort.

**Contribution, next proof and stop rule.** IntraBio has the active program and branded franchise. The **September 2026 FDA label includes NPC and A-T, not GM2**; EMA's GM2 orphan designation names IntraBio and is not approval. A new independent asset thesis is therefore on hold absent a distinct contribution. Reconcile the paper/registry analysis and design controlled, clinically meaningful GM2 proof only if it fills a real gap. Benefit plus a credible product/evidence contribution could advance; another positive uncontrolled comparison alone should not. [N3](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/219132s002lbl.pdf) · [N4](https://intrabio.com/clinical-trials/) · [N5](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/orphan-designations/eu-3-17-1949)

## R8-NL-04 — Roflumilast 0.3% cream in Darier disease

**Article, genotype and route.** The 2026 report concerns once-daily **0.3% topical cream**, not foam, other cream strengths or oral roflumilast. The fifteen-year-old patient had a heterozygous ATP2A2 missense variant, without the exact nucleotide/protein substitution reported. Brand and lot were not verified. Zoryve 0.3% cream is a possible specified prospective article; case evidence does not automatically establish brand identity. [R1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13586814/) · [R2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=ec1bb0d1-f38a-4080-831a-68791d1d1fdb)

**Evidence and biological units.** One patient reported marked improvement within a week and near clearance at four weeks: treated-area BSA approximately 20%→0%, itch NRS 8→0, with residual sideburn disease where application was limited. Historical corticosteroid/tacrolimus failure is not a randomized comparator. The report supplies no controlled maintenance, withdrawal, mechanical-cohesion or barrier-function result. A conference poster mentioned in its discussion was not independently verified or pooled. [R1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13586814/)

**Contrary evidence, exposure and comparator.** Spontaneous fluctuation, vehicle effects and a short follow-up remain alternatives. There is no Darier-specific lesional/plasma PK here. Useful anti-inflammatory symptom control is possible without demonstrating correction of SERCA2. This is **ATP2A2/Darier evidence, not ATP2C1/HHD evidence**. Add the exact cream to the NDC-9009 same-indication comparator plan; retain the distinction between clinical symptom control and delayed adhesion/barrier repair.

**Material and model route.** A specified marketed 0.3% cream and prospective clinical lesion assessment provide a route for independent proof without an author-owned model. If the desired claim is adhesion repair, an independently qualified ATP2A2/control skin system and local exposure assessment are needed; their availability and performance are not assumed from the clinical report.

**Contribution, next proof and stop rule.** Arcutis has a marketed topical franchise; its 2025 Form 10-K describes formulation claims and patent litigation. No freedom-to-operate conclusion follows. A controlled lesion/itch/rescue-burden study could be useful, while a separate asset needs an improved article or other concrete contribution. Skin-model repair need not be a prerequisite for clinical symptom proof, but it is necessary for a repair claim. Advance meaningful controlled function or burden benefit at tolerable exposure; stop independent product expansion if it adds no advantage over appropriate current care. [R3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1787306/000178730626000018/arqt-20251231.htm) · [R4](https://www.arcutis.com/pipeline/)

## R8-EX-01 — W7 in TNNT2-R94C restrictive cardiomyopathy

**Article, genotype and route.** W7/W-7 is N-(6-aminohexyl)-5-chloro-1-naphthalenesulfonamide. The exact salt, vendor/lot and concentration series in the 2025 R94C experiment remain unverified from the accessible text. This is an in-vitro article, not a qualified systemic formulation. One TNNT2-R94C patient-derived hiPSC background was compared with CRISPR-corrected cells. The original record already exists in the inventory. [W1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12802102/)

**Evidence and biological units.** Traction force, diastolic tension, contraction/relaxation and near-simultaneous calcium signals are real functional readouts. Both W7 and mavacamten reduced diastolic dysfunction; W7 had a smaller systolic penalty, **not demonstrated absence of one**. Differentiation batches were modeled as random effects; exact well/batch counts and doses were not recoverable from the accessed figure caption. The 2016 cTnI-R193H mouse work is a different gene/allele, not a second R94C patient. [W1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12802102/) · [W2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4967205/)

**Contrary evidence and exposure.** An independent primary study found direct blockade of hERG (**IC50 3.5 µM**), Kv1.5 (6.5 µM) and Kir2.1 (13.4 µM) in HEK293 cells, separate from calmodulin inhibition. These nominal concentrations overlap the older 1–10 µM sarcomere-effect range. This is not a calculated clinical arrhythmia margin, but it is a substantive reason to deprioritize systemic W7 before assuming a usable window. Older experiments also show reduced contractility in normal myocytes. [W3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2992901/) · [W2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4967205/)

**Model route, comparator and contribution.** Public structure and ordinary channel assays are available. Published patient-line access and detailed methods are not independently established; an engineered R94C/control pair would be a new qualification project. Compare matched useful diastolic benefit while preserving systolic reserve and electrical function, rather than comparing arbitrary equal doses. The September 2026 pediatric mavacamten approval concerns obstructive HCM, not RCM. Selective thin-filament desensitization may have a discovery/chemistry contribution; an old promiscuous probe is not already that product. [W4](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-first-drug-improve-functional-capacity-and-symptoms-children-rare-inherited-heart)

**Next proof and stop rule.** Restrict exact W7 to a bounded public-data/chemistry review unless a measured exposure window separates useful relaxation from electrical and systolic harm. If no such window emerges, redirect to a defined selective desensitizer. That stops the molecule thesis without disproving the target class. The independent Round 8 W7 review strengthens this disposition.

## R8-EX-02 — Epalrestat in PMM2-CDG

**Article, genotype and route.** This is the already inventoried oral epalrestat/PMM2-CDG program. NCT04925960 enrolled molecularly confirmed biallelic PMM2 cases aged two to seventeen, using oral treatment and matching placebo. Exact trial API grade/formulation and variant-specific arm results are not established by the public registry. [E1](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT04925960)

**Evidence and biological units.** The early single-child study reported ataxia/growth improvement alongside sorbitol, transferrin and fibroblast-glycosylation changes. The later randomized trial has **42 actual participants** and official status **TERMINATED; “Due to futility.”** The early child's overlap with that cohort was not resolved, so a forty-three-person count would be unsupported. [E2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34652821/) · [E1](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT04925960)

**Contrary evidence and source reconciliation.** The API has `hasResults: false`; it supplies no arm effects or failed-endpoint attribution. In the same record, `derivedSection.miscInfoModule.submissionTracking` lists an estimated first submission date of 24 August 2026 and release/reset tracking in August/September/October. These are not posted outcome tables. A third-party tracker calling the trial “reported” cannot supply missing results or override the official futility status. No new safety or PK explanation is invented.

**Material, comparator and contribution.** An existing clinical program and published fibroblast route mean the missing-article problem is not the key obstacle. The randomized placebo comparison already reached a negative program decision. Sponsor/developer interests were disclosed in the early paper. Repeating an uncontrolled biomarker experiment would not constitute a persuasive new contribution, and the PMM2 result says nothing directly about enasidenib or unrelated epalrestat indications.

**Next proof and stop rule.** Hold new independent epalrestat/PMM2 development. Maintain a finite public-results watch and reconcile the controlled outcomes, PK and any prespecified subgroups when actually released. Only transparent evidence supporting a specific credible explanation and differentiated next product could reopen it. Missing results are not a license to create a favorable post-hoc subgroup.

## Access limits and independent challenge

The JSON retains every primary URL and access tier. Fluoxetine, lisdexamfetamine and roflumilast full-text XMLs were read. W7's indexed main text/figure caption and the NALL article's indexed methods/results were read, but their blocked or failed direct retrievals are recorded; missing image tables were not silently reconstructed. Regulatory labels, current official registry payloads and primary sponsor/SEC records inform current program status. Sponsor claims are not independent efficacy evidence.

Independent agents challenged W7's exact-molecule window and the channelopathy candidates' genotype, design and exposure interpretations. BLU3797 remains the leading qualification project; enasidenib and ruxolitinib retain affirmative finite proof recommendations. P2, NDC-9009, AAT and earlier reserves are not displaced merely because these six records received fresh attention.
