# Round 22: AAT formulation, active exposure and causal controls

**Evidence date: 4 November 2026, America/Los_Angeles. Retrieval: 5 November 2026 UTC.** Existing AAT / donor-lung-preservation program, aliases BE-N01 and AAT_EVLP. Parent commit: 2db430cd752292d82a6e6a94bd7b08cf2ebb3614; parent tree: 7ba52d7ed428635bcf3a7f338c0ca4cc5ce75766.

## Decision

**Retain the bounded formulation/potency/process-qualification option.** The original independent review ranked this option seventh and attached an illustrative US$30–100k / two-to-four-month envelope to initial qualification, excluding scarce organs and transplant validation. Qualification is therefore the original priced information purchase. Its financial attractiveness remains conditional on the actual deliverable, cost, follow-on requirements and retained value; no quote or executable supply/access package was obtained here.

The useful distinction is between a known manufactured starting product and a qualified organ-preservation process. The existing AAT biological case survives this review. Newly recovered version-specific methods make the analytical and formulation questions more concrete, without proving a twelve-hour active-protein specification or a useful, commercially retained human transplant outcome. The companion [structured audit](aat-formulation-exposure.json) separates observations, analyst inferences, source versions and unknowns.

## 1. Define what was actually added and what was measured

**Zemaira is a defined starting product.** The manufacturer’s currently served prescribing information is revised January 2024. Its vial amount is expressed as functional A1PI measured by human-neutrophil-elastase neutralization; purity is at least 90% and specific activity at least 0.7 mg functional A1PI per mg total protein. Nominal vial presentations and reconstitution volumes are 1 g/20 mL, 4 g/76 mL and 5 g/95 mL. Reconstituted excipients include sodium 73–89 mM, chloride 30–39 mM, phosphate 15–20 mM and mannitol 121–168 mM. The label specifies room-temperature administration within three hours and separate administration from other medicines. These statements do not establish compatibility or retained potency in warm recirculating Steen. [S01](https://labeling.cslbehring.com/pi/us/zemaira/en/zemaira-prescribing-information.pdf), sections 2, 3 and 11; [S02](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=0c3354b5-a1d8-4f98-ad55-2eafe4265c4e), same label text.

This narrows the qualification problem. It is unnecessary to pretend that manufactured AAT begins with no identity or potency information. Conversely, a vial’s release potency is not the amount still active in a particular circuit. Total protein, immunoreactive AAT, remaining protease-inhibitory capacity, AAT–protease complexes and tissue-associated AAT answer different questions. Their numerical values should not be treated as interchangeable concentrations. The quoted clinical-label handling period is also not evidence that activity abruptly disappears after three hours; it defines the validated administration instructions, not an experimentally determined EVLP failure time.

**The base perfusate is itself an active physical comparator.** XVIVO’s STEEN Solution instructions describe 70 g/L human serum albumin and 5 g/L dextran 40, and explicitly discuss oncotic/fluid effects. The additive section acknowledges usual co-medications, replacement-fluid supplementation and incomplete investigation of other additives. This establishes neither AAT compatibility nor approval of an AAT combination. [S03](https://www.xvivogroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/xvivo_steen_solution_ifu_20924-14_955x390mm_221128_print.pdf), English product description and Additives sections.

A clinically meaningful AAT-process comparison should therefore account for the final physical formulation and replenishment policy. A label-based stock-volume illustration in Round 3 remains conditional arithmetic; it is not a recovered recipe for the later human experiment. Actual dilution, albumin concentration, osmolality and oncotic pressure cannot be reconstructed merely by choosing a convenient labeled stock concentration.

## 2. Revalidated patent discrepancy and newly recovered human-study process

The existing CSL patent still reports pig EVLP with nominal 3 mg/mL Zemaira, but Example 8 gives 0.5 mg/mL human-AAT antigen by ELISA during twelve hours. Example 11 reports lower sodium/chloride and late calcium in treatment perfusate. The five-donor human pilot uses nominal 10 mg/mL against saline; its printed circuit volume is 1.5 mL, and case 2 acknowledges dilution from control-only Steen replacement. Its NE readout is ELISA content. [S04](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20190059362A1/en), Examples 2, 8, 11 and 13.

No inspected primary passage resolves the pig mass-versus-antigen difference. It cannot establish a particular fractional loss, adsorption, consumption, oxidation or analytical bias. The differing electrolytes are a reason to specify the physical comparison, not proof that they caused the functional signal. Neither antigen persistence nor lower NE content supplies direct residual NE or PR3 activity. The patent’s five-donor recipe must also remain separate from the eight-donor thesis/article lineage.

**New to this review: a readable 2019 authored thesis gives a more specific eight-donor method.** Chapter 5 names Zemaira at nominal 10 mg/mL in 1.5 L Steen, with scheduled fresh-fluid exchange: 250 mL after one hour, then 100 mL hourly, with/without AAT according to group. Runs ended at twelve hours or failure. It reports AAT-antigen ELISA and NE–AAT-complex ELISA, not direct remaining protease activity. The explicit manufacturer-reconstitution sentence belongs to the separate cell-culture subsection. [S05](https://www.academia.edu/88941544/Alpha_1_Antitrypsin_Treatment_of_Donor_Lungs_A_Translational_Pathway_to_Clinical_Application), chapter 5, printed pp. 127–134.

This is the thesis version, recovered through a public third-party-hosted copy of the authored work. It is not automatically the final 2026 article’s full methods, and no new independent eight-donor replication is created. In particular, one cannot describe its treatment as a single initial addition surviving unchanged for twelve hours. Replacement, sample removal, fluid loss, tissue retention and assay recovery jointly matter to interpretation. The recovered text does not establish the whole-lung stock-reconstitution volume, physical placebo matching, aggregate burden or active-protein mass balance.

The thesis also states an expected antigen level around 5 g/L after nominal 10 mg/mL delivery and gives means rising from 3.0750 to 11.028 g/L between one and twelve hours. Its NE-complex p-value differs between body and caption, 0.043 versus 0.0043. These are retained as source inconsistencies, without selecting a preferred value or reanalyzing the study. [S05], pp. 136–137. Complex detection supports interaction with NE; it does not show the unused inhibitory reserve, PR3 coverage, or a stable free-active-AAT exposure.

## 3. Independent cold-storage evidence is informative, with a narrower product bridge

The 2018 study’s official accepted manuscript supplies a strong independent mechanistic anchor: HEK293-produced human M1(V213) AAT, a reactive-loop mutant and albumin were compared in cold Perfadex; male donor and recipient mice were used. Stored grafts were rinsed with plain Perfadex before implantation. Functional and retention observations were made four hours later, with AAT still detectable in graft tissue. Figures 2–4 report 4–5 recipients per group, while the biochemical NE/PR3 comparison has three independent experiments. [S06](https://push-zb.helmholtz-munich.de/deliver.php?id=21907), Methods pp. 4–7 and figure legends pp. 20–21; [published article record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29776812/).

Active AAT and the NE/PR3-double-deficient recipient comparison support a protease contribution. The noninhibitory mutant did not reproduce the active-protein protection; its loop changes also affect charge/conformation and potential membrane interaction. This is useful convergent evidence, not perfect separation of every molecular property. A double knockout cannot determine whether each protease is individually necessary, and it does not prove that a dual NE/PR3 product must outperform a sufficiently effective simpler comparator. [S06], Results and Discussion pp. 9–14.

The graft/recipient is the biological unit. Multiple microscopic fields and the four-recipient Western-blot panel are not additional functional replicates. Random allocation, blinded functional assessment and complete exclusions were not recovered from this main-manuscript read; their absence from the retrieved text is not proof that they never occurred. The supplement was not independently read. The accepted manuscript’s figures/captions were visually checked; no point-digitization or new effect-size analysis was performed.

**A preimplant rinse is not complete tissue drug removal.** This study supports early post-rinse protection in a cold, recombinant-AAT setting. It does not establish durable benefit from the proposed warm plasma-derived product. Residual local AAT can plausibly be part of the intended protection, so its presence is not automatically a product failure. Claims of drug-free repair, zero recipient exposure or an effect lasting beyond the measured interval need separate evidence. The authors themselves discuss limited hemodynamic monitoring and the short observation period; universal durability or safety conclusions are unwarranted.

## 4. Use comparators to answer the actual product question

Sivelestat is retained as an existing comparator, with direct donor-flush rat evidence rather than only a generic anti-inflammatory rationale. The 2007 primary abstract reports three ten-rat groups, cold preservation and graft assessment at 48 hours, with the higher-dose arm improving oxygenation and other injury measures. It is not a direct comparison with AAT and does not establish an equivalent warm human-EVLP regimen. [S07](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17888675/), DOI 10.1016/j.ejcts.2007.07.017.

A separate 2015 pig paper describes an NE inhibitor during EVLP followed by early transplantation assessment. The accessible abstract reports EVLP functional advantages and a post-transplant PVR difference, but does not name the exact inhibitor or provide a full exposure recipe. It remains class-context evidence in this audit; a reference list mentioning sivelestat cannot fill that identity gap. [S08](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11748-015-0585-0), abstract. Neither report is a new opportunity screen or an AAT head-to-head result.

| Decision question | Evidence needed for that claim | What does not decide it |
|---|---|---|
| Can a defined AAT process be used reproducibly? | Interpretable delivered article, active exposure, compatibility and replenishment/carryover accounting | Nominal mass or antigen alone; the general approval of a starting component |
| Does the AAT-containing process improve useful graft outcomes? | A practical incremental comparison with an appropriate base process and relevant clinical function | A mechanism marker alone; an unbalanced improvement in fluid handling presented as proof of a unique molecular action |
| Is an AAT-specific antiprotease claim justified? | Claim-appropriate activity and controls in the actual formulation | AAT/NE antigen or complex content alone; cold recombinant-protein results automatically assigned to warm Zemaira |
| Is AAT preferable to a simpler alternative? | Comparable useful outcomes, handling, exposure/safety and total costs | Equal nominal molarity, different-study p-values, or an assumed obligation to inhibit both NE and PR3 |
| Can a finite organ-conditioning step create value? | Useful preservation/acceptance or downstream graft benefit, with a workable cost and retained-value route | A universal requirement for permanent repair or complete elimination of tissue-bound AAT |

These are decision requirements, not an executable protocol. A physical formulation mechanism can still produce a valuable process if it supplies incremental useful outcomes that an easier or cheaper formulation does not. Equally, demonstration that a simple matched process delivers the same benefit can remove the case for paying an AAT premium. Mechanistic attribution becomes more important when the claimed differentiation, exposure decision or negative inference depends on it.

## 5. Bounded source close

This pass closes its named public-source questions at the following ceiling: known starting-product specifications, a newly recovered thesis-specific replenishment process, unresolved nominal/antigen/activity relationships, and a more precise early post-rinse mechanistic bridge. It does not certify warm-circuit potency, physical matching, a validated clinical formulation, batch equivalence, supply access, assay access or cost feasibility.

Further desktop review should be triggered by a named artifact likely to change the qualification purchase: final human methods/supplement, credible direct activity or compatibility data in the relevant process, a reconciled concentration/mass-balance explanation, or a concrete scoped cost/access fact. It should not require another general AAT-mechanism paper before considering a finite, informative qualification opportunity.

All research here was document analysis. **No outreach, purchases, recruitment, experiments, simulations or material receipt occurred.** No scientific or financial model utility was run, no new dosing protocol or quantitative advance threshold was created, and no broader new-opportunity screening occurred. Earlier records remain historical evidence, subject to the narrower claim interpretation above and the controlling Round 22 decision.
