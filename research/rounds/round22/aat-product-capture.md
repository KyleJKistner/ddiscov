# Round 22: AAT donor-lung product, rights and retained value

**Evidence date: 4 November 2026, America/Los_Angeles. Retrieved: 5 November 2026 UTC.** Existing program AAT_EVLP / BE-N01; verified parent 2db430cd752292d82a6e6a94bd7b08cf2ebb3614.

## Decision

**Retain the conditional initial formulation/potency/process qualification option.** This is the original AAT next-dollar scope, with an illustrative **US$30–100k / two-to-four-month** envelope and conditional rank **seven**. Scarce organs and transplant validation were explicitly excluded. Qualification is therefore not a lower-information substitute for an originally priced human-function study. The original review's ranking/cost tables and AAT discussion control this interpretation; its exact input is identified by hash in the companion JSON.

The useful investment question is whether that finite qualification can change a later decision about usable grafts, better graft function and a contribution whose value ddiscov can retain. No current quote, supply, access, licensing terms, complete regulatory route or executable budget was established. None is manufactured by combining a licensed starting protein with a positive literature result. Full clinical validation and final commercial licensing are also not universal prerequisites to considering an informative initial qualification.

This pass examined the existing AAT program only. It adds claim/version detail, official product and trial attribution, and a bounded platform check. It creates no new candidate, clinical result, allocation or S-tier promotion.

## 1. The starting article is defined; the final EVLP product is not qualified

FDA identifies Zemaira as CSL's human alpha-1-proteinase inhibitor for augmentation in A1PI deficiency with emphysema. The Purple Book identifies **BLA 125078**, a **351(a)** biological product with IV single-dose 1 g, 4 g and 5 g presentations and an original approval date of July 8, 2003. This supports manufacturing precedent and a specific starting-article route. It does not establish an owned generic-drug asset, an available lot, a supply price or a right to rely on the sponsor's development package. [FDA product record](https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/approved-blood-products/zemaira); [Purple Book](https://purplebooksearch.fda.gov/index.cfm?blaNo=125078&event=productdetails).

The currently linked January 2024 prescribing information concerns IV use, administration within three hours after reconstitution and separate administration without mixing other medicines. These directions do not qualify a twelve-hour warm recirculating admixture. They also do not establish that the research application is intrinsically harmful. The product task remains to qualify the specified final composition and functional activity under its intended conditions. Different AAT brands, recombinant material, inactive protein and systemic recipient regimens do not inherit one another's identity, formulation or performance. [Prescribing information](https://labeling.cslbehring.com/pi/us/zemaira/en/zemaira-prescribing-information.pdf).

## 3. The clinical record is a development signal, not a completed ex-vivo-only result

The official Canadian portal identifies **University Health Network**, **Zemaira**, protocol **23-5745**, control **294199**, for a randomized, controlled, double-blinded PGD-prevention trial. It reports **Not yet recruiting**, updated **July 18, 2026**, with a trial authorization/no-objection date of **February 27, 2025**, phase **Other**, and **No registry match found**. That approval field concerns permission to conduct the trial, not product marketing approval.

The retrieved summary supplies no route, participant count, full regimen or outcomes. It cannot distinguish EVLP-only treatment from peri-transplant recipient administration. No matched detailed protocol or results were recovered in this bounded search; that is a retrieval limit, not a claim of global absence. The stated status is also not independent verification of actual recruitment operations. [Official trial record](https://recherche-essais-cliniques.canada.ca/en/clinical-trial/294199-lung-transplantation).

The US patent's Example 12 describes a possible human transplantation use, including optional recipient IV AAT. It is distinct from Example 13's completed five-donor research pilot. A proposed clinical example is not a recipient-outcome dataset. [Examples](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20190059362A1/en).

These distinctions preserve the earlier evidence rules: declined-donor physiology, recipient augmentation, cold-preservation experiments and an ex-vivo-only finished product address different questions. The clinical-development effort indicates an existing stakeholder and a need for a specific contribution. It does not establish that every commercial field is unavailable or that the intended additive already works in recipients.

## 4. The perfusion workflow is itself a product dependency

FDA's **P180014** record identifies the XPS with STEEN Solution combination for temporary perfusion and reassessment of initially unacceptable donor lungs. The original approval dates to April 26, 2019. The current page warns that supplements can change labeling. Two recent supplement statements inspected here concern sensor sterilization (**S012**) and a lung-cannula luer-cap supplier/material change (**S014**, September 16, 2026). Neither is evidence of AAT clinical benefit or approval. [Base PMA](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfpma/pma.cfm?id=P180014); [S012](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfpma/pma.cfm?id=P180014S012); [S014](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfpma/pma.cfm?id=P180014S014).

XVIVO's current product page specifies STEEN with XPS in the US and describes up-to-five-hour US acellular approval separately from longer clinical-evidence durations. This is a manufacturer statement, not a universal time restriction inferred by this review. Twelve-hour research results do not automatically qualify a clinical configuration or an AAT-modified perfusate. S011/S013 detail and the full current platform-label history were not recovered/audited; no comprehensive platform-clearance conclusion follows. [Manufacturer product description](https://www.xvivogroup.com/products-services/steen-solution/).

The financial implication is a dependency on a defined workflow, data package and integration route. An improved biochemical specification may reduce uncertainty before scarce organs are used. A successful lab qualification still leaves the cost of organ validation, recipient safety/carryover questions, operational adoption and commercial arrangements.

## 5. What ddiscov would need to contribute and retain

The following are **analyst inferences about the existing program**, not secured offers:

| Possible contribution | Why it could matter | What remains unestablished |
|---|---|---|
| Reproducible formulation/activity/compatibility specification | Makes later comparisons interpretable and can prevent expensive organ work using an inadequately defined article. | Actual cost, qualified performance, access and whether the knowledge is differentiated. |
| A better donor-lung process or usable data package | Could create more usable grafts or improve graft function within a practical workflow. | Incremental useful effect, downstream validation cost, permitted data use and adoption. |
| A licensed development contribution or contractual share | Could allow ddiscov to retain part of an improvement that also benefits existing protein suppliers and perfusion operators. | Available field, title, terms, manufacturing/reliance rights, revenue share and retained margin. |
| Waiting or stopping the affected route | Preserves capital when qualification cannot change a decision or when the usable continuation is unattractive. | No automatic stop follows merely from unfinished later validation or a source-access ceiling. |

The original US$30–100k envelope buys **initial qualification only**. It does not finance scarce lungs, transplantation, clinical validation or a finished commercial product. Its value depends on the probability that the information changes a later decision, the scale and timing of subsequent costs, and the share of useful value retained. Those are decision variables, not estimated probabilities or a numerical financial model in this review.

A result explained by physical composition could weaken an AAT-specific/protease-specific differentiation claim while still informing a useful process. Such a process would require its own incremental-value comparison; the mechanism label alone does not decide its usefulness. Likewise, a later need for recipient dosing would define a different regimen and financial commitment, rather than proving all AAT biology worthless. The proposed ex-vivo-only claim must be assessed on its own evidence.

There is no owner-response requirement for recognizing scientific interest. There is also no assumption that data ownership, regulatory reference rights, component supply, a method license and commercial development rights are interchangeable.
