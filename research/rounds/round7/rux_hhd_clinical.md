# Ruxolitinib 1.5% cream in Hailey–Hailey disease: clinical and causal evidence audit

**Round 7 — evidence checked 4 November 2026.** Baseline: `a834a480d74b1ebefbaa3fc072db17f67c2aa3d6`. Scope: clinical lineage, source quality, mechanism and an interpretable clinical proof. Product exposure, manufacturing, intellectual property and competing programs belong to the integrated dossier.

## Decision

**The human signal now supports a controlled local-treatment proof more strongly than the Round 6 dossier did. It still does not establish an S-tier development opportunity.** Five separately identifiable, peer-reviewed single-patient reports describe use of the exact **1.5% cream concentration**. They include monotherapy-style treatment, add-on induction and topical maintenance after systemic induction. Their most defensible common signal is useful local control of painful, recurring lesions. The nine-patient preprint remains an additional, incompletely auditable claim, not an expansion to a 14-person efficacy cohort. [S01–S05, S07]

This distinction preserves the worthwhile proposition. A topical treatment can create real value by reducing pain, erosion-days, rescue medication or application burden without correcting the inherited defect. The development claim should be tested at that level. Genetic repair, proven IFN dependence, durable drug-free cure and HHD-specific systemic safety are not established by the clinical material reviewed here.

Disease naming, a family history or keywords mentioning an ATP2C1 mutation are not a sequencing result. Cream concentration is specified; brand, lot, applied grams, quantitative treated area and patient PK are not established by these narratives. [S01–S05]

The related 1% case includes prior failure of topical tofacitinib, but dose, vehicle and exposure differences prevent a controlled within-class comparison. Its combination treatment also prevents assignment of the full response to ruxolitinib. [S06]

### What remains unknown across the clinical reports

These publications selected interesting responders; they do not provide the total number treated. They cannot yield a response probability, adverse-event rate or confidence interval for population efficacy. Multiple refractory histories strengthen the reason to test, but each patient also entered treatment during active disease, when regression toward a milder state is plausible.

The reports do not consistently document organism testing, antimicrobials, sweat/friction reduction, concurrent skin care, adherence or all background-treatment changes. Only Ly explicitly excludes Candida/herpes. Alhwayek's dizziness/falls followed **oral upadacitinib 30 mg**, not topical ruxolitinib, and the later back flare occurred at a site where cream was not applied. Both details must remain correctly attributed. [S02–S03]

No controlled exact-article HHD trial was located in this bounded primary-source review. A targeted search for ineffective or adverse topical-ruxolitinib HHD reports did not identify a confirmed negative series. That is an ascertainment limitation, not proof that failures have not occurred.

## 2. Nine-patient preprint: what was recovered and what was not

Ceccacci et al., version 1 dated **4 June 2026**, DOI **10.64898/2026.06.01.729262**, PMID **42282850**, PMCID **PMC13252397**, describes IFN-associated multiomic findings and treatment of nine refractory HHD patients. Its abstract reports re-epithelialization and sustained symptom/quality-of-life improvement. **The actual clinical table, regimen, concentration, vehicle, background treatment, genotype inclusion, duration, recurrence and safety denominators remain unverified.** [S07]

The access limit is now characterized more precisely:

- PMC explicitly carries the abstract only because the preprint license does not permit PMC archiving of full text.
- The BioRxiv API yielded metadata and abstract; the public PDF returned HTTP 429 and the API-advertised JATS route HTTP 403. Those responses were not treated as article content.
- The official Zenodo record is metadata-only and embargoed until **30 December 2026**. No embargoed file was accessed.
- The public author repository contains six files: a README, two directory placeholders and three spatial-analysis/plotting scripts. Its README excludes preprocessing/QC/normalization, points to the dataset and supplies no clinical table. [S07–S09]

The public repository is useful provenance, but its existence does not let us reproduce the unpublished clinical results or infer donor counts from image fields. Bulk assays, single cells, spatial spots, tissue sections and nine treated people are different units. Until the full methods and sample map are accessible, every assay-specific independent-donor denominator remains unknown.

This is not an author-email gate. The five detailed clinical reports already support a falsifiable controlled experiment. The inaccessible preprint receives limited evidentiary weight rather than delaying all progress or being promoted to verified proof.

## 3. A relevant controlled HHD benchmark: DERM101

The official results for **EudraCT 2018-000373-80** describe oral **LX3305**, a different drug. Disease-stratified randomization produced **12 active/5 placebo Darier patients and 10 active/6 placebo HHD patients**. HHD completion was **9/10 and 6/6**. Safety was primary; the primary-lesion, five-point IGSA responses were secondary. [S10]

| HHD week-12 result | Active LX3305, reported denominator 10 | Placebo, reported denominator 6 |
|---|---:|---:|
| At least one IGSA grade improvement | 50.0% = 5/10; reported 95% CI 18.7–81.3% | 83.3% = 5/6; CI 35.9–99.6% |
| At least two grades improvement | 40.0% = 4/10; CI 12.2–73.8% | 66.7% = 4/6; CI 22.3–95.7% |

The modified intent-to-treat definition requires randomization, at least one dose, baseline and at least one postbaseline IGSA, with early toxicity dropouts included even without postbaseline assessment. The tables retain denominators 10/6, but **week-specific observed counts and missing-data handling are undisclosed**. One must not infer all 16 were examined at week 12 or invent last-observation or nonresponder imputation. No between-arm statistical test is supplied. [S10]

The inference is methodological: substantial improvement can occur in a small HHD control group. These numbers do not estimate ruxolitinib's effect, establish a general HHD placebo rate, or justify a cross-trial comparison. They argue against another uncontrolled short before/after series as the decisive proof. Nor does prior use make IGSA a validated HHD clinical-importance threshold. Repeated visits must not be added as independent successes; an “any-time” responder endpoint gives more opportunities to register transient improvement.

## 4. Mechanistic support and two source-quality corrections

### The inherited defect and the treatable inflammatory component

The mechanistic hypothesis is coherent: inflammatory signaling may amplify injury in an adhesion-vulnerable epidermis; suppressing it could permit healing and reduce painful recurrent lesions. That would be useful treatment even if the mutation persists. But multiomic IFN enrichment is associative. JAK1/2 inhibition affects several cytokine systems, so clinical response cannot identify IFN as the unique necessary driver. The preprint abstract does not let us distinguish lesion-associated secondary inflammation from an experimentally established initiating mechanism. [S07]

**Lesion closure, barrier function and genetic correction are three different claims.** Photographs can document closure. They do not measure permeability, mechanical resilience, calcium-pump function or a repaired genotype. A barrier-restoration claim needs orthogonal functional evidence and patient-level replication; a clinical-control claim can stand on meaningful controlled outcomes without it.

### Correction 1: the anti–IL-8 citation has the wrong direction

Jobe's discussion says anti–IL-6 and anti–IL-8 restored ATP2C1 expression. Its cited primary study, Mayuzumi 2005, reports **anti–IL-6 prevented UVB-induced suppression, whereas anti–IL-8 slightly accelerated suppression**. The experiment used **normal human keratinocytes and mRNA**, not mutant HHD rescue, ruxolitinib, adhesion recovery or clinical barrier function. The dossier must not inherit a claim that both antibodies repaired the disease mechanism. [S05, S12]

### Correction 2: the 2026 Th2 biopsy paper needs its units and demographics audited

Gaumond 2026 reports seven HHD biopsies/cases, six AD and four normal samples, with higher epidermal IL-4 and dermal IL-13 in HHD than normal skin. These findings supply independent inflammatory association, not a ruxolitinib intervention. [S11]

Recomputing Table 1 shows the text's HHD demographic summary—54.5 years, range 33–75 and 70.6% female—matches **all 17 samples pooled**. The seven HHD entries yield **59.4 years, range 39–75, 5/7 female**. Normal controls are all female abdominal skin, so matching is incomplete. Three sections per biopsy correspond to IL-4, IL-13 and a no-antibody control, not three independent patients. Same-age/sex HHD pairs at different sites make donor lineage worth recording, but do not prove duplication or justify inventing a smaller n. The companion audit reproduces the arithmetic. [S11]

This correction limits precision and generalization; it does not by itself negate cytokine staining. It reinforces the need to keep donor, lesion, section and cell counts distinct in any new study.

## 5. A clinical proof that answers a development question

The first question should be **whether local treatment creates a reproducible improvement in the patient's painful, recurrent disease under credible exposure**. The second is whether the product can deliver that benefit with worthwhile maintenance, tolerability, burden or access advantages. Mechanistic sampling is explanatory; it should not become an unnecessary prerequisite for demonstrating patient value.

The German registry provides a patient-priority anchor. Its baseline analysis included 90 of 102 recruited patients, excluded insufficient/incoherent questionnaires and used varying item denominators. Pain relief, itch relief, ordinary daily life and leisure were prominent goals; it also reported no established HHD severity score at the time of its study. It is a baseline burden survey, not evidence of treatment effect or validation of an MCID. [S13]

### Minimum interpretable structure

1. **Confirm disease and define the use case.** Document diagnostic evidence, disease duration, affected sites and clinically indicated assessment of superinfection. Sequence genotype where it informs an exploratory mechanistic subgroup; do not pretend it was a requirement in the historical case series. Choose induction, maintenance or both prospectively.
2. **Capture trajectory before judging change.** Record the patient's recent flare frequency/duration and, when feasible, a prospective baseline while continuing necessary care. Document heat, friction, infection treatment and background therapy. A useful baseline does not require leaving painful erosions untreated or stopping effective care simply to manufacture a withdrawal/rechallenge story.
3. **Use a contemporaneous counterfactual.** Comparable paired lesions can make a small trial efficient, but randomize sites, standardize assessment and preserve the patient's role as the statistical unit. If matched lesions, credible comparator formulation or masking cannot be supplied, use a justified patient-randomized design with stable background care. Product/vehicle feasibility is a real design input, not an assumption.
4. **Measure sustained clinical value.** A proposed initial 8–12-week controlled window should quantify target-area erosion/fissuring, sustained epithelialization, cumulative erosion-days and rescue treatment, alongside pain/burning and a patient-selected functional limitation. These durations are planning choices, not validated HHD standards. Photographs need consistent lighting, position, scale and blinded review.
5. **Test maintenance explicitly.** A proposed 12–24-week extension can measure controlled days, flare severity/duration, frequency of application, rescue therapy and tolerability. Continued or intermittent control is a legitimate success state. Drug-free cure is not a necessary success criterion.

### Avoid predictable analytical errors

**Split-body interference:** cream transfer, anatomical differences, infection, sweating and systemic spillover can alter both sites. Some interference could attenuate a true difference; unbalanced lesion severity could create one. Record treated grams and exposure with the product/PK team, assess whether local independence is credible, and retain patient-level paired analysis. Whole-person DLQI and sleep cannot be assigned independently to each treated fold.

**Carryover:** healed tissue and altered inflammation can persist beyond drug clearance. Therefore a pharmacokinetic washout alone cannot reset an HHD lesion. A short crossover risks period and carryover effects; sequential paired periods should not be the default, and withdrawal should never be required merely to force recurrence.

**Missingness and rescue:** prespecify what happens when lesions are rescued, infected, photographed poorly or missed. Report both randomized patients and observed assessments at each visit, with a transparent estimand and sensitivity analysis. Do not copy DERM101's fixed denominator without recovering its undisclosed missing-data rule.

**Clinical importance:** no HHD-specific validated minimal clinically important difference was identified in this targeted review. A generic DLQI change or AD itch threshold must be labelled borrowed/exploratory if used. Prespecify a defensible patient anchor—meaningful improvement in pain and a chosen activity—alongside sustained lesion benefit. Early work should estimate reliability, variance and acceptable benefit/burden; it need not claim confirmatory power that its size cannot deliver.

**Decision discipline:** reproducible benefit should survive comparison with background care and remain meaningful after rescue use, treatment effort and tolerability are counted. Failure to lower a speculative IFN biomarker is not by itself failure of a clinically useful therapy. Conversely, attractive staining or rapid itch relief without meaningful control of erosions, function or burden does not establish the intended product claim. After adequate delivery is verified, lack of benefit in the intended value dimension is a stop/redesign result.
