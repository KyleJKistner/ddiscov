# Enasidenib in germline IDH2-R140Q D2HGA2: product, exposure and development proof

**Evidence cut: 4 November 2026.** Candidate `R5_ENASIDENIB_IDH2_R140_D2HGA2`; baseline `a834a480d74b1ebefbaa3fc072db17f67c2aa3d6`. This report owns the exact article, pharmacology, material/model routes, public program and patent facts, and staged development decision. The companion [clinical audit](enasidenib_clinical.md) owns patient identity, clinical outcomes and source inconsistencies. The [structured record](enasidenib_product.json) preserves source hashes, values, access limits and decision gates.

## Development decision

**A bounded pediatric article/exposure and longitudinal organ-function project is scientifically justified.** Existing exact-disease treatment observations move this beyond a target-discovery proposition. The next valuable result is a reproducible treatment article connected to measured parent/metabolite exposure, sustained biochemical control, tolerability and a clearly defined clinical benefit. Repeating generic mouse or cell rescue is not an automatic prerequisite. A mechanistic model earns funding when it distinguishes an exposure, tissue-response, safety or alternative-molecule question that the clinical observations cannot resolve.

The formulation problem has narrowed substantially. A sprinkle has actually been administered in the disease; an adult formulation crossover was completed; official trial records identify a sprinkle product; and a granted European patent discloses a concrete 20 mg free-base-equivalent minitablet capsule. These establish technical precedent. They do not establish that a particular pediatric lot is currently obtainable, equivalent to a proposed infant preparation, or cleared for a new commercial use. [P4, P9–P12; clinical C2]

The strongest initial clinical claim remains **useful benefit in genetically confirmed R140Q disease with an interpretable baseline organ deficit**. Early prevention, established cardiac recovery and neurological improvement require different outcomes. A smaller development project can be valuable before a standalone company proposition is established. The present record does not establish infant dosing, lifelong safety, a controlled treatment-effect estimate, or independent commercial rights.

Sources: [P1, P4–P5, P9–P13]. Enasidenib free base and mesylate are related chemical articles with different mass bases. The mesylate molecular weight is 569.48; using 473.4 for free base gives a salt/base factor of approximately 1.203. The patent's 24.05/20 ratio is consistent with that conversion after rounding. A record that says only “20 mg enasidenib” should not be silently reinterpreted as 20 mg of mesylate salt.

The patent makes formulation risk unusually concrete. Each minitablet weighs approximately 1.67 mg and is approximately 1.2 mm in diameter and height; the 250 mg figure is the total core content per capsule, not one minitablet or the active-drug mass. Development work discusses moisture-associated solid-form conversion, including amorphous free base and crystalline free base under different humidity conditions, with effects on dissolution. The proposed presentation uses protective packaging and refrigerated storage. Compatibility was studied with three specified soft foods: strawberry gelatin, vanilla pudding and strawberry nonfat yogurt. That does not validate infant milk, every food vehicle, dose subdivision or enteral-tube delivery. [P4]

The first article work should therefore resolve **one intended use configuration**, including base-equivalent content, solid form, content uniformity, dissolution, stability and recovery after the intended administration method. It should not assume that crushing marketed tablets reproduces the studied sprinkle. Nor should it claim a novel “first pediatric formulation” when public precedent already exists. Published excipient composition identifies a development starting point; age-specific suitability remains an explicit product question.

## 2. Pharmacology: a strong target match with an unfinished pediatric exposure bridge

### Mutant selectivity is assay-specific

The current label identifies R140Q, R172S and R172K inhibition at roughly 40-fold lower concentrations than wild-type IDH2 in vitro. The original FDA review supplies the underlying context: a coupled NADPH assay reports parent R140Q IC50 0.77 µM at one hour and wild-type IDH2 34.1 µM. Its table also lists R172S 0.155 µM and R172K 0.214 µM. These values do not establish equal efficacy across genotypes or a 40-fold clinical safety margin. [P1–P2]

Enasidenib is a slow-binding inhibitor. The primary discovery paper's indexed full-text excerpt reports different values after longer incubation: 0.10 µM for R140Q homodimer at 16 hours, 0.03 µM for R140Q/WT heterodimer, 0.01 µM for R172K/WT heterodimer, and 1.8 µM for the wild-type oxidative reaction at 16 hours. The reaction, dimer composition and incubation time must accompany any potency comparison. This source was verified as a primary publisher excerpt; the complete publisher article was not locally obtained. [P14]

FDA cell studies also show that biochemical enzyme potency cannot substitute for cellular activity. R140Q-overexpressing lines showed considerably stronger 2-HG suppression than the tested R172 cell contexts. The disease project's direct genotype anchor is germline heterozygous R140Q, not a pooled “IDH2-mutant” population. Wild-type mitochondrial function should remain in the interpretation of prolonged exposure; selective inhibition does not mean zero wild-type activity.

### Parent and metabolite both matter

Current label facts are adult data, not an infant prescription:

| Property | Verified current value/context | Development implication |
|---|---|---|
| Accumulation | Approximately tenfold; steady state within 29 days of daily dosing | Link samples and outcomes to elapsed treatment and dose changes |
| Terminal half-life | 7.9 days | A brief dose interruption is not a drug-free withdrawal test |
| Adult plasma Cmax | 1.4 µg/mL after one 100 mg dose; 13.1 µg/mL at steady state | Do not copy adult dose or total plasma concentration into an infant target |
| Plasma binding | Parent 98.5%; AGI-16903 96.6% in vitro | Total concentration does not directly give active tissue exposure |
| Human circulating species | Parent accounts for 89% of radioactivity; N-dealkylated AGI-16903 about 10% | Quantify parent and active metabolite where feasible |
| Covariate scope | Age 19–100 years, weight 39–136 kg in the reported adult assessment | “No clinically meaningful covariate effect” does not cover infancy |

Sources: [P1–P2]. FDA's review confirms pharmacologic activity of AGI-16903; its reported R140Q IC50 is 0.016 µM, compared with 1.2 µM for R172K in the stated 16-hour context. AGI-17011 is another experimentally active metabolite considered in nonclinical work, but the EMA assessment says it was not detected in humans. It should not be added to a human active-metabolite burden by assumption. [P2–P3]

As an explicitly illustrative calculation, 13.1 µg/mL divided by 473.4 g/mol corresponds to approximately 27.7 µM total parent; multiplying by the adult in-vitro unbound fraction 0.015 gives approximately 0.415 µM. This is **not measured infant free plasma, myocardial or CNS exposure**, and cross-assay potency ratios derived from it are not validated clinical margins. Its purpose is to expose the assumptions that a simple “dose is low” argument hides.

### Directly measured interactions prevent a clean-selectivity shortcut

The January 2025 label includes substantial measured adult interactions. At studied enasidenib exposure, caffeine AUC rose 655%, omeprazole AUC 86%, rosuvastatin AUC 244%, and intravenous midazolam AUC fell 43%; digoxin AUC increased 20%. These probe results involve CYP1A2, CYP2C19, CYP3A and transport processes. The FDA approval letter identifies the PK-label update as arising from study CC-90007-CP-007. [P1, P15]

A pediatric project needs a time-linked record of cardiac, antiseizure and other medicines, with targeted exposure measurements when the actual combination makes them informative. The probe results cannot be converted into an exact interaction magnitude for an untested child's medicine. Conversely, background-therapy changes can alter both apparent benefit and adverse findings and should not be treated as incidental notes.

The secondary pharmacology is also real. FDA's full review reports **adenosine A3 functional-antagonism IC50 values of 5.66 nM for enasidenib and 120 nM for AGI-16903**. The regulator explicitly considered parent A3 activity plausible at therapeutic adult free concentrations. Binding potency and functional antagonism are different rows of the source table; 0.69 nM is the parent binding IC50, not the functional value. [P2, printed p37, visually checked]

This is a reason to measure and interpret chronic exposure, not proof of a harmful A3 effect. The EMA assessment states that clinical consequences of potential A3 antagonism had not been observed; its overall evaluation found limited QT concern despite animal findings. The current label reports no large mean QTc change above 20 ms in its studied hematologic-malignancy setting. Neither statement establishes lifelong individual cardiac safety in nonmalignant infants. [P1–P3]

## 3. Chronic safety and tissue claims must stay attached to their evidence

**Bilirubin handling and hepatocellular injury are separate questions.** Enasidenib inhibits UGT1A1. In the labeled adult AML safety dataset, 80/214 patients experienced bilirubin at least twice the upper limit of normal; 89% of those lacked concurrent transaminase elevation or other severe hepatic adverse events. This supports a mechanistic explanation for many bilirubin elevations. It does not explain away Gold's ALT/CK observations, resolve that report's timing conflict, or make adult AML event rates the expected risk in children with D2HGA2. [P1; clinical C3]

The direct pediatric case audit is the appropriate disease-specific safety anchor. An interpretable prospective dataset should retain bilirubin fractions, aminotransferases, muscle enzymes where indicated, liver synthetic function, clinical findings, concomitant medicines and exposure alongside dose changes. No current source supplies a validated lifelong infant exposure ceiling.

The public oncology nonclinical package is informative but incomplete for that use. The FDA/EMA reviews describe negative standard genotoxicity tests, no carcinogenicity study under the oncology development framework, reproductive-organ findings in repeat-dose animals, and lack of the full fertility/early-embryonic and pre/postnatal study set. The EMA's historical AML pediatric plan waived birth to under two years. That AML-specific regulatory history does not validate or forbid a new infant metabolic-disease project; it identifies why a direct age-appropriate bridge is needed. Public reviews may not contain later unpublished sponsor data. [P2–P3]

Differentiate the context-dependent AML differentiation-syndrome warning from the developmental, metabolic and interaction questions that can transfer across indications. Do not assign the AML incidence to nonmalignant children, and do not infer that the warning's disease context makes every other safety issue disappear.

**Systemic 2-HG correction is not a CNS exposure measurement.** FDA reports low rat CSF penetration of 0.3% and modest brain penetration of 10% following a specified single oral study; these original pages were visually checked. Human infant brain exposure cannot be derived from those percentages. The 2023 clinical supplement supplies neither CSF D-2-HG correction nor a measured CNS drug concentration; one patient lacked MRI/lumbar puncture, and the other's MRI was unchanged at twelve months. [P2; clinical C2]

Consequently, a cardiac project should not sell itself as established brain rescue. A separate neurological claim requires prospective developmental/seizure measures and a defensible tissue-mechanism bridge. Improving cardiac performance, systemic metabolic stress or seizure treatment can influence activity without proving direct neuronal correction.

## 4. What models actually exist, and what each can answer

| Route | Evidence and readiness | Useful role | Qualification or limitation |

Screening fee and commercial-use conditions are stated |
| **Historical patient lymphoblasts** | Three unrelated D2HGA2 patient cultures tested in the 2016 AGI-026 study | Exact-genotype human biochemical response | Publication establishes historical cells, not a catalog accession, current vial or granted reuse scope |
| **Endogenous heterozygous R140Q mouse** | Published native-locus knock-in with systemic expression and cardiac disease | Target-level organismal function and tissue-PD questions | Actual colony access/rederivation not established here; strain-related kidney phenotype and neonatal loss limit direct translation |
| **Inducible R140Q transgenic mouse** | Akbay 2014, separately retrieved full primary paper | Demonstrates that established cardiac dysfunction can improve after mutant-expression shutdown | Non-native promoter and induced transgene; not the 2016 endogenous model or an enasidenib efficacy experiment |
| **Human heterozygous R140Q cardiomyocyte/isogenic pair** | A proposed engineering/qualification route; no public exact-disease ready pair was verified in this bounded search | Compound exposure versus useful cardiac-function discrimination | Must first show a reproducible disease deficit and post-deficit modifiability; multiple wells do not create independent donors |

Sources: [P6–P8]. D2HGDH knockout lines model type-I catabolism, not this gain-of-function IDH2 disease. A reported nuclear-targeted R140Q construct in a cardiomyocyte stress paper is also not an endogenous mitochondrial D2HGA2 model. Those attractive keyword matches should not be promoted to ready exact-disease assays. No identified ready line in this search is a bounded negative finding, not proof that none exists.

### The AGI-026 evidence is useful, but it belongs to AGI-026

Wang 2016 directly tested a different inhibitor in the endogenous heterozygous model. Cardiac abnormalities were present from approximately week four; treatment began at four to five weeks. The primary paper reports major plasma/heart 2-HG suppression, improved cardiac structure/function and survival. It used random allocation but was unblinded and had no formal prospective sample-size calculation. Mouse total 2-HG was used as a surrogate for D-2-HG, whereas the patient-lymphoblast assay separately measured D and L enantiomers. [P7]

The late crossover is particularly easy to oversell. After the preceding 13-week experiment, seven prior vehicle animals started AGI-026 and six prior treated animals stopped it. At one month, late-treatment ejection-fraction improvement was a **non-significant trend**; withdrawal significantly worsened the reported cardiac measures. At eight weeks, survival was 7/7 in late-treated animals and 2/6 after withdrawal. Small groups, prior-treatment selection and interim survivor denominators limit a clean late-reversal estimate. This is useful target/modifiability evidence; it is not a replicated human-recovery effect size. [P7]

The same paper reports AGI-026 total brain/plasma ratios of 1.6 and 1.9 in wild-type and mutant mice at twelve hours after the specified dose. These numbers cannot be assigned to enasidenib. In the separate 2014 genetic experiment, turning off inducible R140Q after established disease improved fractional shortening with reduced circulating 2-HG. That supports a post-deficit genetic positive-control concept without claiming that all late damage is reversible. [P7–P8]

If a new human cardiac model is funded, its role should be explicit: distinguish biochemical correction from useful contractile/relaxation benefit, persistent structural damage from an unmodifiable assay, and compound-specific effects from target suppression. Mutant/WT or corrected controls, a post-deficit genetic rescue and an appropriate chemically distinct comparator are more informative than repeating one 2-HG marker. Exposure, assay duration and cell health must remain linked to the result. A negative result in an unqualified or unmodifiable culture does not override positive human observations.

## 6. Staged proof and first funded outputs

### Stage A — define and qualify one pediatric article and measurement system

**Funded output:** a release-ready research specification for one intended pediatric presentation/administration method, analytical methods, a linked PK/PD/function data dictionary, and a prospectively specified feasibility protocol. “Release-ready specification” means the deliverable defines what must pass; it does not mean this desktop review has produced released clinical material.

The article package should specify base/salt basis, identity, impurities, content/delivery uniformity, solid form, dissolution and stability, including the intended food or tube route only if that route is part of the product. Establish whether the project will use an existing documented clinical article or develop a genuinely different one. Match the measurement plan to clinically feasible sampling, quantifying parent plus AGI-16903 and chiral D-2-HG where feasible, with consistent timing and matrices. Preserve longitudinal organ function, tolerability and background treatment in the same record.

**Advance:** a defined article and methods can produce interpretable delivered-exposure and clinical observations. **Redirect article work:** content, recovery, stability or analytical performance fails. Failure of article delivery is not target failure. **Stop the product claim:** the proposed improvement duplicates an adequate existing presentation without solving a residual problem. No new mouse efficacy experiment is required simply to pass this stage.

### Stage B — exploratory pediatric exposure/PD and organ-function feasibility

**Funded output:** a small prospective, appropriately governed longitudinal dataset connecting actual administration, parent/metabolite exposure, biochemical suppression, tolerability and a prespecified organ outcome. This document specifies the research decision, not a dose or treatment instruction.

Choose one primary clinical question. In established cardiac disease, measure repeatable cardiac function with blinded central review where feasible, age/body-size-appropriate normalization of cardiac dimensions and mass, and contemporaneous height/weight and heart-failure therapy changes. Absolute chamber dimensions alone are difficult to compare through growth. In newly diagnosed infants, define preservation and growth/development trajectories; a normal baseline cardiac measure creates a ceiling, not an opportunity to label stability as reversal. Neurodevelopment and seizures need their own structured assessments and concurrent-therapy record. Skin may be an additional visible outcome in affected patients, not a proxy for myocardial or brain exposure.

Use repeated baselines where clinically appropriate, careful natural-history comparators and supportive care throughout. Do not deliberately withdraw useful treatment merely to create a contrast. Slow accumulation makes a short interruption especially hard to interpret. A formulation comparison may be within-person if justified by the research protocol, but washout/crossover assumptions must not be imported from the completed healthy-adult study.

**Advance:** technically reliable delivery and measurements characterize observed exposure, sustained biochemical control, tolerability within the studied follow-up, and an interpretable functional signal or preservation trajectory. This exploratory experience does not define validated infant safety bounds. **Continue only to resolve a named uncertainty:** precision or observation length is inadequate. **Redirect:** biochemical control is insufficient because delivery/exposure fails; harms appear exposure-related; or function cannot be assessed in the selected baseline state. A positive exploratory series motivates confirmation; it does not establish a controlled efficacy estimate.

### Stage C — confirm the specific benefit and product advantage

**Funded output:** an independently reviewed analysis with a prespecified clinically useful effect, transparent uncertainty and an appropriate comparison. Recruitment size should follow feasible population ascertainment and the chosen outcome's variance; this review does not invent either.

Confirm useful function or preservation across independently characterized participants, with genotype, baseline severity and concurrent therapy retained. Use matched natural history or other defensible controls, masked outcome assessment and explicit sensitivity analyses for maturation, missing data, treatment changes and regression to the mean. Measure whether any simpler administration or exposure adjustment actually retains benefit and improves a patient-important burden or tolerability outcome. A lower measured 2-HG alone is insufficient differentiation when existing treatment already suppresses it strongly.

**Advance to a larger program:** a credible benefit–exposure relation and product advantage survive those comparisons, with a practicable manufacturing and development route. **Narrow the claim:** cardiac benefit is supported but neurological, preventive or wider-genotype benefit is not. **Change article:** target benefit remains plausible but delivery or chronic compound exposure is unsuitable. **Stop independent product development:** no useful improvement over existing treatment is demonstrated. A scientific dataset can retain value even when a company proposition does not.

### Optional model branch — only for a discriminating decision

A first material/PD comparison can use identified TF-1 R140Q and parental cells; this should not be relabeled cardiac proof. A human cardiac model is worth developing when a consequential question remains about contractile recovery, dose–function separation, toxicity or a substitute inhibitor. Require a reproducible baseline deficit, a credible post-deficit corrective control, authentic allele/mitochondrial context, measured exposure, and independent biological replication. Choose one principal functional readout before confirmatory work; additional molecular readouts explain it.

**Interpretation branches:** no target engagement → article/exposure problem; target engagement without function in an unmodifiable model → model problem; reproducible target-specific lack of benefit in qualified modifiable models → narrow or challenge that functional hypothesis; function with unacceptable wild-type/healthy-tissue effects → change exposure/article. These branches prevent a null result from becoming a catch-all rejection or an unexplained positive marker from becoming an efficacy claim.

### Scope, cost drivers and stopping rules

The first package should price one article/administration route, necessary assay validation, feasible sampling, central outcome review and data integration. Major cost drivers are new formulation/CMC work versus an existing article, analytical method development, small-volume parent/metabolite measurements, number and duration of clinical observations, imaging/functional assessments and whether a custom model must be engineered. No vendor quote, actual supply, investigator capacity, budget or clinical execution has been established here.

Further desktop work earns time only if it can change an article, exposure, endpoint, comparator, material or contribution decision. The 2026 infant abstract remains a useful unresolved source; inaccessible metadata does not warrant an extra response count. Repeated searches for the same positive cases should not postpone this defined funding decision. Current material terms and supply will eventually be execution facts, but an owner email is not the scientific gate used to select the project.
