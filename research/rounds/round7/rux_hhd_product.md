# Ruxolitinib 1.5% cream / Hailey–Hailey disease: article, exposure and development opportunity

**Evidence cut: 4 November 2026. Candidate R6-NL-01.** This is the product and pharmacology half of the round-7 dossier. Read it with the [clinical and causal audit](rux_hhd_clinical.md), [staged development proof](rux_hhd_development_proof.md) and [reproducible source-value audit](rux_hhd_source_audit.json). It uses primary publications, product documents, registries, patents and sponsor filings. Proposed studies below have not been performed.

## Decision

**Advance a finite, controlled clinical proof of useful HHD lesion control with the exact marketed 1.5% cream.** The existing human reports and a manufactured topical article justify this stage without first rebuilding the disease in a new cell line. The proposed result is a clinically meaningful reduction in painful active lesions, recurrence and rescue-treatment burden at acceptable exposure. Persistent treatment dependence can be compatible with a useful product.

The commercial conclusion is narrower. The ingredient and cream belong to an established franchise, and independent publications already disclose the HHD use. A successful pilot could produce valuable clinical-development data, but it would not by itself create a proprietary drug asset. A credible standalone product requires a distinguishable benefit and an executable regulatory, manufacturing and rights route. Current public evidence supports the clinical proof more strongly than a new-company investment thesis.

## 1. Define the exact article

The June 2026 US Opzelura label, reference ID 5826015, specifies **15 mg ruxolitinib free-base equivalent per gram**, supplied as **19.8 mg ruxolitinib phosphate** in an oil-in-water cream. Ruxolitinib is the R stereoisomer; free-base and phosphate molecular weights are 306.4 and 404.36. The listed marketed presentations are 60-g and 100-g tubes. The label establishes approved AD and vitiligo uses; it does not establish an HHD indication. [P1]

For an initial clinical proof, the article is an authenticated marketed product with recorded presentation, lot, expiry, storage, dispensed/returned mass and actual application. Research-grade free base, phosphate powder and an independently compounded 1.5% preparation are different articles. A changed vehicle can change dissolved fraction, delivery, irritation and systemic absorption; nominal percentage alone is insufficient to inherit the clinical observations.

The five clinical reports specify the active ingredient, 1.5% concentration and topical route; they do not uniformly establish brand, quantitative vehicle composition or lot identity. The proposed study standardizes the marketed article prospectively. It does not recast all historical cases as authenticated trials of one verified formulation batch.

Cayman publicly defines research free-base article 11609 (CAS 941678-49-5) and phosphate article 23215 (CAS 1092939-17-7). These are plausible analytical or cell-assay starting routes, subject to the actual batch certificate and authentication. They do not supply a clinical formulation, a received batch or guaranteed current stock. A first HHD clinical proof does not need an independent synthesis project when an approved manufactured article is the intended intervention. [P10]

### Vehicle control is a tractable design choice

The public ingredient list is not a quantitative GMP formula. A fully matched commercial vehicle was not established as an independently available article in this review. That should be resolved through the study design rather than turned into an indefinite owner-reply gate.

There are two credible routes. If a qualified matched vehicle can be specified and obtained, a blinded comparison can isolate the contribution of ruxolitinib in that vehicle. Otherwise, an assessor-blinded randomized comparison of the marketed cream plus standardized background care against the same background care can estimate the incremental benefit of the whole marketed product. The second design retains participant expectation and vehicle effects; it answers a different, still useful clinical question. A random moisturizer is not a matched placebo.

This distinction has a concrete formulation basis. US10758543B2 includes a development example in which the placebo and active creams have different pH values. That example does **not** identify the current commercial product's pH or the clinical trials' exact control. It demonstrates why pH, rheology, barrier effects and masking must be qualified if a reconstructed vehicle is used. [P8]

## 2. Systemic exposure is usually low in AD, with a relevant high-exposure tail

| Primary dataset | Actual unit and exposure | What it contributes |
|---|---|---|
| Gong 2021, pooled phase III AD PK | **479** participants in the 1.5% twice-daily PK group; mean treated BSA 9.60%; mean steady-state trough **35.7 ± 55.0 nM**, median 15.4 nM | A relevant manufactured-cream exposure anchor in AD. This is a PK subset, not the complete randomized efficacy denominator. |
| Bissonnette 2022 maximum-use AD study | **41** treated, **40** with combined steady-state PK; baseline BSA 25–90%; mean Css **104 ± 309 nM** | Broad variability matters. Age/BSA strata and visits overlap within the same study. |
| Same study, ≥40% BSA stratum | Css n=12, **274 ± 539 nM**; lower-BSA stratum n=28, 30.9 ± 32.8 nM | Treatment extent and severe barrier disease affect the observed distribution; means alone obscure extremes. |
| Same study, supplementary Table S1 | Five selected highest-exposure participants; the two highest Css values were **711 and 1,870 nM**, at 45% and 90% BSA | These are selected observations within the original study. The latter participant's platelet count fell from a derived 400 to 173 ×10⁹/L, remaining within the source's normal range. This is not an HHD risk estimate. |

Sources: [P2–P3]. The audit preserves the primary tables and their denominators. Its calculation of the last platelet change is **−56.75%**; it is descriptive arithmetic, not proof of a causal drug-related event.

The often-cited **281 nM** reference comes from a whole-blood TPO-stimulated pSTAT3 assay used as a JAK2/myelosuppression proxy. It is not a universal clinical safety threshold, an HHD efficacy target or an unbound-plasma concentration. Dividing total plasma concentration by this value, or applying 97% plasma binding and treating the quotient as a therapeutic index, mixes unlike measurements. [P2–P3]

The label's adult day-1 Cmax of **449 ± 883 nM** belongs to the adult subset; the study's overall day-1 table gives 271 ± 650 nM. These are compatible populations, not contradictory estimates. The **116-hour** apparent terminal half-life was estimated in only **nine** participants and was highly variable. No accumulation was observed over the study's first 28 days. These facts do not supply an HHD washout interval or a model for accumulation through eroded flexural skin. [P1, P3]

### Consequence for HHD

HHD-specific plasma exposure, local drug concentration and a validated exposure–response relationship were not recovered. Maceration, erosions, fold location, applied grams, treated surface, infection and healing may change exposure. The initial development population can reasonably emphasize adults with limited treated area, but that is a prospective scope decision, not proof that systemic risk is negligible.

Collect application mass, area and lesion state alongside exposure samples early in treatment and after healing. Analyze concentration by participant and time; retain outliers. A plasma measurement helps characterize systemic exposure but does not certify adequate epidermal engagement. If an optional local sampling method is used, record tissue composition and assay recovery. The aim is to characterize the observed exposure–benefit and tolerability relationship within the studied population and follow-up, without claiming a validated long-term safety boundary or forcing observations below an imported numerical cutoff.

## 3. Safety: retain the source population and formulation

The current label carries JAK-class warnings and cautions concerning infection, malignancy, major cardiovascular events and thrombosis, with clinical context drawn substantially from systemic JAK treatment. It also specifies local and laboratory precautions, avoidance of strong CYP3A4 inhibitors, and that combinations with therapeutic biologics, other JAK inhibitors or potent immunosuppressants are not recommended. Topical drug-interaction studies have not been conducted; oral interaction results are not topical HHD measurements. [P1]

The label permits treatment of up to 20% BSA for AD and 10% for vitiligo and instructs against occlusive dressings. These are indication-specific usage boundaries, not validated safe HHD limits; anatomical folds and eroded skin still require their own exposure interpretation. [P1]

The June 2026 revision adds hypoglycemia reported with oral JAK inhibitors in patients with diabetes under potential class risks. This is not an observed HHD-cream event rate. Likewise, dizziness/falls reported during oral upadacitinib treatment in a combination case must not be assigned to ruxolitinib cream. The clinical dossier preserves these article distinctions.

For a study in affected folds, infection assessment and consistent management are important both clinically and causally: improvement after treating infection can otherwise be attributed to the study cream. Record local irritation, infection, rescue treatment, relevant systemic events and laboratory measures according to the finalized protocol and label-informed risk assessment. A small case series with no reported event cannot exclude uncommon or delayed toxicity.

## 4. Mechanistic models: useful tools with specific limits

The clinical question is useful lesion control. Direct correction of the inherited calcium-pump defect is a stronger mechanistic claim that the ruxolitinib cases do not establish. A plausible anti-inflammatory effect can improve clinical function without repairing the causal allele.

### The 2026 ATP2C1 model supplies a route, not an already qualified rux assay

Gupta and colleagues' final 2026 paper and its December 2025 preprint belong to **one project**. They describe monoallelic and biallelic ATP2C1 disruption, altered Golgi calcium and junctional proteins, and impaired actin-associated spreading. The recovered full preprint uses an acute spreading/regrowth paradigm. Rescue with SPCA1 expression supports causality, but transfection preceding replating does not establish delayed repair of a mature, mechanically challenged epidermal barrier. [P4]

There are two material transfer issues. First, the manuscript uses N/TERT and Ker-CT names inconsistently; Cellosaurus identifies N/TERT-2 separately from the ATCC Ker-CT line. An ATCC catalog purchase cannot silently stand in for the exact published parent. Second, the indexed final paper reports little restoration of junctional morphology with SERCA activator CDN1163, despite improvements in other cell features. CDN1163 should therefore not be assumed to be a qualified positive control for complete barrier repair. The preprint and final article are retained as versions with different verified access levels. [P4–P6]

ATCC CRL-4048 is a defined Ker-CT parent with a 3D-skin precedent and documented immortalization/genomic features. It is an alternative starting system, not a catalog HHD model. The Brigham cell-core list is an archival 2010 document listing N/TERT cells; it establishes historical identity/provenance, not current stock or an applicable commercial license. A separately engineered ATP2C1-heterozygous model remains possible but needs genotype, identity, function and intended-use qualification. [P5–P6]

### Organotypic and inflammatory context

A separate 2026 MEK/ROCK preprint reports organotypic SPCA1-deficient acantholysis and restored keratinocyte-sheet integrity in its accessible abstract. The full methods, exact intervention details and biological denominators were not recovered. It is an orthogonal model/comparator lead, not verified ruxolitinib efficacy. [P7]

If mechanism work changes a development choice, use a factorial design separating ATP2C1 status from an inflammatory challenge, with measured ruxolitinib target engagement and functional cohesion/permeability. An immune-free keratinocyte assay can miss an anti-inflammatory mechanism; a negative result there should not veto a clinical signal. Conversely, pSTAT reduction, actin spreading or marker normalization alone should not be called restored barrier function. Donors/backgrounds and culture batches are biological units; cells and images are nested measurements.

## 5. Competition is active and differentiated

The ClinicalTrials.gov condition query retrieved seven records on 4 November, with no pagination remainder. None was an exact ruxolitinib/HHD interventional record in that query. This is a bounded registry-search result, not proof that no study or commercial program exists elsewhere. [P11]

**KB111 / HALITE-1** is a topical non-integrating, replication-defective HSV-1 vector delivering ATP2C1. The July registry still displays estimated enrollment of seven and recruiting status. Krystal's **2 November 2026** filing reports enrollment complete at **five**, with weekly dosing ongoing over a 12-week period and no efficacy outcome disclosed. Use the newer dated sponsor fact for actual enrollment; keep registry design facts separate. The protocol permits stable background systemic treatment, so later uncontrolled results will need that context. [P12]

**Guselkumab / NCT06651489** is a Yale single-group phase II HHD proof-of-concept study, estimated n=10, recruiting as of its June 2026 update. It is another active inflammatory strategy; no result is inferred from an expected completion date. Registry outcome descriptions do not establish that the chosen scales have HHD-specific measurement validity or a validated minimum important change. [P13]

Incyte's October 2026 filing places ruxolitinib cream in phase III hidradenitis suppurativa studies with topline results expected in Q4. HS and HHD are different diseases. Those programs show an active skin franchise and delivery experience, not HHD efficacy or a protected HHD opportunity for this portfolio. [P9]

## 6. Public rights and regulatory route

### Existing claims matter even when the indication is new

The official FDA Orange Book ZIP retrieved on 4 November has internal file dates of **9 October 2026**. For NDA 215309, it contains **39 patent rows representing 22 distinct base patent numbers** after pediatric-extension suffixes are normalized. Repeated use codes and extensions are not additional independent patents. The linked audit retains the selected official rows, expiry fields and product/exclusivity fields. [P14]

One concrete claim family, **US10758543B2**, includes composition claims to an oil-in-water cream containing solubilized ruxolitinib phosphate within specified concentration and pH bounds. Those composition claims are not confined to AD. A search that fails to locate an HHD-specific patent does not establish a clear route to making the same formulation. The claim screen identifies a question for a product strategy; it is not a freedom-to-operate opinion. [P8]

The current Incyte Q3 filing reports ANDA notices from Padagis, Taro, Zydus and Encube and pending infringement actions. It says the challenged formulation/use patents extend into 2031 and 2040, while the notices do not challenge the composition-of-matter patents with coverage including a pediatric extension to December 2028. These are the sponsor's reported positions. Patent dates do not guarantee a generic launch, patent validity or the scope of an independently proposed product. [P9]

### A clinical research route is different from a new-indication filing

An initial study using a marketed article can have a concrete preparation path. FDA's marketed-drug IND exemption requires all applicable criteria, including that the investigation is not intended to support a new indication or significant labeling change. A project whose purpose is new-indication drug development should not assume it is exempt simply because the cream is approved elsewhere. [P15]

A 505(b)(2) strategy may potentially rely in part on existing findings or published evidence, but the particular application and bridging requirements remain to be specified. That pathway does not grant patent clearance. Three-year new-clinical-investigation exclusivity is conditional, limited in scope and not awarded automatically for a case series or pilot. The March 2026 FDA Q&A is explicitly draft guidance. Neither mechanism establishes control of ordinary off-label prescribing. [P16–P17]

### What the portfolio could create

| Potential output | Added value | Current limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Controlled HHD efficacy, recurrence and exposure dataset with exact marketed article | Removes uncertainty in the clinical proposition; potentially useful for a sponsored indication-development package | Data are useful without being an exclusive molecule. Publication history and existing franchise affect value capture. |
| A meaningfully improved local product or regimen | Could reduce painful exposure, treatment burden or systemic absorption while preserving benefit | No such improvement is demonstrated here. A different formulation needs its own article/PK/clinical bridge and claim analysis. |
| Evidence for a well-defined treatment sequence or maintenance strategy | May clarify when topical control avoids burdensome systemic therapy or procedures | No comparative superiority or protected sequence is established. Strong standard care remains a comparator. |

The present recommendation is to create the first output through a finite proof if its sponsor and budget fit the intended development objective. A new proprietary formulation should be a conditional response to a measured clinical limitation, not a novelty exercise added to an already available cream. There is no public evidence in this dossier that the portfolio owns the ingredient, the marketed formulation or an exclusive HHD right.

## 7. Reproducibility and access limits

The source audit reads hash-verified primary JATS PK tables, the publisher's maximum-use supplement and the official Orange Book ZIP. It preserves units and changing denominators and calculates only descriptive arithmetic. Raw articles, patent PDFs and labels are working intermediates, not redistributed paper collections. Retrieval manifests record current registry and SEC snapshot hashes in the structured dossier.

The nine-patient inflammatory-signature preprint remains abstract-only in the clinical dossier; PMC explicitly limits its record to the abstract. Its genotype distribution, controls, exact regimens and cohort overlap remain unverified. The 2026 ATP2C1 actin paper's final version was reviewed through indexed primary sections, alongside a fully retrieved earlier preprint. The MEK/ROCK paper remains abstract-level. These access limits are not silently filled with review articles or inferred protocol details.
