# Enasidenib for germline IDH2-R140Q disease: clinical evidence and causal audit

**Evidence cut: 4 November 2026.** Candidate `R5_ENASIDENIB_IDH2_R140_D2HGA2`. This extends the round 6 assessment at `a834a480d74b1ebefbaa3fc072db17f67c2aa3d6`. The scope is exact-disease human evidence, participant lineage, clinically useful function, adverse observations and the clinical question worth developing. Formulation, pharmacology, material/model routes and commercial contribution are separate parts of the development dossier.

## Decision

**Enasidenib has a credible human treatment signal that justifies a defined development project.** The strongest direct observations are rapid biochemical suppression in genetically confirmed disease, improvement of longstanding severe cardiac dysfunction in one child, and a separate child's reported cardiac, seizure and developmental improvement. An enasidenib pediatric sprinkle has already been administered. This supports a more advanced clinical hypothesis than a molecule supported only by surrogate cell phenotypes. [C1–C3]

The next investment should address a specific unresolved treatment question: reproducible pediatric delivery, exposure sufficient for sustained mutant-metabolite control, tolerability, and prospectively measured organ function. An additional cell experiment earns its place when it discriminates dose, formulation, mechanism or tissue response. Repeating a generic rescue assay is not automatically a prerequisite to a clinical formulation/exposure project merely because another portfolio candidate needs that assay.

The evidence does not yet provide a controlled treatment-effect estimate, an infant dose/exposure rule, generalized recovery across IDH2 variants, or a complete count of independently treated patients. These limits should determine the scope and measurements of the next project. They do not negate the existing positive research decision.

## 1. Define the disease before counting evidence

The directly treated, fully described genotype is heterozygous **IDH2 c.419G>A, p.Arg140Gln (R140Q)**. The original gene-discovery study identified Arg140 germline mutations in 15 unrelated patients. R140G is also a reported disease allele, but the clinical treatment records audited here do not establish its response. [C6–C7]

Keep this indication separate from D2HGDH-related D-2-hydroxyglutaric aciduria type I, L2HGDH disease, combined D/L-2-hydroxyglutaric aciduria and somatic IDH-associated skeletal or malignant disease. Elevated total 2-HG is not by itself the exact treatment-selection biomarker. The clinical proposition requires the causal genotype and appropriate D/L-metabolite interpretation. A gain-of-function enzyme that produces D-2-HG is the intended target; clinical observations in another 2-HG disorder do not supply enasidenib efficacy here. [C6–C7]

The proposed clinical value has at least three distinct forms: recovery of a modifiable established organ deficit, stabilization of a progressive deficit, and prevention of injury after early diagnosis. These require different baseline states and outcome interpretations. The existing older-child observations are encouraging for recovery; they do not establish how much neonatal brain injury can be prevented or reversed.

## 3. What the human organ evidence establishes

### Cardiac recovery is the most persuasive established-disease signal

G23-P1 had severe cardiac disease from infancy and began enasidenib at seven. The public supplement records LVEF **20–25% at entry**, **35–40% at six months**, and **40–45% at eight months**. The eight-month value corresponds to an arithmetic increase of 15–25 percentage points across the reported ranges; that is not a statistical confidence interval. The first listed furosemide reduction was at eight months. Subsequent values fluctuate: 36% at 20 months, 45% at 32 months, a later decline during carvedilol adjustment, and 40–45% at 46 months. [C2]

The trajectory is coherent with treatment benefit after established dysfunction, and early improvement precedes the first listed diuretic de-escalation. Concurrent heart-failure treatment, unblinded measurements and absence of a matched counterfactual prevent attribution of the full change to enasidenib. Function remains impaired; “normalization” is inappropriate for this child. Absolute LV dimensions also improved, but age/size-adjusted normalization cannot be reconstructed from the available values.

G23-P2's fractional shortening was 34% at entry, 32% at six months and 40% at twelve months, with normal baseline systolic function. The supplement describes borderline diastolic findings and later normal function; metoprolol began at approximately one year. This trajectory cannot establish recovery from severe systolic failure or prove prevention of future cardiomyopathy. [C2]

G24-P1 had low-normal LV systolic shortening and mild dilation in the pretreatment history, followed by reported normal size/function. Earlier QTc reached 546 ms, whereas follow-up QTc was 430 ms; no visit-level cardiac dataset or baseline/follow-up LVEF series is supplied. The result corroborates cardiac plausibility but has a different starting severity from G23-P1. [C3]

### Developmental and seizure outcomes are valuable but less attributable

The 2023 supplement describes improved mobility, activity, interaction and autonomy over months to years. It does not supply a common, masked longitudinal neurodevelopmental effect estimate. Education, therapies, maturation and reporting by families remain part of the causal setting. These observations should guide patient-important endpoints without being converted into a numerical global neurological rescue. [C2]

Gold's report used Bayley-4 and Vineland-3 before treatment and at 15 and 36 months. Its summary includes gross-motor age equivalents from 4 to 10 months and receptive-language equivalents from 1 to 10 months; cognitive/play skills remained at approximately six-month equivalents. The summary is not a complete visit-by-instrument dataset. Reported seizure freedom continued through withdrawal of antiseizure medicines, but the publication does not provide the seizure-event and medication timeline needed to isolate each contribution. [C3]

Acquisition of useful skills can matter greatly even while the age-adjusted developmental gap remains large. Age-equivalent gains cannot be treated as equal-interval scores or as proof of catching up with chronological age. A future prospective study should retain raw item-level or growth-scale data appropriate to the instrument, treatment dates, concomitant therapies and the patient's own baseline trajectory. The current evidence is sufficient to motivate that work, not to estimate its effect size with precision.

### The skin findings support a multisystem biological effect

Roux's two cases describe different histiocytic/granulomatous eruptions that had not improved with topical corticosteroids and subsequently cleared during systemic enasidenib treatment. One reports a four-month response interval. Full open-access XML was recovered and read in this sprint. It provides useful peripheral-organ corroboration, but no numerical cardiac response, detailed enasidenib formulation/dose or controlled lesion comparison. [C4]

These observations make skin potentially useful as an additional visible clinical outcome in affected patients. A skin response cannot certify cardiac or CNS exposure, and the dermatology paper cannot be used to infer that enasidenib was started at eight months.

## 4. Biochemical engagement is strong; organ linkage remains specific

In G23-P1, serum D-2-HG fell from **24.92 to 0.25 µmol/L by day 8**. In G23-P2 it fell from **17.32 to 0.20 µmol/L by day 28**. Calculated reductions are approximately 99.0% and 98.8%, respectively; later reported values remained within the stated normal range. This is strong within-person evidence that the intended biochemical pathway can be suppressed in the exact disease. [C2]

G23-P1 lacked a contemporaneous pretreatment urine sample. Its diagnosis-age urine result must not be presented as a study-entry measurement. The supplement also records a modest serum rise after four days of medication interruption, still within the listed normal range. With a long-lived drug, a short interruption does not establish drug-free durability. It supplies no reliable withdrawal threshold or clinical recurrence experiment.

Gold's urine table shows large, rapid suppression. A later reduction to three weekly doses reportedly maintained it; attempts at twice-weekly dosing produced detectable trough metabolite. These are observations in one child with an incompletely reconciled timeline, not a validated pediatric schedule. The table's 30-month urine value is 17 mg/g creatinine, but the exact correspondence between that sample and the twice-weekly attempt is not explicitly established. [C3]

Do not pool Gold's reported urine organic-acid values with chiral serum D-2-HG as if they were the same exposure measure. Creatinine normalization, age, matrix, enantiomer resolution and timing differ. No measured patient enasidenib concentrations or CNS concentration–response series were recovered from these exact-disease reports. The original supplement says that G23-P1 could not undergo MRI/lumbar puncture because anesthesia was required; G23-P2's MRI was unchanged at twelve months. Blood biochemical correction does not by itself establish brain repair. [C2–C3]

The underlying biochemical lesion is altered during treatment. The severe cardiac trajectory is the strongest human evidence that this may also change disease course. A broad disease-modification claim remains unproved: the reports do not establish prevention of irreversible neonatal injury, generalized neurological reversal or benefit after drug withdrawal. [C1–C3]

The appropriate development chain is therefore: defined delivered article → measured exposure → mutant-metabolite suppression → organ-specific benefit. A failure at one link must not be silently assigned to another.

## 5. Safety and source-value audit

The original two children had reported asymptomatic bilirubin elevations, with the other listed routine laboratory domains described as unremarkable. The independent Gold case adds a clinically relevant liver/muscle-enzyme signal and a dechallenge-based dose adaptation. This provides direct pediatric disease experience, while leaving a small and selected safety denominator. [C1–C3]

The [reproducible audit](enasidenib_clinical_audit.json) parses **177 populated measurement cells in 23 laboratory series and nine timepoint columns** from Gold's primary JATS Table 1. All are repeated measurements from **one child**. The following conflicts matter for interpretation:

| Issue | What the source actually contains | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Adverse-event date** | The table puts ALT 118 IU/L and CK 334 U/L at six months; the narrative describes the persistent elevation at eighteen months | Do not invent an exact adverse-event or dose-reduction date |
| **“Normalization”** | ALT 31 and CK 240 remain above the displayed upper limits of 28 and 198; CK 290 is also listed at 24 months | Describe improvement, with incomplete laboratory resolution in the tabulated record |
| **Early reduction percentage** | Urine 2-HGA 244→23 mg/g creatinine gives a calculated 90.6% decrease; prose states 84% | Large prompt suppression is supported; the exact percentage is internally inconsistent |
| **Utilization** | Abstract/body use different pretreatment intervals; body/discussion list different prior emergency-visit counts | Do not derive an adjudicated hospitalization-rate effect |
| **Socialization** | Case narrative uses 1→9-month age equivalents; discussion uses 2→10 months | Do not substitute a selected summary for raw longitudinal scores |

These issues narrow the precision of the report; they do not erase its clinical observations. The publication attributes suspected hepatitis to treatment and describes improvement after reduced dosing frequency. This review does not independently establish a pathological diagnosis or resolve the timing discrepancy. It also does not label asymptomatic indirect bilirubin elevation and aminotransferase injury as one interchangeable event.

The early positive laboratory response should not shorten long-term observation. The source itself contains later laboratory abnormalities and dose changes. A prospective project needs a coherent exposure, laboratory, medication and function timeline so that dose reductions can be evaluated for both benefit retention and harm reduction.

## 6. Natural history informs the study, not a market-size claim

Kranendijk's 2012 paper combines a review with **new primary questionnaire data**. Only that primary clinical dataset is used here as the historical phenotype anchor. Its type II subset includes 19 patients: 19 with developmental delay, 17 with hypotonia, 15 with seizures and 9 with cardiomyopathy. The cardiac group comprises seven dilated, one hypertrophic and one unspecified case. Mean onset was approximately three months, with a reported range from birth to two years. [C7]

The cohort contains ten living and nine deceased individuals at ascertainment. This is not a prospective survival curve, an incidence denominator or a reliable untreated outcome distribution for a new trial. The same paper notes that a minority could have static disease or improvement. Maturation or spontaneous stability therefore cannot be assumed absent when interpreting an uncontrolled treatment trajectory.

The paper separately describes 95 identified D-2-HGA patients overall, including 24 genetically classified type II cases at that time. These are different units from the 19-person questionnaire subset. Later papers' broad statements about roughly 20 or roughly 80 reported cases do not establish a current, deduplicated, living, genotype-confirmed, accessible treatment population. Use neither count to build revenue or recruitment projections. The scientific question can be worth funding even while the eventual commercial population remains unquantified. [C3, C4, C7]

For development, separate an established-cardiomyopathy population from newly diagnosed infants without demonstrable cardiac dysfunction and from predominantly neurological patients. A cardiac endpoint may be informative in the first group and have a ceiling in the second. Combining them into one mean response would discard the very baseline differences that make the existing cases interpretable.

## 7. The next clinical proof should answer a distinct question

The evidence supports an initial project that defines one intended pediatric article, measurement system and clinical question. It does not support building a large general-purpose preclinical package before deciding what the product improves. Four outputs would make that project concrete:

1. **A linked article/exposure/response specification.** Preserve salt/base basis, delivery form, administered dose, adherence, parent/metabolite exposure where feasible, sampling time and a chiral D-2-HG measure. Historical fixed-dose cases are benchmarks, not infant conversion rules.
2. **A claim-specific functional endpoint.** For established cardiac disease, use repeatable centrally reviewed imaging, rhythm and clinically relevant cardiac outcomes. For neurological benefit, use prospective function and seizure data with medication and therapy changes recorded. For early preservation, define age-appropriate longitudinal outcomes and a credible external or within-person comparator.
3. **A tolerability strategy capable of explaining dose changes.** Separate bilirubin handling from liver injury, track muscle-enzyme effects and other disease-relevant observations, and assess whether exposure reduction preserves the intended benefit. This is protocol design, not a dose recommendation.
4. **An interpretable comparison.** Retain supportive care, avoid manufacturing evidence through deliberate withdrawal of useful treatment, and prespecify how natural history, repeated baselines and masked outcome assessment will limit bias. A mechanistic model is valuable when it separates compound exposure failure, persistent structural damage and target failure.

**Advance the next development tranche** when the intended article produces reproducible exposure and durable biochemical engagement with a credible, clinically useful organ trajectory and acceptable tolerability. In an exploratory phase, a sufficiently interpretable signal can justify confirmation before a definitive causal estimate exists.

**Redirect the article or regimen** when target suppression is achievable but its intended exposure creates a reproducible unacceptable liability, or when delivery cannot be made reliable. **Narrow the clinical claim** when benefit is restricted to a tissue, severity or developmental window. **Lower the organ-rescue hypothesis** only when adequate engagement repeatedly fails to change an outcome in a system capable of detecting a meaningful treatment response. Persistent severe disability despite some gains is not equivalent to zero clinical benefit.

This remains an affirmative research recommendation. The incremental value could be a prospective clinical package, a defined infant delivery/exposure solution or a superior exposure–benefit relationship. It must be evaluated alongside the existing clinical development route. The primary evidence already supports proceeding to that definition without making an author reply a scientific-selection gate.

## 8. Access, verification and reproducibility

The original Nature main body remains subscription-limited in the retrieved page. Its public abstract, extended-material captions and complete public supplement were inspected; no inaccessible main-text details were filled from secondary summaries. Gold and Roux full-text XML were retrieved from Europe PMC and checked against available publisher/indexed material. The historical questionnaire table was retrieved from the original paper's XML. Source hashes, patient records and uncertainty fields are in [the structured dossier](enasidenib_clinical.json).

The five-week-old infant item remains metadata-only after publisher, Crossref, indexed-title/author and public conference-route checks. Crossref supplies no abstract. The conference page describes publication of accepted abstracts in the journal; poster access required login in the earlier round. No author contact, login bypass or outcome inference was used. The remaining gap is specific: dose, article, baseline, response, safety and cohort linkage remain unverified. It does not block the finite qualification judgment based on the older children.

The audit code reads the primary XML and reproduces the laboratory table and arithmetic. No wet-lab experiment, clinical intervention, purchased material or outreach occurred. Full source papers are working intermediates and should not be copied into the repository.
