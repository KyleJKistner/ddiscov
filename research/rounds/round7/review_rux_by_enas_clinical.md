# Independent clinical challenge: ruxolitinib / Hailey–Hailey disease

**Reviewer:** enasidenib clinical workstream. **Date:** 4 November 2026. Read-only review of `rux_hhd_clinical.md`, `rux_hhd_product.md`, `rux_hhd_development_proof.md` and the two source/clinical audit JSON records. No shared dossier edits were made.

## Decision

**The integrated recommendation passes this clinical challenge.** The evidence supports a finite controlled clinical proof with the defined marketed cream. It does not yet establish a proprietary S-tier asset. The draft correctly makes those two funding questions explicit. No material patient overcount, clinical-outcome inflation or mandatory mechanistic detour was found in the reviewed version.

The plan is appropriately matched to its evidence stage. Human exact-strength observations and an approved manufactured topical article make patient benefit the next decisive question. A new ATP2C1 model may explain or discriminate a product choice, but it should not become a mandatory gate merely to reproduce the structure of a preclinical BLU3797 dossier. Ongoing local control, reduced painful disease-days or lower treatment burden can constitute useful value without genetic correction or drug-free cure.

## Nonblocking refinements before publication or protocol writing

1. **Harmonize the planning window.** The clinical draft proposes 4–8 weeks for its initial controlled window; the integrated development proof proposes 8–12 weeks. Both are expressly assumptions, so this is not a factual conflict. Use one provisional integrated window or say clearly that the clinical draft presents an earlier feasibility option. Avoid presenting two unexplained plans as one executable package.

2. **Keep concordance distinct from several mandatory efficacy wins.** The proof chooses one primary estimand, which is appropriate. Its call for concordant pain, closure, rescue and recurrence evidence should mean that the overall patient value is coherent and not contradicted by major harm or burden. It should not become a requirement for separate statistical superiority on every secondary outcome. Meaningful maintenance or burden improvement can justify confirmation even if some secondary measures have ceilings or wide uncertainty.

3. **Specify background treatment in the eventual protocol.** The whole-product add-on comparison is an executable alternative when a matched vehicle cannot be obtained. It measures the incremental marketed-product effect, including vehicle and participant expectations. Blinded photographs reduce observer bias but do not blind patient-reported pain. The allowed background list should distinguish routine supportive care from therapeutic biologics or another JAK inhibitor. The current label does not recommend those combinations; historical combination reports do not automatically make them appropriate standard background for the first proof. The product draft already contains this label qualification. This is a protocol specification, not a reason to postpone the present scientific decision.

4. **Preserve the maintenance denominator.** If only induction responders enter a maintenance extension, report it as a responder-enriched cohort and retain the original randomized denominator in the overall flow. Ongoing control in that selected group would address maintenance after response, not the probability that an unselected HHD patient benefits. A single-arm extension can describe duration and burden; it cannot by itself isolate a maintenance treatment effect.

5. **Do not use this evidence to overstate near-term commercial readiness.** The plan appropriately treats a clinical package and a proprietary product as different deliverables. A useful randomized dataset can be fundable as clinical research or an existing sponsor's indication-development project. It does not itself establish exclusive use rights, an unoccupied market or a new-company investment case. This limitation is already visible and should remain in the integrated conclusion.

## Endpoint and gate assessment

The preferred patient-randomized parallel design is defensible for whole-person recurrent burden. A paired-lesion feasibility comparison remains a conditional alternative, with appropriate attention to lesion comparability, cream transfer, systemic spillover and patient-level analysis. The draft correctly avoids assigning independent whole-person quality-of-life observations to each fold and avoids treating repeated photographs as additional patients.

The suggested lesion-area estimand is a reasonable exploratory choice when the objective is established-lesion control. For a recurrence claim, patient-level painful-disease days may be better aligned, provided measurement reliability is established before selecting outcomes. Baseline severity, rescue use and infection treatment must remain attached to each patient's trajectory. The proposed useful-difference and precision approach is more defensible than deriving trial power from five selected case reports or the small DERM-101 subgroup.

The stop rules are proportionate. Adequate article delivery and an informative comparison should precede rejection of the intended clinical claim. An imprecise pilot is not definitive futility. Failed suppression of a speculative IFN biomarker is not failure of a useful clinical treatment; a negative immune-free keratinocyte model cannot exclude an inflammation-mediated mechanism. Conversely, marker changes without patient benefit do not establish the proposed product value.

**Final review status: pass with minor editorial/protocol refinements.** No additional laboratory experiment, unavailable preprint, author reply or commercial permission is needed to complete the present evidence-based prioritization. Clinical execution and product commercialization remain separate subsequent decisions, with the concrete preparation outputs already specified.
