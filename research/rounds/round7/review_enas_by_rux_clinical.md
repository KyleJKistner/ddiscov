# Independent review: enasidenib clinical and product dossiers

**Reviewer:** ruxolitinib/HHD clinical workstream. **Date:** 4 November 2026. **Scope:** causal claims, participant lineage, exposure interpretation, useful functional endpoints and the proposed first development investment. This review did not edit either enasidenib dossier.

## Assessment

**The dossiers pass this independent review for an affirmative, bounded research decision.** I found no material unsupported response count, transfer of another inhibitor's CNS penetration to enasidenib, claim of cardiac normalization in the severely affected 2023 child, or assertion that a public sprinkle patent proves present clinical supply. The major limitations are already preserved rather than hidden inside a positive summary.

The supportable project is a disease-specific pediatric article/exposure and longitudinal organ-function study. The record does not yet establish a controlled clinical effect size, validated infant exposure limits, a general neurological-rescue claim or an independently defensible commercial product. Those are distinct development questions; lack of the latter does not erase the case for the former.

## Challenges applied and findings

| Challenge | Finding | Consequence for the integrated dossier |
|---|---|---|
| Are five outcome records being inflated into five independent responders? | No. The clinical dossier establishes the original two and Gold case as distinguishable, leaves cross-publication identity of the short dermatology records unresolved, and excludes the metadata-only infant abstract from efficacy. | Keep “five patient records across three publications,” not “five of five responders” or a complete treated census. |
| Is cardiac recovery being generalized across dissimilar baselines? | No. Severe 2023 dysfunction, normal baseline systolic function in the second child, and Gold's milder pretreatment findings remain separate. The supplement's 20–25% to 40–45% trajectory is retained without calling it normalization. | Recovery, preservation and neurological claims need different baseline strata and outcomes. |
| Do biochemical and developmental changes prove neurological repair? | No. Chiral serum D-2-HG is distinguished from urine organic-acid measures; developmental age equivalents are not treated as equal-interval scores; unchanged/unavailable MRI and absent CNS PK remain visible. | Present useful observed skills as outcomes to test, not established brain repair or catch-up development. |
| Are repeated laboratory cells treated as a safety cohort? | No. The audit's 177 populated cells are explicitly repeated observations from one child. Timing, percentage, reference-range and utilization conflicts are retained. | Do not attach a denominator of 177 to safety, biological replication or response. |
| Is low bilirubin risk substituted for all hepatic safety? | No. UGT1A1-associated bilirubin elevation is separated from Gold's ALT/CK findings, and the exact event/dose-reduction date is not fabricated. | A shared longitudinal medication/exposure/laboratory record is a necessary output of prospective work. |
| Are generic models presented as exact-disease proof? | No. Homozygous erythroleukemia target-engagement cells, endogenous heterozygous mice, inducible transgenic mice and a proposed human cardiac pair have distinct roles. | Do not make a negative unqualified culture override the exact-disease human observations. |
| Is the AGI-026 late crossover overinterpreted? | No. The one-month functional improvement is correctly described as nonsignificant, while withdrawal and survival findings remain separate. The brain/plasma ratios stay attached to AGI-026. | Target-level reversibility supports the hypothesis; it is not an enasidenib-specific exposure or effect estimate. |
| Are adult PK values or A3 potency converted into infant risk bounds? | No. The 27.7 µM total/0.415 µM illustrative free-parent calculation is labelled assumption-dependent, and A3 antagonism is not declared to cause a clinical adverse effect. | Keep actual infant exposure, tissue response and chronic tolerability as measurements rather than inference from adult Cmax alone. |
| Does known pediatric formulation precedent remove all article risk? | No. Actual sprinkle use, public minitablet composition, adult crossover completion, supply and clinical-lot identity remain distinct. | A new formulation project must solve a residual delivery, exposure, burden or tolerability problem. |

## Precision refinements sent to the authors

### 1. Describe actual sprinkle use without implying proven lot identity

The clinical opening originally used “an exact pediatric sprinkle.” I recommended “an enasidenib pediatric sprinkle” because actual administration is verified while the historical lot/CMC identity with the patented 20 mg minitablets is not. The clinical author has incorporated this wording change.

The supplement reports **60 mg/day sprinkle for three months followed by “50 mg/day capsules”** in the second child. Preserve that source wording; do not silently convert the historical capsule account to marketed IDHIFA tablets. This is an article-provenance distinction, not a claim that the original report's dose is wrong. [Geoerger supplement]

### 2. Keep an exploratory exposure range exploratory

Product Stage B says a small feasibility study may identify a tolerable exposure range. I recommended characterizing **observed exposure, PD and tolerability**, or explicitly marking any range exploratory. A small selected cohort cannot validate a lifelong infant safety boundary simply by observing no early event. The draft already addresses delayed abnormalities and indefinite-use uncertainty; this refinement keeps the stage-gate wording aligned with that evidence.

### 3. Define functional benefit beyond a favorable single measurement

This is a design recommendation, not a correction to historical data. For a cardiac claim, central repeated imaging should retain dimensions indexed appropriately to growth, rhythm and concurrent cardiac therapy, plus a clinically useful functional outcome where feasible. A single improved ejection-fraction value should not carry the entire claim. The supplement shows a long trajectory with changing cardiac medicines; the draft correctly preserves that context.

For developmental and seizure outcomes, keep observation dates, item-level or suitable growth-scale data, therapy intensity, antiseizure changes and illness periods together. Counting acquired skills is clinically relevant; calling every acquired skill drug-caused is a separate inference. For prevention in newly diagnosed infants, a credible comparator trajectory is essential because normal baseline cardiac measurements cannot improve through the same range as established failure.

## Residual risks that should remain prominent

**Scientific risk:** strong biochemical suppression may not recover established structural or developmental damage. This should narrow the organ, age or severity claim when necessary; it should not force all patient value into a cure-versus-failure dichotomy.

**Exposure risk:** delivered infant dose, slow accumulation, active metabolite and concurrent-medication effects may determine whether biochemical suppression is achieved with acceptable long-term tolerability. Existing fixed-dose anecdotes are observations, not an infant dosing rule.

**Incremental-value risk:** a sprinkle and a manufacturer-linked development route already exist. A technically successful new preparation that solves no remaining delivery, exposure, tolerability or evidence problem could still fail the independent product thesis. The dossier's separate academic/foundation-enablement and commercial-development paths are appropriate.

**Evidence risk:** publication selection, small numbers, overlapping reports, incomplete source access and natural history prevent precise treatment-effect or recruitment estimates. These limits are explicitly represented. They do not require an author email or another generic animal experiment before defining the next funded project.

## Review conclusion

Proceed with the integrated dossier and the finite next-stage research recommendation. Retain the causal and source boundaries above. There is no newly identified evidence error that requires restarting the screen or stopping progression. The two wording refinements and the endpoint clarification strengthen interpretability; they are not new approval gates or a demand to execute clinical research in this sprint.
