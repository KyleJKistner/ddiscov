# Round 20 — mIGF-Gel clinical and practical comparator update

**Candidate:** `MIGF_CORNEA`. **Base commit:** `357c57c29c3916c005f07e37bf8420ec82d63905`; **base tree:** `3d68f3a5b7993dd4daeceb3f91cf92b1de49addf`. Evidence cut: **4 November 2026, America/Los_Angeles**. Retrieval: **5 November 2026 UTC**.

## Decision

**Retain mIGF-Gel as a conditional product-comparison question.** Topical insulin is a substantive clinical benchmark, including comparative observational evidence in persistent epithelial defects (PEDs). The retrieved evidence does not establish that insulin makes mIGF obsolete, that mIGF offers a clinical advantage, or that another independently capturable candidate clearly merits replacing this sprint. The current evidence makes a vaguely specified comparison against “insulin” inadequate: formulation, clinical setting, background care, dosing and follow-up can change the practical result.

The potentially useful contribution remains better repair or a meaningful reduction in treatment burden with adequate tissue and optical performance. Superiority on every endpoint is not necessary. Equal molar exposure between different molecules is not a universal product-comparison requirement; however, unsupported exposure or frequency equivalence cannot establish a mechanistic or dosing advantage. No clinical recommendation, new protocol, dose, sample size, numerical EV, experiment or spending authorization is created here.

## Human evidence must remain separated by population and design

| Primary record | Exact evidence | Limit for mIGF interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Fai et al., 2017 [C1] | Double-blind randomized study of acute corneal defects created during diabetic vitreoretinal surgery: 32 eyes/32 patients, eight per arm. Insulin 0.5, 1 or 2 units **per drop**, four times daily, versus placebo. At 72 hours, reported healing was 100% with 0.5 units/drop, versus 62.5% with placebo, 75% with 1 unit/drop and 62.5% with 2 units/drop. | An acute postoperative dose-ranging result. These units are not 1 IU/mL, and this is not a refractory chronic-PED or denervation trial. Small groups and one-month follow-up limit generalization. |
| Dasrilsyah et al., 2023 [C2] | Double-blind randomized study in the same general postoperative diabetic setting: 38 eyes/38 patients, 19 per arm; 0.5 units topical insulin four times daily versus Vismed sodium hyaluronate 0.18% four times daily. Baseline-to-closure rate was 1.20 ± 0.29 versus 0.78 ± 0.20 mm²/hour. | Supports an acute clinical healing effect against an active tear formulation. Full formulation and drop volume were not recovered from the primary abstract; no conversion to the 1 IU/mL PED regimens is made. |
| Diaz-Valle et al., 2022 [C13] | Historical-control PED comparison: 61 insulin patients from 2019–2021 versus 23 autologous-serum patients from 2018–2019. Closure occurred in 51/61 versus 11/23. Mean closure time **among healers** was 32.6 versus 82.6 days. | This is relevant comparative evidence, but not randomized evidence. Incomplete follow-up or missing images caused exclusion. Calendar period, selection, rescue decisions and background care can confound the contrast. |
| Abdi et al., 2024 [C3] | Prospective, nonrandomized series of 23 eyes/23 patients with PED after at least two weeks of conventional treatment. Regular Actrapid-derived insulin in balanced salt solution, 1 IU/mL four times daily: 16 complete closures within the reported maximum 50-day follow-up; seven nonresponders. | Deterioration or failure to show significant reduction after five treatment days triggered withdrawal into the nonresponder category and other intervention. The 16/23 result is not a randomized effect or a uniform long-term outcome. |
| Lixi et al., July 2026 [C4] | Retrospective series of 45 eyes/43 patients: 28 eyes with chronic ocular-surface disease and 17 with stage 2 neurotrophic keratopathy (NK). Closure in 44/45; median 29 days, 95% CI 24–38. | An uncontrolled, selected cohort. Active infection and anticipated corneal procedures were excluded. The sole nonclosing eye was lost after 90 days. High closure does not identify the effect attributable to insulin. |
| Lammens et al., October 2026 [C5] | Retrospective cohort of 57 eyes/45 patients with several ocular-surface diagnoses. In the **28-eye PED subset**, 20 closed, six partially closed and two did not improve; the abstract reports closure after 5.2 weeks. | The entire 57-eye cohort is not a PED-closure denominator. “Treatment success” in NK, dry eye and GVHD is not automatically the same endpoint as complete PED closure. Full definitions, treatment histories and recurrence were not recovered. |

The historical insulin-versus-serum comparison used **1 IU/mL Actarapid-derived insulin in a polyethylene-glycol/polypropylene-glycol base** (the paper's vehicle wording) versus **20% autologous serum**, both every six hours; both groups received the same preservative-free hyaluronic-acid tears. Recurrence was reported in 7/61 versus 10/23, but follow-up averaged **8.6 versus 23.0 months**. That imbalance prevents treating the crude recurrence contrast as a demonstrated durability advantage. Nonsignificant baseline comparisons also do not establish exchangeable groups. The authors acknowledge the historical controls, multiple comparisons and referral selection. [C13]

These reports are neither one pooled cohort nor interchangeable product replications. Different insulin preparations, patient etiologies, outcome definitions, observation periods and continuing care prevent ranking their percentages against one another or against rat mIGF results.

## The newly incorporated insulin cohorts refine the claim

In the July 2026 cohort, the published product was Humalog-derived 1 IU/mL insulin in a PEG/propylene-glycol/hydroxypropyl-guar vehicle, with a reported refrigerated 30-day shelf life. Patients used it four times daily, continued for two weeks after closure, and then stopped. Tear substitutes, nocturnal ointments and indicated disease-specific support continued. Median postclosure observation was 95 days; two eyes recurred, one progressing to melting and Gundersen flap surgery. The reported median visual-acuity improvement from 2.0 to 1.0 logMAR is uncontrolled, and corneal-sensitivity categories were unchanged. A finite course with subsequent observation is present, but it is not a controlled demonstration of autonomous, permanent repair. The July 17 article update concerns an author ORCID, not an efficacy correction. [C4]

A separate August 2026 report describes **24 patients** treated with insulin 1 IU/mL every six hours. Its accessible primary abstract reports smaller defects and improved Cochet–Bonnet sensitivity in all quadrants at one month, six partial responders and two recurrences. It does not provide an accessible numeric sensory effect magnitude or randomized control. The abstract describes one month of treatment while also reporting mean use of 45.6 days; the detailed treatment course cannot be reconstructed from the abstract. Complete-closure denominators, maintenance/co-treatment details and the timing of recurrence remain unverified. This is a limited human sensory signal, not proof of structural reinnervation. It prevents an assumption that insulin has no possible functional sensory effect. [C6]

The October cohort reports success of 83.9% in 31 NK eyes, 44.4% in nine dry-eye eyes and 14.3% in seven GVHD eyes. Those etiologic contrasts are observational and their success definition is not available in the retrieved abstract. One transient intraocular-pressure increase was reported. Neither this finding nor “no treatment-related adverse events” in small other cohorts establishes unrestricted ocular safety. [C5]

## “Practical insulin” is a specified product and service

The published stability findings make both a blanket fragility claim and a blanket easy-product claim untenable.

| Source | Tested presentation and reported finding | What cannot be transferred |
|---|---|---|
| 2026 stability study [C9] | Human insulin at 1 or 5 IU/mL in normal saline or Liquifilm, in polyethylene bottles under refrigeration, retained the reported analytical stability for 60 days unopened and 15 days under the authors' in-use condition. Systane preparations developed turbidity after 72 hours. | Another insulin species, vehicle, container, in-use period or mIGF formulation. These are published analytical/physical/microbiological observations; no new testing was performed here. |
| 2025 stability study [C10] | A different presentation—25 IU/mL insulin in sodium hyaluronate 0.18%, in low-density polyethylene bottles—remained stable for one month at the two tested storage temperatures, 5 ± 3°C and 30 ± 2°C. | The 1 IU/mL clinical regimens, every artificial-tear brand, every storage setting, or a clinical potency/benefit guarantee. |
| Spanish randomized-trial protocol [C7] | The proposed comparator is regular Actrapid-derived insulin 1 IU/mL in saline, four times daily; hospital-pharmacy preparation, refrigeration, cold-chain distribution and discard seven days after opening are described. | An observed outcome, a market-ready universal formulation, a verified supply quote or a trivial handling burden. |

The July clinical cohort and historical comparison used other insulin/vehicle combinations. Their reported shelf lives cannot override a separate stability study's results, and the latter cannot invalidate those clinical observations without an exact product comparison. The active protein, concentration, vehicle, packaging, analytical method, storage and use conditions must be kept together.

An inexpensive ingredient does not determine the delivered cost of a sterile ophthalmic service. No acquisition quote, compounding price, supply agreement, reimbursement estimate or clinically validated mIGF convenience advantage was recovered. A product could have value through reduced dosing or handling burden, but a shorter dosing schedule in rats is not sufficient to establish that advantage for people. The inherited $40–150k/3–6-month assumption is not validated by these comparator publications, and a preliminary qualification deliverable cannot inherit the rank of an informative useful-function comparison merely because it fits the same price range.

## Controlled studies and the relevant approved benchmark

The Spanish protocol proposes **234 participants**, allocated to insulin 1 IU/mL four times daily or autologous serum 20% five to six times daily, with continued indicated background care. It distinguishes neurotrophic, chronic ocular-surface and exploratory infectious strata; combination treatment may follow an insufficient response. The publication is a protocol. Its expectation of completing recruitment by December 2025 is not evidence that recruitment finished or that results exist. The legacy EudraCT record displays “Ongoing” without results; the modern official CTIS page yielded no readable content. This audit does not claim a verified live recruiting count or randomized PED outcome. [C7,C8]

For an **NK indication**, cenegermin is an established active benchmark. The current US label describes 20 µg/mL, six daily applications for eight weeks. At week eight, its label reports complete healing in 15/23 versus 4/24 vehicle patients in NGF0214 and 36/50 versus 17/51 in NGF0212; patients with no postbaseline measurement were excluded. Recurrences occurred in a subset of healed patients, and the label calls the mean sensitivity changes clinically insignificant. Those results support controlled epithelial healing without proving universal sensory restoration. The handling burden includes cold storage, a delivery kit and a daily opened-vial limit. They do not establish mIGF equivalence, an available mIGF commercial position or a current price. [C11]

Cenegermin's separate **Orunea PCED Phase 3 program** was announced as having dosed its first patient on June 9, 2026, with approximately 150 adults planned and complete epithelial healing as the primary endpoint. This sponsor disclosure concerns a broader investigational indication, not an observed PCED efficacy result. The direct ClinicalTrials.gov page was a shell. The source was already known in Round 17; checking it for this mIGF product context does not reopen the closed RM4404 source pass or infer new results. [C12]

## Marginal-value consequence and stopping boundary

The evidence favors a **defined clinical use and practical comparator specification** over another generic demonstration of epithelial proliferation. An informative next commitment must address whether mIGF can offer useful repair, acceptable optical and tissue performance, and a meaningful practical advantage over care that patients could actually receive. Retention, biomarker activation and favorable comparisons with PBS do not answer that product question on their own.

The record does not require equal molar dosing of insulin, IGF1 and the peptide as a universal development gate, nor does it require mIGF superiority in every corneal disorder. Conversely, a weak or mismatched insulin regimen cannot establish differentiation. If an exact comparison shows equivalent useful outcomes and comparable burden, the incremental financial case would weaken; that outcome has not been observed here. An epithelial model also cannot disqualify a comparator's sensory or clinical benefit that the model cannot represent.

The bounded pass is closed. Reopening requires an identifiable, decision-changing artifact: randomized PED outcomes; exact-product clinical or tissue-function evidence; a meaningful stability/handling/exposure result; or actionable cost/access information. Missing full details in an abstract are retained as uncertainty, not replaced by invented denominators, and no additional generic insulin search is justified now. No new mIGF-treated person, direct mIGF-versus-insulin clinical result or revised numerical allocation was added.
