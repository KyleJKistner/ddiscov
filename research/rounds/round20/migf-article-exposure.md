## Decision

Retain a **conditional local-product and active-comparator question**. The primary paper supports a rat repair signal, a small sensory result and plausible formulation benefits. It does not establish efficacy in human persistent corneal epithelial defects, chronic denervation, or superiority to topical insulin. This pass closes the accessible main-text audit and explicitly retains the unavailable supplementary details. It produces no new biological result or efficacy-tier promotion.

The inherited fifth-place conditional development rank and **$40–150k / 3–6-month** envelope belong to a useful local-product/comparator question. They are not verified execution terms. A preliminary article, assembly or exposure qualification package that cannot answer that question must be valued separately; it cannot inherit the original rank merely by fitting the same budget. No spending or experimental execution is authorized here. [L1]

## Exact article and assay state

The reported article is **Biotin–D-phenylalanine–YIGSSSR**. The cap, residue order and D stereochemistry are part of the article identity; a generic IGF peptide or another gel is not an equivalent substitute. The main text states that analytical identity was characterized, but the supporting spectra are in the unread supplement. Full terminal chemistry, salt, impurity limits and a future batch's identity are not independently authenticated by this audit. No synthesis recipe or new material specification is generated. [S1,S2]

| Reported observation | Decision interpretation |
|---|---|
| Transparent assembled formulation at 0.4 wt%; critical aggregation concentration 205 µM. | Formulation state matters. A reported nominal concentration alone does not establish the amount or form reaching the relevant tissue. |
| Cell work uses 10–400 nM, with peak proliferation at 50 nM; relevant comparisons use IGF-1 at 10 ng/mL. | These are distinct assay concentrations below the reported micromolar aggregation threshold for mIGF. The active species and persistence of any preformed assemblies in the cell medium were not established here. |
| Single-motif, scrambled-peptide and blank-hydrogel controls appear in the delayed model. | Material controls exist. Their different gelation/solubility behavior prevents treating them as perfectly matched physical controls that isolate one motif's effect. |

The cell results support biological activity under the reported conditions. They do not prove that the cell treatment was the same physical formulation state as the instilled gel, or specify a free epithelial exposure target. Being below a reported aggregation threshold does not by itself prove instantaneous or complete disassembly. Likewise, different doses and physical forms do not invalidate a practical comparison of finished products. They limit mechanistic attribution and claims of equal delivered bioactivity. A commercially useful product may be compared at its feasible regimen; identical molarity is not a universal commercial requirement. [S1; interpretation]

A control-description inconsistency remains visible: the initial control list and proliferation discussion give the scrambled sequence as Biotin–D-FGIYSRSR, whereas the TEM paragraph lists Biotin–D-FGIYSRSS. This audit does not choose between them or infer that the mIGF article itself was wrong. A future control-specific interpretation would require resolution from the relevant primary record. [S1]

All numerical observations in the table are reported values, not recalculated effect estimates. Figure-legend n values are retained as reported sample counts. The record does not permit a complete reconstruction of randomized rats, treated eyes, exclusions, independent tissue samples and technical measurements for each delayed-model endpoint. The general statistical paragraph says at least three independent biological experiments; that statement does not resolve the animal/eye accounting. [S1]

The acute model explicitly describes random allocation. Delayed-model allocation and masking procedures were not independently recovered at the level needed to audit the small functional result. The accessible methods also do not specify the sensory instrument/calibration or a complete sensory scoring procedure. These are reporting/access uncertainties, not findings that the study was necessarily unrandomized, unmasked or improperly performed. No raw individual-animal dataset was recovered; the paper directs data requests to the corresponding author, and no request was made. [S1]

Closure, histological stratification, laminin/integrin localization, nerve-positive area and sensation measure different things. The paper's marker and architecture results support a repair-quality hypothesis. They do not by themselves establish mechanical epithelial adhesion strength, sustained barrier integrity, recurrent-erosion prevention, visual performance or durability after treatment stops. Those claims must remain distinct. A clinical product also need not deliver permanent off-treatment cure to have useful on-treatment value. [S1; interpretation]

## Retention, exposure and product performance

The retention experiments track fluorescent formulations. The main text reports detectable gel-associated signals over tens of minutes and approximately 54% of initial fluorescence at 30 minutes, while using different assays that give different end-of-detection times. Fluorescence location and persistence are not a direct assay of intact, bioactive peptide concentration at the epithelial target. No 24-hour free-exposure coverage can be inferred from a once-daily efficacy regimen. [S1]

The reported **2.4-fold Cmax and 2.6-fold AUC** increases come from **aqueous-humor sampling in injured rabbits**, with HPLC analysis; they are not measurements from the rat healing groups or direct measurements of epithelial target exposure. The accessible methods do not establish dose-normalized equivalence between the distinct molecules or provide enough analytical detail to audit the full PK comparison. The finding is supportive delivery evidence with these compartment, species and assay qualifications. It is not a measured therapeutic window. [S1]

The proteinase-K comparison also uses different analytical readouts for mIGF and recombinant IGF-1. The authors acknowledge that it is supportive rather than strictly equivalent evidence. It should not be converted into a quantified ocular half-life or a human dosing advantage. The applied proteinase challenge is not a direct measurement of proteolysis in a patient's tear film. [S1]

The formulation is reported as transparent, spreading across the rat ocular surface on OCT, and without observed irritation in the short rat assessment. These findings do not establish finished-product sterility, storage stability, tolerability during chronic use, visual acuity/contrast performance or a safe margin across the intended clinical population. The main text explicitly states that in vivo metabolic fate and final clearance were not investigated. The safety signal is bounded to the reported conditions. [S1]

## What the frequency result can change

The comparison justifies investigating a reduced-frequency product. It does not yet demonstrate a practical advantage over topical insulin, and it does not isolate frequency from dose, retention, formulation state or potency. The exact delayed-model topical concentrations, all administered volumes, total delivered active exposure and a qualified response to each practical regimen remain unresolved in the accessible record. The paper contains no direct mIGF-versus-insulin result. [S1]

A future decision should value the benefit delivered by a specified product under a usable regimen: repair quality and recurrence, treatment burden, optical performance and tolerability matter together. That decision does not require every competing molecule to share the same molarity. Conversely, an attractive dosing label cannot compensate for an unqualified product, an uninformative model or lack of useful advantage over simpler care. No dose, protocol, sample size or success threshold is proposed here.

The finite retrieval consisted of the main article, Figure 8 legend, bibliographic/publisher records, one exact-product clinical/follow-up query and one exact-DOI supplementary query. The searches returned the same primary study and unrelated or comparator clinical programs; no additional exact-mIGF human result or independent active-comparator result was recovered. This is a bounded-search result, not proof that no other evidence exists.

The listed **4.6 MB mmc1.docx** supplement was not read. Its PMC link returned a reCAPTCHA interstitial; the publisher article returned 403; the official publisher-CDN attachment path was inaccessible to retrieval. No challenge was bypassed. Those responses do not establish missing or absent data. Additional generic literature searches would not resolve the specific methods and product questions, so this source pass is closed. [S2–S4]

Reopening requires a substantive accessible artifact that could change a named commitment: the exact supplementary methods or animal-level allocation, an independent exact-product functional result, relevant exposure/product data, or a credible active-comparator outcome. A new broad review, another marker result or a differently named gel is insufficient by itself. APR-246, RM4404, BLU-3797, NDC-9009 and earlier named source closures remain intact.
