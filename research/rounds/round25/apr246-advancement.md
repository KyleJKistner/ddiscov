## 2. The intended product and the claim boundary

The retained claim is **useful sustained control of persistent AEC erosions and treatment burden during continued use, with acceptable tissue integrity and local/systemic exposure**. The existing two-child report is an uncontrolled same-disease signal, with later reports conservatively treated as overlapping. It is not a response-rate estimate. The [Round 16 clinical audit](../round16/apr-clinical-evidence.md) remains controlling for patient/site lineage, the unresolved variant annotation and incomplete dose-transfer information.

Coverage, symptoms and care burden can make an ongoing-control product worthwhile. Complete normalization of cornification, elimination of a proposed molecular mechanism and persistence after stopping treatment are not universal requirements. Full barrier restoration and durable off-treatment regeneration remain stronger, separate claims.

The central translational uncertainty is whether a specified, deliverable article produces worthwhile incremental control under an interpretable comparison. A marker-only result cannot settle that question. Neither can an uninterpretable model negative, inadequate product delivery or a different intervention's success. Qualification is valuable when it separates these explanations and changes a continuation decision.

The new model documents therefore support an option, not a compulsory preclinical reset. A properly governed clinical-development route could ultimately be more informative. That alternative requires its own product, exposure, oversight, comparator and cost assessment and lies outside current execution authority.

## 3. A concrete next-commitment specification

**Milestone to evaluate:** obtain a package showing whether the existing APR topical article and a defined evidence route can support an interpretable test of useful ongoing control, with a bounded cost, an explicit right to stop, and a plausible way for ddiscov to retain the resulting value.

This is a scope for evaluating a future commitment, not an instruction to perform biological work.

| Required deliverable | What it must settle | What it does not buy |
|---|---|---|
| Exact article transfer assessment | Whether the historical starting article can be represented and evaluated reliably, which essential product/exposure facts remain missing, and the cost of resolving them. | Clinical supply qualification, a chronic safety margin or a new formulation by default. |
| Evidence-route fit assessment | Whether an existing route can answer the stated uncertainty, which result would change the decision, and whether a negative could be interpreted. | A presumption that an accessible cell line is a qualified disease model or that every model is clinically predictive. |
| Comparative benefit specification | The clinically meaningful increment sought against appropriate care or the relevant reference, including total treatment burden and tissue integrity. | Superiority inferred from appearance, a mechanistic marker, or a comparison of unlike endpoints. |
| Access and output-rights schedule | The required inputs, actual access conditions, permitted use of outputs, and remaining product/data interests affecting capture. | Free commercial rights inferred from research availability, an inactive sponsor program or a public patent. |
| Staged cost and timing | What the first block purchases, which dependencies can expand it, and the distinct decision and price for subsequent work. | An open-ended program concealed inside an initial qualification estimate. |

The documentation presently defines the question and several starting points supported by sources. It does not supply an executable offer, verified materials/model access, a completed transfer package or actual costs. Those unknowns must remain blank in the structured decision record.

### Qualification and useful-function commitments remain separate

A documentation or transfer-only block can be worthwhile when a failure would cheaply prevent a larger commitment. Its success means that the next question can be asked more reliably. It does not mean that APR improves useful function.

If the first available proposal supplies only identity, model access or marker readouts, compare that narrower information purchase on its own merits. If it requires substantial formulation invention, a new model platform, extensive clinical manufacture or expensive rights before informative work becomes possible, the original small-option premise has changed. Recompare the revised opportunity with RM4404, BLU-3797 and cash before expanding it.

The inherited **US$25–100k / two-to-five-month** range is an analyst assumption from the earlier review. It is not a current quote, verified schedule, commitment or evidence of superior EV. It excludes clinical trials, major model construction, licensing, GLP, GMP and commercialization. This round supplies no replacement number.

## 4. Decision branches and stopping rules

| Information eventually established | Next decision |
|---|---|
| The existing article and a suitable evidence route can answer a consequential question at acceptable terms | Consider the specifically scoped first block against the best current alternative. Approval of one block does not release later spending. |
| The first block resolves transfer or model uncertainty but not useful function | Decide separately whether the now-better-defined function or clinical question warrants its own commitment. |
| A credible benefit signal appears at interpretable exposure with adequate tissue integrity | Consider independent confirmation and the next product/capture question appropriate to the narrow claim. Broader genotype claims need additional support. |
| A qualified result excludes worthwhile benefit for the chosen claim | Stop or narrow that claim. Do not relabel the result as success because a marker moved. |
| The result is inconclusive because article delivery, model fit or measurement was inadequate | Diagnose whether a finite remedy would be informative; do not infer biological failure or authorize indefinite assay development. |
| The route expands into substantial new invention or inaccessible inputs/rights | End the assumed small tranche and compare the new opportunity from its actual remaining costs. |
| Useful patient benefit is plausible but ddiscov cannot retain adequate financial value | Decline the commercial thesis at those terms; any public-benefit evidence project needs a separately stated purpose and scope. |
| No better defined or priced opportunity emerges | Keep capital uncommitted and reopen only for a named substantive artifact. |

These are claim-specific decision rules. No new statistical success threshold, clinical minimum important difference or probability of technical success is fabricated. Defining those quantities requires the chosen outcome, variability, intended use and actual work scope.

## 5. Value capture: what would make this an investment

The program has at least three distinct possible forms. None is established as an offered transaction.

| Possible contribution | Value that could be retained | Missing evidence that controls the decision |
|---|---|---|
| A product contribution connected to existing rights and know-how | A negotiated economic interest in a differentiated topical product or indication development. | Current title, required licenses/data access, actual terms and a useful product contribution. |
| A genuinely differentiated product improvement | Value from a demonstrated delivery, tolerability or burden advantage and whatever defensible rights attach to that contribution. | A real unmet product problem and incremental benefit; inventing a vehicle solely to create IP does not establish value. |
| Evidence generation with defined downstream rights | Useful decision information and contracted rights to use resulting data or a resulting option. | Who can use the outputs, which later decision they influence, and why the retained interest exceeds total remaining cost. |

The financial question is the incremental value of the next decision after costs, sharing and dilution, including the option to stop. Publication of another positive observation alone does not answer it. A scientifically useful finding can be worth producing while still failing to support a proprietary drug company.

Current public records cannot price private rights or establish willingness to transact. Repeating generic searches for those absent terms is unlikely to change the decision. An author response remains unnecessary for selecting the scientific question; actual commitment terms cannot be presumed from that principle.
