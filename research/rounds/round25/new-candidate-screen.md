## 1. A36 for Huntington disease — R25-NEW-01

**Exact thesis.** Stabilizing the interaction between endogenous calpastatin (CAST) and calpain-2 could suppress pathological proteolysis while preserving neuronal integrity and useful motor function in HD. The selected article is the specific CHIR99021-derived compound **A36**, not CHIR99021, all calpain inhibitors, or a generic “mitochondrial restoration” platform. The March 2026 Science Advances paper names A36 and links synthetic and analytical material. This screen did not independently authenticate its full supplement or a physical lot. [R25-N01]

**What the primary evidence supports.** The authors connect the cellular response to CAST and calpain-2, with findings consistent with stabilizing their interaction and reducing pathological signaling. HD patient-derived medium spiny neurons show improvement in neurite morphology and neuronal/synaptic markers. These observations strengthen a useful-function hypothesis but are not proof of mature human circuit restoration. The donor and line-level denominator needs supplement reconciliation; repeated experiments are not additional patients.

In R6/2 mice, the paper reports improved locomotor activity, clasping behavior, striatal pathology and survival over the study window. It reports 10–15 mice per behavioral group. Survival is observed through 12 weeks, so the result must not be relabeled as a median-lifespan extension. Animals were assigned cage by cage and outcomes assessed blinded; the male, rapid fragment model and young treatment window limit generality. The separate PS19 tauopathy experiment supplies cross-disease support but is neither an independent HD replication nor a human AD result. [R25-N01]

**What can break the thesis.** The authors acknowledge that A36 still needs improved metabolic stability and in-vivo half-life, and that long-term consequences of reinforcing CAST are unknown. Removing detected GSK3 inhibition in the reported assays does not establish universal selectivity. No independent reproduction, adult clinical reversal, or patient outcome was established. Useful continuing disease control could still be valuable; permanent repair is not required. The important question is whether the actual product can deliver a meaningful function at a tolerable exposure, rather than whether every aspect of pathology is reversed. [R25-N01, R25-N06]

**Rights and financing.** The paper discloses Case Western Reserve University provisional **US 63/662,559**, filed 21 June 2024, and PCT-pending status. Analog material remains in the investigator laboratory and is available subject to scientific review. This identifies a plausible institutional ownership route; it does not establish present title, live claim coverage, freedom to operate, an available license, or a price. The exact A36-to-claims relationship remains unverified. [R25-N01, R25-N07]

A serious proposal would be a licensed or partnered preclinical validation and optimization program. It should not inherit the cost assumptions of a generic repositioning exercise. No supplier quote, financing amount, or success probability has been invented.

**Cheapest decisive next information gate.** Audit the already-linked exact-article analytical record, model provenance and public patent family together. Determine whether the useful-function result survives correct denominators and timing, and whether an obtainable contribution can be defined before constructing a priced scope. Stop discretionary screen work if those records cannot identify a sufficiently specific asset/question or if the proposed scope expands into a broad new CNS discovery program without a better marginal-dollar case. An owner reply is not a prerequisite for scientific selection; an asserted commercial transaction would require evidence of rights.

**Comparison.** A36 combines mammalian motor outcomes with patient-derived neuronal evidence and a plausible differentiated-molecule capture route. The remaining product and rights work prevents it from displacing the inherited conditional queue at an unquoted price. Cash remains the default. [R25-N01, R25-N06–07]

## 2. Dipyridamole for ALS — R25-NEW-02

**Exact thesis and lineage.** The May 2026 bioRxiv preprint proposes that dipyridamole supports mitochondrial pyruvate entry and protects ALS motor neurons. It is one investigator study lineage, accessed as a preprint. Parent dipyridamole and any future CNS-optimized analog are different assets. [R25-N02]

**Denominators and endpoints matter.** The principal experiments use a C9orf72 patient-derived iPSC line and its isogenic control. The paper reports prolonged culture survival, improved mitochondrial bioenergetics, and preserved neurites. Its striking survival observation extends cultured-neuron survival beyond 100 days from approximately 18 days in the comparison condition. That is not a patient-survival result.

The main text also explicitly reports **three additional C9 iPSC lines from different ALS patients**, with mitochondrial and neurite improvement, referring to Fig S6. Thus, calling the entire study a one-patient experiment would understate the evidence. Conversely, claiming all survival, bioenergetic and late-intervention endpoints in four independent patient backgrounds would overstate it. Supplemental provenance and endpoint coverage were not separately audited. Technical replicate counts do not add donors.

An engineered TARDBP/TDP43 M337V model with a reverted control supplies a different genetic model, not another patient donor. PSEN1 A246E cortical neurons are a separate AD use: mitochondrial effects are reported, but survival improvement is limited. These backgrounds remain parts of one research lineage. [R25-N02]

**Positive evidence and limits.** The paper includes treatment after cellular abnormalities emerge, which makes the result more relevant than prevention alone; it does not establish reversal of adult ALS disability. Control neurons also benefit, leaving a culture-specific metabolic-support explanation relevant. The MPC dependence is mechanistically useful, while the precise molecular interaction remains unresolved. Riluzole and edaravone's weak response in this culture system does not demonstrate clinical superiority.

The paper does not present an in-vivo ALS functional result and describes such work as ongoing. CNS exposure is the central practical uncertainty. The authors discuss conflicting blood–brain-barrier evidence, including older negative reports and later animal findings. Neither symptomatic observations in unrelated CNS indications nor disease-associated barrier leakage establish adequate unbound CNS exposure for this ALS use. This screen has not recovered an exposure-matched human efficacy bridge. [R25-N02]

**Capture and financing.** The authors disclose **US 17/756,842** and **PCT/US26/15790**; Ronald Davis is founder and shareholder of Mitochondrial Medicines Corporation. The bounded searches did not resolve exact claims, license availability, or a distinct independent commercial contribution. Generic procurement is not proprietary ALS economics. A new analog or formulation would introduce additional chemistry, product and rights work, with parent efficacy not automatically transferable. [R25-N02, R25-N07]

**Cheapest decisive next information gate.** Reconcile existing human pharmacokinetics, protein binding and CNS measurements with the active cellular exposures; read the disclosed patent families and sponsor scope. Stop if a clinically relevant exposure bridge or a differentiated obtainable contribution cannot be specified. Do not launch an analog program merely to avoid a negative parent-drug exposure result.

**Comparison.** The survival phenotype and additional patient backgrounds are substantive positives. Preprint status, endpoint-specific breadth, uncertain human exposure and retained economics keep dipyridamole behind A36 for bounded information work and outside paid development. It does not displace GA002's distinct clinical-information role or the inherited conditional queue. [R25-N02, R25-N06–07]

## 3. Atorvastatin for SRD5A3-CDG — R25-NEW-03

**Exact use and version control.** The July 2026 Communications Medicine article follows the January 2026 preprint; these are the same study, not independent confirmation. It proposes reducing mevalonate-pathway flux to mitigate the consequences of SRD5A3 deficiency. Prior atorvastatin work in SPG5 is a different exact therapeutic use. [R25-N03, R25-N06]

**The full text is weaker than an abstract-level “human-cell rescue” description.** The worm model shows improvement in movement and other disease-relevant phenotypes. In four patient fibroblast lines, three show a directional reduction in the polyprenol-to-dolichol ratio, but the aggregate change is **not statistically significant, p = 0.08**. The paper explicitly leaves correction of glycosylation for future work. It also reports high-exposure worm toxicity and a mismatch between the worm metabolite response and the human fibroblast ratio response. This is a plausible pathway observation, not demonstrated restoration of human neuronal function. [R25-N03]

**The negative evidence must retain its exact scope.** A retrieved author manuscript reports a single DHDDS p.K42E patient with edema and skin symptoms during attempts with four statins, including atorvastatin; symptoms resolved after withdrawal. It explicitly reports **no retinal worsening** and cannot establish that defective dolichol synthesis caused the adverse symptoms. That is a different genotype and clinical context, so it is not a failed SRD5A3 trial. The same letter mentions unpublished lovastatin sensitivity of glycosylation in SRD5A3/DHRSX-deficient cells. This raises a directly relevant pathway concern, while remaining a different statin and incompletely reported cell dataset. It supports careful reconciliation, not a class-wide conclusion of harm or safety. [R25-N04]

**Capture and financing.** A generic drug simplifies some starting-material questions but does not create a moat. The paper includes Modelis and Perlara-affiliated authors and declares no competing interests; that declaration is not evidence that no relevant rights or programs exist. The exact-use trial/patent query yielded no decisive available-rights or program record. No indication-specific formulation, process or other defensible contribution has been established. Existing use in other populations does not make the proposed neurological pediatric program cheap or clinically ready. [R25-N03, R25-N07]

**Cheapest decisive next information gate.** Reconcile the positive paper with the statin caution at genotype, endpoint and metabolite/glycosylation level using existing records. Determine whether an actual useful human-cell functional result and a differentiated product route now exist. Hold if the thesis remains worm rescue plus a nonsignificant lipid ratio, or if a generic implementation cannot support retained value. Do not extrapolate to other dolichol disorders.

**Comparison.** This is the lowest information priority among the retained three. It supplies no supported reason to divert a development dollar from cash or the inherited conditional queue. [R25-N03–04, R25-N06–07]
